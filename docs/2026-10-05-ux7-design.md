> UX7 and UX7a–d, with the screen halves of ACCT1 and AGENTS2, decided as **D152** (2026-10-05). Nothing here is built.
> `Dnn` is `docs/decisions/Dnn.md`. The owner, looking at the screens UX6 built on the install the same day: *"I think the
> UI/UX is kind of bad right now, and also we should be utilizing the top menu, so instead of calling this 'agent' we
> might need a better menu (more like VS Code); also the account page itself needs its UI/UX improved"* · *"both title
> sections, for quest and session, need their UI/UX improved"*.

page header (D118 §3b, the platform language §5). §8 lists each.

### 1.5 What the three share

- **A title made of whatever its first line was.** It leads three pages and every row, and nothing on screen can make it
  shorter without rewriting it: SESSUX1j's short title is the real fix, at its source (§5.4).
- **One fact, said three times**: the session's title, the account (meta, four chain rows, the opening line), *2 minutes*.
- **A raw id as a line of its own** on all three heads. An id is for copying; it belongs in a ⋯ and a folded detail.
- **Explanation set as content**: the own sign-in's paragraph, the fine print, the meta line's eight labels.
- **English where 中文 was chosen**: the opening line, `ask` inside mono, the version's *(Claude Code)*.

## 2. Five rules

1. **Title first, and short.** A page's head is its title on one line where it can be (two where it is a page's whole
   subject), its state beside it, and at most two facts and an age on a second line. Everything else is folded or moved.
2. **A record whose state moves leads with its state** (a session, a quest, an ask: *status leads*, the platform language
   §4); **a thing leads with its name** (an agent, a plugin, a repository).
3. **A fact is said once on a page.** An id is in the ⋯ and in *Details*; a time is said once; a name repeated on every
   row of a list is said once at its head.
4. **A row is a line at 1546 px.** A list of like things lines its facts up in columns while the main area holds them, and
   stacks below that. A row says nothing it has nothing to say: no *0 sessions*, no *nothing said yet*.
5. **The menu bar holds the application's verbs and places, as an IDE's does, and one table owns them.** The menus, the
   palette, the keys and *Keyboard shortcuts* read one list (§3.5).

### 3.3 How the bar behaves

- **One menu open moves with the pointer**: with a menu open, hovering another opens it, and ← and → walk the bar, as every
  Windows menu bar does. Escape closes, and the focus returns where it was.
- **Alt, pressed and released alone, and F10 focus the first menu**; Alt with a menu's letter opens it (W, E, V, G, R, T, H).
  While Alt is held the letter is underlined in English and shown after the name in 中文 (*工作区(W)*), as Windows menus do.
- **An item is disabled where its surface exists and the record's state does not allow it** (*Stop…* on an ended session,
  *Take* on a taken quest), so its place is learned, as VS Code's Run menu keeps *Stop Debugging* in place. **It is absent
  where its surface is absent** (a browser's *Terminal*). The palette keeps its own rule and omits both (`commands.ts`).
  The View menu's *Reset view locations* is already disabled while nothing has moved; this makes it the bar's rule.
- **A record's group reads what is in the main area**: *This session* the attended session in Sessions, *This quest* the
  quest open on Quests. With neither in front, the group's acts are disabled and its label's tip says what to open.
- **A submenu** (Theme, Language) opens to the side on hover or →, a radio group ticked as it stands.
- The bar never covers the command center, and the command center never covers a menu (U15): at 680 px the center is its
  glyph and the layout toggles leave the strip (§3.1). The seven names fold into one menu only below the width they need,
  which the window's least width may never reach; UX7a measures it.

**Who takes a key inside a field or the terminal.** A field keeps what a field means (Ctrl+Z, Y, X, C, V, A, F), and the
terminal keeps those and Ctrl+N (a shell's next line). The frame's own keys, the places, Settings, the regions, the terminal
and the console, are the frame's everywhere, as Ctrl+B and Ctrl+J already are: the terminal's renderer leaves them to the
frame from the same list (CONSOLE4b). **A browser** claims no key a browser keeps for itself (Ctrl+N, Ctrl+Shift+N, Ctrl+1–8)
and shows none it does not answer.

### 3.5 One owner for every verb

D56 gave each verb one owner. Today the menus are `appMenus.ts` plus a list inline in `App.tsx`, the palette is
`commands.ts`, the keys are `shortcuts.ts` plus `LAYOUT_KEYS` plus the Ctrl+K handler in `App.tsx`, and a record's acts are
`sessionActs.ts` and the quest page's own list. Four lists that agree by care.

**After: one table, `commands.ts`, names every verb once**: its id, its label's key, its menu and group, its key, the surfaces
that have it, when it applies, and what it runs. The menus are built from it, the palette lists what applies, the key
handler reads its keys, and *Keyboard shortcuts* prints it. **A record's act calls its record's one owner**: Run's *This
session* items call `sessionActs.ts` and apply exactly when `offeredActs` offers the header's button; *This quest* items call
the quest page's act rule, lifted out of `QuestPage.tsx` so the header, the right-click and the menu read one function. A
test holds that every menu item is a table entry (the six clipboard items excepted, by name), that no key is listed twice,
that every label fits the `menu` budget (24 / 10), and that each record act's menu state matches its header's.

## 5. The title sections (UX7c)

### 5.1 The rule

**A head is two lines**: the state and the title on the first, with the acts at the right; two facts and an age on the
second. A page whose whole subject is its title (a quest) may give the title two lines. **The id leaves the head** for the
⋯'s *Copy ID* and a *Details* fold. **What the head says is not said again** below it.

### 5.2 A session's head

**Line 1**: the state's pill, then the title, one line, whole in its tip; at the right the loud act where the state has one,
*Stop…* while live, and ⋯ (D126 §3.1's acts, unchanged). The pill leads, so it stays in one place whatever the title's
length. **Line 2**: the facts its state makes matter, faint:

| Shown state | Line 2 | Its loud act |
|---|---|---|
| working, starting, idle | repository · account · how long it has run · *try 4: the 3 before it failed*, where its quest has failed sessions here | — |
| queued | repository · *queued*, with the driver's reason as the row says it | — |
| waiting on you | repository · *waiting 12 min* (the card below says what it asks) | — |
| parked | repository · *after 3 failed sessions* | *Try again* |
| awaiting reply, account cooling | repository · *waits on #q* / *resumes at 16:02* | — |
| to review | repository · *3 commits to review* | *Review* |
| completed | repository · *landed on feature/x* or *not landed* · ran 14 min | — |
| failed, declined, stopped, stood down | repository · its outcome's one fact (*failed after 2 min*, *held here until you try again*) | *Try again* where its stop holds the quest |
| a chat | repository · account · *idle* or *working* | — |
| an intake | *ask #a1* · *intake* | — |

The facts are the row's (`SessionGroups.Read`'s, D126 §2.4) and the record's (its repository, its account, its clock), so
the head and the row cannot disagree.

**Under the head, scrolling** (the record head, `SessionHead`):

- **The whole title, once, only when the head did not show it**: when the head shows a short title (SESSUX1j) or cut the
  title. Body size, two lines, *Show all*. Otherwise the record does not repeat it.
- **The parked card, the go-aheads, the record's note and what its tree left**, as today.
- **Details** (详情), folded to one line naming three facts (*quest #38e9…, Claude Code over protocol 0.84.0, started 2 min
  ago*); open, a definition list: repository, quest (a door), agent (its product, its way in, its version), account, machine,
  started, ran or elapsed, last moved, and the session id with *Copy*. The meta line of eight pairs goes into it.
- **How work ran**, folded to its one line by default (ChainLine), whatever was last chosen; open, a session row says its
  state, its account and how long, and the agent once at the strip's head, never on every row.
- **How it came to be**, folded, as today.

### 5.3 A quest's head

**Line 1**: the state's pill, then the title, **two lines at most** at the view step, whole on *Show all* where it was cut;
at the right the next step loud (*Take* while open, *Mark done* once taken), *Decline…*, and ⋯ (*Mark done* while open,
*Pause…*, *Abandon…*, *Delete…*, *Copy ID*). **Line 2**: *to engine · from ask #f6d947 · filed 4 h ago*, and the one live fact
where there is one: *a session is starting here*, *taken here · its session working 12 min* (a door), *sitting: after 3
failed sessions*, *done 2 h ago · landed on feature/x*.

- **The state line goes** (*published — nobody has taken it*): it is the pill's tip.
- **The id goes** to the ⋯ and *Details*.
- **The three fact rows go** into line 2 and **Details** (folded: the full filed and moved times, the lanes, the id).
- **The body drops its first line when it is the title**, as the ask page's does (POLISH4). With a short title, the head
  shows the short one and the body opens on the whole title.
- What the quest waits on (the sitting sentence, a hold, a conflict, a departure) stays where it is, above the body.

### 5.4 A long title, with and without a short one

**SESSUX1j is the real fix**: a quest's short title, at most 40 characters, written by whoever publishes it and never cut
from the title (the session management design §6). UX7c is built to read well both ways:

- **With a short title**, every head shows it, and the whole title is said once, under the head.
- **Without one**, the head shows the title as written: one line on a session, two on a quest, whole in the tip and on *Show
  all*. The page never rewrites content to shorten it (the platform language §4: content is shown as it is).

The install's titles were not written as titles. A re-filed ask put its note on its first line, and a quest takes its title
from that line. **A note added to an ask goes after its first line**, which is the re-filer's to keep; no screen can recover
a title the record never had.

中文 apart), screens (scroll height over visible height), and presses for a task. **S** is counted from the stories by a
scratch copy of that counter (UX6a's `tools/ux-count.mjs` is owed), English light and 中文 dark, at 1546 and 680 px, each
story's fixture said. **I** is read off the install's screenshots. **After** is a budget: it reports and never fails (D54).

## 7. Considered and rejected

- **A *File* menu first**, VS Code's own name. 文件 and *Files* already name what a quest carries, and D116 gives one word
  one thing. *Workspace* takes File's place and job, and is the name D75 gave the scope.
- **Keeping *Daoris* as the first menu**, File's job under the application's name. An application-named menu is macOS's; on
  Windows, where the install runs, VS Code's bar starts at File, and the owner named VS Code.
- **Keeping D75's rule that a menu opens setup and never a view.** VS Code's Go and View open places and views, and the
  palette's *Go to* group already does. The activity bar stays the one navigation region; Go is its keyboard twin.
- **Agents as a menu** (D75, D150 §2.4). A menu named after one place, holding its sections; the owner asked for it to go.
- **An *Accounts* menu**, as VS Code's activity bar has an accounts glyph. A place's content again; *What needs you*'s rows
  carry *Sign in* where an account holds work, and the page and the palette carry the rest.
- **A dynamic Run menu** holding only what applies to the record in front. A menu's places are learned; VS Code keeps *Stop
  Debugging* in place, disabled. The palette keeps the omission rule, where order is by match.
- **Keys for *Stop…*, *Decline…*, *Delete…*.** Each ends or removes something and asks once; a key would leave the ask as the
  only thing between a slip and the act.
- **Ctrl+1–8 by name instead of position** (VS Code's Ctrl+Shift+E, G, D, X). Daoris's places have no one-to-one VS Code twin
  but Search and Extensions, which keep theirs (Ctrl+Shift+F); a position is shown in the menu and the bar's tip.
- **Quick open (VS Code's Ctrl+P) for sessions, quests and repositories by name.** Wanted, and its own work: the palette
  carries commands, not records. Held until a row asks for it.
- **Folding the seven menus into one ☰ at 680 px**, VS Code's compact form. It hides every verb where the room is short by
  60 px; the command center and the layout toggles give way first.
- **The account's email as its title, its name secondary** (today). The name is what every other surface says.
- **A table with a header row on the agent's page at every width.** At 680 px a header names columns that are not there;
  the stacked row names each fact by its place.
- **Shortening a long title on the page** (cutting a leading parenthesis, or the first clause). It rewrites content, which
  the page never does (the platform language §4); SESSUX1j shortens it at its source, in the publisher's words.
- **Removing the state's text from the quest head**, leaving the pill. The pill keeps its word (status is never hue alone,
  D41 §6); only the second sentence saying the same goes.
- **A smaller title size on the quest page alone.** One page header for every page (`PageHead`); two lines at the view step
  read better than a smaller face the other pages do not share.
