## D256 — An Accept pressed on the run's way to its review is kept and applied, never refused until judged (2026-11-10)

**Decision.** The person's Accept on a run bound to a graph was taken only where the graph's part of the landing gate already waited
on the run's `review`. A press before that, where Daoris had not judged the run at the commit yet or a node before the review was still
to settle, was refused (*has not judged run … yet*, or *waits on …*) once its 20-second wait for a pass ran out, and nothing of it was
kept: the person had to come back and press again, and runs that landed minutes later on their own showed the refusal beside them.

Such a press is now kept on the run's record as the review's Accept, for the commit it was pressed on, and answered *accepted; it lands at
the next look* (or once the step it waits on is through). Each pass leaves it while the review has not begun, and the first pass that
finds the review waiting applies it through the one table, as any word of the person's.

A kept Accept ends at one of three bounds:

1. **The run's attempt moving.** The review begins at another commit than the one pressed on: the work moved since the press, the
   Accept is not this attempt's, and the pass drops it.
2. **The person's stop.** The person's own later word on the review (a stop, their changes, a new Accept) takes its place; a supervisor's
   word waits behind it.
3. **The review settling another way, or the run ending.** The one table refuses it where the review no longer waits, and the pass drops it.

**Rejected.** Refusing the press until the run is judged, and asking the person to press again: the press is the person's answer, and the
gate's own wait is Daoris's, so refusing it put Daoris's lag on the person. Applying the Accept at once past the steps before the review was
rejected too: the review is a step of its own, after the steps the workflow puts first, and a press never lands past them.
