## D178 — Two machines' work on one quest ends as a git conflict does: the loser is kept and the person resolves it, by dropping their machine's work or bringing it forward as a fresh take its kept session goes on from; a late take stays a loss, and handing the work to the winner waits for a way to reach it (2026-10-19)

**Decision (SYNCMERGE1).** The owner, 2026-10-19, after the CLOSE1c18 race was explained to them (machine B takes a chain
step before machine A's reopen of its source reaches it, and B's take loses): *"so if 2 machines work on the same quest,
this is what I mentioned before, more like a git conflict case, so we can use the same logic to solve this."* What they
mentioned before is D68 (2026-09-23): the remote is a git remote, and *"we can have proper merging logic when there is
conflict"*. Daoris has git's first half: a push that reached the remote second is kept as a `conflict` (D68 §2), its
session is stopped with its tree, branch and transcript kept, and the person can dismiss it (SYNC6c), which is git's
*take theirs*. It lacks git's other way out, *pull, rebase onto the new base, push again*, and its choices depend on
which push came first. A read-only opinion from Codex was taken on the analogy, the shape and the traps; this entry
adopts its order (drop, then bring forward, and hand to the winner later), its rule that a late take stays a loss, and
its traps, and records one difference from it (point 5).

1. **Where the analogy holds, and where it breaks.** It holds for keeping rejected work and for making the way out an
   explicit choice. It breaks at ownership, and the resolutions below follow the breaks:
   - A claim is exclusive: two takes of one step are never both right, so nothing merges two claims.
   - The work lives in a checkout on one machine: another machine cannot read it unless someone publishes it.
   - A session is a process: it cannot be replayed like commits. It is stopped, kept, and gone on with.
   - The remote orders quest operations and never merges code: combining two sessions' work stays a person's or that
     repository's agent's (D32, D68's rejected *merging two results into one automatically*).

2. **One set of choices for every lost take and every held step, whichever push came first.** A take lost to another
   machine's take (D68 §2, D69), to the person's reopen (FABRIC-CLOSE1c18), or with the take its chain grew from
   (FABRIC-CLOSE1c22), and a step the reopen held after its take had landed (FABRIC-CLOSE1c's choice 2), are offered:
   - **Drop mine.** On a lost take, the conflict is dismissed (SYNC6c's `dismissed`, unchanged), and the branch, tree and
     transcript stay where they are, named on the quest. On a held step, it is the abandon, which is its decline. The two
     are not the same act, and the words say so: a dismissal moves nothing but the conflict's mark; a decline closes the
     step for every machine (D46 §3).
   - **Bring mine forward** (point 3).
   - **Hand mine to the winner**, later (point 6).

3. **Bring mine forward is one press of the person, on the machine whose take lost.**
   - **It writes, in one transaction:** an ordinary take of the step, stamped with the step's `grewFrom` as it stands
     (FABRIC-CLOSE1c18), and the dismissal of the conflict it resolves, marked as brought forward with the take it made
     (point 5). Reachable, the take is pushed and awaited before any work, as every session's take is (D68 rule 1, D69).
   - **It goes on in the kept session.** The driver carries the kept conversation on in its tree, with its branch brought
     up to date with the step's current source as the go-on does (FABRIC-CLOSE1c5): a conflict in the tree leaves the
     tree as it was, and goes to the session, then to the person. No result the old take submitted comes back: the step
     is judged from its next `ready` (FABRIC-CLOSE1c10's rule).
   - **It may lose again**, as a push can be refused twice. If another machine's take reaches the remote first, the
     fresh take is a new conflict, the old one stays unresolved (point 5), nothing goes on, and the words say the second
     loss. The press is offered again only where the step is open again. Daoris never retries by itself.
   - **It is offered only where it can stand:** the step open, growing from an unheld submission of its source (or
     having none), the conflict not already settled, and the losing session's process ended. While another machine
     holds the step, while its source is held, or once it is closed, the press is not offered, and the page says which.
   - **A held step keeps its go-on** (`POST /api/quests/{id}/continue`, FABRIC-CLOSE1c) as its bring-forward. The new
     press reuses the go-on's machinery (the branch brought up to date, the result cleared) but not its eligibility: the
     go-on needs a reopen's hold, which a lost take's step may no longer have.

4. **A take that reached the remote after the reopen stays a loss.** Making it a held take would give both orders one
   state, but it would let intent formed on reopened work take the exclusive claim, and hold back a machine that took the
   step as it stands. That is the defect FABRIC-CLOSE1c18 fixed: a stale take standing on work the person reopened.
   Consistency comes from offering the same choices, not from one state.

5. **The resolution travels on the dismissal** (the parent's choice, for review; Codex proposed a new `resolved` kind).
   A `dismissed` operation may carry `forward: true` with the take it made (machine and sequence), written only when
   set. It applies only where that take applies: where the take loses, so does the mark, and the rebase forgets it
   (`QuestLog.Lost`), so a failed bring-forward never reads as resolved. A plain dismissal applies as it did, to any
   quest, settled or not, and a conflict already dismissed authorizes no fresh take: the press is refused, and a second
   plain dismissal stays harmless. An older build reads a marked dismissal as plain, so a mixed family degrades to *the
   conflict was dismissed*, with no push refused. A new kind would refuse every such push at an older remote (D144 on D68)
   for a mark that changes no status. The eleven places of `.claude/knowledge/quest-operations.md` take the field.

6. **Hand mine to the winner waits.** Naming a branch is not enough. The winning session needs the repository's
   identity, an immutable commit, a place it can fetch that commit from that the person chose to publish to, and its own
   machine's confirmation that it can. The quest remote carries no git objects, and uncommitted work is a separate
   sharing question. When it is built, it is an offer to the winning take that the winner accepts, never the local
   agent-switch hand-over. Daoris never pushes a branch the person did not choose to share, and never tells a session to
   combine work it cannot reach.

7. **Daoris's own words and refusals point at the choices.**
   - The person's own done, decline or set-up on a step their machine lost stays refused (FABRIC-CLOSE1c18, CLOSE1c22,
     CLOSE1c24), since a remote cannot tell it from the session's. The refusal names the choices that stand for it.
   - The page shows a conflict with its choices, why one is not offered, and, once settled, how it ended: dropped, or
     brought forward and confirmed, unconfirmed (offline) or lost again. In English and 中文.
   - The claim's `why: source-reopened` and the stop's code (`ended.lost-claim-reopened`) stay as they are, beside the
     ordinary loss's.

8. **Offline.** The press works offline: the take stays local and unconfirmed and the session runs (D68 rule 1), and the
   conflict reads *brought forward, unconfirmed* until the remote answers. It is never said as settled before then.

9. **The slices**, each its own row:
   - **SYNCMERGE1a, the service:** the dismissal's mark and its rule in the replay and the rebase; the bring-forward
     verb and its `Person` door on a local host; its refusals.
   - **SYNCMERGE1b, the driver:** the kept session carried on after a confirmed bring-forward, its branch brought up to
     date, once across a restart, and nothing gone on after a second loss.
   - **SYNCMERGE1c, the page and the terminal:** the conflict's choices, their availability and their outcomes, in
     English and 中文.
   - **Hand mine to the winner** waits for a note on publishing a branch (point 6), with no row until one is asked for.

**The traps**, each a test its slice owes (Codex's list, kept):
- Two machines resolving one conflict differently: one choice stands everywhere, and the other is said as superseded,
  with no effect.
- A marked dismissal reaching an older remote and an older machine: kept as a plain dismissal, nothing refused.
- A bring-forward racing a plain dismissal of the same conflict: a settled conflict authorizes no take.
- Offline: pressed, restarted, then reconnected to a remote where another machine's take won: the press is visibly
  unconfirmed throughout, and then lost.
- A restart before and after each durable write and the push's answer: one take, one carry-on, no duplicate spawn, no
  stranded claim.
- A fresh take losing again (another machine's take arriving between the fetch and the push): a new conflict, no branch
  update, no carry-on, and never *brought forward*.
- The claim's reason and the stop's words through the driver, the planner, the hand-over and the abandon, for both kinds
  of loss and CLOSE1c22's later steps.
- A lost session that had already ended normally, or was parked: its ending kept, and gone on with once, only after the
  press.
- The old session's late moves (a delayed done, `ready` or wait): the press is offered only once its process has ended,
  so the fresh take cannot make them stand.
- A chain's later steps lost with it and published again under the same ids: CLOSE1c24's refusals hold for both kinds of
  loss, and no old result is restored.
- History clearing: a closed quest still takes a conflict and a dismissal, marked or not, and a new machine replays both.

**Rejected.**
- **A late take made a held take** (point 4): stale intent would take an exclusive claim.
- **Merging two sessions' results, replacing a claim another machine holds, or releasing it** (D68, D32): the claim is
  exclusive, and only a person or the repository's agent combines work.
- **Publishing the losing branch automatically** (point 6), and **retrying a lost bring-forward by itself**.
- **A new `resolved` kind** (point 5): a mark on the dismissal says the same, and an older build reads it as a plain
  dismissal rather than refusing the push.
- **Bring forward as a take followed by today's go-on**: the go-on needs a reopen's hold.
- **Saying a branch was brought up to date when only the line was** (FABRIC-CLOSE1c5's limit, where the source's commit
  is absent): the words say which.

**Amends D68's rule 2**, which kept the loser *for a person* with dismissal as their only verb: the person may now also
bring their machine's work forward. **Held by:** nothing built here; each slice's row names its proof.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (SYNCMERGE1a, the service's half built).** Points 3 and 5 in the service, with these choices:
1. **The mark is one field:** `QuestOperation.Forward`, the fresh take's machine and sequence, on the log and the wire as
   `forward: {machine, sequence}` on a `dismissed` only, written only when set. Its presence is the mark, so no `true` travels
   beside it. One present and not whole makes the dismissal not whole. An older build drops the field and reads a plain dismissal.
2. **The marked dismissal is the taker's own** (`QuestLog.TakersOwn(QuestOperation)`), with no new `Quest` property. `OnALostTake`
   reads it: a remote refuses a marked dismissal from a machine whose claim reads lost, in the lost take's words. So does the rebase:
   it forgets a marked dismissal made beside a take it lost. The store's verbs that call `OnALostTake` never judge a dismissal, so
   they are unchanged. A plain dismissal stays no taker's.
3. **It applies only to its own machine's take conflict still on the quest** (`QuestLog.Brings`, in `Applies`). The named conflict
   is its machine's, attempted `Taken`, and the take is that machine's too. `Step` and `Lost` are unchanged: a lost marked dismissal is
   forgotten.
4. **A settled conflict authorizes no take, judged in the rebase.** A pending take whose pending marked dismissal would not apply after
   it loses as any take does, kept as a conflict (rule 2's loser), and the mark goes with it. A remote meets this only in a crafted
   push, since a current machine pushes at the remote's base; there the mark does not apply and the quest's push is refused whole.
5. **The verb**, `QuestStore.BringForwardAsync`, writes in one transaction a take stamped with the step's `grewFrom` (no note, no
   graph bind, which the driver makes at the run's start as for any take) and the marked dismissal. Its refusals
   (`QuestForwardRefusal`), in order: no such quest; not this machine's lost take on the quest (another machine's, not a take, none,
   or gone with an earlier publication); settled (a dismissal names it); closed; held here (`TakenHere`: dismiss instead) or by
   another machine; and the source held by the reopen.
6. **The exchange** (`QuestExchange.BringForwardAsync`) pushes and reads the claim as a take's does. It answers confirmed, unconfirmed
   offline (never as settled), or lost again. Lost to another machine's take or the reopen, the conflict stays unresolved and the step
   is not this machine's. Lost because a dismissal reached the remote first, a settled conflict authorizes no take. A loss never says
   *brought forward*. `QuestRespondRefusal.NoLostTake` is new, for a conflict not this machine's, settled, or a step held here.
7. **The door**, `POST /api/quests/{id}/conflicts/forward`, takes `{machine, sequence}` whole (400 otherwise). It is a `Person` door on
   a local host only. A shared host maps the dismissal's door, outside its local-only block, and not this one.
8. **Limits left to the next slices.** A failed bring-forward leaves no trace of itself (point 5), so a page reads it as a second take
   conflict of the same machine beside an unresolved first. A fresh take that loses on an open step whose source was once reopened
   reads `LostToTheReopen` (FABRIC-CLOSE1c18's reading of where the latest take conflict landed), so the claim's `why` says
   `source-reopened` even where a dismissal settled the conflict first. Only an offline-run session meets it; the exchange's answer
   reads the settled conflict first. Proven by `QuestReopenSyncTests` and `QuestSyncTests` (two machines and the remote),
   `QuestShareTests` (the answers), `QuestBringForwardTests` and the census.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (SYNCMERGE1a2, the superseded loss).** `QuestLog.LostToTheReopen` now clears its reopened flag at a plain
dismissal (no mark) of the machine's own conflict, so a fresh take lost after it reads lost, not lost to the reopen, and the claim's
`why` and the driver's stop no longer say `source-reopened`. A reopen after the dismissal sets it again. Proven by
`QuestReopenSyncTests.A_fresh_take_lost_after_a_plain_dismissal_settled_the_conflict_is_not_lost_to_the_reopen`, run online and offline first.
