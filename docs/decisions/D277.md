## D277 — Work the person already asked to have delivered goes through to its pull request on their quoted words, kept on the ask; an agent never presses their step, and a departure still waits for them (2026-11-12)

**Decision (THROUGH1).** An Ask Daoris conversation made five asks straight to repositories (`daoris ask --to`); each session closed
its quest done, and every run then stopped at *its done is unchecked* (D255) and at *Your Accept*, two presses the person had in effect
already made in words ("we should merge all changes (all repo) to remote too", "pr merge should be automated…", "please do without
my accept") and that Ask Daoris may not press, so the person typed ten presses for work they had asked for twice. The ways the
person's words reach a run were three: a requirement (`ask publish --require`, verified against the words on the ask), a choice kept on
the ask with words (`--review`, `--workflow`), and their own press. A direct ask reached none: it carries no requirement, the
conversation's words are not on the ask, and the Accept is a press. The rule:

1. **Requirements at the ask.** `daoris ask [--to <repo>] … --require "<the person's words>" "<the check>"` (repeatable, as
   `ask publish` takes it). The press from Ask Daoris is proved by its delegate key, which names the conversation; the driver checks
   each quote verbatim (whitespace and case aside, `QuestRequirement.QuotedIn`) against the person's own messages in that conversation and
   sends the service the verified quote as words the person said on the ask, where the service's own quote judge reads it as it does
   a message added to an ask. A quote the person never said is refused naming it, as at `ask publish`. A done checked against a
   requirement is checked (D255 stands: an ask with none still waits unchecked).
2. **Through words on the ask.** An ask carries *through words*: the person's verbatim words asking for the work to be delivered
   without their press (`--through "<the person's words>"`, and `daoris ask through <id> "<…>"` on an ask already made, as
   `ask review` and `ask workflow` change an ask's choices), verified against the conversation as point 1 does and kept on the ask like
   its review and workflow words. They are read live by each run of the ask's quests, so a run already started needs no new version
   of its graph; they are the person's word only as far as they go, and the driver, not the agent, applies them.
3. **What they pass.** At a run's `review` waiting on a record, and at an unchecked `quest.holds`, the driver applies the person's Accept
   (and, naming it, the pass of each unchecked done, `UncheckedAccepts`), bound to the record and revision drawn, its words saying
   *accepted on your words, "<the quote>", kept on ask #…* and never `From: supervisor`, only where every one of these holds, else the
   run waits for the person as it does now: no requirement of the ask's quests answered *departed* or unmet; no other hold, second
   opinion's dispute, `look`, `go-ahead`, `choose` (a return's both-changed file) or approval open; the line, quest and ask not stopped
   or paused by the person; the ask's through words not revoked. What follows the review (landing, push, the pull request with its
   automatic completion) then runs as the graph says, so the person's request for the merge reaches the pull request with no press.
   Production stays the person's press (D157 point 9): a `go-ahead` is never passed on these words.
4. **Seen and taken back.** The ask's and the run's views say *Accepted on your words* with the quote and *Take back* (the person's press,
   as *Overrule* is for a supervisor's word, rule 49), until the effect after the review has started; a person's own press on the node
   replaces the standing words for that run.
5. **Ask Daoris presses the ask, never the step.** `ask --require`, `--through` and `ask through` are verbs of the ask, which Ask Daoris
   presses at its tier (a card in restricted mode, at once in full access); `workflow skip`, `accept`, `changes`, `done`, `approve`,
   `reject` and `fix` stay *yours alone* with no way for it to press them, and its brief says to put the person's requirement and
   through words on an ask when it makes it, rather than to press afterward.

**The two other frictions of the same case**, decided here and built as THROUGH1's later rows:

6. **A push rule that can never open a push is refused where it is proposed.** The approved rule (`Bash(git … push:*)`) was a
   harness rule, and rule 27 of the git area says only git access's Write opens the push (`no-push` has no switch elsewhere), so the
   door refused the push however the rule read. A permission proposal naming a push is answered at the proposal with the place's Write
   and the verb that sets it (`git access … write=ask|allow`, a loosening confirmed as rule 24 says), never accepted as if it would
   work.
7. **A session's browser tabs are its own.** The in-app browser is one set of tabs driven over one CDP endpoint, so a session's
   navigation and its Playwright listeners reach the session beside it. Each session's `${browser}` server gets a browser context of its
   own, ended with the session, with the person's and Ask Daoris's tabs in contexts the sessions never take (`desktop-app` rule 55's
   seam). The clicks that time out waiting to settle on the identity and Azure DevOps pages are not yet measured: the row measures
   them first on one context, and does not claim them fixed.

**Rejected.**
- *The supervisor accepts for the person* (a supervisor `accept` and a hold's skip, rule 49): its word is *From: supervisor*, bound to a
  role the person appointed, and its decision window is for work the person did not speak to; applying the person's own quoted words is
  a different authority, and two ways to accept one review is a second way to one job (D228). The supervisor's path stays as it is.
- *Letting Ask Daoris press `skip` and `accept`*: it makes an agent's reading the person's step, the thing D255 and D157 point 10
  refuse; an agent would choose which words authorise, with no record the person can take back.
- *Saving a version of the workflow without the review or the holds' `yes`*: every run keeps the version it started on, so it releases
  none of the runs in flight, and it lowers the person's part for every later ask, not the ones they spoke to (D157 point 10).
- *The ask's whole sentence as the requirement* (D255's rejection stands): the check would be written by whoever did the work. The
  quote is the person's words from the conversation, chosen by the press, verified, and shown.
- *Reading any message of the conversation as through words by meaning:* no model decides what the person authorised; the words are
  verified verbatim and the person sees which were used.

**Supersedes nothing.** It adds the ask's requirements and through words, and a run's reading of them; D255, D157 and rule 49 stand.
