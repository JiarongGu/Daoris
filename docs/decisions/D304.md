## D304 — Workflows are written in actors, and the system does not tell a person from an agent (2026-11-16)

**Decision (RELEASEFLOW1, by a fresh-design pass: two designs from a purpose brief alone, reconciled against the code).**

1. A workflow is written in actors. A step names the actor that takes it (worker, tester, second opinion, acceptor, release
   manager, approver, operations, config changer, lead, …), its outcomes and the evidence that settles each, never who fills it.
2. The system does not tell a person from an agent. Any agent, an automation or the person can hold any actor, and an actor
   passes from one holder to another (an agent to the person and back, one agent to another on a cap or a stall) with the same
   states, waits, records, doors and display: "waiting on <actor>: <holder>". The only difference is the holder's channel, and a
   channel is an adapter: as Daoris builds adapters and tools for agents (a harness, its tools), it builds the person's (the
   window's presses, the terminal, Ask Daoris, a page in Daoris's browser, an act observed in another system), each bringing
   the same acts into the same flow.
3. Every actor reports to an actor, never to a person. The owner is an actor, the top of every line, held by the person. A holder
   that needs help (a question, a conflict, a vacancy, a bound reached) asks the actor its actor reports to, which answers, finds
   another holder or passes it up; only owner-class kinds (doctrine rule 1's list) reach the owner actor, and other help unanswered
   at the top ends as *gave up* with what was tried.
4. The place chooses each actor's holder by its risk and the owner actor's marks, never step by step. At low and medium risk an
   appointed agent holds an accept; a marked destination (the work workspace's production releases) keeps its approver the
   owner actor's.
5. A step is settled by its holder or by an observed fact, whoever made it: a step done outside Daoris is done by its holder,
   with its evidence, and the run goes on. Stop is "end this work", the only act that ends a run early; Skip is "not needed this
   run"; a per-run decision (does this release need a config change?) is a step whose "not needed" passes its dependent step on
   the record.
6. A plugin is an actor's means. What a plugin can do decides the channel: an approval the plugin can make is one press in
   Daoris; otherwise Daoris opens the system's page; otherwise the holder acts there and Daoris observes it. A plugin failing for
   access is a step waiting on the actor that keeps credentials, held by the person today and by an agent that manages them
   where one is appointed.
7. At the work workspace, authoring a configuration version beside the released one (a version not yet released to users) is
   low risk: its actor may be held by an agent.
8. The runtime evolves in place: its lifecycle is already the same for every kind, and only the authority to settle a step
   (today bound to the step's kind) moves to the step's holder.

9. On the window and in the room an actor is called a role (role / 角色, the glossary's one word for a position Daoris fills):
   "actor" names the design, never a word a person reads. A step names its role; "waiting on <role>: <holder>".

**Why.** A real release (2026-11-16) showed the cost of steps typed by action: a target edited into a file by hand, a release,
its trigger and its production approval made by hand in Azure DevOps that Daoris neither showed nor offered, a hand-made release
recordable only by Stop, which ended the run and its session, and an after-release step restarted by hand.

**Rejected:** a second engine beside the first with a drain between them (the lifecycle is already kind-agnostic); the release as
one bundled step (its facts are observed one by one); risk set per step (the place sets it once); a step or a reporting line
naming the person (the person is a holder like any other); a separate CI/CD identity for agents before the credentials actor
exists (the plugin's kept key serves until then).

**Trial and its criteria.** First on the work workspace's reporting app, its release path from the pull request's merge to the
after-release check, measured against a baseline recorded before it starts (owner-actor presses and interventions per run, time
from merge to released, runs ended by stop, waits with no holder). **Keep** when, over the first three releases or two weeks
(whichever is later): no file is edited and no session carried on by hand to move a run; no run ends by stop but a deliberate
end; the owner actor presses at most twice per release, sign-ins apart; every wait on an actor the person holds is in What needs
you with its press within one look; no wait with no holder outlives its deadline off its reporting line; no outside act is made
twice, across a forced restart included; merge to released is no slower than the baseline's median. **Retire** (the release path
returns to the bundled step; the rest stays) on any one: an approval at a marked destination settled by any holder but the owner
actor's; a release created or a stage started twice; two of three releases needing a hand repair of the run; the person twice
unable to tell from the wait line who holds a step or what to press.

**Left:** the rows ACTORFIELD1 to ACCESSSTEP1 in `TASKS.md`; after a keep, the holder's authority (CASTING1, HOLDDOOR1) across
every step, path by path.

**Amended 2026-11-18 (D318).** A step cast to the role's agent is the run's working agent's to take: its session is handed the step as its
next turn, resumed where it ended, and settles it through one tool (`step_settle`) with its outcome and evidence at the settle door; where it
cannot, its request goes up its reporting line. A decision its agent holds is settled by the agent's answer.

**Amended 2026-11-19 (D325).** An act with an outside effect is a tool of the holder's plugin, journaled on the step it holds and settling it
by its receipts; the graph keeps the constraints (checks before landing, an approval before a marked destination) and the roles, and the
procedure between them is the repository's delivery knowledge, which the step names.
