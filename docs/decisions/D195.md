## D195 — Each kind of thing Daoris writes is kept by one rule, set per kind in the driver's standing choices and shown on This Daoris with its size, its next and its last clean; a tree set aside drops at once what git says can be made again; the look, a free-space floor and the person's press run the rules (2026-10-21)

**Decision (CLEANUP1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, for D191's reads: Codex reads it; whether Fable reads is the steward's call,
since it adds a record and a standing choice and amends two decided rules, and changes no protocol between processes and no graph.
One choice waits for the owner (point 7). On 2026-10-21 the install's set-aside trees held 37.4 GB in 1,982,441 files, 87% of its
`data/`, of which 90% of the bytes lay under a build folder's name and most of the rest was git's own files; each kind Daoris keeps
cleaned itself by its own rule or by none, and This Daoris showed sizes and no rule (design §0, §1). The contract is
`docs/2026-10-21-cleanup-design.md`.

1. **Every kind has a rule, read from one table.** The design's inventory names every entry of D172's table, its writer and reader,
   its rule today and what may remove it, and marks the kinds with none (§1). A table in the driver library, `KeepKinds`, holds each
   kind's default, what the person may set and its remover; the route, the terminal and the pass read it (§2.1).
2. **The person's choice is a standing choice**: `driver.json`'s `keep` (set-aside days and `whole`, downloads' days, logs' days up to
   30, history's age, the floor), absent meaning the default, judged by `StandingChoices` on both paths, set at the terminal by
   `daoris driver keep`, which D194 hands to the driver's program, and proposed by Ask Daoris through the `setting` door (§2.2). What
   the rules removed and what each big kind measures is a machine record, `data/keeping.json` (§2.3).
3. **A tree set aside keeps only what cannot be made again.** Before the look moves a tree, it asks git for the tree's commit, its
   tracked files (all equal to the commit, since a tree with uncommitted or untracked paths never moves) and the folders it ignores
   whole; after the move it removes exactly those tracked files and the ignored folders that TREEKEEP1's rule already lets the press
   delete unasked (a build folder of the fixed list that the checkout holds too), never following a link and never a path written
   since the judgement. Everything else waits its days, and the record names the commit that makes git's files again (§5).
4. **Three triggers.** The look's pass beside the tidy runs each rule at its pace (§3.1). Below a free-space floor (by default the
   smaller of 10% of the volume and 20 GB, read from the operating system for the Daoris folder's own path, never a walk) it runs at
   once only what can be made again: an unfinished slim, unused downloads, the browser's caches; and says the rest in *What needs you*
   (§3.2). The person's *Free up space…* lists first what each rule takes now and what could go early, then removes exactly what was
   listed, each judged again (§3.3).
5. **This Daoris shows each kind** on a *Disk space* card: its size with when it was counted, its rule with *Change…* where it is the
   person's, its next and last clean; sizes come from a ledger the removing acts keep and a bounded walk per kind, never a walk of the
   whole home on a look (§4). The terminal twins are `daoris-driver space` and `daoris-driver space free` (§4.4).
6. **Safety**: nothing a session uses is removed; nothing outside the home is touched; only what can be made again goes by itself
   before its time; every removal is said; what a clean would remove is seen before it runs; each kind keeps its one remover (§6).
7. **History's age waits for the owner.** The design offers an age for finished history that only the person sets, default never,
   cleared through the clear with every refusal holding; it amends the history clearing design §7, so it is asked under D191 point
   3: yes builds KEEP1h, no drops it (§3.6).
8. **The rows**: KEEP1a, the slim; 1b, the card and the read; 1c, the rules set; 1d, the press; 1e, the floor; 1f, the downloads' rule;
   1g, an opinion's packet with its clear; 1h, history's age, on the owner's yes (§9). They are not named CLEANUP1a and on, since
   CLEANUP1b and CLEANUP1c already name notes of 2026-10-17.

**Why.** The removers existed and judged well; what was missing was a rule a person could see and set for each kind, a reason to free
what can be made again before a disk fills, and a judgement of a tree's contents made while git could still give it. At the rate of
2026-10-20, fourteen days of set-aside trees would hold about 385 GB; slimmed, what the sessions wrote of their own.

**Rejected** (design §7): deleting a tree's folder whole at the move; taking every ignored path as made again; slimming the folders
already set aside without git; keeping git's own files in a set-aside folder; one size limit for the whole folder; a floor that removes
what cannot be made again; slimming a live tree's build outputs; an age for history on by default; setting the agents' own retention;
a browser cache switch before it is measured; the machine log past 30 days; a walk of the whole home at each pass; a separate rules
file; a word of its own for each act; holding sessions below the floor.

**Amends,** once accepted (design §8): D88's AUTOTIDY1 note (what goes at the move, and the purge's days the person's) and its
CACHECLEAR1 note (unused versions by a rule and below the floor; its terminal twin); D102's TREEKEEP1 (its rule also the slim's);
D172's layout §4 (the logs' days as set; `data/keeping.json`); the This Daoris design §3.1 (an earlier build's folders offered by the
press) and §9 (the keep rules set on the page); the machine log design §4 (five events); the history clearing design §7 only on the
owner's yes.

**Held by:** the rows' proofs (design §9): a `Process` class over real git for the slim, the read's and the press's modules tests, the
both-ways test over every `keep` choice, the floor's stand-in free-space read, the card's tests and stories in both languages.
