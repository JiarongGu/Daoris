## D191 — A design is settled by three makers' reads, and the owner is asked only for what is theirs (2026-10-20)

**Decision (the owner's direction).** On 2026-10-20 the owner directed that design decisions no longer wait for the owner's
review. A design is written by one maker and read by two others: Claude Opus writes it, Claude Fable and Codex each read it
independently, and the steward verifies every finding against the code, takes what holds, and settles the design.

Before this, D185, D186, D188, D189 and D190 each waited as *proposed* for the owner's review, and two of them (D189, D190)
were already read by Codex, whose findings reshaped them. The owner's review added a round trip of hours to days to every
design, while the other makers' reads were the ones that found the defects: on D189 Codex's read found eight, all confirmed in
the code; on D190 it found twelve, eight of them blocking.

1. **Three makers.** The design's author (Opus by default, D160's build-judgement role) writes the design and its decision as
   *proposed*. Fable and Codex read it independently, neither shown the other's read before it gives its own, so neither
   anchors the other. Each finding names the file and line it rests on.
2. **The steward settles.** Every finding is checked against the code before it is taken (D160's step 9: a finding is never a
   fact). What holds is written into the design and the decision, a table of the reads and their verdicts kept in the design.
   Where the two readers disagree on a choice, the steward decides and writes why; where a finding is dropped, the design says
   why. A design whose blocking findings are all taken is *accepted* by the steward, with a dated note saying so.
3. **What the owner is still asked.** Only what is theirs: a change of the product's direction; anything that spends the
   owner's accounts or money beyond the development's own runs; a sign-in; anything that publishes, pushes or rewrites history;
   anything that loses the owner's data. A choice a design leaves "for the owner" otherwise goes to the readers and the steward.
   The owner can reopen any settled design; a reopened decision is amended, never rewritten.
4. **Recorded, not quoted.** The decision note says what was decided and why, in its own words.

**Why.** The defects these designs carried were found by other makers reading the code, not by the owner's review, and the
owner's time is better spent where only the owner can decide. Three makers disagree in different places: a design that holds
against two independent reads has been tested where one author would have stopped.

**Rejected.** The owner's review of every design (the round trip this removes); one reader (a single other maker missed what
the second found on D189's §2 and D190's attribution questions); the readers settling it by vote (a finding is checked against
the code, never counted).

**Applies to** D189 and D190, both proposed and both read by Codex, now read by Fable, and every design after. Fable reads as a
read-only agent on its own model; Codex reads as D160's step 9 runs it, in its own detached worktree.

**Amended 2026-10-20: Codex reads every design; Fable joins only where a design changes a foundation.** The owner directed that the second reader be used sparingly, since a Fable read costs many tokens and a Codex read runs remotely at no cost beside the gates. Codex reads every design, as point 1 had it. Fable reads only where the design changes a foundation (the data model of a record or the graph, a protocol between processes, concurrency or a decided rule other designs rest on) or where Codex's read and the steward's check leave a fundamental disagreement the steward cannot settle from the code. The steward says in the design which reads it had and, where Fable did not read, why the design changes no foundation. Point 1's *neither anchors the other* still holds where both read. Why: on D189 and D190 Codex's read found the defects (eight and twelve findings) before Fable read; Fable's read added most where the design itself was the foundation.

**Amended 2026-10-23: Claude Opus 5.5 takes Fable's place for now.** The owner directed that this development use Opus 5.5 for its
reasoning work for now, since it reasons as well as the work needs. Where this decision has Fable write a proposal or read a
foundation, an independent Opus reader does it. That reader is a separate agent: it does not see the other makers' work before
writing, so point 1's independence holds. Codex stays the other maker and reads every design. The rule of the 2026-10-20 amendment
stands with Opus in Fable's place. Why: a Fable reader of MODELROLE1's proposal (2026-10-23) was stopped for this. The reads so far
(D198 to D204) show Codex's read finding most defects round by round, and the steward's check against the code settling the
rest. `docs/model-roles.md` says the same in its roles.

**Note, 2026-10-24: one maker's acceptance builds one row and stages nothing.** While Codex's credits were out on 2026-10-24, five
designs were accepted on the Opus foundation reader's reads alone, Codex's read recorded as owed: KEEPSIZE1 (D195), AGENTERRORS1
(D210), CONFIRMWAIT1 (D156), CHATFLOW1 (D157) and STEWARDROLE1 (D209). When Codex's credits returned the same day, its owed read of
each found blocking defects the Opus rounds had not: 8, 8, 9, 8 and 7 blocking, three of them *reopen*, and rows of three of them
(1b of KEEPSIZE1; 1a and 1a2 of AGENTERRORS1; 1b and 1a-i of CONFIRMWAIT1; a and b of CHATFLOW1) were already built on main, though
none was staged. So: where a design is accepted with the other maker's read owed, only its first row is built until that read lands,
no build carrying a row of it is staged before the read and its amendments land, and a design that grants authority or deletes is
not accepted on one maker's read at all. Why: point 1's two independent makers are the check that catches what a reader of one
lineage shares with the author; the day's five reads measured how much one reader misses.
