## D219 — A session's handback is its quest's closing note, kept with the session that wrote it and read whole (HANDBACK1, 2026-10-29)

**Decision (accepted 2026-10-29, built the same day).** The steward settled the shape before the build (the record, the refusal and the
reads below); this records each with its reason, and where the code made one more exact. It is the follow-up D215 §7.1 names: the steward
read every handback out of a session's event log, and it moves to a record the session writes once, which the connector and the terminal
serve and which RUNENDS1's end will read. The evidence is HANDBACK1's row: two sessions of another workspace closed their quests with a
note that only pointed at their last message, and in the second the event log held no such message, so each quest's record was rebuilt from
its commit; and that workspace's steward could not open a closed quest's note from its session.

1. **The record is the quest's closing note.** A session's handback is the note it gives through the connector's `quest_respond` as it
   closes its quest `done`, says it `ready`, or declines it. It is written once, at that act, on the operation the act makes (a done, a
   submission or a decline) in the same write as the move, and read back from the quest's history, never from the session's event log
   afterwards. A done or a decline made through the connector now names the session that wrote it, as a submission already named the
   session that said its ready (FABRIC-CLOSE1c4): one predicate, `QuestStore.KeepsSession`, names the three kinds for the log's payload,
   its reader and the wire, and the field is written only where a session said it. Why: the note already was the one record of the work
   written at the act and kept with the quest wherever it travels; what it lacked was its writer, so no reader could tell a session's
   handback from a person's close (their abandon's reason, their own done) or from a later session's on the same line. Every other door's
   close and every operation from before reads as it did, and a remote built before this keeps the close without the name (an existing
   field a further kind carries, `.claude/knowledge/quest-operations.md`). A session that served no quest has no handback.

   *Once* holds per act, and the code makes it exact: a ready the person reopened is answered by a later ready, each written once at its
   act, so a quest may carry more than one handback over its life, and the read answers the latest. A done the closure graph's close writes
   carries the submission's note it closed on and names no session, so a graph-closed quest's handback stays that submission.

   *Rejected: a store of its own* (a handback table, or a field on the session's ledger record). The note is already written once at the
   act and travels with the quest; a second copy is a second place to drift, and a ledger field would not reach another machine of the
   workspace, which the quest's history does.

2. **A closing note that only points elsewhere is refused at `quest_respond`.** The rule is read from the note's text, narrow and by
   named shapes (`HandbackNotes`): the note is split into parts at a sentence's or a clause's punctuation, a dash set apart, a line's end
   and `and`, and it is refused only where every part is a pointer or a bare word that says nothing of the work (`Done`, `Handback`,
   `Closing as done`), and one part at least is a pointer. A pointer is a part that is, whole, a reference to another message, a commit,
   a change, a record of the repository's, a file or a place, in one of these forms: `see …` or `refer to …` with what it names and at most
   *for the details*; a label (`details`, `handback`, `summary`, `everything`) said to be *in*, *at* or *under* one, or *above*; one said to
   *have* or *hold* the details; `as described above` and its kin; `it's all in …`; `in my last message` alone; and the Chinese forms of a
   detail or a handback *seen in* or *in* the last message, above or the commit. The refusal quotes the pointer, says the note is the one
   record whoever reads the quest later reads, which none of the session's messages is kept with, and names what a handback holds: what
   changed, where (the commits), what was proved, and what was not. Nothing moves.

   A short real handback goes through (a fix named with its commit and the tests that passed), as does a pointer beside words of its
   own, and a note with no pointer at all. *Rejected: a length bound*, which would refuse exactly that short real handback and pass a long
   pointer. The refusal is in English, as every refusal the service says to an agent is: the exchange's other refusals are its words to a
   connector, untranslated, and only the page's chrome translates. It applies at the connector's `quest_respond` alone. The respond door's
   other callers keep their words: the person's abandon, sent by the driver, and a page's decline carry the person's reason, which is theirs
   and no session's handback. *Not refused: a done with no note.* An empty note points nowhere, the tool asks for one, and requiring one
   would change every door's done; the read says the session gave none.

3. **The reads return the record whole, by quest or by session, in any state.** `HandbackReader` phrases the answer once, and three doors
   answer through it: the connector's `handback_read`, the local host's `GET /api/handbacks/{id}`, and `daoris-driver sessions handback
   <session|quest> [--json]`. An id names a quest, or a session for the quest it served (its own quest, or the one a conversation took).
   The answer is the quest, then its handback: the act, the note whole, how it answered each of the person's requirements, when, and the
   session that wrote it, said as that session's claim, as the co-working skill reads another session's words; a handback another session
   of the same line wrote is said as that session's. A close that names no session (one from before this, or through another door)
   answers the quest's own note, said as the quest's and never as a session's handback; an open quest has none yet. A session reads any
   quest of its own workspace this way, as `quest_list` scopes it, and another workspace's quest or session is refused naming both. The
   connector's default rule allows the read beside `line_read`; a reviewer and a helper are offered it beside `quest_list`; a partner is
   refused it in both layers, as it is the quest list; a brainstorm's pass is denied it with every other tool.

4. **Why that workspace's steward could not read a closed quest's note.** Proved by `McpHandbackToolTests`'s list test: in this tree
   `quest_list` is scoped by workspace alone, and has printed a closed quest's note whole with `includeClosed` since 2026-08-05
   (`5ab518b4a`), so a session's quest tools do not reach only open quests or its own, and the gap as the row words it is not this tree's
   reach. What the steward lacked is a read of one quest: the list's default leaves every closed quest out, and with them in it answers
   every closed quest of the workspace, each note whole, with no way to ask for one; the test's thirty closed quests make the list's answer
   more than ten times the one quest's read. Not proved here: which build that install ran at the steward's pass, and whether its harness
   cut a long answer. The new read is the one the steward uses: one quest's handback, whole.

5. **RUNENDS1's end will read the record, and the record does not depend on how the end is judged.** RUNENDS1 is being reworked on
   another branch and reads nothing of this yet. When it does, a session's end reads whether this session handed back (the latest close
   naming it), instead of reading the quest's state or the session's last words. The record is written at the session's own act, before
   and apart from its end, so no judgement of the end changes it.

**Left as it was.** The moves read (`GET /api/quests/{id}/moves`, D186) answers a session on a submission alone, its allow-list unchanged,
so the flowchart's answer keeps its shape; the handback door answers the closing session. The driven prompt's closing instructions are
unchanged: the tool's own words and the refusal say what a handback holds where the session writes one. A role's terminal (D209 §2.2) runs
its listed open reads alone, and `sessions handback` is not among them; a role's session reads `handback_read`.

`McpHandbackToolTests` (the reads, and the steward's list), `LocalHostTests` (the door), and `SessionsCommandTests` (the verb).
