## D284 — Daoris extends every agent and narrows none: full access is at least the harness's own, restricted mode is the harness's own approval mode, every prompt is one approval request to the place's approver, and a workflow's step is the only wait Daoris makes (2026-11-12)

**Decision (ACCESS1, the owner's direction of 2026-11-12).** Daoris coordinates agents; it exists to extend what they can do. A
restrictions audit of the main checkout that day found that it did the opposite in every mode: Daoris composed its own denies beside
the harness's mode, so full access carried them; git access refused every session push and every force, delete and tag whatever the
mode; each session's environment signed `gh` and `az` out; the connector refused by kind of work and by subagent lineage; and
restricted mode added Daoris's own confirm cards, terminal tiers, a 15-minute refusal and a refusal of the harness's *always* on top
of the harness's prompts. So Ask Daoris in full access could do less than the same harness the person runs by hand. The owner
directed that there is no door kept for the person, that a limit exists only where a workflow requires one or the person sets one,
that restricted mode is the harness's own approval mode, which asks and blocks nothing by itself, and that approval is generalised
across the agents Daoris runs. The contract is `docs/2026-11-12-access-model-design.md`, built in its §10's order.

1. **Daoris extends an agent and never takes capability away.** In full access any agent Daoris runs has at least its harness's own
   full-access reach (Claude Code's `bypassPermissions`, Codex's `agent-full-access`, an ACP agent's declared full posture) and Daoris's
   doors besides: pushes, force pushes, branch deletes and tags, `gh` and `az` with the person's own sign-ins, the person's own tool
   home, every Daoris verb. A start hands the harness only the person's own rules and the opt-in rules the person switched on.
2. **No person door.** Every Daoris act (a bridge route, a terminal verb, a connector tool, a service door) is open to every caller:
   the person, Ask Daoris, a session, a terminal, a role. The person key, the delegate keys and the pipe's placement of a process stay
   for attribution and for knowing who answered a wait; they never decide whether an act runs.
3. **The only waits Daoris makes by itself are a workflow's steps** (`review`, `human`, `go-ahead`, an unchecked done, `choose`),
   because a workflow's author put them there. Each names its waiting party, the person by default or an appointed role; a press by
   anyone else becomes that caller's request to the party. A change that removes a wait or moves who answers it waits on the party that
   answers it now. Full access lifts no step.
4. **Restricted mode is the harness's own approval mode.** Its prompts are the only asking, and Daoris relays them with every option
   the harness offers. Daoris adds no confirm card, no deny and no clock: no request is refused on a time-out, *always* is offered
   wherever the harness offers it, a duplicate joins the request held, a one-job session asks like any other, and a harness with no
   mapped mode starts in its own and says so.
5. **One approval request, one approver.** Every harness's prompt (Claude Code's permission prompt, Codex's approval, an ACP agent's
   `request_permission`), the git door's held act, a rule widening and a wait a person's rule asks for become one Daoris approval
   request: who asks, what act, where. It goes to the place's approver: the person, or an agent the person appointed for a repository,
   a workspace or the machine through the role system's supervisors of a kind. The approver answers allow once, allow always for this
   place, or deny. It shows as one row of What needs you (read by Notifications and `daoris notices`), as the card in the asking
   session's conversation, and over one route and one verb. An appointed agent's answer takes effect at once, is kept as its decision,
   and falls to the person where it leaves the request; no approver lifts a rule the person set. Workflow waits resolve their party by
   the same rule, so permission prompts and workflow waits are one approver model.
6. **Every other limit is an opt-in rule, off by default**: `no-push`, `no-force`, `no-publish`, the tree guard, keeping sessions in
   their repository or workspace, others' uncommitted paths, Daoris's git secrets, credential isolation, the person's processes, every
   seeded supervisor rule, the routing, hand-over and helpers waits, `checks-accept` and the rest the design lists. The person's own
   rules hold as set, in every mode. A home's migration turns off what was Daoris's default and keeps what the person set.
7. **The floor binds Daoris's own code, never an agent.** Daoris's clean-up, landing, withdrawal, tidy, tree moves, `sync` and act
   engine never delete or overwrite work Daoris did not make, tracked files or nested repositories. An agent may do any of these with its
   own tools.
8. **Ask Daoris finishes a simple act itself and sends work that changes a repository's content to that repository** as an ask, a
   quest or a session. The rule is the same in both modes and is its working rule, not a gate.

**Rejected.**
- *Today's blocking model*: person-only doors, Daoris's fixed and default denies, git access apart from the mode, confirm cards on top
  of the harness's prompts, terminal tiers and a refusing clock. Each gap became a backlog row, and full access was less than the
  harness's own.
- *A Daoris approval layer beside the harness's*: two ways to ask for one act (D228), the person answering twice.
- *Daoris answering prompts by policy in full access* for a harness with no mode that asks nothing: the judgement is the harness's or
  the approver's (D81, D52).
- *Keeping the defaults on behind one switch that lifts them*: the person would have to find a limit to lift it; a limit is theirs
  to add.
- *An approver per harness or per source of asking*: three lists for one question.
- *Lifting workflow steps in full access*: the mode is the agent's reach; the steps are the work's process.
- *Refusing an answer from a party that is not the approver*: it becomes that caller's request to the approver instead.
- *The floor as a limit on agents*: it would narrow the harness.
