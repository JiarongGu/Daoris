## D226 — A role's staffing may be a policy: weighted choices, and for each condition Daoris observes what moves a line to another choice, kept apart from the choices, judged inside the one walk, today's order the default (2026-11-02)

**Decision (STAFFPOLICY1, the owner's direction of 2026-11-02; co-designed under D191, accepted by the steward 2026-11-02).** Per
`docs/2026-11-02-staff-policy-design.md`. A role's policy is a row of `role_policy` in the desktop store, bound by a digest to the
staffing it was set for: Daoris's own writes of the choices re-key it in their transaction, and any other writer that changes the
choices leaves it *stale*, so the role runs in order and says so (a write that leaves them as they were keeps it valid). The store's
step that adds the policy raises the writer stamp, so an older Daoris on the home reads but writes nothing and starts no choice past
a policy it cannot read. A choice is known by its agent, model and effort. Two rules are built: `order`, today's walk and
every role's default, and `weight`, a deterministic pick over the role's last 20 new starts, ties by order, two starts in one look
reserved apart; *Spread evenly* is equal weights. A choice may take only some of the role's works, hold a share of the starts, and keep a
daily budget of starts or reported tokens that acts at a start only. One pure judge, `StaffJudge.Arrange`, orders the role's own
entries inside `StartPlan`, the one walk; D197's account walk inside a choice is unchanged, and no account is named or assigned. The
conditions (limit, signed-out, model-refused, transient, failed, quiet, budget) are facts of D212's one course decision, each with
actions whose default reads *as today*. A move to another choice is a new part handed D189's brief at a part's end, never mid-turn;
a pick and a handed agent never walk, *as before* waits, a turn ended in the agent's own words is never a condition, and an uncertain
send is never replayed. Once the person sets any move, a line makes at most 3 such moves in 24 hours, then waits for them; a
conversation's transient episode keeps D210's five tries. It is said on the run, the head, the role and the record (`chosen`), as *move
to another choice* (改用其他选择), never *switch*. The person sets it through `SET_ROLE`, the terminal's `role policy` and the
one `RoleEditor`; Ask Daoris proposes it through `role_propose`.

**Why.** The owner wants a role's several agents and models used by a policy the person sets (weights that spread work, conditions
that move a line), with the reason a run is on its agent visible. Both sealed proposals reached one judge in the one walk, with
today's order as the default that changes no spending. The steward settled where they differed (design §1): weights cannot live in the
choices, since both of today's writers of choices drop unknown fields (`Settings/ActorKey.cs`, `Roles/RoleStaffing.cs`).

**Rejected.** Codex's single `staffing` value inside the role set, and weights in the choice objects. A random pick, a time-window
spread, and round-robin as a rule of its own. Cheapest-first by price, and selection by measured success before it is measured: both
are filed behind measurements (row h). Layering a place's policy over another's. A move mid-turn, a replay elsewhere, moving a pick
or a handed agent, a policy ending a quiet run. The budget's mid-session hand-over, filed. The word *switch*. A separate reads round
before building: both makers' reads run beside the first rows.
