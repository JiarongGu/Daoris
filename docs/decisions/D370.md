## D370 — The update flow is one state machine that always says what it waits on; its check runs off the look and is let go of when the stop ends (2026-11-27)

**Decision (UPDATEFLOW2, amending D139's close, D327's drain and D363's idle; the owner's direction: one state machine, every
request acted on or answered within one look, *Update now* stops at once and says what it waits on, a refusal or a cancel
returns to taking work at once, `daoris update` always says where it stands; no compatibility, D323).** Three failures in two
days (the install's logs, 2026-11-26 and 2026-11-27): a build staged at idle waited 1.5 h with 9+ sessions; an update refused by
the home proof left the desktop answering every ask "Daoris is updating" for about 3 h and `update --cancel` did not release it;
a build restaged over a deferred one was not read for 49 minutes, and *stopping* refused every ask for 24 minutes without a
word. The cause of the last two is one: `ApplyAsync` set the close's hold (every press and hello refused as *stopping*) and
then ran the build's check, whose home proof copied the whole home (8.7 GB there, 7.9 GB and 168,000 files of it a history
folder no proof opens), could not be ended, and held the loop's only look, so a word, a restage and a cancel were read when it
ended (introduced by 0ee0884f18, the proof's first build; its reading of what is copied by 5ac8eaf857).

1. **One state, eight words:** `none`, `staged` (*Not now*: work goes on), `draining`, `deferred`, `stopping`, `applying`,
   `refused`, `installed`. `UpdateFlow` is the table of what may follow what (every state any other, `applying` only from
   `stopping`, where the launcher is started); a move it does not list throws. Every transition is said once in the machine log
   as `update.state` (from, to, why, build, the moment's counts) and kept as `state` and `transition` on `UPDATE_STATE`; the
   whys are `UpdateWhy`'s words (`seen`, `restaged`, `when-idle`, `now`, `not-now`, `idle`, `bound`, `work-began`, `checked`,
   `stop-bound`, `refusal`, `launcher`, `close`, `started`, `swap`, `dismissed`, `gone`). `update.staged`, `update.draining`,
   `update.deferred`, `update.applying` and `update.refused` are gone: one line says each of them.
2. **A look is quick and the stop is its own task.** Looks are one at a time and answer within `InstallUpdater.Look`; the look
   that decides the stop starts it and does not wait on the check. Every look judges a stop under way: a word other than the one
   it was asked under, a build other than the one it is for, or its bound by the clock (`StopBound`, three minutes) ends it,
   its check cancelled and the presses let go, and the look reads on from the state it finds. The staged folder's change wakes
   the look (it did already); a restage ends what was under way for the build before it and drains the new one anew.
3. **`stopping` says what it waits on**: the check, the launcher, then (as `applying`) the close, each with the seconds waited and
   its bound, on every look (one a second while it stops) to the page, and in the log every ten seconds (`update.waiting`). A
   refusal, the bound, a cancel or a restage return to taking work at once. *Update now* holds the presses from the moment it is
   said, as D139 §3 says it closes; what it waits on is told, and bounded.
4. **The check is let go of, and its refusal is said.** The updater runs the check (`UpdateRefusal.Of`, then the proof the shell hands in) off the
   stop's task and stops waiting for it when the stop is ended; what the proof does meanwhile (its copy, its removal) finishes on its
   own, and a stop a look ends is let go of after one look. A refusal's sentence is in the `update.state` line (`message`), in
   `update/state.json` and printed by `daoris update`. The proof's copy of the home (`HomeProbe`, `HomeCopy`, `HomeProof`) is not
   changed here: its cost (8.7 GB copied on the install) and its refusal of 0.0.40 are the steward's own change.
5. **The terminal reads the state, never asks for it.** The application keeps `update/state.json` beside the staged build at
   every look (state, why, what it waits on, the last transition, the time of the word it read, its process); `daoris update` prints it first, saying where the
   process that wrote it is gone, and a word waits up to five seconds for a look of the application that has read the word, and prints that state, exit 1
   where that is a refusal. The terminal runs no check that takes a minute: its going-back refusal (a delivery unresolved) is said
   at once, the rest is the application's, said in the state. The "Daoris is updating" refusal points at `daoris update`.

**Why.** A person's *Update now* must never leave Daoris deaf: the hold on every press is the right price for a close that takes
seconds, and the wrong one for a check that took 24 minutes (53 on 2026-11-26). What was missing was not a faster check but that nothing
could end it and nothing said what it waited on. The states and their whys were spread over five flags (`_applying`,
`_deferred`, `_drainedFor`, `_closingSince`, the refusal kept), each read by the look that held the lock; one table says each
once, and a test replays each of the three failures.

**Rejected.**

- *Prove the build ahead, when it is staged, and keep the verdict.* A proof is about the home at the moment of the close (an
  account added or removed since); a verdict kept for minutes would be read as one it is not. With the stop let go of when it ends and bounded, running it at the close is bounded; a proof ahead remains open if the measured cost
  on the install says it is needed.
- *Run the check without the hold, then close.* A press let through while the check runs begins work the close then cuts, which
  D139's close reads again for (`work-began`); holding the presses is what makes the second read mean something. The hold now
  ends with the stop.
- *Make the drain cyclic, or cut at the bound, to find idle among sessions always in a turn.* D327 and D363 rejected both; the
  evidence (9 sessions, no idle in 1.5 h) is answered by what the owner has: *Update now*, which stops at once, and a state that
  says what it waits on and since when.
- *Keep the update's old log lines beside `update.state`.* Two ways to say a transition would drift; the rehearsal reads the one.
- *A real-time `CancelAfter` for the bound.* The bound is the look's, by the clock the updater was handed, so a test moves the
  clock and a stop that the process's own timers could not wake is still ended.
- *A repeated word restarts the stop.* The same mode for the same build, said again, is the word already acted on; only a different word ends a stop.
