## D369 — A repository's copies are a few leased slots under one keeper, saved before every clean and reset in place; rooms first where they share no build (2026-11-28)

**Decision (TREELIFE1, closing D214's open rounds; under D323, D231 and D349; the owner's direction: a finished copy is
reused or removed and copies never pile up, tens of GB written and deleted a day wear the drive, a better way deletes the
second, a structure is rebuilt when due, and D214's rooms-first plan stands with the kept slots as its fallback).**
Evidence: drive D fell from about 101 GB free in mid-November to about 20 GB on 2026-11-28 while the owner did not use
it. A census of main at `cf528b99d9` found nine ways to make a tree and about fourteen to remove one; none removes a
finished session's tree while its quest stays open, its commits landed under other commits, or it holds uncommitted
files; removed trees sit 14 days in `.tidied`; a `tree remove` left a 1.6 GB folder no press takes. The kept-slot pool
(`Workplace/Pool/`, 3,100 lines, four store tables, 37 test files) was built and connected to nothing, and D214 was still
proposed after ten rounds. Three warm trees grew apart from it: the check slots under the home's data folder (about
7.5 GB, which the update probe copied whole and the space view counted as history), the integration trees and the
replays. The fresh-design brainstorm `treelife-1128` (Fable 5.2 and DeepSeek, each from a purpose brief alone) agreed on
every structural point; its condition for evolving the pool instead (fixed paths, one owner, save before clean and a
journal already there) does not hold.

1. **Rooms first, where they help.** A repository whose declared rooms share no build closure works in place by rooms
   (D214 round 5). This repository's rooms share their closure (D214 round 6), so it, and every repository without such
   rooms, uses slots. Rooms are proved on such a repository first (ROOMS1).
2. **Slots under one keeper.** Each repository has a small set of slots at fixed paths, worktrees of its one repository.
   One keeper alone makes, leases, resets, parks and removes them; sessions, checks and the lander ask for a lease and
   give it back. A slot is made only when a request waits, under the cap and above the drive floor; otherwise the
   request waits, shown with its reason and place: a queue, never one more copy. The built pool's claims, generations,
   switch in place and recovery are kept where they fit this shape; the rest of it goes.
3. **Save before any clean.** On every return, park or removal the keeper pins the slot's head under a kept ref and
   commits its staged, unstaged and untracked-not-ignored files, and the ignored paths the repository names as work,
   onto it; only then may files be cleaned. A failed save holds the slot and is shown. Landed is judged later by content
   (patch id, then an empty diff after merging the line in); an unlanded save stays until the steward or the owner drops
   it.
4. **Reset in place.** A take switches the slot to its base, so git rewrites only what differs. Each kept item the
   repository names (dependencies from the store, build outputs, caches) has a key (lockfiles, toolchain versions):
   equal keeps it, different refreshes it. A check failing in a reused slot runs again once after a scrub; a changed
   toolchain or lockfile key, and a weekly pass, build from scratch.
5. **Budget and floor.** The slot count is the configured maximum or the budget divided by the measured slot size,
   whichever is smaller. Over budget the keeper trims free slots (outputs, then dependencies, then the least recently
   used slot), never a leased one. Below the drive floor (by default 30 GB free, a machine setting) no slot is made and
   free slots are trimmed.
6. **Parking.** A session idle for its repository's park time is saved and its slot freed; it resumes into its old slot
   if free, else any slot, through the seeded resume CONTEXT1 built.
7. **Crash safety.** Every move journals its intent before acting and its completion after, and each step can be run
   again. At each start the keeper reconciles its journal, the slot folders, git's worktree list and the live sessions:
   a take half done is cleaned, a reset half done runs again with its save first, a dead holder is parked, an unknown
   folder is saved and then removed (which also takes today's leftovers). A lease's processes run in one job object,
   ended before any file is touched.
8. **Checks and landing.** A session's checks run in its own slot. One line slot per repository serves merging, the
   landing check and the landing, replacing the check slots and the integration trees. No tree lives under the home's
   data folder.
9. **Measured.** A daily ledger per repository: bytes written, copies made, reused and removed, the reuse rate, take to
   ready at p50 and p95, queue time, slot bytes against the budget and saves pending, reconciled against the drive's
   free-space change; alerts on a budget breach, a floor crossed and no reuse in 24 hours.
10. **Configured.** One file per repository (slots minimum and maximum, budget, park time, kept items and their keys,
    ignored paths that are work), read from the line's tip; for a repository whose owner keeps tooling files out,
    Daoris holds the same file beside it (D349). Machine caps only narrow it; with no file a repository gets two slots
    and nothing kept.
11. **One cut-over.** The per-session worktree and its sweeps (the look's tree removal, the 14-day release, `.tidied`,
    the open's undo, the remake on resume), the pool's parts that do not fit, the separate check and integration trees,
    raw `worktree add` and prune calls and the duplicate deletes go in the release that switches slots on; sessions open
    then resume from their branch and their save. No compatibility is kept (D323).

**The trial comes first:** save-then-remove of today's per-session copies of this repository, with the ledger
recorded the day before. Kept if every removed copy restores from its save with its head and every listed file
identical by hash; no copy needs removing by hand for seven days; the landed test by content agrees with a hand check on
twenty copies and never calls unlanded work landed; saves total under 1 GB and take under a minute each; free space
rises by at least 95% of the removed copies' measured bytes. Retired if a file is missing or different after a restore,
a kill at any step deletes files before their save record exists, more than one copy in ten cannot be saved or removed
after two tries, or unlanded work is classed as landed even once.

**Rejected:** switching the built pool on as it stands (it lacks the keeper's invariants: save before clean, the budget,
the floor, parking and one owner of every tree); per-session copies with a better sweep (no bound, and removal still waits
on questions the sweeps fail to answer); copy-on-write clones on a Dev Drive as the route (they save a copy's bytes, not
its install and build), kept as an optional accelerator behind the same lease if the machine gains a ReFS volume.
