## D69 — The take claims by push; the quest sync lives in the hosts (2026-09-24)

**Decision.** The owner, choosing between three mechanisms for D68's rule 1: **the take itself claims
by push.** Every take commits on the machine where it was made. When the quest's workspace has a
remote, the take is then pushed and awaited before the answer returns:

- **accepted**: the quest is now taken;
- **lost** (another machine's take reached the remote first): the take is rebased into a conflict,
  and the answer is the existing *already taken, stand down*, so the session stands down before any
  work;
- **the remote cannot be reached**: the take stays local and unconfirmed, and the session runs.

This holds for a driven session and an outside one alike. So that the take and the tick run one
implementation, **the quest half of the sync moves from the driver into the hosts**. The driver's
tick asks its local host to sync. The driver still owns its processes: a session whose unconfirmed
take later loses is stopped by its own machine's driver (D68 rule 2).

**Amends D68's mechanism, and keeps D46 whole.** The sync design's §4 had the driver take the quest
before spawning. That was the alternative D46 rejected, and the reasons still hold. Taken has no way
back to Open, so a spawn that failed after the driver's take would strand the quest for good. Driven
sessions would need telling not to take. And driven work would become distinguishable from outside
work at the one layer where symmetry is the guarantee. Claiming at the take keeps the session the
claimant. It keeps the table without a release, and it keeps D68's promise of no duplicate work while
the remote answers. D68 §6 placed the sync in the driver because git provenance needs a spawn. That
reason is the knowledge feed's, not the quests', and the knowledge feed stays in the driver.

**Found while deciding, and part of it.** Once a machine's take on a quest has lost, that machine's
later pending moves on the same quest are made on a claim it never held. The rebase turns them into
conflicts too. Without this, an offline session that lost its take but finished its work would
still close the quest over the winner's take, because the table allows done from taken.

**Rejected.**
- **The driver takes, then spawns** (the design's §4 as first written): it reopens D46's rejected
  alternative, for the reasons above.
- **Claim, then stop the loser**: sessions take as before, and the driver pushes each take as soon
  as it sees one and stops a session whose take lost. It keeps the sync in the driver, but a real
  race online would still run a duplicate session for a few seconds. D68 promised none.

**Built 2026-10-07 (WAITCLAIM1): a wait made on a take that lost goes with the take.** QUESTOP1 found that the rule
above reached moves only. A wait (D79) moves no status, so a wait this machine made after its own take still applied to
the winner's quest when that take lost, and the winner's session was held by a question it never asked. Decided: **a
wait whose basis is this machine's own take is part of that take's move.** When the take loses, the rebase
drops the wait with it (`QuestLog.Lost` already forgets a wait), and the loss this machine reports names the dropped
wait. That loss is the take's conflict: in the pass's conflicts, and on the quest, which every machine shows. Its note
is the take's own note as given, then a sentence naming each question (*This take's wait on #q2 went with it, so this
quest does not wait on that question's answer.*), so the person who asked learns that the quest no longer waits on it. The
conflict is rewritten before it is pushed, so every machine keeps the one note. A wait on a quest this machine did not
take is unaffected, as is one made before any take of its own: only this machine's pending operations after its own
losing take are its move. The question stays a quest of its own and still goes up. Held by three tests in
`QuestSyncTests`: the race (A takes then waits, B's take wins; after A's pass the winner's quest waits on nothing and
A's conflict names the question), seen failing first with the winner's quest waiting on A's question; a wait on a quest
A did not take, kept by the same pass; and the note's words. `.claude/knowledge/quest-operations.md` says it under the
rebase's rule.

**What the gates do not cover.** The rule reaches a wait made before the pass that finds the take lost. A wait or a move
made after that pass, before this machine's driver stops the session, is judged against the winner's take, which
applies: `QuestStore.RebaseAsync` marks the claim lost only as it rewrites a pending take, never from a conflict already
written. The driver's pass note (`RemoteSyncPayloads.Pass`) and the HTTP sync answer's conflicts name the quest and the
attempted status, not the note, so the dropped wait is read on the quest's conflict, not in the pass's lines.

**Built 2026-10-07 (WAITCLAIM2): a move or a wait made after the pass that found the take lost is refused where it is
made.** This closes the first gap the note above names. The pass rewrites this machine's take into a conflict, and its
driver stops the session at its next look. In between, the session does not know, and a wait or a close it made applied
to the winner's quest and was pushed. Decided: **the store's verbs refuse them as the claim reads.** Inside its write,
`MoveAsync` or `WaitAsync` reads this machine's claim from the quest's history by the rule `ClaimAsync` answers from
(`QuestLog.Claim`). A done, a decline or a wait the table allows on a taken quest, where that claim is lost, is refused
and nothing is written (`QuestMove.ClaimLost`). The exchange answers it as the take's own loss is answered:
`AlreadyTaken`, a 409 at the HTTP door, saying the take lost and nothing moved, and for a wait that the question stays a
quest of its own. The judgement is local, like the table's, so D68's every verb commits locally holds: no remote is asked.

**Why this shape.** Refused, nothing enters any machine's log that was never this machine's to make, so nothing is
rewritten later and no note is amended. The session reads why on the answer it acts on. **Rejected: the rebase reading a
lost claim from a conflict already written.** The verbs would answer that the quest is done or waits, this machine would
show that until the next pass, and a close would publish a chain's next step only to forget it again. The done would then
become a second conflict that every machine shows over the winner's take. The wait would be dropped with nothing naming
it, since the remote has numbered the take's conflict and a rebase rewrites only what is pending.

**Its reach** is D69's rule's: every done, decline or wait this machine makes on the quest after its take lost, a
person's included. A dismissal, a yes and a verdict are no move on the take and are unaffected. So is an open quest, one
published again after a delete (D95), where nobody's work stands. The pass and the verb are each one transaction, so a
verb lands before the pass, where the rebase loses it, or after it, where the store refuses it. Held by
`QuestSyncTests.A_wait_and_a_close_made_after_the_pass_that_found_the_take_lost_are_refused_and_never_reach_the_winner`,
seen failing first with the winner's quest closed Done on A's note and waiting on A's question, and by
`QuestShareTests.A_wait_or_a_close_on_a_take_that_lost_is_refused_saying_so_and_nothing_reaches_the_remote` for the words,
seen failing with the wait answered `CannotWait`. WAITCLAIM1's and QUESTOP1's tests are unchanged.

**What the gates do not cover.** A remote judges a push by the table alone, so a machine on an older build still pushes
such a move. The family rehearsal's race checks compare a lost take's conflict notes, which this leaves as they were; they
were read, not run.

**Built 2026-10-07 (WAITCLAIM3): a remote refuses a move made on a take that lost.** This closes the gap the note above
names. A machine on a build before WAITCLAIM2 does not refuse a done, a decline or a wait its session makes after the pass
that found its take lost, and the remote judged the push by the table, which allows each on a taken quest, so the winner's
quest closed, or waited on the loser's question, on every machine. Decided: **the remote judges a push by the store's
rule.** `QuestLog.OnALostTake` is now the one rule the store's verbs and `QuestStore.ReceiveAsync` ask: a done, a decline or
a wait on a taken quest, whose machine's claim (`QuestLog.Claim`) reads lost in the remote's history and the push so far.
Such an operation refuses the push for that quest, as the table's refusals do, in the words the store's refusal is
answered in (`QuestExchange.LostTake`): the take lost and is kept on the quest as a conflict, this `done` is not this
machine's to make, the remote kept nothing this push carried for the quest, and stand down. The pass lists it under
`refused` and the driver relays it as it relays any refusal, so the wire is unchanged and an older machine reads it as it
reads every refusal. Its claim already reads lost, so its driver stops the session (D68 rule 2).

**Why a refusal.** Kept at the remote as a conflict, the operation would be two things: the pushing machine records the
number on its own done and replays a close, since a fetch numbers an operation a machine holds and never rewrites it.
Answered *behind*, it would send the machine round on a quest nothing moved, and its rebase, which reads a lost claim only
as it rewrites a pending take, would push the same done each round.

**Its reach** is WAITCLAIM2's, judged where the push lands: a winner's moves, a dismissal, a yes, a verdict, and every move
on an open quest (one published again after a delete, D95) are kept as before. Held by
`QuestSyncTests.A_move_or_a_wait_an_older_build_makes_after_its_take_lost_is_refused_at_the_remote_and_never_reaches_the_winner`
for a done, a decline and a wait, seen failing first with the winner's quest Done on the loser's note, Declined, and
waiting on the loser's question; and by two tests of what the remote keeps. WAITCLAIM1's, WAITCLAIM2's and QUESTOP1's tests
are unchanged.

**What the gates do not cover.** The older machine keeps what it made pending and shows it on its own copy of the quest.
Each pass pushes it and is refused again, with anything that machine makes on the quest after it, since a refusal is for
the quest's whole push. Upgraded, its rebase still applies the operation, so it stays pending there and is refused at
each pass. The test writes the older build's operation into the log by hand. No family-rehearsal check compares what a
remote answers a push: its race checks read a lost take's answer, its conflict and the tick's words through the hosts' own
passes, which no build of this one sends such a move through. They were read, not run.

**Built 2026-10-07 (WAITCLAIM4): what an older build left pending on a lost take is lost at the rebase.** This closes the
gap the note above names. Decided: **the rebase judges each pending operation by `QuestLog.OnALostTake`**, the rule the
verbs and the remote judge by, reading this machine's claim from the history as it replays it, so a take-conflict
already written counts, numbered or not. Such a stray goes as one made on a take the same rebase finds lost goes, by its
kind's rule (`QuestLog.Lost`): a done or a decline becomes a conflict of its own, with its note, and a wait is forgotten.
`QuestStore.IntegrateAsync` also rebases each quest of the circle where this machine holds a done, a decline or a wait
beside a take-conflict of its own, since no fetch touches the quest once that conflict has come back. The pass then
pushes the conflict, and the circle pushes clean.

**Why a conflict for a move, and no note for a wait.** A move that no longer applies is kept for a person, never dropped
(D68 §5): a done's note may be the only record of the loser's work. Pushed and numbered like any conflict, it gives every
machine one log and one note, the same as a done rebased with its pending take. WAITCLAIM2 rejected this shape for a live
verb, which can still refuse; a stray is already in the log, so it can only become a conflict or be forgotten. A wait
names no status, and the wire refuses a conflict that names none (QUESTOP1), so it is forgotten. WAITCLAIM1's note on the
take's conflict is not open to it: that conflict is already written, a remote may have numbered it, and no machine
rewrites a numbered operation, so an amended note would be this machine's alone. None is owed elsewhere: the remote never
kept the wait, so no other copy of the quest waits on the question, and each refusal the pass relayed said so.
**Rejected: rewriting only after the remote refused**, by a mark kept per refused quest and read at the next pass. It adds
a refused pass and a stored mark to wait for a verdict the machine reads from the same history by the same rule.

**The driver's words.** `RemoteSyncPayloads.Pass` said a refusal as *quest `#x` was not taken by the remote: …*, which
beside the lost take's reason reads as a take refused. It now names what was refused, the quest's push, and gives the
remote's reason whole after it: *quest `#x`: the remote refused what this machine pushed for it. Quest `#x` was taken on
another machine first: …*. The pass answer's shape is unchanged, so an older shell reads every refusal as it did.

Held by `QuestSyncTests.A_move_or_a_wait_an_older_build_left_pending_on_its_lost_take_becomes_its_loss_and_the_circle_pushes_clean`
for a done, a decline and a wait on a quest no fetch touches, seen failing first with every upgraded pass refused, and
failing again without the circle's sweep; and by
`RemoteSyncTests.A_refused_push_is_said_as_what_this_machine_pushed_with_the_remotes_reason_after_it`, seen failing on
the old words. WAITCLAIM3's test ran its older build's push through this build's pass, whose rebase now loses the
operation before it goes. It pushes as that build did (`PushAsAnOlderBuildAsync`), with every assertion unchanged, and
still fails with the remote's rule taken out. WAITCLAIM1's, WAITCLAIM2's and QUESTOP1's tests are unchanged.

**What the gates do not cover.** An older build's done published the chain's next step as a quest of its own, which may
have gone up. The rebase forgets a step only while its publish is unpushed (`ForgetFollowUpsAsync`), so a numbered step
stays on every machine though the chain did not move. No family-rehearsal check makes a stray or compares a refusal's
words, so no line it compares changes: its race checks and the sync output were read, not run.
