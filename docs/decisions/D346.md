## D346 — A merge gate is never killed for load, background runs yield the machine, and the time ratchet fails a merge only on what the branch made slow (2026-11-20)

**Decision (MERGENOKILL1, RATCHETOWN1; reverses TIMEWHY1's run again alone for a gate killed at its budget).** A gate past its
budget while the load sampler sees more heavy runs than its own is not killed: it runs on to its own end, its verdict its own and
its time a `load` breach that fails nothing, and only three times its budget ends it, as a runaway that fails. A gate past its
budget alone is killed and fails as its time, as before; a gate whose hang bound killed a test under load still runs again alone,
since that kill left it with no verdict. `reached` declares 20 minutes (13.4 min p90 of the merges run with no budget, times 1.5).
The machine's admission has two kinds of run: blocking (someone waits on it) and background (the heavy tier after a landing and the
CI beside, marked by `DAORIS_ADMISSION_BACKGROUND`, below normal priority). A background run takes a place only while no blocking
run waits, the background runs hold one place at most between them, and a background run starts no group, even one that takes no
place, while a blocking run waits; a blocking run waits only for the blocking runs asked before it. Both twins judge the shared table
(`fixtures/machine-admission.json`) and keep the `background` field; a writer that drops it makes the entry blocking, today's rule.
At a merge the time ratchet fails only on a test whose file the merge adds or changes, or one over half slower than main's alone
record of it; any other breach is inherited from main, a known red said with its cause and row, failing nothing. Main's record is
kept in `local/time-main.json`: each judging run writes each test's alone time with the commit it ran on (an open merge's
`MERGE_HEAD`, a clean tree's `HEAD`), up to five a test, and main's record is the slowest of those whose commit is in main's history.

**Why.** Measured 2026-11-14 to 20: half the merge failures were time, not tests (53 of 103 attempts, 22.6 h); since 11-18, 28 of 31
`reached` failures were budget kills, 22 of them under load, and the run again alone was killed again in 24 of 46 cases, since the
machine was not free within ten minutes: the heavy tier ran about 13 h a day at normal priority in the same first-come admission. A
kill under load gives no verdict, so it caught nothing, and the steward already turned the budget off on 152 of 234 runs. TIMEWHY1's
premise, that a rerun costs only when a kill happens and the machine frees within minutes, did not hold. The ratchet was red on 33
of 155 merges; 7 of the 13 reds with causes were one pre-existing `tools/merge/flow.test.ts` case (10 to 31 s against 10) on merges
that touched `tools/merge/`, the rest census scans, census or grid cases and one disk case: reach (TIMEREACH2) made a test the
merge's whenever a changed path reached it, however long it had been slow on main.

**Rejected.**

- *Keep the kill and run again alone, with a longer wait for a free machine*: the machine was busy for hours, not minutes; every
  wait is a merge held, and a kill still yields no verdict.
- *Stretch the budget with the load*: the budget is the one measure of a gate's own time; a scaled budget hides a slow gate on a busy
  machine and stays a kill on a very busy one.
- *No budget at a merge (the steward's `--no-timeout`)*: a runaway or a hang then holds the queue without end; the cap at three times
  the budget ends it.
- *Background runs at idle priority, or pausing them by a halt file*: idle starves behind a busy machine and the Process half's
  timing tests flake (CIPRIO1's finding); a halt file is one run's, while the admission is the one record every run already reads.
- *Judge the ratchet by reach, as before*: it failed merges for tests they never touched, seven of thirteen reds with causes.
- *A slowdown against the newest single alone record*: one tool case ran 10 to 31 s alone within a week, so a newest-record rule
  would call its own noise a slowdown; the slowest of main's last five records keeps a real slowdown (over half again) red.
- *Write the inherited breaches into the CI's findings (`local/ci-findings.json`)*: those are a stage's red of a gate on a commit, read
  as main's by every merge; a time breach is the ratchet's and the heavy tier measures it alone, so it rides on the breach report.
