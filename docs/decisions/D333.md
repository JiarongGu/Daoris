## D333 — The title strip holds the scope, search, the waits and + Ask; no menu bar, one app menu (2026-11-20)

**Decision (UISH4, the window redesign `docs/2026-11-19-window-design.md` §4.2, §4.4 and §15.1; superseding D152's menu bar and
D75's naming of the scope in two places).**

1. The 36 px title strip holds, in order: the mark with its **app menu** (the window acts with no page of their own: new window,
   zoom, theme, language, shortcuts, about, quit), the **scope switcher** (*All workspaces* or one workspace; the scope's only
   home), the **search field** (the palette's door), the **waits pill** (the window's one number), **+ Ask** (Ctrl+N), the Ask
   Daoris door and the caption buttons.
2. There is no menu bar. Every verb has a visible button where it works and a palette row; a census over `menus/commands.ts`
   fails a command with no visible door unless it says why it is palette-only. The keys stay in that one table and are printed on
   their buttons.
3. The status bar's copy of the scope, the layout toggles, the bell and the review chip go.
4. Ctrl+1–5 and Ctrl+, open the places; Ctrl+N opens a new ask (work starts as an ask); a new conversation keeps Sessions' + and
   gains Ctrl+Shift+N.

**Why.** The window has no editor: every verb already has a button and a palette row, so seven menus were a third way to each
job, and the strip needs their room for the scope, the waits and + Ask. The scope named in the command centre and again in the
status bar was one fact in two places.

**Rejected.**

- *Keeping the menu bar beside the palette* (D152): a third door to every verb.
- *The scope in the status bar*: it is a choice the owner makes, not an ambient fact, and it filters every list.
- *Ctrl+N for a new conversation*: the purpose starts work as an ask; a conversation is opened from Sessions.
