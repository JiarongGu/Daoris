## D202 — Daoris's git reads through one reader per repository, and Review is a held dependency graph of id-keyed reads, depth 2 and at most ten starts in the ordinary cold case, none per untracked file and none when a valid watch says nothing moved, read as an identified and validated observation with one head id for both halves; the person gets the reads a git management app offers, the ref acts, linear history edits from objects, and the working-tree acts in their own checkout behind a switch, never in a session's tree (2026-10-23)

**Decision (GITAPP1).** **Status: proposed**, round 2, revised by Claude Opus after Codex's read and Fable's foundation read of round 1
(D191; the notes below). Round 1 was the synthesis of GITAPP1's co-design from the brief and the three sealed proposals (Claude's,
Codex's through Daoris's helper, Fable's), at `92c7b8ed5`. The steward accepts or sends it round again. The contract is
`docs/2026-10-23-git-app-design.md`; the reads are `docs/2026-10-23-git-app-reads.md`, answered in the design's §16.

The direction (GITAPP1, 2026-10-23): Daoris's git as comprehensive as a git management app, and Review (审阅) fast. Measured on
this machine under its working load, one Review open made `12 + I + M + Uᵣ` serial git starts before the Accept offer (I an
independence read where the candidate bases differ, M REVIEWBASE3's merge, Uᵣ the untracked files sent to git before the patch budget
stops), and the offer a comparison per eligible branch after six: 16.2 s for 368 committed files, 44.1 s with 50 untracked, 8.4 s for
1,771 files. A start costs 0.2-1.6 s here; the likeliest cause, an inference from PROC1a's and UIFREEZE1's stacks and two probes, is
process creation slowed by a real-time scanner and load: programs that open no repository cost as much, and eight starts together
finish in the time of one or two.

1. **Review is a dependency graph** (design §3), built as data in the driver (`ReviewGraph`) and held by a test: each node a read with
   its kind, its key and the nodes it needs, each started the moment its inputs answer. The layout and the branch name come from disk;
   the refs from one observation (point 4). **Ordinary cold: depth 2, at most nine starts, ten with untracked files**; depth 1 once the
   observation stands; every other path (two candidates, REVIEWBASE3's merge, no line, a displaced base, a landed session, an older git)
   has its own depth and ceiling, computed from the graph and failing the census on one start more. **None per untracked file**: each is a
   display addition read from disk, exact for a stated subset of ordinary text files, never a staging patch, one start for all their
   attributes. **None on an open where a valid watch says nothing moved.** ReviewBase's semantics are kept whole. The Accept offer and
   the hand-off plan are asked after the diff answers, and timed apart.
2. **The targets, provisional**, under the brief's load, median and p90: 368 files **≤ 3 s / 6 s** (16.2 s today); with 50 untracked
   **≤ 3.2 s / 6.4 s** (44.1 s); 1,771 files, a complete list with bounded patches, **≤ 4 s / 8 s** (8.4 s); opened again with nothing
   moved **≤ 0.1 s**; a live session's commit shown **≤ 3 s** after its ref write. They are acceptance targets for the bench and the
   installed window, not derived from arithmetic; the graph's depth and start counts are the gate.
3. **No lock and no index refresh in a tree Daoris does not own: REVIEWLOCK1's rule as it measured it.** `git --no-optional-locks
   <command>`, the option before the subcommand, on every `status` and `diff`; and `-c diff.autoRefreshIndex=false` on a diff against
   the working tree, which git 2.53 refreshes whatever the option says, its stat-only rows dropped. REVIEWLOCK1 is built on its own branch
   and lands first; this decision points to it, widens its census to the reader's one launch seam, and states which configured programs
   each read kind may run (no external diff or textconv on an object read; no merge driver in `merge-tree`).
4. **One `GitReader` per common git directory** (§4-§5), identified by that directory, one registry per process, in the driver library.
   Every git it starts goes through one launch seam that classifies, flags and counts it, then `WorkingTree.GitStart` and the gate of four
   as `StartClass.Background`. **Two schedulers, said apart**: the gate bounds creation; the reader bounds whole runs, attended reads
   first, long reads one per reader and two per process. **The refs observation** is identified and validated, never an atomic view:
   stamps and the watch generation taken before and after its `for-each-ref`, small ref contents read whole, git's racy-time rule,
   linked trees' membership and the stash's log included, read again before every use, failing closed on a layout it cannot read. **One
   head id answers both halves of a review**: the uncommitted diff is taken against it and checked against status's own `branch.oid`.
   The cache's keys carry ids, options, the parser's version and a **context id** validated before lookup, joining and completion and
   carried into the page's keys; nothing kept where replace refs, grafts or a shallow history change git's answer; a partial clone never
   fetches on a read. **Who owns each git process** is one table. **No held git process for Review**; the screens may hold one `cat-file
   --batch-command`, framed by length, owned and retired by its reader, 30 s idle, four per process, ended by a repack sign, as its own row
   and only after the repack hazard is measured.
5. **Invalidation, never expiry.** A review depends on its tree's actual `HEAD`, the branch it names, the line here and on origin, and
   its recorded base: a line move invalidates every review in the repository. The reader's ref roots are a watch of their own, beside
   today's, so its widening wakes no judgement; every attended mutable tree, live or ended, keeps its uncommitted answer under a tree
   watch installed before the answer is read, and any loss of the watch costs a counted validation read. The page says *Read at … · HEAD
   …* and, where the tree moved during the read, *Changed while it was read*.
6. **The person's git** (§7-§8). The reads §7.1 lists, which is the capability boundary (an image diff, interactive history editing, a free
   three-way editor and others are deferred, said). The ref and remote acts: fetch, create, rename and delete a local branch, local tags,
   push one branch at a commit. **Linear history edits with no working tree**: pick, revert and rebase a range of non-merge, non-root,
   unpublished commits onto a branch no tree has checked out, computed from objects and applied by one ref transaction. **The
   working-tree acts in the person's own checkout, behind the switch *Daoris may change this checkout's files* in its Setup's Reach, off
   by default**: stage and unstage by file, hunk and line from separate HEAD → index and index → worktree comparisons, commit, amend an
   unpushed commit, stash, switch, and resolve a conflict a side per hunk or in the person's editor, in a Changes tab beside Branches and
   History, every act re-judged under one **checkout lock** that the merge landing and a conversation's start in the checkout also take.
   Refused: a hard reset, `clean`, any force, a remote delete, a recursive submodule write, `push -u`, and any checkout act while a
   conversation runs there or a landing holds it.
7. **Every write says how it recovers.** Ref removals, renames and rewrites reuse the judged-delete transaction: a verify for each ref
   the judgement read, the recovery ref created in the same transaction, absence only on git's own "missing", and a branch checked out
   between judgement and effect put back. A stash apply that conflicts leaves the conflicts and keeps the stash; a stash is dropped by its
   commit id. Plans name the hooks an act runs; Daoris's own fetch runs no maintenance.
8. **D198's prerequisites.** A remote act needs Daoris's git doors installed; a person's push needs GITAUTH1f and a grant its press
   writes, never a landing's; a tag push waits for GITAUTH1g's transport judge; a terminal writing verb is a person's press through
   `PersonPresses`, the `DAORIS_SESSION_ID` refusal kept as an extra guard.
9. **A session's tree is the session's.** The person reads it in full and reaches it by words; no person's act writes it. Agents get
   nothing new.
10. **Nothing of Daoris's own choosing written into `.git` and nothing cached on disk**: no ref, configuration or file beyond the named
    exceptions (a recovery ref, a renamed branch's section); objects where git's own commands write them; no `commit-graph`, fsmonitor
    or untracked-cache press, the command shown to copy where one would help.
11. **Both doors** (D50): `daoris-driver git review <session> --time` is the measurement door, built before the page changes; each read
    and act has its verb. Ask Daoris's `git` kind proposes the acts; the reads stay exempt.
12. **The rows**, each landing alone: REVIEWLOCK1 (built); GITAPP1a-i the reader and Review's graph, benched; 1a-ii Review's page on the
    observation; 1b Review follows the tree; 1c the judgements through the reader; 1d-i the commit page and diff views; 1d-ii blame, file
    history, compare, search; 1d-iii the Branches tab's reads and the conflict preview; 1d-iv the held object reader; 1e the ref and
    remote acts; 1f history edits from objects; 1g-i the switch, the lock and the Changes tab read; 1g-ii stage, unstage, commit, amend;
    1g-iii switch and stash; 1g-iv resolve; 1h history at scale; 1i the install, seen.

**Why.** Review's cost is the number of dependent starts on a machine where a start is slow: fewer starts and fewer dependent steps
are cheaper under any cause, and git's binary already gives parallelism, so no library is needed (D147 §4.3 stands). A graph held as
data makes its depth a fact a test can hold, where a count of "rounds" was a scheduling label. An answer named by ids cannot change, so
the second look is free once a reader keeps it and knows when the ids moved; but git moves between any two reads, so what the reader
knows is an observation it identifies and validates, never a view it assumes current. The working-tree acts go where the person's
changes are, and a switch the person sets makes that their choice, made once and visible.

**Chosen among the three proposals**, each question by question in the design's §2 and §14: Codex's range semantics, display addition
and effect policy over Claude's four starts (which drop REVIEWBASE1's union counts and REVIEWBASE3); Fable's layout from disk, stamp,
per-reader bound, Background class and medians; Claude's offer off the critical path, history edits from objects and the person's
checkout behind a switch.

**Rejected.**
- **Claude's ≤ 1.5 s and four starts**: met only by changing what Review shows. **Codex's 4-5 s**: not taken as the targets; the
  targets stay provisional and the bench decides.
- **Three rounds as the promise**: the cold path's depth is the graph's, per path.
- **Codex's manual worktree for the person**: the person's edits are in their checkout, and it is a second kind of tree with its
  own lock, clean-up and ownership.
- **Fable's acts in an idle session's tree**: the session may go on and find commits its transcript and trace do not explain;
  its work is the session's until it lands (D51, D164).
- **Review on the person's place in the gate**: a level of starts would take the reserved place, and an agent the person asked for
  would queue behind git; `waitedMs` decides any later priority inside the background class.
- **A held process for two idle minutes, or a cap across the machine**: it keeps packs open while a repack runs, which Git for
  Windows answers with a prompt; a registry is per process, so the cap is too.
- **A hand-built untracked patch claimed byte-equal to git**: exact only for a stated subset, and never staged.
- **The `commit-graph`, fsmonitor and untracked-cache presses**: nothing measured needs them, and D147 §4.2 stays whole.
- **`ahead-behind` for both counts**: not REVIEWBASE1's union when the line here and on origin diverge.
- **libgit2, a reader of git's files, an index or cache on disk, polling, a patch per file from git, a free three-way editor,
  interactive history editing this round, a force push, a delete on origin, fetching on a timer, a wider gate.**
