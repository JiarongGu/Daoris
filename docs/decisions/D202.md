## D202 — Daoris's git reads through one reader per repository and Review asks git at most ten questions in three parallel rounds, none per untracked file and none when nothing moved; the person gets every read of a git management app, the ref acts, history edits from objects, and the working-tree acts in their own checkout behind a switch, never in a session's tree (2026-10-23)

**Decision (GITAPP1).** **Status: proposed**, round 1, the synthesis of GITAPP1's co-design under D191, written by Claude Opus
from the brief and the three sealed proposals (Claude's, Codex's through Daoris's helper, Fable's). Codex's and Fable's reads
follow through the steward; Fable reads because this changes a foundation (one reader every flow's git goes through, and a held
process). The contract is `docs/2026-10-23-git-app-design.md`.

The direction (GITAPP1, 2026-10-23): Daoris's git as comprehensive as a git management app, and Review (审阅) fast. Measured on
this machine under its working load, one Review open made 13-19 serial git starts plus one per untracked file: 16.2 s for 368
committed files, 44.1 s with 50 untracked, 8.4 s for 1,771 files. A start costs 0.2-1.6 s here because process creation is slow
under a real-time scanner and load, not because git is slow: programs that open no repository cost as much, and eight starts
together finish in the time of one or two (Fable's probe, PROC1a, UIFREEZE1).

1. **Review in rounds** (design §3). The tree's layout and the refs come from disk and a stamped snapshot, with no process. Round
   1 starts `status`, the uncommitted diff, both merge-bases and the recorded base's ancestry together; round 2, only where needed,
   `--independent` and REVIEWBASE3's merge; round 3 the committed diff and REVIEWBASE1's two union counts. **At most ten starts
   cold** (eleven with REVIEWBASE3), **none per untracked file**, whose patch is written from the file and proved byte-equal to
   git's, and **none on an open where nothing moved**. ReviewBase's semantics are kept whole. The Accept offer and the hand-off plan
   are asked after the diff answers, and timed apart.
2. **The targets**, under the brief's load, median and p90: 368 files **≤ 3 s / 6 s** (16.2 s today); with 50 untracked **≤ 3.2 s
   / 6.4 s** (44.1 s); 1,771 files **≤ 4 s / 8 s** (8.4 s); opened again with nothing moved **≤ 0.1 s**, no start; a live
   session's commit shown **≤ 3 s** after its ref write. Start counts are a gate; times are a bench and the installed window,
   reported, and a row's acceptance.
3. **No optional lock in a session's tree.** Every read-only `status` and `diff` Daoris runs in a tree passes
   `--no-optional-locks` (REVIEWLOCK1, built first, held by a census), so a Review never takes a running session's `index.lock`.
4. **One `GitReader` per repository** (§4), keyed by its common git directory, in the driver library, one registry per process:
   the layout, the refs snapshot validated by the ref files' stamps before every use and dropped by the ref watch and by Daoris's
   own moves, the one shared id-keyed cache (64 MiB, memory only, its key carrying the options, the parser's version and the
   repository's context, nothing kept where replace refs, grafts or a shallow history change git's answer), in-flight joining,
   and at most three of its own starts in flight, a long read holding at most one. Every start passes `WorkingTree.GitStart` and
   the gate of four as `StartClass.Background`. **No held git process for Review**; the screens may hold one `cat-file
   --batch-command`, 30 s past its last question and four across the machine, only after it is measured against the person's own
   repack.
5. **Invalidation, never expiry** (§5). An answer names the ids it was read at, and the page says *Read at … · HEAD …* where it
   shows a kept one; a stale answer never overwrites a newer one or enables an act. An open review of a live session keeps its
   uncommitted answer under a watch of its tree. The ref watch widens to every registered repository with a checkout, within its
   cap, and hears tags.
6. **The person's git** (§7-§8). Every read a git management app offers: the graph with filters, a commit, diff views, blame, a
   file's history, compare, search (the pickaxe as a long read), stashes, tags, remotes, worktrees, the reflog, submodules, LFS
   pointers, and a conflict preview before Accept. The ref and remote acts: fetch, create, rename and delete a local branch, push
   one branch at a commit, local tags; a tag push waits for GITAUTH1f's Dangerous. **History edits with no working tree**: pick,
   revert and rebase a branch no tree has checked out, computed from objects and applied by one ref write. **The working-tree acts
   in the person's own checkout, behind the switch *Daoris may change this checkout's files* on its Setup, off by default**: stage
   and unstage by file, hunk and line, commit, amend an unpushed commit, stash, switch, and resolve a conflict a side per hunk or in
   the person's editor, in a Changes tab beside Branches and History. Refused: a hard reset, `clean`, any force, a remote delete, a
   recursive submodule write, `push -u`, and any checkout act while a conversation runs there. Every ref rewrite keeps a recovery
   ref first.
7. **A session's tree is the session's.** The person reads it in full and reaches it by words; no person's act writes it. Agents
   get nothing new: they run their own git, and a writing `daoris-driver git` verb refuses under `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`.
8. **Nothing written into `.git` on Daoris's own and nothing cached on disk**: no `commit-graph`, fsmonitor or untracked-cache
   press; where one would help, the command is shown to copy.
9. **Both doors** (D50): `daoris-driver git review <session> --time` is the measurement door, and each read and act has its verb.
   Ask Daoris's `git` kind proposes the acts; the reads stay exempt.
10. **The rows**: REVIEWLOCK1; GITAPP1a Review fast; 1b Review follows the tree; 1c the judgements through the reader; 1d the reads
    drawn (takes GIT1f, GIT1i); 1e the ref and remote acts (takes GIT1g, GIT1h, GIT1k); 1f history edits from objects; 1g the
    person's checkout; 1h history at scale; 1i the install, seen.

**Why.** Review's cost is the number of serial starts on a machine where a start waits on its scanner: starting fewer and starting
them together is the cure, and git's binary already gives parallelism, so no library is needed (D147 §4.3 stands). An answer
named by ids cannot change, so the second look is free once a reader keeps it and knows when the ids moved; the stamp is git's own
files read at use, so it catches what a watch missed. The working-tree acts go where the person's changes are: a git management
app works in the person's checkout, and a switch the person sets makes that their choice, made once and visible.

**Chosen among the three proposals**, each question by question in the design's §2 and §14: Codex's ten-start ceiling and its
range semantics over Claude's four starts (which drop REVIEWBASE1's union counts and REVIEWBASE3); Fable's rounds, layout from
disk, stamp, per-reader bound, Background class and medians; Claude's offer off the critical path, history edits from objects and
the person's checkout behind a switch.

**Rejected.**
- **Claude's ≤ 1.5 s and four starts**: met only by changing what Review shows. **Codex's 4-5 s**: looser than the arithmetic, so
  a chain still half serial passes.
- **Codex's manual worktree for the person**: the person's edits are in their checkout, and it is a second kind of tree with its
  own lock, clean-up and ownership.
- **Fable's acts in an idle session's tree**: the session may go on and find commits its transcript and trace do not explain;
  its work is the session's until it lands (D51, D164).
- **Review on the person's place in the gate**: five starts would take the reserved place and an agent the person asked for would
  queue behind git; `waitedMs` decides any later change.
- **A held process for two idle minutes**: it keeps packs open while the person's tools repack, which Git for Windows answers
  with a prompt in the person's terminal.
- **The `commit-graph`, fsmonitor and untracked-cache presses**: this repository already has a `commit-graph` with changed-path
  filters, nothing measured needs them, and D147 §4.2 stays whole.
- **`ahead-behind` for both counts**: not REVIEWBASE1's union when the line here and on origin diverge.
- **libgit2, a reader of git's files, an index or cache on disk, polling, a patch per file from git, a free three-way editor,
  a force push, a delete on origin, fetching on a timer, a wider gate.**

**This amends** D147 point 2 and its design §2.8 (the acts now offered), point 3 and design §3.1 (acts in the person's checkout
behind its switch; a history edit writes objects and one ref), and point 6 and design §4.1 (one process a view becomes the reader:
bounded rounds, a held object reader for the screens, a stamped snapshot); design §3.1's "D51 rule 7" reads D102's SQUASHTIDY1c
note and CLEANUP1. It amends D113 §5 by the switch and D150 point 3 by the Changes tab. D147 §4.2, D198, D51, D55, D109, D164,
D173 and D181 stand.

**Not measured**: the installed window; LANDING, HANDOFF_PLAN and the offer beside the diff; hundreds of real untracked files;
the held process's pack-hold hazard; the stamp on a reftable repository; the tree watch under a dependency install;
`GIT_NO_LAZY_FETCH` on this git; an idle machine. Each row measures what it rests on before it is accepted.
