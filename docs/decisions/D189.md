## D189 — A session's context is Daoris's record: the agent's own conversation is resumed wherever it holds, and a session that moves to another conversation, on any agent, account or access, is handed Daoris's brief of it, composed without a model (2026-10-20)

**Decision (SESSCTX1, accepted).** **Status: accepted** on 2026-10-20 under D191, after Codex's and Fable's reads (design
§14); the note of that date says what changed. Its rows build in the design's order (§11).

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-20: Daoris manages a session's context, so that a conversation can change its
permissions, and even its agent, mid-session. Read at `4e12716b9`, a conversation's context lives in its harness's own store,
in the configuration home of the account it ran on (D131 point 2). An access change keeps it only while the same conversation
resumes (ACCESSSWAP1, in flight). Every other move, to another agent (D163), another account (D125 point 4, D165 point 3) or
past a refused resume (D131 point 3), hands the new part four facts: the request as first written, the last plan, the last
words and what is uncommitted. A refused resume of a conversation hands nothing until the person presses *Start a conversation
with these words* (D137 §2.2). The contract is `docs/2026-10-20-session-context-design.md`.

1. **Two contexts.** The agent's own conversation is the working context: exact, the harness's, one per record (D131 point 4,
   D167 point 1), never read, copied or written by Daoris. Daoris's record is the portable context: the words the session
   took and who said them, the agent's messages, the plan, each tool call's kind, title, files and status, Daoris's notes, the
   kept attachments, and the tree. Daoris keeps all of it already, and adds no store of the conversation.
2. **Resume where the conversation holds; otherwise a new part.** The same agent, account and tree, a kept id and a door that
   resumes: the same part goes on in its own conversation, under whatever access is set now (ACCESSSWAP1). Another agent,
   another account, or a resume refused: a new part of the session's entry (D167), in the same tree, on the agent, account and
   access chosen. The old part ends at its turn's end, never mid-turn, before the new starts. A tree gone, a teammate's record,
   an intake and a stand-down stay as they are.
3. **A refused resume no longer ends a line.** A conversation, or a closed quest's session, whose own conversation cannot resume
   goes on by itself in a new part handed the brief, said why; an access change whose resume is refused goes the same way.
   *Start a conversation with these words* stays the person's own press. A driven fallback, a carry-on after a limit and a
   hand-over stay new parts, handed the brief in place of the four facts.
4. **The brief** is a pure function of the line's records (the part moved from and, by `from`, those before it), written in
   Daoris's one English with no model. It carries its opening (what it is, and that it is not the agent's memory), the request,
   every word the session took (the person's verbatim, another agent's as its claim), each turn's last message and one line of
   what the turn did, the plan, the uncommitted paths and commits, the attachments, the outgoing agent's own note where it
   wrote one, the entry's other parts, and what was left out. It is at most 40,000 characters: the person's words first, then
   the turns newest first. It is kept as the new part's first event with its CONTEXT1 account (design §3).
5. **Never handed:** the agent's thoughts, a tool call's output, a summary any model wrote, an account's name, or anything read
   or written in a harness's store.
6. **A left conversation is not resumed again.** It stays in its account's home; going back to its agent or account opens
   another new part.
7. **Compaction.** Inside a part, the harness's own. Daoris's compaction is a new part handed the brief: at a move, and at the
   person's *Go on fresh* once measured. No automatic threshold (D160).
8. **Cost and record.** Each move keeps the part's context at its end and the brief's size; each part keeps its own usage,
   cache reads and writes as its door reports, never a price (D24). Each move is a coded line on both parts, `from` gains the
   why `resume`, and the machine log writes `session.moved`. The *Parts* strip says how a part began, in both languages.

**Why.** The person's words after the first are what a move loses today, and they are the person's instructions as the work
went on. Daoris already keeps them, with every answer and every turn's trace, on records that are its own and agent-neutral,
so a brief composed from them carries a session to any maker's agent without a model and without reaching into an account's
home. The agent's own conversation is exact and, while it holds, the cheapest way on, so it is tried first: a move writes a
bounded brief in place of the whole context, which is cheaper on every later request (design §5) but costs the agent's exact
memory. D167 already shows a session's parts as one session, so a move needs no new record shape. A refused resume was left
to the person's press because a new conversation knew nothing; the brief removes that reason.

**Rejected.** Daoris as the only context, with no resume; copying or writing a harness's store across accounts or makers; a
model-written summary (D163 stands); a closing turn asked of the outgoing agent before every move; a record holding several
conversations in turn (D167); handing every event as kept; going back to a left conversation with what came after; an
automatic compaction threshold; a fork or an in-place mode change to change access (D183). Design §9 says why.

**Amends,** once accepted: D131 point 3 (its fallback is handed the brief); D137's design §2.2 (a closed quest's session or a
chat goes on by itself where it cannot resume); D163 point 1 and its rejection of carrying the transcript (the brief, bounded,
never the transcript whole); D167 §1.5 (what a new part is told); D125 point 4 and D165 point 3 (what a carry-on or an
account move is handed); D183 (an access change whose resume is refused goes on in a new part). D131 points 1, 2 and 4, D167
point 1, D184, D188 and D24 stand. Each amended decision carries a note pointing here.

**The rows** are design §11: SESSCTX1a measures first (§12), since the cost and the resume-first rule lean on what has not
run here; then SESSCTX1b-e; SESSCTX1f is held.

**What the checks do not cover.** Documents only; nothing is built. The statements about today were read from the code at
`4e12716b9`; those about the harnesses were read from earlier designs that read the makers' packages and source, or from
Daoris's code. None was measured: Claude Code's native `--resume` has never run here, nor a Codex thread resumed after its
process ended, nor the cache's reads and writes at a resume under changed access (design §12). `verify` checks this entry's
place and shape and the design's links, and none of their words.

*2026-10-20, another maker's read (design §14).* Eight findings, each checked against the code, all taken, still proposed.
Point 3 narrows: a conversation whose own conversation is gone or not resumable here goes on by itself; a resume refused as
`elsewhere` (another client holds it) or for a reason that does not say it is lost waits for the person, the brief ready.
Point 4's line is the chain of parts each continued, one link per continuation, which SESSCTX1b writes first, since a carry-on
on the same account writes no `from` today (`Driver.cs:3298-3320`); a word counts once by its id, a word taken back is left
out, an answered word is a past exchange, and the brief adds the last check with its commit, the open questions and the
plan's age; its bound is provisional, filled by per-section caps with room for what was left out, and counted in both
languages. The *Why*'s cost claim is withdrawn: a move is not claimed cheaper, since the context's high-water says nothing of
what a compacted conversation sends, and the resume comes first for continuity. A resumed start says the access it asked for
and the posture the agent answered, since an optional posture the agent does not offer is left as it was
(`Acp.cs:1880-1910`). The owner's review has a fourth choice: whether the line's history as a tool (SESSCTX1f) is built with
the brief.

*2026-10-20, settled under D191:* Fable read the design after Codex, fifteen findings, each checked against the code by the
steward and taken; the steward also settled the four choices the design had left for the owner. D189 is accepted, its body
amended as follows (design §14 keeps both reads and their verdicts; the code was read again at `d0e51b54b`).
- **Point 2.** "Under whatever access is set now" holds only as far as it is confirmed: a resumed start keeps the access it
  asked for and the mode the agent reports as current where the protocol reports one, and otherwise says the mode is not
  confirmed, since today the answer to `session/set_mode` is discarded and a mode update dropped (`Acp.cs:1946-1954`, `:2134`).
  Whether the native `--permission-mode` holds on `--resume` is SESSCTX1a's to measure (design §2.1).
- **Point 3.** A session moves by itself only where its conversation is proven lost: the protocol door's `resource_not_found`,
  `unkept`, `unable` or `offered`. The native door's failed exit before it named its conversation, marked `gone` today
  (`ChatRunner.cs:1840-1846`, `DriverContinuations.cs:427-432`), is tried once more as a resume and then waits for the person,
  and SESSCTX1c splits it from `gone`. A refusal on the wire and `elsewhere` (Codex's alone) wait for the person, the brief
  ready; an adapter no longer installed waits with the hand-over's offer; a resumed run that could not start is a start
  failure, never a move. The rule is the same for a driven record's fallback, whose new part's `from` says `retry` (design §2).
- **Point 4.** Every continuation writes its `from`, with three new whys, `resume`, `carry-on` and `retry`, added with their
  twins in both lanes in one branch, and the *Parts* strip says how a part began by its why rather than taking every link as a
  hand-over (`Trees/SessionEntries.cs:128`). A channel's message gains its own mark on its `user` event, so the brief never
  reads Daoris's English for it. A new part may read the kept folders of the parts its line continues. The tree's head is read
  as the brief is composed, since a part's end commit is not recorded; a check is a tool call matching a gate the repository
  declares, and the last failed one carries its kept output, up to 2,000 characters, so point 5's "a tool call's output" has
  that one exception. A driven part's brief is a section of its composed instruction, accounted within it; a conversation's
  is its first prompt's event. The outgoing agent's note waits on MIXAGENT1c's tool, which does not exist yet; until then it
  is the closing note below. A driven part stopped for a move has no last message for its interrupted turn, and its outcome
  is its tool lines. The bound stays a provisional 40,000, cut into sections (design §3.2) so the turns' outcomes keep at
  least 12,000, a Han character counted as three until SESSCTX1a's counts.
- **Rejected.** The closing turn's rejection now covers a move the account forces (`near`, `limit`, `curve`) alone. Where
  the account has room and the move is decided while the outgoing agent can still take a turn (the person's press, a change
  of agent), it is asked at its turn's end for one closing note of at most 4,000 characters, kept as its claim; a move never
  waits more than that turn. An access change whose resume is refused asks none, since that refusal is met only after its
  part ended. The fork stays rejected because it keeps the same agent and account and gains nothing a resume does not where
  a resume holds.
- **The rows.** SESSCTX1f is no longer held: it is built right after SESSCTX1b, a read-only connector tool over the line's
  events on this machine, its reader twinned with the driver's as the helpers' box is, since the connector references the
  service alone. SESSCTX1c follows ACCESSSWAP1b for the driven door; both ACCESSSWAP1 and ACCESSSWAP1b are merged (D183's
  notes), so it follows SESSCTX1b alone.

*2026-10-20, the steward's amendment at the merge (D191).* Two readings of the settling are settled. The closing note is never
asked where an access change's resume is refused: that refusal is met at the resume, after the part already ended at its
turn's end, so no turn is left to ask, and a lost conversation cannot be asked at all. And the proven-loss rule binds a
conversation and a closed quest's session, not a driven record's fallback: a driven quest's session runs without a person by
design (D131 point 3, D184), so where its resume is refused on the wire, or its native door exits before naming its
conversation and one retry fails the same way, it carries on by itself in a new part handed the brief, `from` why `retry`, as
D131 point 3 always did with the four facts. Only `elsewhere`, another client holding the conversation, holds a driven record
for the person, since a second writer in its tree could redo its work. SESSCTX1c's row says so.

*2026-10-20, SESSCTX1b:* the brief is built (point 4), and every continuation writes its link first. A driven carry-on names
`retry` where its resume fell back and `carry-on` otherwise, `person` where the person's *Try again* or bring-forward carried it on;
a hand-over and a move onto another account keep their own why, and `resume` waits for SESSCTX1c to write it. `HandOverWhy.Known`
stays the box's five, and `Continues` names all eight, each worded in both catalogues (`note.handed.why.*`). Three readings were
settled in the build. A part with a link says how it began by its why, `resume` as a `PartBegan` of its own, `moved`, and a link
whose why this build has no word for reads as a hand-over, as every link did. A check is a call whose command, as its kept input
has it, holds a declared gate's command whole, or runs `daoris-driver run-heavy`; the output carried is that of the newest failed
check whose gate was not since run green, since a failure fixed later is no longer the work's state. A record whose events kept
none of the agent's words gives its transcript's last plain lines as its last message, so no carry-on loses the last words it was
handed before. Two fields were added to the `user` event, a stored shape read as before where absent: `channel`, the channel
message's id, and `continues`, on a brief kept as a conversation's first prompt, which the conversation's opening and first ask
are never read from. The brief's sections and cuts are `brief-*` codes in `HandedSections` and `HandedCuts`, twinned in both
catalogues. The outgoing agent's note is MIXAGENT1c's, where its agent asked to hand over, carried as the brief's own section in
that row's one wording (`HandOverNote`); SESSCTX1c's closing note fills the same parameter. Not built here: the brief's notice
names no history tool until SESSCTX1f builds it; *Start a conversation with these words* stays a fresh
start with no link; a resume of a quest whose question closed (D79), opened as a new record, writes no link. The bound and the Han
weight were checked by unit tests over recorded events only; no brief has been counted on a harness (SESSCTX1a), and the window has
not been seen.
