## D193 — Daoris is framed as a development company run by agents: the person owns it, Ask Daoris is its front desk, repositories are its departments, the fabric is how each works, message levels keep its rhythm, the CLI and the connector are its network, knowledge is its growing know-how, and machines are its offices (2026-10-21)

**Decision (the owner's direction).** On 2026-10-21 the owner set the picture the project is described and planned by:
Daoris runs a development company whose staff are agents and whose product is software. Every part of that company has a
home Daoris already has; each part keeps the rules below, is measured by whether it works as a company's part would, and
places the work that makes it work. The concept, with each part's mechanisms, is `docs/2026-10-21-company-concept.md`;
the order of the work is `ROADMAP.md`.

### The parts, what each keeps, and how it is seen working

Each part says what it is, the rules it keeps (all already decided, cited), where it stands, and the test a person can
apply to see it working.

1. **The owner — the person.** Sets the company's direction and its targets, accepts or declines what is delivered, and
   holds the accounts and the money (D37). **Keeps:** the owner is asked only what is theirs: direction, money beyond the
   company's own runs, sign-ins, publishing and pushing, history, and their data (D191 point 3); everything else is the
   company's to settle and record. **Stands:** D191 moved design review off the owner; the steward settles choices a
   session meets (HEADFIT1b's, 2026-10-20). **Seen working:** each wait on the owner is one only they could answer.

2. **The front desk — Ask Daoris.** Where every request arrives, in the owner's own words. **Keeps:** it answers from what
   the company has driven and decided, never a guess; by default it proposes, and the owner applies (D110); it acts by
   itself only where the owner granted it, in full access or through a grant they chose in restricted mode, each judged
   at every call (D183); a request that is work becomes an ask, and the intake routes the ask to the departments that
   own it (D65). **Stands:** its modes and chosen grants are built (HELPMODE1a-c3); it does not yet
   see only what the company drives (STARTERSCOPE1) nor hold the checkouts grant (HELPMODE1c4). **Seen working:** the
   owner never has to say where a request belongs.

3. **Departments — repositories.** Each owns its domain, its code, its records and its way of working. **Keeps:** a
   department never edits another's; it sends a work order, and the receiving department decides how
   (`repository-owns-its-work`, D45); the one exception is the owner's: a write the owner declared, from one named
   department into another, in one direction (D107). Staff read the workspace's other departments by default (D107). A
   department's own facts live in its own repository, versioned (`no-global-memory`). **Stands:** a workspace adopts
   departments one at a time by their own sessions (D124). **Seen working:** no change reaches a repository except through
   its own session or a write the owner declared.

4. **Staff — sessions on accounts.** An agent working in one department on one work order, on one account (a paid seat
   with an allowance). **Keeps:** a seat is held only by work under way: the cap counts what the driver starts, never a
   conversation the owner holds (D130's CAPCOUNT1 note), and a run ends when its work does (D83's DONEEND1 note); a
   worker who moves to another agent or seat carries its context, Daoris's own brief of its line (D189); the owner sets how
   many work at once (CAPSET1). **Stands:** the cap, its count and the brief are built; the move's rules and a fresh start on
   the same seat are next (SESSCTX1c-f). **Seen working:** the number at work equals the work under way, and a moved
   session goes on without being told again what it was doing.

5. **Work orders — asks and quests.** How one department asks another, and how the owner's request becomes work.
   **Keeps:** a work order says what is needed and why, with its evidence, never the change the asker would make; its
   outcome comes back to the asker; the request travels and the edit does not (D45's core loop, D65). **Stands:** built;
   one piece of work is one row wherever it is listed (QUESTLISTS1) and has one name (TITLEONE1, next). **Seen working:** the
   owner can follow any request from words to landing as one thing.

6. **The handbook — doctrine and harness.** How every member of staff behaves, whatever the department. **Keeps:** the canon
   is project-agnostic, distributed to every repository and drift-checked against its lock (D11, D13); a department's own
   additions are its own; each agent's settings and permissions are the owner's (D183). **Stands:** built; the co-working
   skill teaches staff the rhythm (part 9). **Seen working:** a new department's first session behaves as an old one's does.

7. **How a department works — the fabric.** The configured way work moves through a department: its workflows, their graphs
   and pipelines. **Keeps:** how work moves is configuration the owner can read and change, never an instruction written to
   each worker; a workflow's start node routes each piece of work into a pipeline by declared facts (its kind of task, its
   paths, the owner's choice), never by a model's reading (D157, D173 and its note of 2026-10-20); a run is governed by the
   graph it was bound to (D190). **Stands:** workflows, named versions and bound graphs are built in part (D190's CUTOVER
   rows); routing into pipelines is being designed (FABRIC-PIPELINE1). **Seen working:** changing how a department works is
   an edit to its workflow, and no worker needs a written instruction to follow it.

8. **Quality assurance — verification.** The company's checks on what it delivers. **Keeps:** the declared gates decide,
   never a model's opinion of the work (D54); a worker still checks its own work, with the light checks its change reaches;
   the heavy gates (the whole suites, the real-process halves, the rehearsals) are verification's own pipeline, CI-like,
   taking landed work, and never run inside a worker's session (D180, D173's note of 2026-10-20, FABRIC-PIPELINE1); a
   second opinion and a look before a landing are configured per department (D154); a failure becomes a work order for the
   department that owns it. **Stands:** the merge tool's blocking checks and its full set beside are run by the steward by
   hand today; the design is D192, proposed. **Seen working:** no worker's session runs a heavy gate, every worker's change
   carries its own light checks, and every landing has a verdict.

9. **The rhythm — message levels and natural points.** How the company talks without breaking its work, the way a team keeps
   a sprint. **Keeps:** work runs undisturbed between natural points (a turn's end, the space between steps, a part's start),
   as a sprint runs between its reviews; an ordinary message waits for the next natural point; an urgent one reaches the
   next step and cuts nothing: the owner's words are urgent by default, Daoris sends one only for a fact that makes the
   work in flight wrong, and staff may send few (three of a part's ten posts); only the owner cuts a step in flight (*now*);
   work is stopped only by the owner's stop or by Daoris's own timeouts, pauses, limits and hand-overs, never by staff;
   a solicited answer may carry its asker's work on, but a line of work goes on by answers alone at most twice before it
   waits for the owner (D188); the outcome of a sprint is reviewed when its work lands (D154). **Stands:** the levels, their receipts and the agents' skill are built (COWORK1a-d); the bound
   on answers going on alone is next (COWORK1e), and meetings that bring several departments to one question follow
   (MEETUX, AGENTROOM). **Seen working:** no message ever stops a worker's turn, and what changed mid-sprint was something
   that made the work wrong.

10. **The network — the CLI, the connector and the service.** How anyone reaches the company and how its records move, as a
    company works through its internet and its chat. **Keeps:** every door the desktop has, the terminal has too, and Ask
    Daoris has an answer for (D50, D110); agents reach the company only through the connector's tools, never a side channel;
    the running app owns the machine's state and pushes it to every window (D182); the service carries records, never a
    machine's private streams or paths (D45). **Stands:** the CLI and the connector are built; the terminal's verbs as
    clients of the running app are in progress (RPC1e, PUSH1f2). **Seen working:** the owner can do from the terminal what
    they can do from the window.

11. **Know-how — knowledge.** What the company learns, kept so the next piece of work starts ahead. **Keeps:** each
    department's decisions, fixes, rules and knowledge stay in its repository, indexed and searchable (D129, D151); what
    holds for every department is promoted into the canon and handed back to all (D9); knowledge is local-first and shared
    by configuration (D21), within the workspace that owns it: a workspace is a disclosure boundary, and a conversation bound
    to one does not read another's (D48); a department keeps its knowledge when it moves between workspaces
    (OVERVIEWNOISE1d). **Stands:** indexing and search are built, and a caller bound to no workspace may read one named or
    all; a moved department's entries keeping their old workspace's stamp, and the canon's promotion as a routine, are not.
    **Seen working:** a lesson learned in one department is found by the next session that needs it, in any department of
    the same workspace, and travels further only as the canon or as a record the owner shares.

12. **Offices — machines and workspaces.** Each machine runs its own staff on its own accounts; a workspace is the unit of
    sharing, served by a host of its own (D48). **Keeps:** a remote exchanges the permitted records of a workspace between
    offices (quests, asks, knowledge, decisions) and never a machine's private state, accounts or streams (D45 part 3, D47);
    local work never needs the remote. **Stands:** the remote host and its sync are built; the offices agreeing on how work
    closes is next (FABRIC-CLOSE1i). **Seen working:** work started in one office can be followed from another office of
    the same workspace.

13. **Records — decisions, the archive, the changelog and session records.** The company's minutes and history. **Keeps:**
    what was decided and why is recorded when it is decided, in the company's own words, never a quotation of the owner;
    finished work moves to the archive; history is amended, never rewritten (`persist-working-state`, `task-lifecycle`,
    D127). **Seen working:** any choice can be traced to its reason.

14. **Equipment and vendors — managed tools, Daoris's browser and plugins.** What staff work with, and capabilities brought
    in from outside. **Keeps:** a vendor's capability runs only under the owner's approval (D120, D148); a tool is the
    machine's own, a version Daoris manages, or a named file, as each repository and machine chooses, and only a managed
    version is pinned by Daoris (D121); Daoris's browser is the application's own (D99). **Seen working:** a department
    knows which tool and version each of its sessions ran.

15. **Operations — incidents and recovery.** What the company does when work goes wrong or the office's own systems fail.
    **Keeps:** an ask's work is paused, resumed and abandoned whole, an abandon discarding only what nothing else holds
    (D132); a session cut off keeps its words and goes on from its record (D189); an update is staged beside the install,
    drained and swapped, and rolled back by its journal when it does not start (D139); a branch's work is kept for recovery
    before it is tidied. **Stands:** built; a bad landing's own undo as one act is not. **Seen working:** no failure loses
    the owner's words or work, and every stop says how to go on.

16. **Delivery — after a landing.** How landed work reaches its users. **Keeps:** what follows a landing (a pull request,
    external checks, stages, a production approval) is the workflow's, each step after the landing as its plugin or the
    owner speaks for it (D157); publishing and pushing are the owner's (D191 point 3); a release is stamped by the release
    workflow, never by hand. **Stands:** the landing and a pull request are built; checks and stages are not. **Seen
    working:** the owner can see where each landed piece of work stands on its way to users.

17. **Finance — accounts, usage and capacity.** What the work costs and how much the company can take on. **Keeps:**
    accounts switch on their own by a curve the owner may set, on each account's real use (D165); how much work each usage
    window holds is estimated from the company's own turns and the makers' readings, history first, never as a price
    (D179); each role is given the model its work needs, and an expensive reader only where the work is a foundation
    (D160, D191's note of 2026-10-20). **Stands:** rotation, usage history and the model roles are built; the capacity
    estimate waits for three weekly resets of history (USAGECAP1b). **Seen working:** the company plans its work against
    what its accounts can hold, and the spend per landed row falls batch on batch.

18. **Management — the steward.** Plans the work, orders it, dispatches it, merges and verifies it. **Keeps:** management
    orders competing work by the owner's priorities (the backlog's priority section and `ROADMAP.md`), with each
    dependency named on its row; it dispatches and verifies and never edits a department's work from outside it; it settles
    what is not the owner's and records why (D160, D187, D191). **Stands:** today a session outside the owner's install
    runs it; the install already builds the company's own rows alongside it (DOGFOOD1); general dependencies between quests
    are held work, not yet a capability. **Seen working:** the share of rows the company builds and lands through its own
    roles grows.

### What it changes

- **The roadmap is ordered by these parts** (`ROADMAP.md`): each priority names the part of the company it makes work.
- **Work is placed by the part it serves.** A design says in its first paragraph which part of the company it makes work,
  and a gap is read as a part that does not yet work.
- **The picture is the test of a feature's shape.** A feature that would make a company's part behave as no working
  company's does (a department commanding another, the heavy checks run at every worker's desk, know-how kept in one
  office, the owner interrupted for what staff could settle) is the wrong shape before it is built.
- **No mechanism changes by this decision.** Each part keeps its own decisions; this one orders and explains them.

**Why.** The project had grown as features named by mechanism (drivers, slices, routes, nodes), and its order was hard to
read from them. The company picture names what each mechanism is for in terms anyone can check against how a working
company behaves, and the failures it names are the ones this repository met on 2026-10-20 while the owner's install built
its rows: workers running the whole test suite at their desks until their shifts timed out, a finished worker holding its
seat for an hour, a front desk pointing at a department the company does not run, and lists that counted one piece of work
twice. It keeps the boundaries the project already holds: departments own their work, a request travels and an edit does
not, and the owner is asked only what is theirs.

**Rejected.** A hierarchy of agents directing agents (a department never commands another; it sends a work order, and the
receiver decides how); a model judging outcomes, routes or landings (D24, D54, D157); the picture as a mechanism of its own
(it orders and explains; each part's contract stays in its own decision); a new vocabulary on the page (the window keeps
the words its glossary holds; the picture is how the project is planned and explained, not a rename of its screens).

**Relates to.** D45 (Daoris drives the work) is unchanged and now read through this picture. Refined the same day, at the
owner's request, from a first version naming the parts alone into the parts with what each keeps and how each is seen
working. The owner gave the picture in outline and left its shaping to the steward: where a part of the picture did not
fit how Daoris works, the part was fitted to Daoris and the picture kept.

**Read by Codex, 2026-10-21 (D191 and its note; the framing changes no foundation, so no second read).** Eleven findings, each
checked against the cited decision or code and taken: Ask Daoris acts without the owner's Apply where they granted it (D183);
work is also stopped by Daoris's own limits, Daoris's urgent words are only for a fact that makes work wrong, and answers may
carry work on twice (D188); a moved department's knowledge is a stamp to fix, and reading across workspaces is bounded by D48;
tools are pinned only where Daoris manages them (D121); the owner's declared writes across departments (D107); a worker keeps
its light checks and only the heavy gates move to verification (D173's note); workspaces are disclosure boundaries (D48);
operations, delivery and finance with capacity added as parts (D132, D139, D157, D165, D179); management orders work by the
owner's priorities; and in the roadmap, the network after the fabric, the owner's corrections before expansion, and building
with the install alongside every priority rather than last.
