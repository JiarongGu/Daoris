## D356 — The Daoris bus: every fact, request and answer goes through one driver-owned log, each with an obliged handler and a deadline; reducers replace the looks, and plugins join last (2026-11-21)

**Decision (BUS2; the owner's direction of 2026-11-21, designed through the brainstorm `bus-1121`, Fable and Sol, its choices the
steward's in that direction; replacing the window's bus rows BUSMAP1, BUSTYPED1, BUSBATCH1 and BUSPIPE1, and amending D181 point 2:
a judge the events wake becomes a reducer over the log; D143, and D181's refusal of a second event system, stand).**

1. **One log, the driver's.** Every fact, request and response in Daoris is appended to one append-only SQLite log in the home (WAL),
   written only by the process holding the home's lease. It grows from what the driver already journals there: the desktop store and
   its tellers' cursors (`journal_cursors`), which `News` moves in the transaction that keeps what it told. It is the driver's, not the
   service's: machine-local state stays on the desktop, and a shared host exchanges permitted records, never the bus. The service's
   records stay the service's; their facts reach the log through its change stream, a transport. D143 stands: the log is no second copy
   of the records, it carries each fact's identity, causes and references, and the trace reads it as one more store where it is. D181's
   refusal stands: the change watch, the judges' topics and the state hub become the log's producers and transports, never a system
   beside it.

2. **One envelope.** Each entry is one typed, versioned envelope: `id`, `seq` (the log's order), `kind` (its type and version, a fact,
   a request or a response), `subject` (the key its order is kept by), `emitter`, `answerer` (who must answer a request), `deadline`,
   the `effect` key of an outside act, and `causes` (the ids it follows from). A payload carries ids, codes, counts and times; words,
   diffs and output stay in their records, referenced by id. A type is declared once, in one registry a census reads, and checked at
   emit and at subscribe.

3. **What rides it, and what stays a call.** Bridges, pipes, stdio and MCP become transports that carry envelopes. View reads, git,
   outside HTTP and token streams stay direct calls: a read changes nothing, and git and a provider's stream keep their own order.

4. **Disk and frames.** Delivery runs in memory, in frames of at most 60 a second, each frame carrying a subscriber's due envelopes in
   order; full snapshots coalesce and increments never drop. Durability is the log's own: WAL with `synchronous=NORMAL`, appends written
   behind, a flush at most about once a second while dirty. A commit class (a settle, a give-up, an acknowledgement the system must not
   lose) flushes at once, grouped with whatever else is due. When idle, nothing is written. Session and terminal output never enter
   the log; they ride the frames from memory. Segments, their retention and the views' snapshots bound replay, and the bytes written a
   day are measured and held to a budget, a rise past it said once.

5. **Obligations.** Every type names its obliged handler (a fact's) or answerer (a request's), a deadline, a retry budget and a
   give-up consequence from a closed list:

   | Consequence | What it does |
   |---|---|
   | `refused` | the bus answers the request itself with a typed refusal naming the miss, and the requester goes on |
   | `gave-up` | the subject the entry obliged (a step, a wait) ends as gave up with what was tried, as D354's vacancy does |
   | `held` | the gate the entry would open stays shut, its miss said on its run and on Stalls, never passed for want of an answer |
   | `dropped` | an observation no one must act on is dropped and counted, as `events.missed` is |

   Appending an obliged entry arms a durable timer row in the same transaction, and its acknowledgement clears it. Past the deadline the
   bus appends a `stalled` fact, shown on the Stalls view, retries within the budget, then appends the typed give-up and applies its
   consequence. A request always ends in a response, the bus's own if need be. Nothing waits silently, and the bus asks the owner
   nothing: the owner holds no role (D354), and whether anything waits on the owner is the risk policy's verdict alone. A late response
   is kept and said; it reopens nothing `gave-up` closed, and a `held` gate opens only on a fresh fact.

6. **Delivery.** A consumer is a durable named subscription: its types and one cursor, acknowledged in the transaction that writes
   what the consumer did, so an acknowledgement means handled, never received. Delivery is at least once, ordered per subject and
   parallel across subjects, so one subject's slow handler never holds another's. An outside effect runs once by its effect key; a word
   to a harness is attempted once, read `uncertain` after a crash and never resent. One job has one executor: a consumer's ownership
   carries the lease's fenced epoch, and an acknowledgement from an older epoch is refused.

7. **Reducers replace the looks.** A look's judgment becomes a keyed, deterministic reducer over the log: its state and its cursor
   commit together, time enters only as timer events, and its view is rebuilt by replay, so a restart resumes at the cursor and loses
   no clock. Absent evidence stays unknown, never zero.

   | Today's look logic | Its reducer |
   |---|---|
   | a check's red rounds before it gives up | a count |
   | a vacancy's clock | a timer |
   | every reviewer cooling until a reset | a keyed table with expiry |
   | a flake (failed, then passed alone) | a sequence per tip |
   | a session's usage over its curve | a window sum |
   | an opinion owed at a moved tip | a join of the tip, the opinions given and their answers |
   | stale generated files | a join of the contracts and the tree |
   | what needs the owner now (*Needs you*) | a join of the open waits |

8. **Readers.** The window and the agents read operator views, never the log. A view feeds the existing pushed slices (`StateSlices`,
   D169) with its offset and whether it is judged yet; the agents' MCP tools read a view or append a request. A press becomes a request
   generated from the route marks (`DriverRoute`, `Publishes`), its bridge handler the answerer and the route its transport. No second
   page bus is built.

9. **In-process, in .NET.** The bus is built in the lease holder's process on Microsoft.Data.Sqlite, dispatched on
   System.Threading.Channels, its reducers written by hand. Node speaks it through a thin client over stdio, with no library. Not
   built: a broker, delivery across machines, a stream query language.

10. **Plugins last.** A plugin joins over its existing hook wire. Its manifest declares namespaced, versioned types it subscribes to,
    answers and emits. Declaring is not permission: the owner's per-place switches (plugins rule 5a) grant type families, and an update
    gains no authority by itself. A plugin sees a type's public projection only and cannot emit a core type. Today's points become
    aliases of bus types. This comes only once the bus has proved itself inside Daoris, because this contract leaves the repository.

11. **Shadow first, then one job at a time.**
    - Step 1: opinion, check and answer facts are teed into the log beside today's writes, with no consumer. A shadow append can never
      fail the write it rides beside. Measured: events a day, a flush's cost, bytes a day and each look stage's time.
    - Step 2: a watchdog, the Stalls view and a comparer run in shadow against the looks, each disagreement reported.
    - Step 3: the opinion gate moves to a subscription under a fenced ownership epoch, and its look is deleted in the same change. Its
      proof: the seven runs stalled on 2026-11-20 replayed to their right ends, a kill before a due time keeping that time, and no word
      sent twice across a crash.
    - Then one job per change, never two executors of one job, until each job has one way.

**Why.** The installed Daoris showed eight stalls of one shape on 2026-11-20 and 21: a fact written in one place and never, or late,
read by the part that should act on it. Seven runs sat at *Second opinion working* for hours, two of them with clean rechecks; a third
red was never told; answers given after a restart were never read; checks ran on stale generated files; words waited behind another
quest's start; waits nobody served stalled silently; a restart lost its due times. State lives in many files, each part polls and
re-derives it (a look of the opinions stage costs about 5 s a subject), a fact has no identity, nothing acknowledges that the right
consumer handled it, and nothing alarms past a deadline. The log gives a fact its identity, an obligation names who must handle it and
by when, a cursor committed with the handling makes *handled* a fact, and a reducer reads each fact once instead of every file each
look. The lease holder owns it because it already keeps the machine's facts and its tellers' cursors. Plugins come last because theirs
is the one contract that leaves the repository.

**Rejected.**

- *A service-owned log* (Sol's): it places machine-local state where a shared host serves, and amends D143 for nothing the driver's
  log lacks.
- *A broker or a library framework* (Rx, Trill, TPL Dataflow, MediatR, Wolverine, MassTransit, Akka.NET, FASTER, NATS JetStream): each
  brings a runtime, a model or a process of its own for what one SQLite file, a channel and hand-written reducers do on one desktop.
  Their properties and licences are recalled, not checked; step 1's measures confirm the choice or reopen it.
- *A second page bus*: the window already has its pushed slices and its route marks; a second bus is two ways to move one message.
- *An inventory of every route before step 1* (Sol's): 324 routes and 39 service calls make a larger first step than the proof needs;
  the routes join when the readers move, and its fencing and crash injections are step 3's.
- *Global exactly-once delivery*: no outside effect can be proved once across a crash; at least once, with effect keys and
  `uncertain`, says what is true, and exactly once is claimed only for a consumer's own committed state.

**Risks, and what holds each.**

- *Deadlines chosen ahead*: one set too short gives up live work by itself. Deadlines come from step 1's measured handling times, and
  step 2 runs each in shadow, counting the stalls it would raise, before any give-up acts.
- *The event-first contract is hard to reverse*: once a job is cut over, the envelope and its type names are a contract every
  participant spells, and a revert cannot unsend a word or undo a give-up. Each cut-over moves one job, its look restorable from git,
  and every type is versioned from its first append.
- *Retention against replay*: a segment cut behind a snapshot may lose what a rebuilt view or the trace (D143) needs. A segment goes only
  when no cursor, snapshot, supported view version or trace read needs it, and step 1's bytes a day size the bound.
- *The plugin permission surface*: a type family's switch and a type's public projection are new surfaces, and a mistake shows a
  plugin facts the owner did not grant. Plugins come last, each public projection is declared and tested as its own contract, and a
  type with none is never seen by a plugin.
