## D325 — A role's acts are its holder's tools, settled by their receipts; the graph keeps only the constraints, the procedure is the repository's knowledge (2026-11-19)

**Decision (ACTORFLOW1b, amending D304 points 1, 5 and 6 and D318; the designs of ACTORFLOW1c and ACTORFLOW1d are below and built there).**

1. **An act is a tool of the holder's plugin.** An act with an outside effect (create a release, start a stage, a configuration write, a
   pull request) is a plugin command marked `tool` and `writes`, reaching the holding agent through `daoris-plugins` (plugins rule 29a,
   the one way a plugin's command reaches an agent), under the place's `tools` switch as its grant; a read of the act's fact is a tool
   that does not write. Azure DevOps' plugin hands the release manager `create-release` and `start-stage`, and `release-status` to read
   the stage to its approval and its end; each reads its fact first, whoever made it, makes it only where it is not there (a release keyed
   by its effect, a stage started once), and answers a **receipt** in its data: `{fact, state: reached|waiting|failed, url, by, message,
   waitsOn?}`, the plugin's own reading after the act. The approval is the approver role's act: whoever holds that role takes it, the person's press (the window's card, the terminal command) where the person holds the role, and the holding agent's tool where the place casts the approver to an agent; a destination the place marks keeps the approval with the owner role.
2. **Journaled by the round, attributed to the step held.** Every round of a session's plugin tools whose answer carries a receipt is kept
   for that session (`<session>.step-acts.json`, written by the tools server alone, whatever its exit); the run's next pass at the look's deadline
   journals each round once on the run (`workflows/acts/<key>.json`, `StepActs`) against the agent-held step waiting on its holder whose `receipts`
   take its fact, with its attempt and epoch, where the session is the run's working session (D318's) and the round came after the step
   waited. A round no held step takes is journaled nowhere.
3. **Settled by its receipts.** A `task` step's `receipts` option maps each evidence name to the fact that gives it; once the newest
   receipt of each fact is reached, the pass settles the step done at the settle door as its holder, the receipts its evidence ("by its
   receipts (release: …; ended: …)"), and the next role's step is handed on. `step_settle` takes no settle of such a step (`by-receipt`):
   a holder's words are a report, never a receipt.
4. **The graph keeps the constraints and the roles.** Checks before landing and an approval before a marked destination stay nodes, each
   with its role; the steps between them that a holder works out are one step of the role's. The shipped `pull-request-release` preset
   holds, after the merge, the release manager's `release` step and, beside it, the approver's `release-approve` (the plugin's
   observation, its holder's act `approve-release`, run by the card's press where the person holds the role and the holding agent's tool
   where an agent does, its receipt `approved` settling the step), both before `config-needed`. In Azure DevOps a stage's approval follows its
   start, so the holder's start act waits on it: `release-status` answers `waiting` with `waitsOn: approver`, and the approval node reads
   "waiting on `approver`: <holder>" until it is given.
5. **The procedure is the repository's delivery knowledge.** What the fixed steps and their options carried (which definition and stage,
   which acts, how a configuration write is backed up and verified, what checking a release means) is a knowledge entry of the repository
   (`.claude/knowledge/<name>.md`, its `applies_when` found in `.claude/INDEX.md` and by `knowledge_search`), naming the procedure, the
   acts it takes and the evidence settling each. A step names the entry (`knowledge`), and its hand-off names it to the holder; no new
   store. A repository's release values (`workflowValues`) move into its entry: `workflow apply` onto the actor form proposes the entry
   with the values it held through `memory_propose` to that repository and clears the values with that reason once the entry lands.
6. **What the run shows while the holder acts.** One row per journaled act in the run's passage, under its step: the fact, its state,
   its credit and page, and "waiting on `<role>`: <holder>" where a receipt waits on another role.
7. **The reader kept.** The five fixed release steps (`release-target` to `release-watch`) and the release values they and the approval
   node read stay, so a run bound to a version naming them finishes and the approval finds its stage. They end together, in the plugin's
   next major version, once the approval node takes its stage from the holder's `stage-started` receipt (ACTORFLOW1c) and no unended run is
   bound to a version naming the fixed steps.

**ACTORFLOW1c, the request and the loops (designed here).** (1) One way to ask another role: `question_ask` stays the door, naming the
role asked and, for an act, the act; the role's holder picks it up, an agent's session as its next turn or the person in the dock.
`go_ahead_ask` folds in as a request for an act the person allows, `helper_ask` as a request to the helper role, and a request to
another repository's role travels as a quest, the transport and not a second door. (2) The short loop: every answer, done with what
happened and its receipt or refused with why, is the asker's next turn through the say door, and a receipt moves the run; nothing waits
on a person or the steward telling the session. (3) The long loop: what an act came to (a failure, the person's correction, a check's
finding) is proposed into the repository's delivery knowledge, so the next run's hand-off carries it. Its proof:
a release whose start is refused for its sign-in asks the credentials keeper; the person's sign-in answers it, the agent retries as its
next turn, the receipt settles the step with no word from anyone else, and the correction lands in `release-path` for the next run.

*As built (ACTORFLOW1c).* `go_ahead_ask` and `helper_ask` stay as channels of the one request (`RoleRequests`), their callers working
through the fold; a role of another repository is pointed to `quest_publish`, the quest its transport. A request a run's working session
asks as it ends its turn awaiting its step is kept for its answer and drawn in its dock, as a park's question is. The long loop's
proposal is Daoris's, not `memory_propose`'s: `memory_propose` carries a proposal to another repository's memory entry and refuses the
asker's own, a delivery knowledge entry is the repository's own `.claude/knowledge` file and no memory entry, and a quest to itself is
refused; so a step its receipts settle whose act reached only after a receipt that did not proposes what it came to for the entry it
names (`workflows/acts/proposed.json`), and the next run's hand-off of a step naming that entry carries it once to its holder, the
repository's own agent, who amends the entry in its own change. A proposal, never a procedure: the entry stays the one place.

**ACTORFLOW1d, a quest across repositories (designed here).** (1) Parts per repository: `team_plan`'s parts, each held by its
repository's own role and following its delivery knowledge; no actor changes another repository, it asks that repository's role through
1c's door. (2) A wait between parts is an edge of the plan another part's receipt settles ("the configuration part starts after the
app's `stage-ended`"), no new graph. (3) Testing together is a joint constraint: a check held by the tester or operations role over all
parts' receipts before the marked destination, a failure sent back to the part that caused it through the request door and the long
loop. Its proof: an app part and a configuration part in two fixture repositories, the second started by the first's receipt, a joint
check failing on the second, its holder asked and its fix's receipt passing the check, then the approval.

*As built (ACTORFLOW1d).* The wait is an `after` edge's `receipt` (the fact, in place of a milestone), never a build dependency, so
the train orders no landing by it; a waiting part's run hands its holders no step until the newest receipt of that fact on the run of
the part it follows is reached. The joint check is a `task` step's `joint` option (each part and the fact its receipt gives) beside its
own `receipts`; the part a failure is on is the failed receipt's `part`, which the tester's plugin names. Daoris's pass sends the
failure back as the request: said to the part's working session and journaled on the part's step (`returned:<id>`), not a new quest,
since the part's quest already stands in its repository. The check's pass journals the fix, as a part's run may have ended at its
own landing before the check; the check is handed again on it and passes only on a test newer than every part's receipt.

*As built (ACTORFLOW1e).* The window draws them from the run's one reading, which reads the ask's plan and the parts' journals as the
pass does (`PartViews`): the receipts a part still waits on, on its graph run, and on a joint check's step the receipt of each part it
holds over and the part its failed test went back to once the return is journaled there; the run's view and the session page's dock
(while its session awaits its step) name each with that part's quest. The look's one read of the run bindings gives every open run's
ask. No shipped preset holds a joint check: no team preset releases, and the `joint` option names the ask's own parts and the fact each
gives, which only its plan knows, so a preset written before any plan cannot name them.

**Why.** A fixed graph must foresee every path: a wait on another repository, testing together, a sign-in in the middle of a step, a
configuration change after a release. Each new case became another node kind and another press, and the work workspace's release runs
kept outgrowing it (2026-11-18/19: five fixed release steps, then the steps after them handed over as tasks). With roles, tools and the
repository's knowledge, the holder works the path out from the plan and what it finds; Daoris keeps only what must hold: the
constraints, the roles' holders, the receipts and the answers that come back.

**Rejected:** a node kind and a press for each new case (the graph grows with every path and still misses the next); the fixed release
steps with the agent pressing them (still a step per act, the run stopping where the steps end); a generic `step_act` tool on the
knowledge server running a plugin's command (a second way to reach a plugin's commands); the holder reporting its acts by words (a
report is not a receipt, and an agent's word is no grant); the target kept in node options or `workflowValues` (no agent reads them);
a new store for procedures beside knowledge and skills (one way per job); a Daoris approval before the start act at a marked stage (in
Azure DevOps the stage's own approval follows the start, so it would be a second press for one approval).

**Left:** the approval node's stage from the receipt (point 7; the run view's act rows, point 6, are ACTORFLOW1c's, built); a role
cast to an agent other than the helper picking a request up as its own session's next turn (such a request is a question in the
asker's dock, a supervisor answering it where no owner mark keeps it); the dock naming the role asked; `go_ahead_ask` and `helper_ask`
going once every caller (the canon's skill, the permission lists, the goldens) asks through `question_ask`; the values'
migration through `memory_propose` (point 5); a configuration write and a pull request as acts of the same shape; refusing before it
runs an act whose session holds no step of its role (a role's act, `role` on its command, reaches a session only where the place casts
that role to an agent, APPROVEACT1, not yet only the session holding the role's step); a shipped preset holding the joint check, which
waits on a form of `joint` that reads its parts from the plan (ACTORFLOW1e).
