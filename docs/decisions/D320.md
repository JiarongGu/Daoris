## D320 — A session is one panel: one timeline between its state above and what runs and waits on the person below (2026-11-18)

**Decision (SESSIONVIEW1, the owner's direction of 2026-11-18; by a fresh-design pass: two designs from a purpose brief alone,
reconciled against the code in `docs/2026-11-18-session-view-design.md`).**

1. One panel is a session's whole surface: a fixed head (the state line, the title, the acts, the workflow's track), one timeline
   that scrolls, and at its foot a live band, the dock of what waits on the person and the composer. Everything that happened is a
   row in the one timeline, whoever did it: the agent's turns and calls, its sub-agents and background tasks, Daoris's helpers and
   opinions, pieces, other sessions' messages, the workflow's steps, part boundaries.
2. The state line reads the driver's one reader (`SESSION_GROUPS`' group and reason, a line's by `LineStandings`), with its cause
   and age, never the record's state word or the agent's report. An open wait the person settles places the session in Needs you
   whatever its record says, so it cannot read done while it waits.
3. Every wait the person settles stands in one dock above the composer with its own press: never folded, filtered or scrolled away,
   oldest first with a count, with a marker where it arose and a jump to it; settled, it is a row there. It is the only place a
   wait is pressed in the panel (desktop-app rule 38's head copies go), and a run's step held for the person or left failed is
   pressed there too. A press is today's press, moved.
4. What runs now stands in one live band: the agent's current act, each sub-agent and background task, the run's running step, a
   helper or opinion working for the session, each with its elapsed time, last line of output, a quiet mark, *Follow*, *Open* and
   *Stop* where its door can stop it, asked once in place and kept with who stopped it. A sub-agent is a row under the call that
   spawned it, opened in place one level deep; a background task is a row where it started and one where it ended, linked. *Open*
   is the panel's focus on that child, with a breadcrumb back: never a tab, a drawer or another page.
5. The workflow is drawn once, as the head's track, and its steps' starts and ends are rows placed by time between turns, both read
   from the run's one reading.
6. A returning person lands at their read mark (per conversation, on this machine, in a desktop store table of its own) under a
   digest computed from the records with no model; each ended turn folds to one line, and open waits' markers, failures, the
   person's words, closing words, step results, acts with an outside effect, messages to the person and part boundaries are never
   folded.
7. The envelope stays agent-family neutral: the session's record keeps each sub-agent's and task's start and end with the call
   that started it, its parent and who says it; a sub-agent's own events are kept in a record of its own beside the session's,
   never in it; a door's capabilities are stated, so what it does not report is a label, never a blank.
8. A teammate's session reads the same with no press, band or composer; its conversation stays on its machine, and the panel says
   so.
9. The RightDock's per-session views (Timeline, Workflow, At work) and the output panel's sub-agent and task tabs are removed as
   their jobs move into the panel; *Notifications*, Ask Daoris, Review, the file preview, Console and Terminal stay.

**Why.** On 2026-11-18 a session waiting on the owner read *completed*: its head named the record's state while the run waited on
the Accept; the press to approve was nowhere in its output and a *go to the Accept card* link went nowhere. Its surface was split
over a head with a dozen folds above the conversation, wait cards drawn in two places, a run's step pressed only from *What needs
you*, and five per-session views and per-child tabs in two docks, so the owner had to know where to look before knowing what
happened; the agent's sub-agents and background processes showed only as console tabs, never linked from the calls that started
them. Two designs from the purpose alone, by different makers, agreed on the shape.

**Rejected:** lanes or tabs over one timeline (a tab is today's split with a border round it, a wait in an unopened one is buried
again, and lanes leave cause and effect to be joined by eye); state reported by the agent or read from the record (a reported state
goes stale, which is how a waiting session read *completed*); waits pressed wherever they scrolled to (an hour later the wait is
thousands of rows up behind a link that can break; the dock is always in view and the marker keeps the context); the RightDock's
per-session views as separate tabs (each is a place to know about before reading, and the session's story is told in three
orders); a right rail for helpers, opinions and the channel (a second place to look, and its width does not fit beside the dock at
the window's minimum); a drawer for children (it covers the timeline at the dock's widths); a model's summary on open (slow,
unverifiable, and a cost on every look); a content store of its own (the record's bound, its heads and offsets and a task's own
output file already serve).

**Trial and its criteria.** Measured on the installed window against a baseline taken before SESSIONVIEW1b lands: on 10 recorded
sessions that ended with an open wait, the presses from opening to the wait's press, and how many read done while a wait stood.
**Keep** when, over the week after SESSIONVIEW1j: no session reads done, finished or completed while a wait it holds is open; every
open wait's press is in the dock within one press of opening the session; every digest line checked against its record is true (10
per session on 10 sessions); the digest is on screen within a second of opening; a 100,000-event record holds frames under 60 ms and
scrolling at 30 fps. **Retire** (the panel's arrangement goes back; the state reader, the dock's one place and the envelope stay) on
any one: a wait hidden or its press not found twice; a digest error that would change a decision (a failed check shown as passed, a
running process shown ended); frames over 100 ms at 100,000 events; an answer or a press that needs a modal, a card or another page.

**Left:** rows SESSIONVIEW1b to SESSIONVIEW1j in `TASKS.md`; the waits read in the driver as well as the page, after which the
dock and the state line read one list (until then a disagreement reads *Waiting on you* and is logged); filters, a landmark scroll
map and a composer that writes to another session, each on a measured need.
