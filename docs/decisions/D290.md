## D290 — An ask across repositories is delivered under one plan: TEAMWORK1's plan gains deployables and an evidenced dependency graph, parts build at once against a versioned contract, a frozen joint receipt gates every landing, releases wait on the upstream release receipt through `wait.unit` with guarded, journaled writes, coordination persists outside any chat, and the ask is done only when released and checked in production (2026-11-13)

**Decision (DELIVERY1, 2026-11-13, under D284 and D285).** One feature crossed three repositories and four deployables on an
installed Daoris, and the person supplied its order, its release targets and its coordination by hand: a sequential chain built the
parts one after another, the contract lived in channel posts, sessions composed their own joint stacks, the release steps guessed
their targets and were stopped by hand to keep the order, both asks read Done with nothing released, and an update left the work
unwatched for nearly three hours. The owner directed that Daoris derive such an ask's order from code and resource dependencies, show
why, enforce it, and count the ask complete only once released and checked in production. Two independent proposals answered (Codex,
on gpt-6.1-sol, and Fable 5.2), beside a draft the installed Daoris's own session wrote on its quest (CROSSREPO1). The steward took
their convergence, choosing where they differed. The contract is `docs/2026-11-13-delivery-framework-design.md`, built in its §8 and
§9 order.

1. **No flow engine.** TEAMWORK1's `TeamPlan` becomes the ask's delivery plan across repositories; each repository keeps its own
   workflow, runs and review. The plan gains a base per repository, deployables, edges with their evidence, contract revisions,
   joint checks, the production example, step settings set ahead and one queue of the person's questions.
2. **Deployables are declared** in each repository's `daoris.json` (`deployables`, with `provides` and `consumes` by resource and
   mode), beside `domain`'s `owns` and `accepts`. D288's release target names the deployable its node releases and gains
   `deploysOn`, the earliest act that deploys into it; undeclared, every exposing act waits.
3. **Planned before coded.** The intake is the lead for an ask across repositories: before any part is published it writes the parts,
   the deployables, the dependency graph with each edge's evidence, the targets, the contract's first revision, the joint-check recipe
   and the production example with its accounts. The person is asked only what evidence cannot settle, once each, in one queue on the
   existing obligation keys.
4. **Evidence makes an edge.** A pure judge proposes edges from declarations, stage conditions, contracts and the lead's cited code
   reads. The registry's `uses` guides the reading and never proves a release prerequisite; an order the owner imposes without
   evidence is labelled policy; a model's reading alone makes no standing edge.
5. **Parts at once, landed together.** Every part is published when the plan is saved, under `(ask, part)`, named by repository and
   part; a quest a session publishes serving a part is a plan revision adding one. Consumers build against an acknowledged, versioned
   contract revision owned by the keel part. A frozen joint receipt over the exact candidates, run once through the check broker,
   gates every review offer and landing; a moved input voids it.
6. **Released in dependency order on observed success.** The existing `wait.unit` kind gains its run-time reader and waits on the
   upstream deployable's release receipt, with no new kind; a person's skip is a recorded waiver, never a satisfaction. Where the line
   moved past the checked result, the recipe runs again on the commits that will ship, and the release plan names everything each
   release ships by ask. Every provider write a Daoris step makes is judged and journaled before it is sent; watchers and
   reconciliation only observe; `no-release`, skipped and partial outcomes satisfy nothing. An agent's or person's own act is observed
   and labelled, never refused (D284).
7. **Typed failures, routed** to the part's session, the coordinator or the person, each a durable notice; only one needing a decision
   or a remedy is a row of *What needs you*.
8. **Durable coordination.** Plan revisions, receipts, obligations, subscriptions with their marks, deadlines and invocation journals
   persist; one desktop lease reconciles them on restart; events wake reads, and a bounded reconciliation by code catches a missed
   one. Ask Daoris is the front desk, the lead plans, the appointed supervisor judges, the workflow engine performs; no chat process
   owns coordination state.
9. **Done means delivered.** The ask's phase is one projection of its plan and receipts, and `Done` is one rule: every required node of
   the standing plan reached its end, so an ask with deployables is done only when each is released and the production example
   passed. Review is D289's: receipts and the release plan as outputs, and at the ask a card whose group press writes one
   revision-bound word per run.
10. **Built narrow first**: one desktop, one provider, the case's three repositories and four deployables, then the rest. TEAMWORK1d
    and f go ahead of its b and c (f runs its reached checks where it would replay them), and parts across repositories come with d
    and f rather than after TEAMWORK1z, proved by this design's installed rehearsal.

**Rejected.**
- *A flow engine above the workflows, or one graph across repositories*: two ways to move work, past the graph's bound.
- *A sequential chain for independent parts, and contracts or dependencies as channel posts*: no join, no revision, no enforcement.
- *`uses` as proof of a release prerequisite*: a neighbour is not an order.
- *An order derived by a model alone, or declared by the person alone*: unrepeatable, or blind to evidence the records hold.
- *A new waiting kind*: `wait.unit` is that wait.
- *Order kept by holding merges or pull requests, and D288 point 3's person gate before each downstream release*: merging is not
  deploying, and the upstream receipt answers what the gate asked.
- *A coordinator chat holding the state and polling*: it ended with the app and spent a fifth of the output watching.
- *Watchers allowed to write, and refusing an agent's own release or plugin write*: a write after invalidation, and a narrowing.
- *A separate activation of dormant plans*: the planning step's holds already keep an unresolved plan from releasing.
- *Atomic landing or rollback across repositories*: the providers offer separate effects.
