## D342 — The workflow step editor goes; a workflow change arrives as a before-and-after card, and only a change to the owner's part waits on them (2026-11-20)

**Decision (UIWF8, UIWF10, the window redesign `docs/2026-11-19-window-design.md` §11.5 and §11.6; the owner's direction of
2026-11-19; amending D223 and D221's editor of its own).**

1. A workflow is read on its repository's (and workspace's) **Workflow tab**: who takes each step and what must hold there, how
   each step is done (the repository's knowledge, D325 point 5), the line's verdicts, recent runs and what they taught; the
   read-only chart is one press away. It is the workflow's one home: Agents › Setup's workflow section, the verifications list as
   a section and the side bar's workflow view go.
2. A change arrives as a **proposal** (Ask Daoris's draft, an agent's `workflow apply`), classified by the existing judgement
   (`Involvement`, `WorkflowDiff`, `KindLowering`): a change to the owner's part or a dropped check waits on the owner as a card
   showing the version before and after; any other is applied and listed as changed without them, with its proposer, model and
   reason, reversible to the version before.
3. The step editor (`workflow/edit/` but for its change list) and its open draft go; a held workflow is answered on a card, never
   by a terminal command.

**Why.** D325 leaves the graph only its constraints and roles; the procedure is the repository's knowledge, so there is little
left for the owner to compose, and composing it is not the owner's job. A change the owner does read is clearer as before and
after than as a chart to compare by eye.

**Rejected.**

- *Keeping the node editor beside the Workflow tab*: the owner's answer; a second way to change a workflow.
- *Every workflow change waiting on the owner*: most changes are the agents' to make and report.
