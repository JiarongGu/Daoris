## D192 — How work moves begins at a start node that routes each piece of work into a pipeline: a pipeline is a named workflow its route chooses; a development pipeline's sessions run only light checks, the heavy gates refused to them; verification is a pipeline of its own, a `check` Daoris performs on landed work, batched, each batch run to its verdict, its failure made a fix ask (2026-10-21)

**Decision (FABRIC-PIPELINE1, accepted 2026-10-21).** **Status: accepted** (the steward, under D191), revised twice under D191: after Codex's read of 2026-10-21
(twenty-three findings) and after Fable's read of the design's §2 to §4 the same day, which D191's note asks since the design changes
a foundation (twenty-nine findings and simpler shapes, five of them blocking). Every finding was checked against the code and taken,
two in part (the design's §9). The third round removed mechanisms and changed no part of the model the reads tested, so it is ready
for the steward to accept once each change is verified as taken, with no further pair of reads; nothing in it waits for the owner.
The direction is D173's note of 2026-10-20: how work moves is configured in the fabric, a workflow begins at a start node routing
each piece of work into a pipeline the person configures, and development is kept apart from a CI-like verification. Read at
`300626e52`, a driven session is told its heavy gates in a sentence (`HeavyTold.cs:43-46`), this repository marks none heavy (D180's
HEAVYRUN1c note), and its CI beside is a command the parent runs by hand (D180 point 2). The contract is
`docs/2026-10-20-pipeline-design.md`.

1. **The start node routes on facts.** A chain's work by D157 §4.1's order (the person's choice for the task, the kinds, the
   defaults, Current), unchanged; landed work, a person's ask to verify, and a fix Daoris asks for by the repository's `landed` route
   and the kinds. A kind's declared paths are read against the paths the work changed before its first gate; outside them, where
   the kind's pipeline lowers the person's part, the run holds for the person's choice of route, and nothing switches by itself
   (D157 §4.4). No route reads a model's judgement. Design §1.1.
2. **It sits before the graph.** The start node is the choice table D157 point 5 keeps, gaining `landed` (`{ workflow, gates }`),
   which names only a verification pipeline and keeps git's blob id of the `daoris.gates.json` the person accepted; it resolves on its
   own (the repository's, else its workspace's), out of the rule by which a repository's development routes replace its workspace's.
   It is resolved at the bind D190 §8 placed, and is no node of a version's graph. A route changed applies to the next run. Drawn
   first on the *Workflow* tab, with the pipelines it routes to as one graph; changed at Setup and by `workflow use … --landed`, and
   by Ask Daoris's proposal once its proposal carries a route and a graph version. The person's part is lowered only by the person's
   press: the lowering table gains a route removed or narrowed, a verification pipeline's check dropped or narrowed or a stage's wire
   to it removed, a check's `failed` moved off `wait`, and `light` with nothing running the heavy gates, each shipped with the first
   row that lets its change be set. Design §1.2, §1.3.
3. **A pipeline is a named workflow, chosen by its route**, each versioned and bound alone, governed by D190. A sub-graph and a path
   through one graph are rejected for their costs. A workflow's file marks its pipeline, `development` (absent) or `verification`,
   written when the workflow is created and kept by every later save, and both readers hand it to each version's reading: D190 point
   4's one landing holds a development pipeline; a verification pipeline's versions are graphs holding at least one `check` and no
   `claimed`, `opinion`, `look`, `go-ahead`, `handed` or `quest.close`. Design §2, §2.1.
4. **A development pipeline says its sessions' checks**: `checks` on its work (`claimed`'s option, schema 1's `work` field, or under
   Current the repository's `sessionChecks` rule), `light` or `heavy-at-end`, absent being today's. Under `light` a session runs the
   gates its repository does not mark heavy and the checks its change reaches, filtered; the heavy gates are the verification
   pipeline's. Design §3.1.
5. **A heavy gate is refused, and said, by four layers**: the start's words composed from the bound version; `run-heavy` refused
   from a tree a session holds under `light`, on every harness; where the harness takes Daoris's rules (Claude Code), each declared
   heavy command denied exactly, with a sentence for auto mode's classifier, and where it takes none (Codex, dsh) that layer absent and
   said; and a call past `callMinutes` said once at urgent level, never ended by Daoris. Under any policy, a session's `run-heavy`
   never waits behind a verification run. Design §3.2.
6. **Verification is a pipeline of its own, its node `check`**: a built-in Daoris performs through the performer, no agent session,
   never skipped by its kind, its `perform` its kind's and its reach and failure its own `gates` source. Its attempt is written before
   anything runs and sent to `run-heavy`'s executor made asynchronous in the driver, which takes the repository's heavy-run slot and
   the home's verification slot in one order without waiting, runs the job below normal by its first process's class with
   clock-bound gates at normal, never counted against the cap, and owns the bound (the node's `timeoutSeconds`, 7200 by default,
   counted once both slots are held), ending its job before either slot goes. Its answer comes through the performer's reconcile
   alone, from the attempt's folder: a verdict reached or failed, an unknown held for the person, and a check cut off before its
   result (a restart, a drain) sent again under its key. It runs the repository's declared runner (which owns its checkout, and where
   it declares that it answers by file, writes each declared gate's verdict, judged by those verdicts alone, every gate the check
   names required) or its heavy gates in a verification tree of Daoris's held as a replay's tree is, as the person accepted the
   declaration by its blob. The slots are per home: D180's one run at a time becomes one per home, said. Design §4.1, §4.2.
7. **A run per batch on the line.** A landed run, its id its own and its binding in a folder of its own, observes itself at the tip
   it bound; its batch is every commit since the newest verdict (the line's history, for its first). One runs per line at a time, and
   it runs to its verdict: nothing supersedes it, and what lands meanwhile is the next batch. An unknown holds the line for the
   person's *Run again* or *Not now*. The line keeps no record of its own: it is read from its runs. A person may verify a branch or a
   commit as a run of its own. Design §4.3.
8. **Its verdict** is the check's on the run's record, each gate's verdict, exit, wall and log its evidence, shown on the tab, the
   run's view, the repository's *Details* and *What needs you*, and asked by any door. **A failure becomes work**: `wait` (the person's
   *Ask for a fix…*), or `ask`, the person's standing say-so, publishing one fix ask per gate to the repository, found first among the
   asks Daoris wrote so a restart never publishes twice, with the gate's log as its file and the kind the person named, so the start
   node routes it into development. **A stage** after the check waits on it by any wire, which holds only on its reach since the check
   is never skipped, and reads the exact commit's verdict at its dispatch; the install's `--from-gated` stage keeps its rule and holds
   the gate tree's lock through its build. Design §4.4 to §4.6.
9. **This repository**: `universal`, `code-map` and `orient-index` light; its suites, Process halves and rehearsals (the release and
   family rehearsals declared beside them) marked heavy once its development pipeline is `light`; its runner `node
   tools/merge-branch.mjs --full --beside --daoris-verify`, which alone reads the batch's tip handed to it, runs the declaration's
   gates alone and writes its result under their declared names. On the owner's install, `daoris-development` (`light`) and
   `daoris-verification` on `main`, and the kind `fix`. Design §5.

**Why.** A sentence an agent can drop held this repository's sessions back, and three of them still ran a whole test project,
held their place in the cap to the timeout and committed nothing, while the full set beside was run by hand and superseded as main
moved. A route the graph holds, a refusal Daoris and the harness enforce, and a verification Daoris runs on landed work move each
of those out of anyone's memory and into the process. A route that only selects keeps each pipeline whole and versioned under
D190, and a run on the line is what lets verification batch, which a node of each development run could not. Running each batch to
its verdict needs no state the node lifecycle lacks and no end of a job in flight, and every landing still comes under a verdict.
The check reuses what already holds: the performer's attempt and reconcile, `run-heavy`'s slot and job, and a repository's own
runner with its checkout, so what is new is the executor's place off the pass, the run on the line, and the runner's result.

**Rejected** (design §6): a route by a model's reading or a kind inferred from paths; the start node inside a version's graph, a
pipeline as a sub-graph or a path; a verification pipeline told by a dummy landing, a mark per version or its graph's shape; landed
work routed by its paths; a versioned route set; verification as nodes after every `handed`; the instruction as a standing text; a
heavy run guessed from command text; a `light` pipeline refusing a harness that takes no rules; Daoris ending a session's call; a
bound of a session's own on its background work; Daoris's own CI engine with lanes; a landed edit of the declaration run unasked; a
canonical digest of the declaration's keys; an install of Daoris's own in the verification tree; the check awaited inside the pass;
its answer heard as a plugin's completion; the performer asking a check's attempt at its deadline; a busy runner as a transient
failure; a check a person may skip; a `timeoutMinutes` beside `timeoutSeconds`; a machine-wide slot outside every home; a session's
`run-heavy` waiting behind a verification; a record of the line's own; landed runs beside the chains' runs; superseding a run in
flight, with a bound or without; replacing a run that waits for its slots; an unknown letting the next run bind by itself; a client's
key on the service's asks; a checkout pinned per stage; a fix ask published with no say-so of the person's by default; an automatic
bisect, revert or rollback; a verification run counted against the cap or run in the main checkout; this repository's suites marked
heavy before its development pipeline is light.
