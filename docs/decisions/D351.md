## D351 — The window's palette is near-neutral beside the blue: amber and red are its only warm things, and the taskbar count is amber (2026-11-20)

**Decision (amending D330 §1, its "warm paper of D41 stays in the neutrals", and D196's white-on-red taskbar count; the window
design §7.1 holds the values).**

1. **Neutrals lean faintly cool.** `page`, `raised`, `sunken`, `overlay`, `hover`, the three lines and the four inks go from
   D41's warm yellow-grey to near-neutral greys with a faint cool lean (light `page` `#F6F7F8`, `ink` `#17191C`; dark `page`
   `#111316`, `ink` `#EAECEF`). The scrim and shadows take the same base, `15 17 20`; their alphas stay.
2. **The hues.** The blue accent stays (`#2F5BD3` / `#5F86F2`). Red moves from orange-red to crimson (`fail` `#CF2D3F` /
   `#F05C6C`), so amber and red stand 51° apart in light and 61° in dark (41° and 51° before). Green turns slightly darker and
   towards teal (`ok` `#1F8050` / `#49B279`). Amber keeps its hue (`you` `#B27400` light, `#F0B03A` dark); the labels on the
   dark fills and on amber are one dark ink, `#0E1014`.
3. **One field for the accent's family.** `accent-soft` and `live-soft` are one value (`#E1EAFC` / `#1C2A4D`): a selected row
   and a live band are the same blue field. Selected text is that field under `ink`, a base `::selection` rule, with no token
   of its own; the focus ring stays `accent`, 2 px.
4. **Real margins at the edges.** `line-strong` reads at 3.3:1 in both themes (3.1 and 3.0 before), `ink-faint` at 4.8 and 5.0.
   `ink-disabled` reads at about 3:1 on a control's field, and a disabled control is told by that label alone: the whole-button
   `opacity: .55` is gone from the base layer, and the atoms (`Button`, the chip, the select, the check box) put a disabled
   control in the neutral frame with its label in `ink-disabled`.
5. **The taskbar count is the owner's amber** (`#F0B03A` with `#0E1014`, 10:1) in either theme: what it counts waits on the
   owner, which the window marks amber, and red means a failure alone. The close button's hover stays red, the OS's convention
   in the palette's crimson (`#CF2D3F` with white light, `#F05C6C` with `#0E1014` dark).

The identity hues (`ident-*`) are unchanged; each stays ΔE 20 from every new state colour (`plugins/icon.test.ts`).

**Why.** Warm paper was tuned beside D41's bronze accent. Beside a blue one it reads yellow, and it dilutes the owner's amber,
which should be the one warm signal on a screen besides a failure. The orange-red sat 41° from amber in light, close enough
that "failed" and "waits on you" shared a family at a glance. A red count on the taskbar said "something failed" when it counts
what waits on the owner. The faded disabled button put its label under 2:1 and turned a disabled primary into a second, paler
blue. Four candidate sets were compared side by side in both themes; the owner chose this merge.

**Rejected.**

- *Today's warm set*: tuned for bronze; beside blue it reads yellow and makes amber one warm tone among many.
- *A cool blue-grey set* (Claude's proposal: neutrals tinted toward the accent, `#F4F6F9` / `#0F1217`): the greys turn into a
  pale version of the accent, so the blue no longer stands alone as what acts, and the window reads tinted rather than neutral.
- *A warm set with alias tokens* (OpenAI's proposal: warm greys kept, a neutral close button, an amber count that follows the
  theme, extra alias names for the same values): it keeps the yellow cast this change exists to remove; alias tokens are two
  names for one job, which the role set refuses; and its `ink-disabled` sat a hair from `ink-faint`, so a disabled label read
  as an enabled one.
- *A red taskbar count, as before*: red is a failure's alone; the count is what waits on the owner.
