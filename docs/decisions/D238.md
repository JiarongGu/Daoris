## D238 — A conversation runs in the running Daoris alone; a terminal holds one over the pipe, under one Confirm per conversation, and is sent its lines by that hold; with no instance, nothing runs alone (2026-11-06)

**Decision (RPC1g; accepted by the owner, 2026-11-06).** The contract is `docs/2026-11-06-terminal-conversation-design.md`. It finishes D171
point 3 for the two verbs the terminal RPC design left alone, `chat` and `help resume`, and adds area T to ONEWAY1's census (D228).

1. **One runner.** A conversation runs only in the running Daoris's `ChatRunner`. A terminal never runs one: it opens one with the
   route the window presses (`START_CHAT`, `SESSION_START_FROM`, `SESSION_HAND_OVER`) or asks to hold one already open.
2. **The hold.** The person's Confirm in the window, once, binds one pipe connection to one conversation. While it lasts, that
   connection's words in the verb's form, its turn stops and its finish for that conversation run with no further card. It ends
   when the connection closes, the terminal lets go, the conversation ends or the person takes it back in the window. It is kept in
   memory only and is never standing. One connection holds a conversation at a time; the window is never a holder.
3. **The hold is the subscription.** The pipe forwards the held conversation's console lines, its events and its end to the
   connection that holds it, bounded, with a `gap` the terminal reads again from the backlog. The module-wide `SUBSCRIBE` of the
   terminal RPC design §6 is not built.
4. **No instance, no conversation.** Where no Daoris answers on the home, a conversation verb does nothing and says how to open it.
   A headless loop runs no conversation. A headless instance that serves the pipe is built only once a machine with no screen is a
   use.
5. **One verb holds.** `daoris chat` starts (`--repository`) or holds (`--session`) a conversation. `session start-from` and
   `session hand-over` hold what they open. `ask-daoris resume` retires, since `session say` says one line.
6. **Leaving the terminal lets go.** Ctrl+C at rest and a lost connection end the hold and leave the conversation running in
   Daoris. End of input finishes it.
7. **Retired:** the terminal's own runner and its agents' job, `TerminalOwner`, `TurnMarks`, the request folder's stop for a
   terminal's conversation, and the window's branches for a conversation another process runs.

**Why.** A conversation run in the terminal's process is a second runner of conversations on one home. The window cannot reach it,
the loop's looks do not run there, and three patches (turn marks, a stop through the request folder, a branch in the window) exist
only because of it. Every conversation route is the person's over the pipe, so a client that sent each word would put a card in the
window for every line. A hold bound to one connection and one conversation gives the person one Confirm for what the terminal did
ungated before, and nothing more.

**Rejected.**
- **A card for every word.** A card pressed for each line just typed is pressed without reading.
- **The standing confirmation naming words.** D201 §2.2 never covers words said as the person: a process outside an agent's job
  would ride it.
- **Words as an open route.** The pipe confers no person's authority, so every agent of the account could speak as the person.
- **Acting alone when no instance runs.** That is today's second runner, and a desktop opened beside it shows a conversation it
  cannot reach.
- **The verb starting Daoris.** A `chat` typed in an agent's shell would start the person's loop and window on an agent's word.
- **The terminal's process becoming the instance for the conversation.** It would hold the home's lease for hours, so the desktop
  could not open, or it would be a third host of the loop.
- **`SUBSCRIBE { modules }`.** It forwards every conversation to every subscriber, and it has no other consumer.
- **Ctrl+C at rest stopping the conversation.** The conversation is Daoris's. A stop is the window's press or `session stop`.

**The cost.** A machine with no screen has no terminal conversation until the conditional headless instance is built.
`ChatConsole`'s reading of D50 ends; D50's rule for configuration stands.

**Amends, once accepted:** D171 point 3 (the terminal RPC design §6's `SUBSCRIBE`, §7.1's `chat` and `help resume` alone, §11);
D201 (the terminal-trust design §3.3 point 3's case of a terminal's `chat` that runs agents). Each amended decision takes a dated
note pointing here.
