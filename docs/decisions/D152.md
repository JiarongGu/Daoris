## D152 — The menu bar holds the application's verbs and places from one table, an agent's accounts are one line each with one act by state, and a page's head is its state, its title and two facts (2026-10-05)

**Decision (UX7, UX7a–d; the screen halves of ACCT1 and AGENTS2).** The owner, looking at the screens UX6 built on the
install: *"I think the UI/UX is kind of bad right now, and also we should be utilizing the top menu, so instead of calling
this 'agent' we might need a better menu (more like VS Code); also the account page itself needs its UI/UX improved"*, and
*"both title sections, for quest and session, need their UI/UX improved"*. The look found more than the words: an account
a rotation held read *unknown* with no act on its row, so the owner pressed *Sign in to another account* and made a new
account no list held (ACCT1); a session's page put its conversation 436 px down the 761 px above its composer and said its
title three times; a quest's page spent eight lines before its body and repeated its title as the body's first line. The
contract is `docs/2026-10-05-ux7-design.md`.

1. **The strip's menus are *Workspace · Edit · View · Go · Run · Terminal · Help*** (工作区 · 编辑 · 视图 · 转到 · 运行 · 终端 ·
   帮助): VS Code's bar less *Selection*, with *Workspace* in File's place and job (new asks and quests, the scope, the
   repositories, the workspace's setup, Settings). *Daoris* and *Agents* go as menus. Go opens the places and View the
   regions and views, as the palette's *Go to* already does; the activity bar stays the one navigation region.
2. **One table owns every verb.** `commands.ts` names each once, with its menu, its key, the surfaces that have it and when it
   applies; the menus, the palette, the key handler and a *Keyboard shortcuts* drawer read it. A record's act in the menu
   calls its record's one owner and is enabled exactly when the record's header offers it. A menu item is disabled where its
   surface exists and the state forbids it, and absent where the surface is absent; the palette omits both.
3. **Keys follow VS Code's where the meaning is the same**: Ctrl+Shift+P, Ctrl+, Ctrl+N (a new ask), Ctrl+F, Ctrl+Shift+F,
   Ctrl+`, Ctrl+Shift+`, Ctrl+Shift+U; Ctrl+Shift+N starts a session and Ctrl+1–8 open the bar's places in order. **No act
   that ends or removes something has a key.** A field and the terminal keep what they mean; a browser claims no key it
   keeps.
4. **An agent's page heads in one line** (its name, maker, version and whether installed, *Add an account…*, ⋯), and **each
   account is one row**: its name (the person's once ACCT2 lands) and who signed in where that differs, its state and when
   read, the workspaces it runs for (*no workspace* said), what runs on it now, and one act by state: *Sign in* when signed out, *Read* when unknown, *Try now* when cooling,
   *Use in a workspace…* when signed in and in no list. The own sign-in is the last row; its explanation and the fine print
   fold.
5. ***Add an account…* replaces *Sign in to another account…***: it offers an existing signed-out or unknown account's own
   *Sign in* first, and at its end offers the account's name (ACCT2's, the email by default) and asks which lists it
   joins, saying where that moves a workspace's starts.
6. **The agents' list holds every agent Daoris knows**, the ones not installed as a group with *Install* or *How to install*.
7. **A page's head is its state, its title and two facts.** A record whose state moves leads with its state; the title is one
   line on a session and at most two on a quest; a second line holds the facts the state makes matter and an age. The id
   leaves the head for the ⋯ and a *Details* fold; what the head says is not said again below it; a quest's body drops a
   first line that is its title. SESSUX1j's short title is the fix at the source, and the head reads well with or without it.

**Why.** The owner's two complaints are one shape: a surface built to hold everything says everything at the same weight.
The menu bar held four domains and taught no key, so a person looking for *stop* or *a terminal* found no menu for it, and
the palette and the menus were two lists that agreed by care. The account page put its explanations before and between the
accounts it explained, set every fact in the same grey, and gave the one account that needed the person no act, which is
how ACCT1 happened. The heads put a raw id, a repeated title and eight facts before the work. VS Code is the model the
owner named and the hand most people bring; its bar is verbs and places, and its keys are worth copying where the meaning
matches. Measured (the design §6): 26 menu items, 3 with a key, 7 with a palette twin; an agent's page of 344 words on the
install, a third of them explanation; a quest's body 239 px down after eight lines.

**Rejected.**
- **A *File* menu first**, VS Code's own name: 文件 and *Files* already name what a quest carries (D116). *Workspace* takes
  File's place.
- **Keeping *Daoris* as the first menu**: an application-named menu is macOS's; the install runs on Windows, where VS Code's
  bar starts at File.
- **Keeping D75's rule that a menu opens only setup**: VS Code's Go and View open places, and the palette already does.
- **Agents, or Accounts, as a menu**: a place's content; the page, the palette and *What needs you*'s *Sign in* carry it.
- **A Run menu holding only what applies now**: a menu's places are learned, and VS Code keeps a disabled *Stop Debugging*
  in place. The palette keeps the omission rule.
- **Keys for *Stop…*, *Decline…* or *Delete…***: a key would leave the ask-once as the only guard.
- **Folding the seven menus into one at 680 px**: the command center and the layout toggles give way first.
- **An account's email as its title, its name faint**: the name is what every other surface says.
- **Shortening a long title on the page**: it rewrites content; SESSUX1j shortens it at its source.
- **A smaller title on the quest page alone**: one page header for every page.
- **Quick open for records (Ctrl+P)**: wanted, and its own work; held until a row asks for it.

**This amends:**
- **D75 §1 and D150 §2.4.** The menus are the seven above; D75 §3 (the workspace always named) and §4 (one word) stand.
- **D56 §3a and D66.** The strip's menus hold verbs and places; the activity bar is still the one navigation region.
- **D150 §5.2.** The agent's header is one line, its accounts rows with one act by state, the own sign-in last and folded,
  *Add an account…* in place of *Sign in to another account…*. D150 §5.3's states, hues and never-probed rule stand.
- **D126 §3.2 and SESS2 H7.** A session's header leads with its state and carries a facts line; its id moves to the ⋯; the
  record head drops its repeated title and folds its meta line; the chain opens folded on every session.
- **D118 §3b and the platform language §5 (Quests).** A quest's header leads with its state, two title lines at most, a facts
  line in place of its state line, id and fact rows; its body drops a first line that is its title.
- **The platform language §2 and §4**, once built.

D41, D47 §4, D50, D52, D55, D57 §c, D130 §3 and D150 §5.3 stand.

**What the checks do not cover.** These are documents only, and nothing is built. The install was read from three
screenshots, its counts pixel readings and one transcription counted by UX6's word rule; the stories were counted by a
scratch copy of UX6's counter, untracked, and their fixtures are tidier than the install. No key was tried on the window:
whether the engine takes Ctrl+N, Ctrl+1–8 or Alt first, and whether Edit's clipboard items reach a field after a menu took
the focus, are UX7a's to find. Whether every quest's failed sessions reach a session's facts line through the session
reader is UX7c's to test. The install listed both re-filed quests as parked while a session of each worked; this entry does
not say why. The new names are proposals for D116's check. `verify` checks this entry's place and that it names what it
rejected; it checks none of the words here or in the design.
