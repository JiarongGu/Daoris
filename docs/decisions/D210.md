## D210 — One error policy for every agent: each door's failure is read at the agent interface into one of four kinds (limit, access, transient, fatal) by the adapter's own codes and recorded sentences; a transient error is tried again in the same conversation after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes, said once, never a strike, park, cool-off or walk; a limit is D206's; access goes to the person; a fatal error fails with its reason (2026-10-24)

**Decision (AGENTERRORS1, proposed 2026-10-24).** **Status: proposed**, round 1, under D191. It changes what every adapter answers at
the agent interface, and the rules TURNFAIL1, D206 and D205 rest on, so Codex reads it and an independent Opus reader reads it as a
foundation before it is built. The contract is `docs/2026-10-24-agent-errors-design.md`, read at `64059110a`. The direction (the
owner, 2026-10-24, through the quest that asked for this design) is one error policy for every agent of every harness and door,
designed into the agent interface, not a fix for one code.

What the install showed (design §0, from its session records). Two runs on `claude-code-acp` met `API Error: 529 Overloaded` on
2026-10-24, in the door's two shapes. In shape 1 the prompt failed with a JSON-RPC error. The record ended `failed`, a strike, and the
next look carried it on briefed in a new session, so its own 219,284-token conversation was left behind. In shape 2 (SUPERVISOR1a-ii)
a word the person added was refused with the error and the prompt answered `end_turn`, so the run parked on the person. Thirty-three
minutes later the person's *Go on where the API overload stopped you* resumed the same conversation, and it worked. The wire carried
the adapter's `data.errorKind`. Daoris kept it on `AcpRefusal` and dropped it at the conclusion (`Loop/Driver.cs:4325`), and nothing in
the driver reads a 529, an overload, a 5xx, `api_error_status` or `codexErrorInfo`.

1. **Four kinds** (design §3): `limit`, `access`, `transient`, `fatal`, decided in that order. A limit is read first by
   `AccountLimits.Read`, unchanged, so a limit is never retried. Anything no table reads is `fatal`, as today. The agent's own message
   is never read (D125 point 1.4), though it carries the same text first. The harness's own end, the person's or Daoris's stop and a
   choice refused at the open are not errors of this policy.
2. **One reader at the interface** (§4). Each door hands a `DoorFailure`: the wire's code, kind word, status and sentence, and where
   it came (prompt, steer or result). `AgentErrors.Read` answers an `AgentError` from the adapter's own `ErrorWords` (new on
   `HarnessToolchain`, beside `Limits` and `SignIn`), else its owner's. Each entry is a code, or a sentence recorded and held both
   ways. ACP's `-32000` is `access` on every protocol door. A steer's refusal followed by `end_turn` ends the turn on it (shape 2).
3. **Transient: tried again in the same conversation** (§5.1). Inside the run, before its conclusion, on the same process
   (protocol) or by `--resume` (pipe), the same account, choice and slot. It waits 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes: five tries in 31
   minutes, after the harness's own retries. Each try sends the person's words the door never took, verbatim and first, then
   Daoris's one go-on word, kept once per episode under `DaorisKeys.TransientRetry` and re-sent by id. The conversation says
   `agent.transient-retrying` once, and the head says *Trying again at {{time}}*. The session watch reads the wait as waiting. The
   person's stop ends the episode as theirs, and their word goes at once as the next try. Past the bound the record is `failed` with
   `ended.transient`, its words waiting for the person, marked so no look takes them, and *Try again* or their word resumes the same
   conversation. It is never a strike (`ReadStrikes` passes the code), a park, a cool-off, a `limit` flag or a walk.
4. **Limit: D206, unchanged**, read in the same reader from every door's failure. The pipe door's limit read (D206 row 1f) and a
   steer's limit are new.
5. **Access: to the person** (§5.3). The account is marked refused or signed out as today, with no strike and no retry, and is said
   at the account. The next start walks past it, or is held in today's sentence where none is ready.
6. **Fatal: fails with its reason** (§5.4), a strike, as today. APIREFUSAL1's provider refusal is one.
7. **With D206 and D205** (§6). A transient failure never cools, never reaches D206's planner and is never *not lifted*. Inside a limit
   resume it is retried with `limit.reset` kept. It never walks: D205 point 5 stands. Past the bound, *Go on now with…* offers the next
   choice as the person's press. TURNFAIL1's look-paced retry is kept for fatal failures only.
8. **Every kind of run takes the same retry in its run** (§7). Past the bound:
   - a session waits for the person, as point 3 says;
   - a conversation's turn ends `error` with `chat.transient-stopped`, and the next word goes on in it;
   - a helper is refused `transient`, not *no answer*;
   - an opinion's gate is `Unavailable` / `transient`;
   - an intake's ask is unbound once, then held for the person;
   - a partner's lead is told `partner.transient`;
   - a brainstorm voice retries only within its minutes.
9. **The record** (§8). No new state, column or flag. The note codes, the machine log's `agent.error` and `agent.transient`, and the
   en and zh words are in the design. `{{words}}` is the provider's sentence, kept as written.

**Why.** The provider's outage is not the work's, not the account's and not the person's. Shape 2's fix was the person typing *go on*
in the same conversation, which Daoris can do. The words a failure arrives in belong to each adapter, so the table is the adapter's,
and the policy is the interface's, so every kind of run gets it once. Reading the limit first keeps D206 whole, and reading an
unknown error as fatal keeps today's bound for what nobody has recorded.

**Rejected** (design §10):
- the look's retry, which briefs and loses the conversation;
- a new state;
- cooling the account for an outage;
- walking at once;
- reading the agent's message;
- guessing transient for an unread error;
- retrying within seconds;
- no bound, or a second tier now;
- a setting for the delays;
- declaring JetBrains AIR's typed session failures on every protocol door now, which would drop the error's words on
  `claude-code-acp` (weighed for `codex-acp` alone in row 1f);
- a table per kind of run.

**This would amend**, on acceptance and each by a dated note: D137's TURNFAIL1 note; D125 point 1 and D206 point 1 (the limit read
from every door's failure, in the one reader); D80 (`ended.transient` is no strike); D200 (a run waiting on a retry is not quiet). It
would also re-read APIREFUSAL1 and ROSTER1b as the `fatal` and `access` kinds, with their behaviour unchanged. D205 point 5 and D206
point 6 stand.

**Rows** (design §11), in build order:
- AGENTERRORS1a: the reader, the protocol door's two shapes, the transient retry for a driven session, and the record. It gives the
  most: the install's door, both measured shapes and every driven session.
- 1b: conversations.
- 1c: the pipe door, which closes D206's row 1f gap.
- 1d: the other kinds of run.
- 1e: access on every door.
- 1f: `codex-acp`, its frames recorded before its table.
- 1g: the install's proof.

**What the checks do not cover.** This is a document. The code was read at `64059110a`. The adapters' behaviour was read from
`claude-agent-acp` 0.84.0's and `codex-acp` 2.1.1's source, in the install's toolchain cache. Shapes 1 and 2 are measured from the
install's records, but shape 1's `data.errorKind` is read from source, since the record keeps the message alone. Unmeasured: the pipe
door's `api_error_status`, every Codex terminal error (until row 1f, one parks as shape 2 did), and how long real outages last, which
the 31-minute bound only reads from two records.
