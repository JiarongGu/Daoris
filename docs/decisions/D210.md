## D210 — One error policy for every agent: each door's failure is read at the agent interface into one of four kinds (limit, access, transient, fatal) by the adapter's own codes and recorded sentences; a transient error is tried again in the same conversation after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes, said once, never a strike, park, cool-off or walk; a limit is D206's; access goes to the person; a fatal error fails with its reason (2026-10-24)

**Decision (AGENTERRORS1, proposed 2026-10-24, accepted 2026-10-24).** **Status: accepted** by the steward under D191 (the acceptance note below; Codex's read owed), round 3 with its read's named changes. It changes what every adapter answers at

What the install showed (design §0, from its session records). Two runs on `claude-code-acp` met `API Error: 529 Overloaded` on
2026-10-24, in the door's two shapes. In shape 1 the prompt failed with a JSON-RPC error. The record ended `failed`, a strike, and the
next look carried it on briefed in a new session, so its own 219,284-token conversation was left behind. In shape 2 (SUPERVISOR1a-ii)
a word the person added mid-turn was taken, its own prompt was refused with the error, and the run read the turn as clean, so it parked
on the person. Thirty-three minutes later the person's *Go on where the API overload stopped you* resumed the same conversation, and it
worked. The wire carried the adapter's `data.errorKind`. Daoris kept it on `AcpRefusal` and dropped it at the conclusion
(`Loop/Driver.cs:4325`), and nothing in the driver reads a 529, an overload, a 5xx, `api_error_status` or `codexErrorInfo`.

1. **Four kinds** (design §3): `limit`, `access`, `transient`, `fatal`, decided in that order. A limit is read first by
   `AccountLimits.Read`, unchanged, so a limit is never retried; a `rate_limit`, a 429 or Codex's `usageLimitExceeded` with no
   sentence the table reads is a limit with D125's unstated 60-minute reset, never transient. Anything no table reads is `fatal`, as
   today. The agent's own message is never read (D125 point 1.4), though it carries the same text first; on the pipe door the
   harness's own synthetic frame's `error` field is read, never its text. The harness's own end, the person's or Daoris's stop and a
   choice refused at the open are not errors of this policy, and the pipe door's model refusal on a silent turn stays D205's.
2. **One reader at the interface** (§4). Each door hands a `DoorFailure`: the wire's code, kind word, status and sentence, and where
   it came (the open, a prompt, the pipe door's result, or the harness's own lines, its stderr and its stdout outside its protocol,
   read for access alone). `AgentErrors.Read`, given the adapter set, answers an `AgentError` from the adapter's own `ErrorWords` (new
   on `HarnessToolchain`, beside `Limits` and `SignIn`) **and then its owner's, both, own first**, so `claude-code-acp` reads
   `claude-code`'s provider entries. Each entry is a code, or a sentence recorded and held both ways. ACP's `-32000` is `access` only
   where the adapter's own `AuthRequiredCode` declares that meaning from its source (`claude-code-acp`, `codex-acp`), never inherited;
   elsewhere it is fatal. A 429 or `rate_limit` is always a limit, and only the transient codes a table names make a transient. A
   mid-turn word's prompt refused after the word was taken is the turn's prompt failing (shape 2); refused before, it is that word's.
3. **Transient: tried again in the same conversation** (§5.1). Inside the run, before its conclusion, the same account, choice and
   slot: on the protocol door inside `AcpSession.RunAsync`, around its prompt sites, handed the reader, the clock, the delays and its
   owner's check, and handed to a run of a kind its row has reached (row 1a: driven quest sessions alone). Five tries, with 1, 2, 4, 8
   and 16 minutes of waits before them, after the harness's own retries.
   - **The wire settles first.** A settling turn that answers, or a turn the harness woke, ends the wait and never the episode: its
     answer shows only that the agent answered that word, and ended there the run would park a taken quest or fail an open one
     `ended.untouched`. The owner's check is asked, and where another turn is still wanted Daoris's go-on goes at once and counts as
     a try. A settling turn that fails transient counts as a try.
   - **Each of the person's words has a disposition**: *held* (never written), *taken* or *unacknowledged* (on the wire, its prompt
     answered with an error before any update of its turn, whatever Daoris's `Taken` mark says). Only held words are ever sent by a
     try; a word once on the wire is never sent again. An unacknowledged word is D188's COWORK1c uncertain word, said by its `lost`
     note under its id and offered as *Say it again*. The dispositions go to every conclusion, whatever its kind, so a later
     limit or fatal end, D206 and TURNFAIL1 carry only the held words.
   - **The tries.** During a wait every word is held; each try is one prompt, the person's held words and then Daoris's go-on word,
     kept once per episode under `DaorisKeys.TransientRetry` and, as D188 point 6 has any word, taken off the record by id once on
     the wire, the conclusion's take-off kept as the fallback at a first run's conclusion and a continuation's. The go-on says to go
     on *after anything said above*. Each try goes through the wake gate (BGWAIT1) and asks its run's owner
     whether another turn is still wanted: for a driven session, its quest not done, submitted, awaiting, stood down, closed or
     deleted, an Open quest going on and an unread check a yes; for an intake, `IntakeObservation.Conclude`'s authoritative endings.
   - **What is said.** The conversation says `agent.transient-retrying` once, the provider's error is shown once and the go-on once,
     and a quiet `agent.transient` event per try gives the head its *Trying again at {{time}}*.
   - **The watch** reads the wait as `WaitsOnTransient`, not quiet, and the wait's end stamps the session heard.
   - **The person.** Their stop ends the episode as theirs, and their word goes at once as the next try.
   - **The open.** A failure at the open repeats the raw open and then sends the original prompt once. Until row 1a2 a failure at
     the open keeps today's end.
   - **Past the bound** the record is `failed` with `ended.transient`, worded with the tries made and the minutes waited. Daoris's
     go-on is off at every conclusion. **The planner holds the quest**, Open or Taken, by that note code, directly after `WordsOn` and
     the person's stop and before every other path. A hand-over made from the held record passes it, and an Open quest's is carried
     on in its tree, as D206's step 0 is.
   - **The release.** Only the person releases it: a new word of theirs said after the record ended (MSG1b's go-on, their words
     alone), since no word of Daoris's, a helper's or another agent's does, and the time needs no mark, so a word an open never sent
     is held with no write; or, from row 1a2 beside its press, *Try again* (D206's step-1 resume). *Try again*'s release is its own,
     keyed to the end it releases (the record's session and `Updated`), so a released run that fails past its bound again is held
     again. An Open quest's release carries its record on whatever its tree holds.
   - **Never** a strike (`ReadStrikes` passes the code), a park, a cool-off, a `limit` flag, a walk or a carry-on.
4. **Limit: D206, unchanged**, read in the same reader from every door's failure. The pipe door's limit read (D206 row 1f), a taken
   mid-turn word's limit and a limit by its code alone are new.
5. **Access: to the person** (§5.3). The account is marked refused or signed out as today, with no strike and no retry, and is said
   at the account. The next start walks past it, or is held in today's sentence where none is ready.
6. **Fatal: fails with its reason** (§5.4), a strike, as today. APIREFUSAL1's provider refusal is one.
7. **With D206 and D205** (§6). A transient failure never cools, never reaches D206's planner and is never *not lifted*. Inside a limit
   resume it is retried without sending `limit.reset` or the person's taken words again, and a limit on a try leaves for D206 once.
   It never walks: D205 point 5 stands. Past the bound, *Go on now with…* offers the next choice as the person's press, which the hold
   passes, and *Try again* is D206's step 1, which is the protocol door's until row 1c records that the native door's resume of a
   missing conversation fails closed; until then a native *Try again* goes briefed and says so, while the person's word goes on in
   the same conversation by MSG1b's resume. TURNFAIL1's look-paced retry is kept for
   fatal failures only.
8. **Every kind of run takes the same retry in its run** (§7), within its own minutes: no delay is waited past a pass's deadline, and
   minutes that end inside an episode end with the transient reason. Past the bound:
   - a session's quest is held for the person, as point 3 says;
   - a conversation's turn ends `error` with `chat.transient-stopped`, and the next word goes on in it;
   - a helper is refused `transient`, not *no answer*;
   - an opinion's gate is `Unavailable` / `transient`;
   - an intake's ask is unbound once, then held for the person;
   - a partner's lead is told `partner.transient`;
   - a brainstorm voice retries only within its minutes.

   The native door's live conversation retries on its own process, its driven run by `--resume` with `--model` and effort, and a
   one-turn pass by an id it never keeps.
9. **The record** (§8). No new state, column, flag or mark; the transient release is the driver's own config beside the stop's. The
   note codes (`held.transient` among them; an uncertain word is COWORK1c's `lost` note, never a new one), the machine log's `agent.error` and `agent.transient`, and
   the en and zh words are in the design. The zh's *try again* is the glossary's 重试, and its running text names the product
   *Daoris*. `{{words}}` is the provider's sentence, kept as written.

**Why.** The provider's outage is not the work's, not the account's and not the person's. Shape 2's fix was the person typing *go on*
in the same conversation, which Daoris can do. The words a failure arrives in belong to each adapter, so the table is the adapter's,
and the policy is the interface's, so every kind of run gets it once. Reading the limit first keeps D206 whole, and reading an
unknown error as fatal keeps today's bound for what nobody has recorded. Holding the line past the bound, by its note, is what keeps an
outage longer than the bound from cycling into a new briefed part every half hour with no strike to end it. That holds only if
nothing but the person releases it, and a release frees one end alone. A word once on the wire is never sent again, since an error
does not say whether the agent read it, and repeating an instruction is worse than asking the person to say it again.

**Rejected** (design §10):
- the look's retry, which briefs and loses the conversation;
- the retry in the capture's catch, where no live session is left;
- a new state;
- a word mark to hold the line past the bound;
- sending a taken word again;
- cooling the account for an outage;
- walking at once;
- reading the agent's message;
- guessing transient for an unread error, or for a 429 by its code;
- `-32000` as access on every protocol door;
- reading Codex's `willRetry` notices as a terminal failure;
- retrying within seconds;
- extending a pass's minutes for its tries;
- no bound, or a second tier now;
- a setting for the delays;
- declaring JetBrains AIR's typed session failures on every protocol door now, which would drop the error's words on
  `claude-code-acp` (weighed for `codex-acp` alone in row 1f);
- a table per kind of run;
- the stop's session-keyed release for the transient hold, or clearing a release as its start continues the record;
- reading Daoris's `Taken` mark as proof that a word was not accepted;
- any waiting word releasing the transient hold;
- *own or owner* for `ErrorWords`, which would hide the provider's entries from `claude-code-acp`;
- a temporary rate limit read transient by its sentence, in this delivery;
- an answered settling turn or a woken end ending the episode;
- a note of its own for an unacknowledged word;
- a mark for the words an open never sent.
