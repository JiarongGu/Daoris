## D210 — One error policy for every agent: each door's failure is read at the agent interface into one of four kinds (limit, access, transient, fatal) by the adapter's own codes and recorded sentences; a transient error is tried again in the same conversation after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes, said once, never a strike, park, cool-off or walk; a limit is D206's; access goes to the person; a fatal error fails with its reason (2026-10-24)

**Decision (AGENTERRORS1, proposed 2026-10-24).** **Status: proposed**, round 2, under D191. It changes what every adapter answers at
the agent interface, and the rules TURNFAIL1, D206 and D205 rest on, so Codex reads it and an independent Opus reader reads it as a
foundation before it is built. The contract is `docs/2026-10-24-agent-errors-design.md`, read at `64059110a` and again on main at
`fb3eb89eb`, where no file it cites changed. The direction (the owner, 2026-10-24, through the quest that asked for this design) is
one error policy for every agent of every harness and door, designed into the agent interface, not a fix for one code. Round 1 was
read at `6f089fe1f` (`docs/2026-10-24-agent-errors-reads.md`): Codex asked another round, and the Opus foundation reader would accept
after named changes. Round 2 answers every finding of both in the design's §14.

What the install showed (design §0, from its session records). Two runs on `claude-code-acp` met `API Error: 529 Overloaded` on
2026-10-24, in the door's two shapes. In shape 1 the prompt failed with a JSON-RPC error. The record ended `failed`, a strike, and the
next look carried it on briefed in a new session, so its own 219,284-token conversation was left behind. In shape 2 (SUPERVISOR1a-ii)
a word the person added mid-turn was taken, its own prompt was refused with the error, and the run read the turn as clean, so it parked
on the person. Thirty-three minutes later the person's *Go on where the API overload stopped you* resumed the same conversation, and it
worked. The wire carried the adapter's `data.errorKind`. Daoris kept it on `AcpRefusal` and dropped it at the conclusion
(`Loop/Driver.cs:4325`), and nothing in the driver reads a 529, an overload, a 5xx, `api_error_status` or `codexErrorInfo`.

1. **Four kinds** (design §3): `limit`, `access`, `transient`, `fatal`, decided in that order. A limit is read first by
   `AccountLimits.Read`, unchanged, so a limit is never retried; a `rate_limit`, a 429 or Codex's `usageLimitExceeded` with no
   sentence the table reads is a limit with D125's unstated 60-minute reset, never transient. Anything no table reads is `fatal`, as
   today. The agent's own message is never read (D125 point 1.4), though it carries the same text first; on the pipe door the
   harness's own synthetic frame's `error` field is read, never its text. The harness's own end, the person's or Daoris's stop and a
   choice refused at the open are not errors of this policy, and the pipe door's model refusal on a silent turn stays D205's.
2. **One reader at the interface** (§4). Each door hands a `DoorFailure`: the wire's code, kind word, status and sentence, and where
   it came (the open, a prompt, the pipe door's result, or the harness's own lines, read for access alone). `AgentErrors.Read`, given
   the adapter set, answers an `AgentError` from the adapter's own `ErrorWords` (new on `HarnessToolchain`, beside `Limits` and
   `SignIn`), else its owner's. Each entry is a code, or a sentence recorded and held both ways. ACP's `-32000` is `access` only where
   the adapter declares that meaning from its source (`claude-code-acp`, `codex-acp`); elsewhere it is fatal. A mid-turn word's prompt
   refused after the word was taken is the turn's prompt failing (shape 2); refused before, it is that word's.
3. **Transient: tried again in the same conversation** (§5.1). Inside the run, before its conclusion, the same account, choice and
   slot: on the protocol door inside `AcpSession.RunAsync`, around its prompt sites, handed the reader, the clock and the delays. Five
   tries, with 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes of waits before them, after the harness's own retries; a try's own time is the watch's. The
   wire settles first, and a word already taken (`Acp.cs`'s mark, or a resume's prompt on the wire) is never sent again. During a wait
   every word is held; each try is one prompt, the person's words never taken and then Daoris's go-on word, kept once per episode
   under `DaorisKeys.TransientRetry`. Each try goes through the wake gate (BGWAIT1), and asks its run's owner whether another turn is
   still wanted. The conversation says `agent.transient-retrying` once, and a quiet `agent.transient` event per try gives the head its
   *Trying again at {{time}}*. The session watch reads the wait as `WaitsOnTransient`, not quiet. The person's stop ends the episode
   as theirs, and their word goes at once as the next try. A failure at the open repeats the open and then sends the original prompt
   once. Past the bound the record is `failed` with `ended.transient`; Daoris's go-on comes off at every conclusion, and **the planner
   holds the quest**, Open or Taken, by that note code, until the person's word or *Try again* resumes the same conversation through
   D206's step-1 resume. It is never a strike (`ReadStrikes` passes the code), a park, a cool-off, a `limit` flag, a walk or a
   carry-on.
4. **Limit: D206, unchanged**, read in the same reader from every door's failure. The pipe door's limit read (D206 row 1f), a taken
   mid-turn word's limit and a limit by its code alone are new.
5. **Access: to the person** (§5.3). The account is marked refused or signed out as today, with no strike and no retry, and is said
   at the account. The next start walks past it, or is held in today's sentence where none is ready.
6. **Fatal: fails with its reason** (§5.4), a strike, as today. APIREFUSAL1's provider refusal is one.
7. **With D206 and D205** (§6). A transient failure never cools, never reaches D206's planner and is never *not lifted*. Inside a limit
   resume it is retried without sending `limit.reset` or the person's taken words again, and a limit on a try leaves for D206 once.
   It never walks: D205 point 5 stands. Past the bound, *Go on now with…* offers the next choice as the person's press, and the
   person's release is D206's step 1. TURNFAIL1's look-paced retry is kept for fatal failures only.
8. **Every kind of run takes the same retry in its run** (§7), within its own minutes: no delay is waited past a pass's deadline, and
   minutes that end inside an episode end with the transient reason. Past the bound:
   - a session's quest is held for the person, as point 3 says;
   - a conversation's turn ends `error` with `chat.transient-stopped`, and the next word goes on in it;
   - a helper is refused `transient`, not *no answer*;
   - an opinion's gate is `Unavailable` / `transient`;
   - an intake's ask is unbound once, then held for the person;
   - a partner's lead is told `partner.transient`;
   - a brainstorm voice retries only within its minutes.

   The native door's live conversation retries on its own process, its driven run by `--resume` with `--model` and effort, and a
   one-turn pass by an id it never keeps.
9. **The record** (§8). No new state, column or flag. The note codes (`held.transient` among them), the machine log's `agent.error`
   and `agent.transient`, and the en and zh words, the zh's *try again* the glossary's 重试, are in the design. `{{words}}` is the
   provider's sentence, kept as written.

**Why.** The provider's outage is not the work's, not the account's and not the person's. Shape 2's fix was the person typing *go on*
in the same conversation, which Daoris can do. The words a failure arrives in belong to each adapter, so the table is the adapter's,
and the policy is the interface's, so every kind of run gets it once. Reading the limit first keeps D206 whole, and reading an
unknown error as fatal keeps today's bound for what nobody has recorded. Holding the line past the bound, by its note, is what keeps an
outage longer than the bound from cycling into a new briefed part every half hour with no strike to end it.

**Rejected** (design §10):
- the look's retry, which briefs and loses the conversation;
- the retry in the capture's catch, where no live session is left;
- a new state;
- a word mark to hold the line past the bound;
- sending a taken word again;
- cooling the account for an outage;
- walking at once;
- reading the agent's message;
- guessing transient for an unread error, or for a 429 by its code;
- `-32000` as access on every protocol door;
- reading Codex's `willRetry` notices as a terminal failure;
- retrying within seconds;
- extending a pass's minutes for its tries;
- no bound, or a second tier now;
- a setting for the delays;
- declaring JetBrains AIR's typed session failures on every protocol door now, which would drop the error's words on
  `claude-code-acp` (weighed for `codex-acp` alone in row 1f);
- a table per kind of run.

**This would amend**, on acceptance and each by a dated note: D137's TURNFAIL1 note; D125 point 1 and D206 point 1 (the limit read
from every door's failure, in the one reader, a limit's code with the unstated reset); D80 (`ended.transient` is no strike, and its
quest is held, not carried on); D126 §3.3 (the stop's release also releases a transient hold); D200 (a run waiting on a retry is not
quiet); BGWAIT1's note on D105 (a try goes through the wake gate). It would also re-read APIREFUSAL1 and ROSTER1b as the `fatal` and
`access` kinds, with their behaviour unchanged. D205 point 5, its pipe-door model refusal, and D206 point 6 stand.

**Rows** (design §11), in build order:
- AGENTERRORS1a: the reader, the protocol door's prompt failure, the transient retry for a driven session inside `AcpSession`, the
  record, and the planner's hold with its release. It gives the most: the install's door, its measured shape 1 and every driven session.
- 1a2: shape 2, the open, and the page (the head's line, *Try again*).
- 1b: conversations.
- 1c: the pipe door, which closes D206's row 1f gap, and the native door's two process lifecycles.
- 1d: the other kinds of run, within their minutes.
- 1e: access on every door.
- 1f: `codex-acp`, its frames and its typed marker recorded before its table.
- 1g: the install's proof, a controlled stand-in now and the next real outage later.

**What the checks do not cover.** This is a document. The code was read at `64059110a` and on main at `fb3eb89eb`. The adapters'
behaviour was read from `claude-agent-acp` 0.84.0's and `codex-acp` 2.1.1's source, in the install's toolchain cache. Shapes 1 and 2
are measured from the install's records, but shape 1's `data.errorKind` is read from source, since the record keeps the message
alone. The pipe door's `api_error_status` and `error` field are measured for a model refusal and a limit (MODELROLE1a), not for a 5xx.
Unmeasured: every Codex terminal error (until row 1f, one parks as shape 2 did), how long real outages last, which the 31 minutes of
waits only reads from two records, and what the prompt cache costs a try after a long wait.
