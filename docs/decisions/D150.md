## D150 — A setting lives on the thing it is about: a repository's setup and its git on the repository, an agent is a place with one account list, a plugin brings its tools, and What needs you leads Overview with what it can settle (2026-10-04)

**Decision (UX6, PLUGTOOL1).** The owner, in five messages: *"this is confusing right now since I have logged 3 accounts
in before and also there should be just one account management for Claude Code"*; *"you kind of need to improve the
UI/UX since this is getting more and more complex, and we do need to add features later for coworking between agents"*;
*"there probably will be more types of agents later too so the UI/UX redesign is needed"*; *"I saw the Azure CLI is in
Tools, which should be in a plugin"*; and *"the current permissions in Settings are mostly about repositories, and we
probably should have a repository setup in the repository screen, since repository management (with git) is also
planned"*. The same day, two asks sat proposed until someone pressed through, and an intake waited behind one cooling
account while two signed-out accounts went unsaid. The contract is `docs/2026-10-04-ux6-redesign.md`.

1. **A setting has one home, on the thing it is about.** Anywhere else it is shown read-only, with a door to its home.
   Settings keeps what is the machine's or the person's. Every move keeps its terminal twin (D50). A section of settings
   folds to a line naming its values, those at Daoris's default marked; a value the person set opens it.
2. **Nine places**: Overview, Sessions, Quests, Repositories, Map, Knowledge, Agents, Plugins, and Settings at the foot.
   Search and Convergence become one place, *Knowledge*, whose list switches between them. Refresh and the language toggle
   leave the bar for the Daoris menu, the palette and Appearance. Settings keeps seven domains: Get started (renamed from
   *Setup*), Appearance, AI features, Driver, Tools, Browser, Machine log. A tenth place is a decision with its own
   counts, never a default.
3. **Repositories holds each repository's setup and its git.** Its list groups repositories by workspace. A workspace's
   page has Details, Branches and Setup: its defaults, its remote, what a start runs on, its rules. A repository's page
   has Details, Branches, History and Setup. Setup is driving, its line, how its work lands, its session language and
   standing answer, reading and writing across, and its rules, in four sections. Settings → Workspace and Settings →
   Permissions retire into these pages. D147's lists and pages become the Branches and History tabs, and no Git place
   joins the bar. The session language, set in two places today, has one.
4. **Agents is a place, and an agent is a product.** Its list holds each agent once, whatever doors reach it; adding an
   agent adds a row. An agent's page holds one account list, how accounts are used, its workspaces (D130 §3.2, moved
   here), its ways in as a property, what it may do, model and effort, and usage, each section present only where the
   agent has that concept. Settings → Agents retires.
5. **An account's state is what was last known, and when**: *signed in*, *signed out*, *cooling until …*, or *unknown*,
   each with when it was read. **An account is never probed on a timer, at a look, at the start, or when a view opens.**
   It is read on the person's press of *Read again*, one account at a time and never two against one home, or as the
   result of something that ran (a start's refusal, a sign-in's end). A signed-out account a list holds wears open's hue.
6. **What needs you leads Overview, in three groups**: *holding work* (a start waiting for accounts, a signed-out account
   a list holds, a parked session or quest, a go-ahead, trust, a plugin that cannot land), *waiting for your word* (an ask
   to publish, an intake's question, a departure, a rule widening, a quest nobody can take), and *ready for you* (work to
   review). A row acts where one press is safe and says what it does (*Publish to …*, *Sign in*, *Try again*, *Accept
   the departure*). A widening asks once. Reading-first is a door. No row starts a process to find out: every fact is
   already the page's or the tick's.
7. **A plugin declares the tools its process runs** (`tools` in its manifest: an id, a version range, why, at most four
   readiness checks run only on a press or at *Try*). Its page shows and manages them. Settings → Tools keeps Git,
   Node.js and PowerShell, and any known tool no installed plugin declares that still has a way or a download. A tool's
   way stays one per machine in `tools.json`, every child keeps the tools' environment (D121 §3), and a plugin never
   carries a download. A problem in `tools` is shown, never a refusal.
8. **A remembered choice ends with what it chose, and the side bar follows only live work.** A view reopens its chosen
   item only while that item still waits on something. Off Sessions, a session's views follow a running or waiting
   session, else the side bar opens on Ask Daoris. A long note folds in the timeline.
9. **Each screen is measured** by its concepts (glossary terms on it), controls, words, screens of scroll, and the
   presses a task takes. One counter runs over the stories and on the install's window. The budgets report and never
   fail (D54).

**Why.** The owner's complaint is one shape seen five times: a thing's settings live on a page about something else, so
each new thing grows someone else's page. A repository's setup was four lists of 31 rows across two Settings domains
plus its own page, about 430 controls to find 18. An agent's accounts were drawn once per door. A plugin's tool was a
screen away from the plugin. The terminal never had that shape: `daoris driver <verb> <repo>` and `daoris agent …`
already group by thing, so the screen follows the terminal. What needs the person was shown as doors, not acts, and two
kinds of wait were not shown at all. Probing accounts at each look is what signed the owner's accounts out (TOOL6g), so
the only honest screen shows the last known state with its time. Merging Git into Repositories keeps the bar at nine
while it gains agents and git, and answers the owner's *repository management (with git)* in one screen.

**Rejected.**
- **Tidying today's screens in place** (a denser Permissions, folded Workspace lists): fewer words, the same three
  screens for one repository.
- **A drawer per repository from each Settings row**: a drawer is a form (D118 §3d), and the setting stays elsewhere.
- **Git as its own place beside Repositories** (D147 §6): two places listing the same repositories. D147 §7's reasons,
  a graph needing the main area and two pages in one, are met by tabs that are each one page.
- **Branches nested under each repository in the list pane**: three levels, 29 deep, in 280 px.
- **Agents kept in Settings**, and **an agent per door**: a long scroll with no page, and two rosters for one product.
- **An Inbox place beside Overview**: two places whose first job is the same.
- **Live account states** (a probe at a look, at the start, on a timer): what signed the accounts out, and a waiting look
  starts no process (D125 §4).
- **An act on every needs-you row**: answering a parked session or accepting a review needs reading first.
- **Map folded into a workspace's page**: it spans workspaces (D67 §3), and its canvas needs the main area.
- **A plugin carrying a tool's download URL and hash**: a plugin would become a source of programs. Downloads stay with
  the lists the person adds (D121 §5).
- **A plugin's tool on its own process's `PATH` only**: it breaks D121 §3's one answer, and a session running `gh` would
  lose the managed one.
- **An Advanced switch per page**: a second concept on every page, where a folded section keeps each value in sight.

**This amends:**
- **D66 and D56 §3b.** The bar's places are the nine above, and its foot holds Settings alone.
- **D75.** The menus' items open the new homes, and Settings has seven domains.
- **D118 §2.** Repositories' list groups by workspace with a workspace's page; Knowledge's list has two modes; Agents has a
  list and a page.
- **The dock design (DOCK1a).** Off Sessions, an ended session is not followed.
- **D147 §6–§7.** The Git place becomes Repositories' Branches and History tabs; GIT1e and GIT1f land there. The rest of
  D147 stands.
- **D121 §1 and §4.1.** Daoris's code names five tools and runs three; GitHub CLI and Azure CLI are declared by plugins
  and managed on their pages. §3 and §5 stand.
- **D64 §3 and D101.** The manifest gains `tools`, and the kit checks it.
- **D119 §3.2.** A plugin's page gains Tools after Points.
- **D130 §3.2 and D125 §6.** A workspace's accounts are set on the agent's page.
- **D97 and D116.** Settings' first domain is *Get started* again, and *Setup* names a repository's and a workspace's tab.
- **The working surface design §4 and the platform language §5.** What needs you gains its groups, kinds and acts.

D55, D47 §4, D50, D52, D37, D24, D49 §4 and D57 §c stand.

**What the checks do not cover.** These are documents only, and nothing is built. The install was read from eight
screenshots, two of them the same Settings screen; Sessions, Repositories, Agents, Tools and Workspace on the install
were not seen, and their counts are from stories and the source, scaled to the install's 29 repositories and two
workspaces by each list's per-row cost. The scaling assumes each list holds a row per repository. Claude Code's accounts
read twice is TOOL6g's reading, relayed. How the driver learns *signed out* without a probe at a look is TOOL6g's to
settle. The plugins' version floors are illustrative, and their readiness commands are the CLIs' documented forms, not
run. The new names are proposals for D116's check. `verify` checks this entry's place and that it names what it
rejected. It checks none of the words here or in the design.

**UX6c, built (2026-10-04): What needs you leads Overview and settles what it can.** The band is Overview's first
content (`AttentionBand`, drawn by `AttentionList` and `AttentionRow`), in §6.2's three groups, and one line, *Nothing
needs you* with the sessions working, when nothing waits. It gains go-aheads, departures and work to review. What the
design left open, settled here:
- **Within a group the longest waiting comes first, whatever its kind**, as §6.2 says, so a folder held for trust since
  September comes before a park from this noon. A tie keeps §6.2's order of kinds. `needsAPerson` had ordered by kind;
  its tests now hold the groups.
- **Every fact is one the page already holds** (§6.3). A go-ahead is read from its ask's `goAheads`, which the asks list
  carries; a departure from the quests list's `held`; work to review from the groups Sessions' list reads
  (`SESSION_GROUPS`), which the frame holds under the same key on every view of a shell, so the band asks nothing more.
  A review's title needs the list with the ended sessions, also the frame's, asked only while a review is listed. A
  review row's why is the session row's own line (`placedFact`, now exported), so the two never say different things.
  A suite holds that rendering the band asks the bridge only those two reads.
- **One rule per kind** (`attentionActs`): *Publish to …* where the declarations named receivers, then *Choose…*, else
  *Choose where it goes…*; *Try again*, saying it starts a session; *Approve…* and *Refuse…* on a go-ahead; *Trust this
  folder…*; *Accept the departure*; *Accept…* and *Decline* on a widening. Asking once (`ASKS_ONCE`): a go-ahead's
  answer, which takes the person's words; a folder's trust, which is the agent's own question (`TrustAsk`) under the row,
  so the drawer the row opened is gone; a widening's accept, naming what it widens; and a chosen receiver. A park, an
  intake's question and a review are their door alone (*Answer…*, *Answer ask #id*, *Review*, which opens Sessions with
  the review open); every other row's door is *Open* at its end. A row holds three controls at most, held by a test.
- **Publishing to several receivers is one press and one publish each**, on the ask page's route, each sentence said,
  stopping at a refusal, since the service publishes an ask to one receiver at a time.
- **Two names keep the words their pages already use**, against §6.2's proposals: a go-ahead's presses are *Approve…*
  and *Refuse…* (the ask's page, and the glossary's *approve is the go-ahead's press*), since *allow* is the rules'
  list; a widening's is *Accept…* (Settings' card, and *accept* is a proposal's move in the glossary), not *Apply…*. The
  groups are *Holding work* · *Waiting for your word* · *Ready for you* (阻塞工作 · 等你发话 · 待你审阅); a go-ahead's
  word is *needs your go-ahead* (等你放行), since the names check reads *asks* as an ask.
- **Left out.** The rows of accounts (a start waiting for accounts, a signed-out account) are UX6d's, after TOOL6g. A
  plugin a landing rule names that cannot land is left to PLUGTOOL1c, whose §7.5 adds a missing tool to D100's reasons:
  the landing rules are read today only by Settings' lines and the plugins' states by the Plugins view, so the band would
  hold a read of the lines it does not hold now. The rule row's door still opens Settings → Permissions until the agent's
  page exists (UX6e).
- **Not covered by a gate run here.** The window was not looked at: the stories were, at 1546 and 680 px in both themes
  and both languages. `test:web`'s ask test was changed to the new row and not run. Overview's badge counts every row,
  work to review included, which on a machine with many unreviewed sessions is a larger number than before.

**UX6b, built 2026-10-04: a remembered choice ends with its item, and the side bar follows only live work** (point 8).
- **A remembered choice** is what a launch reads from a list's keys, and what a view held when a door that names nothing
  (its place, a menu, the palette) opens it again. Each list view tells the frame what its chosen item is now, *live*,
  *closed*, *gone* or *unread* (`ListSpec.standing`). The list's memory lets a remembered choice go once its item reads
  closed or gone, before the page is drawn (`listPanes.ts` `reopen` and `settle`; `ViewListPane`). It never decides on
  *unread*, so a refusal or a slow answer keeps the choice. An item chosen now, in the list or by a door that names it,
  is never let go: one that closes keeps its page, and one that goes while open shows the *gone* state.
- **What still waits**, by view: a quest open or taken, or closed while a departure holds it for the person or a move from
  another machine waits on their dismissal (`questStanding`). An ask proposed, open or published, or done with an unanswered
  go-ahead (`askStanding`). A finding, an entry, a repository or a plugin has no life to close, so it reopens while it is
  still listed. Settings' domain and Sessions' attended session are unchanged.
- **Off Sessions** a session's views follow the attended session only while it is queued, starting, working or waiting on
  the person (`followed`, `SESSION_ACTIVE`). That covers all four: the timeline, the review, the console and a file's
  preview. With none followed, the side bar opens on Ask Daoris (`dockOpensOn`). The timeline still opens on a press, and
  says the session attended in Sessions has ended, with *Open Sessions* (`NothingFollowed`).
- **A long note folds** in the timeline: a session's note and a quest's closing words. A note longer than four lines shows
  four, with *Show all* and *Show less*. Its length is estimated at 56 letters a line, an ideograph counted as two
  (`linesTaken`), because the page measures nothing before it draws. An evidence bundle is not a note and does not fold.
- **Held by** `listPanes`, `ViewFrame`, `records`, `RightDock`, `noteLines` and `SessionTimeline` tests; each list view's
  suite, where a remembered closed or gone item opens nothing chosen and a door's item opens whatever became of it; and
  `rememberedChoice.test.tsx` on the whole window. `test:web`'s Quests flow now expects its done quest not to reopen after
  the reload.
- **What the gates do not cover.** The fold's length is an estimate, so a note near four lines may fold one line early or
  late at a side bar wider or narrower than usual. The clamp was looked at in Storybook, on a record shown as kept and
  across blocks (Daoris's line above the agent's words), in both themes and languages. Nothing was looked at on the window,
  and `test:web` was not run on this branch.

**UX6e, built 2026-10-04: Agents is a place, with one account list per product** (points 4 and 5). The activity bar holds
*Agents* after Search, a shell's alone, its badge the signed-out accounts a list or a default holds, in open's hue. Its list
is each agent once (`agentRows` over `byTool`), its accounts in a phrase and the waiting mark; its ⋯ shows the agents not
installed. An agent's page (`AgentPage`) holds its accounts, one list, then *How accounts are used*, *Workspaces*, *Ways
in*, *What it may do*, *Model and effort* and *Usage*, each folded to a line naming its values. Settings → Agents is
gone, its cards and its tests with it (`agents/AgentsView.test.tsx`). What the design left open, settled here:
- **When each account was read is the modules' to keep** (`RosterReads`), since the roster's report says a state and not
  when. A press, a sign-in's end, a key and an account's edit each ask the roster again and date every account then, but
  for one a session runs on, whose word the probe kept and whose time stays. A report the driver made by itself dates only
  the words that changed, when the window first hears them. A first unknown, and the tool's own sign-in before a press,
  is *never read*; an unknown after a read is *the last read failed*.
- **Opening the place asks nothing.** It reads the roster the frame already holds and `ACCOUNTS`, a file read. *Read again*
  is the Accounts section's, and asks that agent alone: `HARNESSES` with `refresh` and `agent`, through its account-owning
  door (`ReportAsync`), so each account is asked once, not once a door, and the tool's own sign-in with them. An account's
  ⋯ has no *Read again* of its own: the driver reads no single account into the roster on a press.
- **The state words**: *signed in*, *signed out* (the roster's `harness.login.out`, renamed from *not signed in*, so the page
  and the list editor say one word), *cooling until …* and *unknown*. Signed out comes before a cool-off, since a reset
  frees nothing. A key account is *unchecked*, by its handle. Signed in is neutral, never done's green.
- **What it may do** shows on the agent whose doors take the rules file (`takesRules`, the adapter's `TakesSettings`, now
  on the roster). It holds the proposals, Daoris's defaults and the rules for every session. Settings → Permissions keeps
  a workspace's and a repository's rules, with a line and a door to the rest, until UX6f and UX6g (`AgentRules`'s
  `part`). *What needs you*'s rule row, the setup guide's rules step and Ask Daoris's `permissions/proposals` open it.
- **A door that names a part and no agent** opens the agent that has it: the rules on the agent that takes them, usage and
  accounts on the agent driven work starts on. The Agents menu lists each installed agent, then *Sign in to another
  account…*, *What agents may do*, *Proposals* (Overview), *Usage* and *AI features* (§2.4).
- **The twins are unchanged.** The driver's `HelpPlaces` still names Settings → `agents`, its `usage` and
  `permissions/proposals`; the page resolves each to its new home (`places.ts` `MOVED`), so the tables stay line for line.
  `HelpCoverageTests` reads the Agents place as it read the domain, under the domain's id.
- **TOOL4m's rest.** The start form's account picker lists the accounts the repository's workspace's list holds first, in
  its order, then every other under *Not in work's list* (or this machine's), a group of the select (D130 §3.2). A session's
  head says *on your own sign-in* where its record names no account and its agent is on the roster (D125 §3.7).
- **Left out.** The header's ⋯ (*Update…*, *Pin a version…*, *Unpin*, *Install…*): each door's acts stay on its row in *Ways
  in*. The account rows on *What needs you* are UX6d's.
- **Not covered by a gate run here.** The window was not looked at: the stories were, at 1546 and 680 px in both themes and
  languages. The `Process` half (two new route tests in `DriverModuleAgentsTests`) and `test:web` were not run. The
  driver's sentences, its room and the CLI's `agent list` still say *Settings → Agents*.
