## D237 — The merge tool cut by what each part becomes: what Daoris takes moved whole and deleted at the flip, the rest a thin runner over modules, the rehearsals a runner over phases; run folders kept by age and size; a gate killed from outside said as lost, and the queue's runner outside its submitter's tree (2026-11-05)

**Decision (MERGETOOL1 with QUEUEHANG1; accepted by the owner, 2026-11-06).** The contract is `docs/2026-11-05-merge-tool-design.md`. It takes
the integration design's settlement of who integrates (D215, its §6 and §7.1) as given.

1. **One way between the tool and the product.** Integration is Daoris's. The queue, its runner, `tools/merge-queue.mjs`, the merge in
   place and the resolvers' tool half retire at INTEGRATE1k's flip, which is their register entry under D228. Until then they are
   moved whole into modules of their own and changed only where every merge needs it. What reads this repository's layout stays its
   development tool: the plan, the lanes, the reach table, the verdicts, the gates' run, the gate tree, the declared runner Daoris
   calls, the CI and its watch, the stage's evidence and the prune, each until the row named for it.
2. **A thin runner over modules by responsibility.** `tools/merge-branch.mjs` keeps its path, its `main` and a re-export per module.
   The modules sit in `tools/merge-branch/`, each under 40 KB, imports running one way from leaves to modes. The cut follows the
   lines Daoris takes over (the flip, the background check, the update step, the subagents), so each takeover deletes files.
3. **Moves are mechanical.** A mover moves named declarations with asserted counts, so no builder retypes moved code. Each move is one
   commit and its comments, cut to the why, a second.
4. **The rehearsals stay harnesses,** each a runner over phase modules with an explicit world, a phase's facts returned by name. The
   proof of a move is the transcript's check labels, unchanged.
5. **Run folders are kept by age and size.** One unit makes and removes them, through the prune. It keeps a folder a live run holds,
   one a standing verdict may read, the last green run's and stage's, and the newest three of a kind. Of the rest, it removes what is
   older than seven days, then the oldest while the kinds exceed 2 GB. An unknown size keeps, each removal is said, and only folders
   the tool marked are touched.
6. **QUEUEHANG1.** A step's end is read as ended, passed, failed, died or lost. A lost gate gives no verdict, files nothing and is
   never judged or bisected. A died gate runs once more alone. The queue's runner and the watch beat, and `--queue` names the live
   runner or says it lost, with its log's last lines. The queue's runner starts outside its submitter's tree.
7. **The duplicates this design finds.** The full set on the checkout and the publisher's path from the checkout to an install retire,
   since the gate tree is the one way to gate and to stage. `--full --beside` folds into `--ci --night`. The publisher imports the gate
   tree's names rather than spelling them again.

**Why.** Landing is what Daoris does, and its integration is built, twinned with the tool's and waiting on the flip. Reworking the
queue would pay for code whose deletion is scheduled, and keep two integrators longer. What stays is used all day and is the CI
every verification runs through. Cut along its future owners, each later move deletes a module rather than lines in a 525 KB hub. A
mover removes the cost and the risk of retyping 1.5 MB of code. Run folders were kept forever until 204 of them, 3.5 GB, were removed
by hand. Age and size together bound both a busy day and a quiet week, and the never-sets keep what a run or a stage still reads. On
2026-11-01 a `timeout` ended the runner and every gate under it. Each death was judged as the change's failure, and `--queue` said
*running* for 80 minutes with no runner.

**Rejected.**
- **Refactoring the queue like the rest:** it retires at the flip.
- **The flip done inside MERGETOOL1:** INTEGRATE1k has its own fence, the owner's sitting and the steward's set-up.
- **The development tool rewritten into the product:** the runner reads this repository's layout, which the product must not carry.
- **A split by verb:** the modes share the plan, the store and the gates' run.
- **One file with a better index:** the ratchet already stops it growing, and every reader pays for its size.
- **The rehearsals as many small gates:** their phases share hosts and state built in order.
- **Run folders by count, by age or by size alone:** each misses either a disk filled or evidence lost.
- **Each writer pruning its own folders, or removing one at its run's end:** seven writers would carry seven rules, and a failed run's
  logs are read the next day.
- **Daoris's home keep removing them:** it never walks a repository's checkout.
- **A gate's timeout, every nonzero end read as failed, a watchdog process, or a scheduled task hosting the runner:** a running holder
  is never timed out; an outside kill read as a failure is the defect; a watchdog is one more process to leak; a scheduled task is a
  registration left on the owner's machine.
