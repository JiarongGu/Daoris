## D200 — A working session has no time limit: Daoris's watcher looks in on every running session at each look, from what Daoris already holds, and supports one that has gone quiet by steps (a word, the person with an end and a carry-on one press away, and an automatic end only where the record shows such quiet is not work), never a silent end and never at a cost that grows with the sessions it watches (2026-10-23)

**Decision (SESSWATCH1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, round 3, written by Claude Opus at `360b27bc6` and revised after Codex's read and
Fable's foundation read (D191; the notes below). The steward accepts after reading this round. The contract is
`docs/2026-10-23-session-watch-design.md`.

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-22: a working session never has a time limit, and a watcher supports one that gets stuck, light on
resources, read as a manager's cycle of support in D193's company rather than a kill switch. The same day three driven sessions doing real
work were killed mid-work by `timeoutMinutes` (90 on the install, 30 in the code) and paid to read their context again: one about 40 seconds
after its last tool call, one while a test run it had started in the background still ran. Read at `360b27bc6`, `Driver.WaitAsync` ends every
driven run, intake and pass by the wall clock, measured per run and never reset by a turn, and is also what lets go of a capture a
descendant holds open (design §0.3).

1. **No wall clock on a working session.** A driven session, an answered park's resumed run, a native go-on and an intake are never ended for
   running long. `timeoutMinutes` leaves the record and stays in `driver.json` as stored, for the builds before this one; nothing reads it for a
   bound.
2. **Working, waiting or quiet.** A running session is working while it shows a sign of work: heard (the door read a line or a frame from its
   harness, stderr and a subagent's included, its time stamped in memory by each capture's own parameter), busy (its job's CPU between the two
   newest reads, a minute apart at least, above an idle floor first set at 0.2 seconds a minute, read only once it has gone unheard for half
   its bound, and unknown where the job's accounting cannot be trusted), or, waiting on its own background work, that work moving. It is
   waiting while a call is held for the person's answer or waits for the heavy-run slot. Otherwise, past its state's bound, it is quiet. The
   signs can show work but never prove its absence: a model's first token, a call to a server and a subagent can all be silent and idle.
3. **The bounds are silences, never a run's length:** quiet at 10 minutes (the person's `quietMinutes`, from 5 to 120), counted on a clock
   that stops while the machine sleeps; unheard at three times that whatever the CPU; background work silent for three times that. First
   values, read again on the install (SESSWATCH1d).
4. **The support steps, each once per episode and each said** (a coded note, a console line, `session.quiet` in the machine log with how the
   episode ended):
   - **A word** at the quiet bound, to a quest session: Daoris's own, urgent, under a key kind of its own, kept on the record's `said`
     through the session's dispatcher. It reaches the agent at its next step where the door takes words then, at once as a turn of its own
     where no turn runs, and otherwise waits. A word not yet on the wire is withdrawn when its episode ends and before any conclusion, never
     counts toward a go-on, is settled at the come-up, and resets no answers-alone count.
   - **The person**, at twice that bound again, and at three times the bound unheard whatever the CPU: a `quiet` row in *What needs you*. A
     quiet session's row offers *End and carry on*, *Hand over…*, *Stop* and *Leave it running*; a busy one's, *busy, nothing heard*, offers no
     end. *End and carry on* is the person's stop released at once (D58, D126 §3.4): no strike, the line carried on in a new part handed the
     brief, and the person's waiting words handed to that part as theirs. *Hand over…* is built on the page for a quest session. Daoris ends
     nothing more. The row also comes when a part the watcher carried on is quiet before it was ever heard, when the person's words wait on
     their way, and for a heavy-slot wait past three times the bound.
   - **An end and a carry-on** automatically only in a door and state that SESSWATCH1d's record of quiet episodes shows was never work, listed
     in a note of this decision, or in a headless run given `--quiet-end`; never where busy is unknown, the person's words wait, or the door's
     quiet is unmeasured. The end is the watcher's own cause through the run's own wait, read by every conclusion path, its job terminated so a
     held capture drains: `failed` with `ended.quiet`, counted as a strike, and the next look carries the line on as D80 carries a timeout's,
     in a new part handed the brief (D189). An approved hand-over waiting for the turn's end is taken at it instead, with no strike, its
     descendants ended with the job. Never another agent of Daoris's choosing.
5. **A capture held open is bounded, not ended as a cut-off.** A run whose root has exited is its turn's end: after 60 seconds with its
   capture still held by a stray, the job is terminated, the capture abandoned, and the run concludes by its exit and what it said, a strike
   only where that exit fails.
6. **Light by construction.** The watcher rides the look that already runs. For a session heard within half its bound it reads one
   in-memory time: no system call, HTTP call or file. A session unheard longer costs one job read a look (13 µs) and, where its repository
   runs heavy gates, the slot's records; an episode costs one kept word and a note a step. It starts no process and asks no model. Measured:
   about 3 ns a look for each recently heard session, against about 0.12 ms a session the look already spends.
7. **Watched, and not:** quest sessions get every step; an intake gets the mark, the row with *Stop* and *Leave it*, and the capture's bound,
   since it takes no words and is never carried on. A person's conversation is only said to be quiet. A helper's, a reviewer's, a partner's, a
   brainstorm's and a meeting's pass keeps its own minutes, no longer capped by `timeoutMinutes`, until SESSWATCH1f asks each. The headless
   host's row is its console line and its operator's Ctrl-C its stop.
8. **The cap and spending.** A hung session keeps its slot and tree until a press: with the cap at two, two hung sessions stop all driven
   work until the person acts, which is why the row counts on the bell. An end frees the slot, and the carry-on starts behind the cap like any
   start. No spending budget replaces the wall clock: the account's allowance, the strikes where set, one end per episode and the cap bound a
   stuck line, and any later budget holds a line's next start for the person, never a turn. A line heard while it circles is not bounded by
   the watcher.
9. **What replaces `timeoutMinutes`:** `quietMinutes`, set by `daoris driver quiet <minutes>`, Ask Daoris's `quiet` door and `settings_quiet`,
   and a field on Settings → Driver. The old verb, door and tool change nothing and say why. An install that set the timeout (90) keeps the
   value for an older build and starts at the default quiet bound: a run's length is never mapped onto a silence.

**Why.** A wall clock cannot tell work from a stall, so it ended the sessions that were working longest, which cost most to start again.
What Daoris already holds tells much of it: the door reads everything the harness sends, and every driven session's harness runs in a job
whose accounting carries the CPU of all it started. What it cannot tell, remote work that is silent and idle, and work that is busy but
says nothing, it leaves to the person until the install's own record of quiet episodes shows where such quiet is never work. A manager does
not send staff home at a set hour; they look in, offer a word, bring the owner in with the fix ready, and restart work themselves only where
they have learned a stall from a pause. Each step reuses a door that exists: Daoris's urgent word (`LongCalls`, COWORK1b), the timeout's own
end and D80's carry-on with D189's brief, the person's stop and *Try again*, the strikes, and a row of *What needs you*.

**Rejected.** A longer timeout, or one per repository (D80's reason: it still ends a working session); ending on silence and low CPU alone
(remote work is both); a model judging health (D24, D54, D193); a process or a heartbeat per session; the keeping stop as Daoris's end (it
can cancel the person's words on their way, the native run has none, and D188 keeps it the person's press); ending through the processes'
registry (it concludes `stood-down` or `stopped`); resuming the quiet conversation at the end (its strike would go with the reopened record,
and a line that hangs the same way could loop); ending a run whose root exited as a cut-off; busy as a rate from one baseline; leaving busy
silence unbounded; counting the person's press as a strike; a hand-over Daoris chooses (D163 point 3); a second end without the person;
reserving a carried-on line's slot; a spending budget now; a machine-wide process query or a file read per session per look, the heavy
slot's records for a silent session aside; stamping heard from recorded events alone; resetting the count when looks are far apart;
mapping 90 onto the quiet bound. The design's §9 says why each.
