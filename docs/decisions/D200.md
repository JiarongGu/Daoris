## D200 — A working session has no time limit: Daoris's watcher looks in on every running session at each look, from what Daoris already holds, and supports one that has gone quiet by steps (a word, the person with an end and a carry-on one press away, and an automatic end only where the record shows such quiet is not work), never a silent end and never at a cost that grows with the sessions it watches (2026-10-23)

**Decision (SESSWATCH1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, round 2, written by Claude Opus at `360b27bc6` and revised after Codex's read
(D191; the note below). Fable's read is owed, since the decision amends rules other designs rest on (D80's carry-on trigger, D188 points 1
and 2, D193 part 9). The contract is `docs/2026-10-23-session-watch-design.md`.

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-22: a working session never has a time limit, and a watcher supports one that gets stuck, light on
resources, read as a manager's cycle of support in D193's company rather than a kill switch. The same day three driven sessions doing real
work were killed mid-work by `timeoutMinutes` (90 on the install, 30 in the code) and paid to read their context again: one about 40 seconds
after its last tool call, one while a test run it had started in the background still ran. Read at `360b27bc6`, `Driver.WaitAsync` ends every
driven run, intake and pass by the wall clock, measured per run and never reset by a turn, and is also what lets go of a capture a
descendant holds open (design §0.3).

1. **No wall clock on a working session.** A driven session, an answered park's resumed run, a native go-on and an intake are never ended for
   running long. `timeoutMinutes` leaves the record and stays in `driver.json` as stored, for the builds before this one; nothing reads it for a
   bound.
2. **Working, waiting or quiet.** A running session is working while it shows a sign of work: heard (the door read a line or a frame from its
   harness, stderr and a subagent's included, its time stamped in memory), busy (its job used more than an idle floor of CPU, first set at 0.2
   seconds a minute, read only once it has gone unheard for half its bound, and unknown where the job's accounting cannot be trusted), or,
   waiting on its own background work, that work moving. It is waiting while a call is held for the person's answer or waits for the
   heavy-run slot. Otherwise, past its state's bound, it is quiet. The signs can show work but never prove its absence: a model's first token,
   a call to a server and a subagent can all be silent and idle.
3. **The bounds are a silence, never a run's length:** quiet at 10 minutes (the person's `quietMinutes`, from 5 to 120), counted on a clock
   that stops while the machine sleeps; background work silent for three times that; a process gone without an end at 60 seconds. First
   values, read again on the install (SESSWATCH1d).
4. **The support steps, each once per quiet episode and each said** (a coded note, a console line, `session.quiet` in the machine log with
   how the episode ended):
   - **A word** at the quiet bound, to a quest session: Daoris's own, urgent, under a key kind of its own, kept on the record's `said`
     through the session's dispatcher. It reaches the agent at its next step where the door takes words then, at once as a turn of its own
     where no turn runs, and otherwise waits; it is withdrawn when its episode ends and before any conclusion, never counts toward a go-on, and
     is settled at the come-up, so it can never turn a carry-on into a resume.
   - **The person**, at twice that bound again: a `quiet` row in *What needs you* with *End and carry on*, *Hand over…*, *Stop* and *Leave it
     running*, as each applies. Daoris ends nothing more. The row also comes when a part the watcher carried on is quiet before it was ever
     heard, when the person's words wait on their way to a quiet session, and for a heavy-slot wait past three times the bound.
   - **An end and a carry-on**, by the person's press, and automatically only in a door and state that SESSWATCH1d's record of quiet episodes
     shows was never work, listed in a note of this decision; never where busy is unknown, the person's words wait, or the door's quiet is
     unmeasured. A process gone without an end is ended from the first row. The end is the watcher's own cause through the run's own wait,
     read by every conclusion path, its job terminated so a held capture drains: `failed` with `ended.quiet`, counted as a strike, and the
     next look carries the line on as D80 carries a timeout's, in a new part handed the brief (D189). An approved hand-over waiting for the
     turn's end is taken at it instead, with no strike. Never another agent of Daoris's choosing.
5. **Light by construction.** The watcher rides the look that already runs. For a session heard within half its bound it reads one
   in-memory time: no system call, HTTP call or file. A session unheard longer costs one job read a look (13 µs) and, where its repository
   runs heavy gates, the slot's records, so the reads grow with silent sessions, not with the sessions watched. It starts no process and asks
   no model. Measured: about 3 ns a look for each recently heard session, against about 0.12 ms a session the look already spends.
6. **Watched, and not:** quest sessions get every step; an intake gets the mark, the row with *Stop* and *Leave it*, and the gone process's
   end, since it takes no words and is never carried on. A person's conversation is only said to be quiet. A helper's, a reviewer's, a
   partner's, a brainstorm's and a meeting's pass keeps its own minutes, no longer capped by `timeoutMinutes`, until SESSWATCH1f asks each.
7. **The cap and spending.** A quiet session keeps its slot while its run lasts; an end frees it, as an ended record holds none, and the
   carry-on starts behind the cap like any start. No spending budget replaces the wall clock: the account's allowance, the strikes where set,
   one end per episode and the cap bound a stuck line, and any later budget holds a line's next start for the person, never a turn. A heard
   but circling line is not bounded by the watcher.
8. **What replaces `timeoutMinutes`:** `quietMinutes`, set by `daoris driver quiet <minutes>`, Ask Daoris's `quiet` door and `settings_quiet`,
   and a field on Settings → Driver. The old verb, door and tool change nothing and say why. An install that set the timeout (90) keeps the
   value for an older build and starts at the default quiet bound: a run's length is never mapped onto a silence.

**Why.** A wall clock cannot tell work from a stall, so it ended the sessions that were working longest, which cost most to start again.
What Daoris already holds tells much of it: the door reads everything the harness sends, and every driven session's harness runs in a job
whose accounting carries the CPU of all it started. What it cannot tell, remote work that is silent and idle, it leaves to the person until
the install's own record of quiet episodes shows where such quiet is never work. A manager does not send staff home at a set hour; they look
in, offer a word, bring the owner in with the fix ready, and restart work themselves only where they have learned a stall from a pause. Each
step reuses a door that exists: Daoris's urgent word (`LongCalls`, COWORK1b), the timeout's own end and D80's carry-on with D189's brief,
the strikes, and a row of *What needs you*.

**Rejected.** A longer timeout, or one per repository (D80's reason: it still ends a working session); ending on silence and low CPU alone
(remote work is both); a model judging health (D24, D54, D193); a process or a heartbeat per session; the keeping stop as Daoris's end (it
can cancel the person's words on their way, the native run has none, and D188 keeps it the person's press); ending through the processes'
registry (it concludes `stood-down` or `stopped`); resuming the quiet conversation at the end (its strike would go with the reopened record,
and a line that hangs the same way could loop); a hand-over Daoris chooses (D163 point 3); a second end without the person; reserving a
carried-on line's slot; a spending budget now; a machine-wide process query or a file read per session per look; stamping heard from
recorded events alone; resetting the count when looks are far apart; mapping 90 onto the quiet bound. The design's §9 says why each.

**Amends,** once accepted: **D80** (a cut-off's *timed out* is the watcher's end, of a process gone, at the person's press or where the
record allows, counted as a strike; the terminal timeout is retired; the carry-on itself is unchanged); **D188** point 1 (*timeouts* among
Daoris's stops becomes the watcher's end) and point 2 (Daoris's urgent word gains the watcher's one word to a quiet quest session); **D193**
part 9 (as D188 points 1 and 2); **D83**'s BGWAIT notes (a background wait is bounded by the watcher, not the timeout); **D131** point 5's
wording; **D163** (an approved hand-over is taken at the watcher's end of a hung turn); **D110**'s `timeout` door and **D183**'s
`settings_timeout` grant, retired for `quiet`; **D155**, **D163** and **D199** (their passes' minutes are no longer capped). D184's held
permission bound keeps its 15 minutes on a reason of its own. Each carries a note pointing here. D46 §4, D51, D104, D105 §3, D124, D131
points 1-4, D132, D136, D137, D165, D167, D189, D24 and D130's CAPCOUNT1 stand.

**The rows** are the design's §8: SESSWATCH1a (no wall clock; the watcher's look, the mark, the person's row with its presses, the gone
process's end; first, so the kill goes at once and nothing quiet is ended by Daoris), 1b (the word), 1c (the quiet setting in every door),
1d (measured on the install, the parent's with the owner: which cells allow an automatic end), 1e (the automatic end where 1d allows), 1f
(the passes' minutes, asked).

**What the checks do not cover.** Documents only; nothing is built. The statements about today were read from the code at `360b27bc6`, not
run. The look and the watcher's primitives were timed once on one machine with scratch programs, the look over the driver tests' in-process
stand-in ledger, with no HTTP; the idle floor on node stand-ins, not the real harnesses. codex-acp's frames, dsh's long steps, the real
harnesses' idle CPU and the longest quiet of a working turn are unmeasured. `verify` checks this entry's place and shape and the design's
links, and none of their words.

*2026-10-23, Codex's read (round 1, D191):* fourteen findings, ten blocking, each checked by the steward at the lines it cites; all held and
were taken, and the design's §10 keeps the table. The read's central finding moved the decision: no line and no CPU cannot show that work
stopped, since a model's first token, a call to a server, a subagent and dsh's silent step are all quiet and idle, so an automatic end now
waits for the install's record of quiet episodes and is enabled per door and state in a note here; until then the person decides, with *End
and carry on* one press away, and the measuring row comes before the ending row. The rest: heard is stamped at the door's read of every line

has a key kind, is withdrawn before any conclusion, never counts toward a go-on and is settled at the come-up; an end frees the slot and the
