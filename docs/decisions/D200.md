## D200 — A working session has no time limit: Daoris's watcher looks in on every running session at each look, from what Daoris already holds, and supports one that has gone quiet by steps (a word, an end and a carry-on, then the person), never a silent end and never at a cost that grows with the sessions it watches (2026-10-23)

**Decision (SESSWATCH1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, round 1, written by Claude Opus at `360b27bc6`; Codex's read is owed (D191),
and Fable's, since the decision amends rules other designs rest on (D80's carry-on trigger, D188 point 1's stops, D193 part 9). The
contract is `docs/2026-10-23-session-watch-design.md`.

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-22: a working session never has a time limit, and a watcher supports one that gets stuck, light on
resources, read as a manager's cycle of support in D193's company rather than a kill switch. The same day three driven sessions doing real
work were killed mid-work by `timeoutMinutes` (90 on the install, 30 in the code) and paid to read their context again: one about 40 seconds
after its last tool call, one while a test run it had started in the background still ran. Read at `360b27bc6`, `Driver.WaitAsync` ends every
driven run, intake and pass by the wall clock, measured per run and never reset by a turn (design §0.3).

1. **No wall clock on a working session.** A driven session, an answered park's resumed run, a native go-on and an intake are never ended for
   running long. `timeoutMinutes` is kept in `driver.json` as written for the builds before this one, and never read for a bound.
2. **Working, waiting or quiet.** A running session is working while it shows a sign of work: heard (an event its door recorded from its
   harness, stamped in memory as it is appended), busy (its job used more than an idle floor of CPU, first set at 0.2 seconds a minute, read only once it has gone
   unheard for half its bound), or, waiting on its own
   background work, that work moving. It is waiting while a wait its record names holds it (a call held for the person, the heavy-run slot behind
   another session, the person's words on their way, a hand-over at its turn's end). Otherwise, past its state's bound, it is quiet. The
   states and what each door says in them are the design's §1.4; silence is never read as success nor as failure.
3. **The bounds are a silence, never a run's length:** quiet at 10 minutes (the person's `quietMinutes`, from 5 to 120), its background work
   still at three times that, a process gone without an end at 60 seconds; a look more than two minutes after the one before starts the count
   over. First values, measured again on the install (SESSWATCH1d).
4. **The support steps, each once per quiet episode and each said** (a coded note, a console line, `session.quiet` in the machine log):
   - **A word** at the quiet bound: Daoris's own, urgent, kept on the record's `said` through the session's dispatcher; it reaches the agent at
     its next step where the door takes words then, at once as a turn of its own where no turn runs, and otherwise waits and is withdrawn when
     the session is heard again.
   - **An end and a carry-on** at twice that bound again, quiet and never busy since, or at once for a process gone: the run is ended as the
     timeout ended it, `failed` with `ended.quiet`, counted as a strike, and the next look carries the line on as D80 carries a timeout's, in
     a new part in the same tree handed the brief (D189), its instruction naming the quiet. Never another agent; the third end parks the quest.
   - **The person**, when the carried-on part is quiet before it was ever heard, or cannot start: a `quiet` row in *What needs you*, with
     *Hand over…*, *Stop* and *Leave it running*. Daoris ends nothing more.
5. **Light by construction.** The watcher rides the look that already runs and reads what Daoris already holds: for a healthy session an
   in-memory heard time and nothing else, no system call, HTTP call or file; for a quiet one, its job's accounting (13 µs) and, where its
   repository runs heavy gates, the slot's records. It starts no process and asks no model. Measured: about 3 ns a look for each healthy
   session, against about 0.12 ms a session the look already spends.
6. **Watched, and not:** driven sessions and intakes. A person's conversation is only said to be quiet; a helper's, a reviewer's, a partner's,
   a brainstorm's and a meeting's pass keeps its own minutes, no longer capped by `timeoutMinutes`, until SESSWATCH1e asks each decision.
7. **The cap and spending.** A quiet session keeps its slot through every step; the cap still counts records. No spending budget replaces the
   wall clock: the account's allowance, the strikes, the one end per episode and the cap bound a stuck line, and any later budget holds a
   line's next start for the person, never a turn.
8. **What replaces `timeoutMinutes`:** `quietMinutes`, set by `daoris driver quiet <minutes>`, Ask Daoris's `quiet` door and `settings_quiet`,
   and a field on Settings → Driver. The old verb, door and tool change nothing and say why. An install that set the timeout (90) keeps the
   value for an older build and starts at the default quiet bound: a run's length is never mapped onto a silence.

**Why.** A wall clock cannot tell work from a stall, so it ended the sessions that were working longest, which cost most to start again. What
does tell them apart is already held: the door records the harness's events as they come, and every driven session's harness runs in a job
whose accounting carries the CPU of everything it started. A manager does not send staff home at a set hour; they look in, offer a word,
restart the work where it stalls, and bring the owner in only when that did not help. Each step reuses a door that exists: Daoris's urgent
word (`LongCalls`, COWORK1b), the timeout's own end, D80's carry-on with D189's brief, the strikes, and a row of *What needs you*.

**Rejected.** A longer timeout, or one per repository (D80's reason: it still ends a working session); an idle timeout on events alone (a
foreground command, a silent model step and a background test run are quiet and working); a model judging health (D24, D54, D193); a
process or a heartbeat per session; the keeping stop as Daoris's step (it can cancel the person's words on their way, the native run has
none, and D188 keeps it the person's press); resuming the quiet conversation at the end (its strike would go with the reopened record, and a
line that hangs the same way could loop); a hand-over by Daoris on its own (D163 point 3); a second end without the person; a spending
budget now; a machine-wide process query or a file read per session per look (20 to 45 ms, and I/O per session); mapping 90 onto the quiet
bound. The design's §9 says why each.

**Amends,** once accepted: **D80** (a cut-off's *timed out* is the watcher's end of a quiet run, counted as a strike; the terminal timeout
is retired; the carry-on itself is unchanged); **D188** point 1 (*timeouts* among Daoris's stops
becomes the watcher's end) and point 2 (Daoris's urgent word gains the watcher's one word to a quiet session); **D193** part 9 (as D188
point 1); **D83**'s BGWAIT notes (a background wait is bounded by the watcher, not the timeout); **D131** point 5's wording; **D110**'s
`timeout` door and **D183**'s `settings_timeout` grant, retired for `quiet`; **D155**, **D163** and **D199** (their passes' minutes are no
longer capped). D184's held permission bound keeps its 15 minutes on a reason of its own. Each carries a note pointing here. D46 §4, D51,
D104, D105 §3, D124, D131 points 1-4, D132, D136, D137, D165, D167, D189, D24 and D130's CAPCOUNT1 stand.

**The rows** are the design's §8: SESSWATCH1a (no wall clock; the watcher's look, the quiet mark and the person's row; first, so the kill
goes at once and a stuck session still reaches someone), 1b (the word and the carry-on), 1c (the quiet setting in every door), 1d (measured
on the install, the parent's), 1e (the passes' minutes, asked).

**What the checks do not cover.** Documents only; nothing is built. The statements about today were read from the code at `360b27bc6`, not
run. The look and the watcher's primitives were timed once on one machine with scratch programs, the look over the driver tests' in-process
stand-in ledger, with no HTTP; the idle floor on node stand-ins, not the real harnesses. codex-acp's frames, the real harnesses' idle CPU and
the longest quiet of a working turn are unmeasured. `verify` checks this entry's place and shape and the design's links, and none of their words.
