## D186 — Processes are drawn as flowcharts: one auto-laid diagram, laid out in layers by Daoris's own code with no library, drawing each process from its record, with the rail kept as its narrow placement; the Map stays the map of requests (2026-10-20)

**Decision (FLOWCHART1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, pending a second maker's read and the owner's review. No row
builds before the owner's yes.

The owner's report of 2026-10-20 (TASKS' priority list, under UIDESIGN1) asks for Daoris's processes to be drawn with
flowchart logic. It judges the Map a map of requests, not a process view, and its UI/UX poor. Read at `02632075f`, no
process is drawn as a flow:
- WORKFLOW1 asked for a flowchart, and the workflow design drew a vertical rail instead and rejected a free canvas (its
  §6.2 and §12.1);
- the Map draws repositories and requests;
- the chain strip, the parts strip and the commit graph are lists and lanes.

Since D157, the fabric's part E has made every workflow a graph, with forks, joins and failure paths. Its §E8 asks for an
editor laid out automatically and a running view of each node and wire, and neither drawing was designed. Every process
is already kept in a record: a quest's operation log, a session's ledger, a line of work's parts, a landing's tries, a
unit's milestone, a node's lifecycle. The contract is `docs/2026-10-20-flowchart-design.md`.

1. **One primitive draws every flow**: `design/Flow.tsx`, with a pure layout, `design/flowLayout.ts`. It is a molecule:
   the nodes and edges arrive worded, and the doors go out. It imports no data hook.
2. **Auto-laid in layers, never by hand.**
   - The layout finds the back edges by a depth-first walk from the entries, ranks by longest path, gives a waypoint to
     each edge that skips a rank, and orders each rank with four barycentre sweeps. These are the steps the Map's layers
     and the code map already take.
   - It is deterministic, and it reads only the graph's shape, so a change of state moves no node.
   - It has no stored positions, no panning, no zoom and no dragging.
3. **Placed by the pane's width.** The same nodes, in one reading order, are placed left to right where every rank fits
   side by side, top to bottom where the widest rank fits, and as **the rail** otherwise. The rail is a list whose edges
   are words, and it is the placement wherever nothing measured the pane. At the side bar's 300 px floor a fork is the
   rail. A 680 px window's main area holds a rank of three. Text is never shrunk to fit.
4. **Three kinds of flow, each drawn from its record, never inferred from a conversation:**
   - **a state machine**: the code's table, the current state lit, and the path the record kept;
   - **a process**: a version's graph, each node lit with its own state;
   - **an instance**: the nodes the records made.
   Where a record keeps no path, none is drawn, and the drawing says so.
5. **The flows and where they are mounted:**
   - **a quest's life** (state machine with its path), on the quest's page. It needs one new read, a quest's moves, on both
     hosts and in the trace (FLOWCHART1a2).
   - **a session's states** (state machine; its record keeps no path), at the head of the side bar's *Timeline* view.
   - **a line of work's parts** (instance), in the parts strip's place.
   - **a workflow** (process), on the *Workflow* tab.
   - **its run and the fabric's running view** (one process view, lit), in the side bar's *Workflow* view.
   - **a node's lifecycle** (state machine with its attempts), unfolded under its node.
   - **landing and clean-up** (instance, from the trace's landing and the unit's milestone), on a landed branch's page.
6. **A node** shows its state's word and tone from the existing tables, who acts, when, and a foot naming each edge out.
   **An edge** is ink, never a status hue: taken, possible, failed, rejected or refused, with an optional label where it
   fits. Its whole cause is always in the source's foot and the reader's description.
7. **Loops and size.** A state machine's back edge runs on an outer lane, marked ↺ and counted. An instance's retry is a
   new node, and like nodes stack into a fold. Past 24 nodes, settled stretches fold, then the flow is focused on the
   current node, and *Show whole* opens the folds.
8. **Keyboard and reader.** The nodes are one ordered list and the edges one `aria-hidden` drawing. The flow is one Tab
   stop: ↓ and ↑ move in reading order, → and ← follow an edge, and Enter opens a node.
9. **Laid out by hand, with no library.** A flow is bounded (24 authored nodes, 96 decoded), and the nodes are HTML that
   wrap in both languages, so their size is known only once they render. elkjs is rejected. @dagrejs/dagre (MIT, about
   17 KB gzipped) is the named exit if FLOWCHART1a1 cannot hold its crossing table.
10. **What is drawn today.**
    - The workflow rail is kept, as the narrow placement of the same primitive.
    - `WorkflowChart` and `PartsStrip` move onto `Flow`.
    - The chain strip and the commit graph stay as they are.
    - **The Map stays the workspace's view of repositories and requests**, draws no process, and has a design row of its
      own (MAPUX1).

**Rejected.**
- **A free canvas**: stored positions, panning, zoom and dragging. D157's rejection stands.
- **elkjs.** Measured 2026-10-20, its bundled build is 1,609,707 bytes (466,978 gzipped), about half again a page of
  3,209,506 (904,638). It is EPL-2.0 or GPL-3.0-or-later, and it lays out asynchronously with every node sized up front.
- **@dagrejs/dagre now.** It needs every node sized before layout, and answers coordinates the page would set aside. It
  stays the named exit, not the choice.
- **Mermaid or another text-to-diagram renderer.** Its text is SVG, its diagrams are not the page's controls, and it lays
  out with one of the two above.
- **A canvas drawing, or SVG text in a node.** No reader or keyboard reaches a canvas, and SVG text neither wraps nor
  measures Chinese.
- **A drawing per flow** instead of one primitive.
- **A process drawn on the Map.**
- **A second running view** beside the side bar's *Workflow* view.
- **A path inferred from a conversation**, or a state the record does not keep.
- **A code's transition table copied to the page** with no test holding it to the code.
- **Animated moves.**

**This amends:**
- **D157 point 12** and the workflow design **§6.2, §6.4 and §7**. The workflow is a layered flowchart laid out by Daoris,
  with the list as its narrow placement, and the keys gain → and ←.
- **§12.1's reason**: a list answers a sequence, and a graph's forks must be drawn. The free canvas stays rejected.
- **D173's part E, the fabric design's §E8.** The running view is drawn on this primitive, as one view with D157 §7's. The
  editor is laid out by this layout, and a node's lifecycle unfolds under its node.
- **D167's parts strip** (the co-working design §1.6), which becomes the parts flow's rail.
- **platform-ux §4**, which gains the flow component.

**Held by:** FLOWCHART1a1-a2, 1b-1d and MAPUX1, filed as the design's §10 proposes. **The rows follow D187**: each is

*2026-10-20, DESIGNREAD1:* a second maker read the design before the owner's review, and each finding was checked against
the code at `872520fa5` before the design changed; the design says each change where it applies. **What the read changed:**
- **The layout** (point 2) is a crossing heuristic: its table holds a ceiling per fixture, never a minimum. *A state
  change moves no node* holds for an unfolded flow, and a fold opening or closing with the state is its one exception.
  The promise that a growing instance only appends is withdrawn: a graph that grows is laid out again and its nodes may
  move, so the focus is kept by node id.
- **Routing**: a return leaves and enters through an escape slot at its rank's outer end and runs on a lane of its own,
  never shared, every lane counted in the width; a reciprocal pair is two paths; parallel edges of one style are one path
  marked ×n with each cause in words, and of different styles separate paths.
- **The proof and the engine** (point 9): a measured-box collision test in a rendered browser over the hardest graphs (the
  node lifecycle's multigraph, a dense `allOf`/`anyOf` graph at the bounds, unequal boxes in both languages, an append,
  several current nodes and none), run for the hand layout and for @dagrejs/dagre, and the engine is locked on that table
  in FLOWCHART1a1 before any later row. Measuring is no reason against a library: elkjs stays rejected on its size and
  licence, and dagre is evaluated whole on the same fixtures rather than named as an exit.
- **Each drawing promises only what its record holds** (point 4): a quest's moves in replay order, each saying whether it
  applied and carrying its reference, a take naming no session, who asked read from the quest, a history from before the
  log drawn as recorded, the moves' fields set by allow-list; a session's state with no entry time and going on with its
  own guards; parts as lineage, with the landing plan a prospective node; a node's counters and retained entries, with no
  path; a landing read by its branch with its advances and units (FLOWCHART1c gains the driver lane), its push and its
  pull request's answer as snapshots; a workflow's steps in order until the decoded graph, its run with no path and each
  skip by whom the record names.
- **Widths** (point 3): the placement is chosen by the measured inner width against the tracks, waypoints, gaps and return
  lanes it needs. A 680 px window's main area holds a rank of three only with nothing else in it; left to right holds six
  ranks at 1,200 px. *Show as list* is offered at every width and remembered.
- **Keyboard and reader** (point 8): nodes take the focus by a roving `tabindex`, a reader hears the keys once, each foot
  line is a door to its target so every branch is reached directly, Escape returns from a node's controls, and the focus
  is kept by node id; each row's window proof includes a screen reader's pass.
- **Folding** (point 7) centres on every current node, or on none, and a fold keeps every edge across its boundary with its
  cause. Each row in §10 names its trap.

**What it did not change.** No finding was rejected whole. Two held in part. Parallel edges are not each drawn apart
where they share a style: a cause lives in words, never in the ink (point 6), so two like inks side by side add width and
no meaning. A quest's close note and answers are not added to the moves: they are the quest's own, already answered by
its record. The hand layout stays the first choice until the fixtures' table says otherwise, since one CSS pass reflows by
itself on a resize or a change of language; no positions are kept between layouts (D157's rejection stands); the rail
stays the narrow placement, and the Map stays the map of requests.

*2026-10-20, accepted:* the owner reviewed the design as revised by another maker's read and accepted it as it stands; its rows are built under D187, FLOWCHART1a1 first.

Chromium, measures every node's and label's box and samples every edge by the browser's own geometry. The same frames were
then laid out by @dagrejs/dagre 3.1.1 whole (its ranks, order, coordinates and edge points over the same measured node

- **Why by hand.** It holds every fixture with no edge through a box and no label over one, inside the width the
  placement chose, in one CSS pass that reflows by itself. dagre crosses less on the lifecycle and about as much on the
  dense graph, but its straight segments ran through nodes and labels it had placed, it added a crossing to the small
  flows, and its coordinates came out wider than the pane, so the placement's budgets would be its to keep as well. Its
  one gain, every label shown, comes of widening the drawing for them; the hand layout drops a label that does not clear,
  which loses nothing, since the foot says the whole cause. No dependency was added.
- **The table is the check's.** Each drawn frame's ceiling is what the hand layout drew that day (`measured/flow.spec.ts`;
  the slots' half in `flowLayout.test.ts`: lifecycle 5, dense 155). The check is not yet a declared gate:
  `daoris.gates.json` is the tools lane's.
- **Built as the design reads, and where it is read closer:**
  - A return leaves its source's far side into the gap after its rank, runs along that gap to a lane of its own, along
    the lane, and back along the gap before its target's rank. The escape slot is the outer end of those two gaps, each
    run set apart in its gap by its lane, rather than a track in the rank, which would widen the rank and lead nowhere a
    gap does not.
  - *Show as list* is offered wherever the drawing fits; where only the rail fits, the list is what is shown.
  - A quest's flow lights its status in `QUEST_TONE`, as its own pill does, so an open quest's Open wears open's hue;
    §2.1's *the one place that hue appears* reads for a held Done once FLOWCHART1a2 draws holds.
  - With no moves read, a closed quest's Taken is drawn neither reached nor not: the page cannot say whether it was taken.
  - The dense graph needs 1432 px top to bottom, so it is the rail at every frame width until FLOWCHART1d's folds.
- **What the checks do not cover.** No window was looked at, and no screen reader's pass was made: both are the row's
  proof after a republish. The node lifecycle and the dense graph are fixtures, not read from the driver.

*2026-10-20, FLOWCHART1a2:* **a quest's page draws how it moved** (point 5), from a new read on both hosts,
`GET /api/quests/{id}/moves`, which `TRACE`'s quest link carries and `daoris-driver trace quest` says.
- **The allow-list** (§2.1) is Core's `QuestMoveReading`, read by `QuestMoves.Read` from `HistoryAsync` in its replay order.
  Every move answers its kind as the wire spells it, its time, machine and sequence, the remote's number (absent while
  pending), whether it applied, and the hold it set (its code) or let go (`none`). Its kind's own field is one of a
  conflict's attempted status, a dismissal's conflict by machine and sequence, a submission's session, an evidence read's
  code (`found`, `missing`, or `commit` for a commit read with nothing to find), a set-up's (`local` or `deployed`) and a
  verdict's. Two fields beyond §2.1's list were added:
  - a wait's quest: its note is a quest's id, answered only where it reads as one, so no sentence or path can pass;
  - `from`, the status each move reached the quest at, the replay's as `applied` is, so neither the page nor the terminal
    replays the table again.
  The census holds each kind's row (`QuestOperationKindsTests`), `QuestMovesTests` holds the reading's properties to the list,
  and `quest-operations.md` gains it as its row 12.
- **How "applied" is computed.** No operation keeps it. The history is folded once, as `QuestLog.Replay` folds it, and each
  operation is judged by `QuestLog.Applies` against the quest the operations before it replay to, which is the same judgement
  as replaying each prefix. A pending move is judged where it stands in that order; on a machine with no remote every move is
  pending, and the page says nothing of the number, which the terminal says.
- **Open's hue for a held Done is settled.** A Done its hold keeps waits on the person, so its node wears open's hue with the
  words its own pill says (*awaits your yes*, *awaits review*), as an open quest's Open wears it on its pill. The hold is the
  quest's own, so it is drawn with or without the moves.
- **Drawn as the record holds it.** The path's edges are drawn heavier, an edge's one new field (`taken`), which the layout never
  reads, and say so in words for the rail and a reader. A move that changed no status, or did not apply, is listed inside the
  status it reached, three before a fold. A conflict that attempted another status is also a refused edge toward it until a
  dismissal names it by its reference. One that attempted the status it happened in, as a lost take does, is listed alone,
  since an edge into its own node draws nothing. A history with no moves, an empty one or a host from before the door (its 404)
  is drawn as 1a1 drew it, and says the way it went is not kept.
- **Where the page reads it.** The page reads the door, in a browser as in the window, once the section opens. *How this came
  to be* stays the shell's and does not draw the moves again: the flowchart above it does.
- **What the checks do not cover.** The measured check gained the drawn quests (`quest/Went` in both languages, and
  `quest/Refused`, whose refused edge ranks Done after Declined so the side bar's floor draws it top to bottom), each with no
  crossing and no edge through a box. No window was looked at, no screen reader's pass was made, and the family rehearsal,
  which compares some answers whole, was not run.

*2026-10-20, FLOWCHART1b:* **a session's states and its parts are flowcharts** (point 5), the states at the head of the side
bar's *Timeline* view and the parts in the parts strip's place on the session's page.
- **The states** (§2.2) are `work/head/sessionLife.ts`, the page's copy of `SessionLedger.Allowed` and the nine states, held by a
  test that reads the service's source (the switch's arms and the enum's members, the twin's shape). Back edges on their own lanes
  were already built in FLOWCHART1a1, so this row only proved the working ⇄ awaiting-person return and going on in a rendered
  frame (`session/*` in the measured table, each with no edge through a box).
  - **Which word lights the current state.** The record's state lights its node, and the node says the word the page shows the
    session as (*idle*, *parked*, *awaiting reply*, *answered*, *going on*), in that word's `SESSION_TONE`, never a tone table of its
    own. Where the shown word is the state's own name the pill says *here now*, as the quest's flow does; the outcomes node, which
    names no outcome of its own, always says the word. The frame passes the word it already derives (the list's reader, else the
    record's own reading); an older caller with none gets the record's reading.
  - **No path, no time.** No state before the lit one is drawn reached or not, and no node says when it was entered, since `updated`
    is the last write. Only the outcomes are drawn not reached while the record is live.
  - **The outcomes are one node** whose members are words, the reached one marked by a dot and weight and `aria-current`. The
    person's stop is said once on the node (*stopped: by you, from any live state*) and also named, with who makes each move, on
    each live state's edge into the node, so a reader on any state hears that stopping is open to them without an edge drawn
    for it. A stand-down is said never to go on on the node and on the going-on edge.
  - **Going on** is the ledger's other move and is not in `Allowed`. It leaves the outcomes node as a dashed (`conditional`)
    return to working, saying both guards the page cannot see (the person's words waiting, the working tree free). Where the record
    itself rules it out, in the ledger's own order (another machine's record, a stand-down, an intake, a reviewer's pass, a
    helper), the edge is the refused style, ended in ✕, with its reason. The page draws the guards in words and never judges them.
  - **A state the page does not know** is lit as recorded, with no move drawn to or from it.
  - **Mounted** above the observed events, which stay as they were. The section folds to its line, and the choice is kept on this
    machine, since the rail is tall at the side bar's floor.
- **The parts** (§2.3) are `work/head/partsLife.ts`: each part a node oldest first, hung by `EntryPart.after` and labelled by how it
  began, in the strip's own words. An asked part hangs from a faint node for the repository that asked it, and a part with no
  `after`, or whose `after` the page does not hold, hangs from the entry's start. Parts with none between them are branches of the
  start and no edge joins them, so neither the list's order nor a fork is drawn as a cause or a concurrency. A part's agent and when
  it began are said only where the page holds its session record, since a part keeps neither; the part the page is on is lit, and a
  line says which part's record is the list's row.
  - **How the strip and the flow share the parts.** The strip's list became `PartsList`, used by both: the page mounts
    `PartsFlow`, and its `Flow` is given the list as its `rail`, so the strip is what is shown wherever only the rail fits or the
    person chose *Show as list*, as it was, and the nodes are the list wherever the drawing fits, never both. A part is shown by Enter
    on its node and by a control inside it, as the strip's button did. The strip's heading and its count are the flow's too.
  - **The prospective landing.** Once Accept's ask is open and its plan read, the plan is a dashed node, *Accept would land these on
    {target}* (or *merge these into*, for a line that merges), with a dashed edge from each part the page holds and what holds or
    leaves out that part on the edge. It is a plan, never a join: nothing it says is in the past, and what did land is the landing
    flow's (§2.7). The plan is the frame's own read, so no new request was added.
- **Shared pieces added to `Flow`** (all additive): a node may be `faint`, drawn as a node not reached is without claiming it was not
  reached, for the repository that asked; an edge may be `conditional` (dashed like the failures), for going on and the landing; a
  caller may hand the rail it draws where only the rail fits. `list()` gained an *or* for alternatives.
- **What the checks do not cover.** No window was looked at, no screen reader's pass was made, and the family rehearsal and the
  desktop suites' `Process` half were not run. The measured table's ceilings are what the hand layout drew that day: the states'
  1200 px frame crosses seven times (its three skipping edges and the two returns' runs), and the six-part fork's 400 px frame is
  the rail, since it needs a rank of three.
