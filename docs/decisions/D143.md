## D143 — A choice Daoris makes keeps the facts it was made from, and a door answers why: one rule for every feature that chooses, and a trace that reads a change back to its ask (2026-10-03)

**Decision (TRACE1).** The owner brought in an outside analysis on 2026-10-03, `docs/DAORIS_FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`. Its §66
asks that every automated action answer *why now, why this repository, this agent, this account, this permission, this
context, this dependency, and why it was considered complete*, from facts stored with the choice. The review
(`docs/2026-10-03-future-directions-review.md`) found that principle already held, but feature by feature, and
recommended it as one decision (§4). Its §3 also found that six stores keep one change's provenance and that nothing reads
them together, and it proposed TRACE1 as that read.

1. **A choice keeps its facts.** Daoris chooses things for the person: which quest starts or sits, which account a start
   takes, which line a tree grows from, which tier answered, how a session's record ends, what a session is handed. For
   each choice it keeps what it chose and the facts and reason it chose by. They are kept where the choice is recorded,
   at the moment it is made, and never as the outcome alone. A code carries them where a screen words them (LANG1a), and
   the driver's sentence where a terminal reads them.
2. **A door answers why.** A kept choice can be read at a terminal door, and on the screen wherever a screen shows the
   choice (D50). Ask Daoris answers from the same record (D110). A read changes nothing, so it has no proposal to make
   and is exempt, as `driver list` is (D110 §4).
3. **A fact not kept is said missing.** A reader says where the fact would have been and why it is not there. It never
   rebuilds the fact from what stands now: today's standing answer is not the one that stood at an earlier start. Absent
   is never zero (D57, D130 §7).
4. **The person's choices stay theirs.** Their words (D133), their go-aheads and their standing answers (D135) are kept
   with when each was given. A choice Daoris makes never stands in for one the person makes (D130).
5. **A new feature that chooses says, in its design, where its facts are kept and which doors read them**, or why it
   keeps none. Review checks this, since no gate parses a design.

**What already holds it** (the review's §4, read from the decisions):
- D24: a model-backed answer names the tier that answered.
- D46 §3: a sitting quest always says why. The planner's verdicts say it, and D126 reads them into a session's group
  without writing a state back (D126 point 2). Its `Stopped` verdict says whose stop holds a quest (D126 point 6).
- D86: a line is answered with what set it. The toolchain design's §3 says which pick won.
- D130 and TOOL6e, its note. A start's walk over the accounts is weighed by `AccountRotation.Next`, with a closed set of
  reasons (`NextReason`) and holds (`NextHold`). A start whose list offered a choice, or that rotated, opens its record with
  a line naming the step that chose its account and what each account said (`RotatedOpening.Say`, TOOL4f and TOOL6b), and
  that line is kept in the session's own record. D130 §7 reads what an account has left only from the agent's own word,
  and an account that said nothing is never empty or full. Under D130 §5, a wait asks the person.
- D133 and D135: the person's words and go-aheads are kept verbatim, with when, the session they went to, and their
  answers.

**What TRACE1 adds** is one read across those stores: `daoris-driver trace <commit|session|quest>`. It starts from a commit
(one a landing made, or one in a session's evidence), a session or a quest, and prints the chain from the ask to its
commits. Each link comes from the store that keeps it, and a link nothing keeps is said missing. Its note below says what
it reads and what it cannot reach.

**Why.** The record already keeps nearly everything §66 asks for, but each decision says so for its own feature. The next
feature could choose without keeping its *because*, and nothing would tell it otherwise. One rule makes keeping the facts
part of what a choosing feature is. Facts kept apart also answer nothing until something joins them: reading one change's
provenance took six doors, and knowing where each one was.

**Rejected.**
- **A ledger store of its own**, a copy of the six stores' facts in one place. A second copy of the record drifts from the
  first, and counts are derived, never stored (D58). The trace reads each store where it is.
- **Rebuilding a reason from what stands now**, such as today's account order or today's standing answer. That is a guess
  that reads as a fact.
- **A `because` field on every record, by schema.** The choices are of different kinds, and their facts live in different
  stores. The rule binds what is kept, and each store keeps it in its own shape.
- **The instruction's words in the trace.** They are transcript-class and stay on the machine (D76, D47 §4). The trace
  names the instruction's event and size, and the session's page shows its words.
- **§67 as a decision of its own.** D24, D54 and D37 already state its three columns (the review's §4).

**This amends** no decision. It binds every feature after it. D110's exemptions gain `trace`, a read like `driver list`.

**What the checks do not cover.** Point 5 is a review's to hold, and no test reads a design. The list of what already holds
the rule is the review's reading of the decisions, and nothing here re-checks those features against their code. TRACE1's
note says what its own tests cover.

**TRACE1, built 2026-10-03: `daoris-driver trace`.** The library reads (`Trace.cs`), prints (`Trace.Words.cs`) and takes the
terminal's words (`TraceCommand.cs`). The headless host routes the verb to `TraceConsole`. It reads the service's session
records and quests (`ServiceClient.QuestsJsonAsync` is new, a read like `SessionRecordsJsonAsync`), the ask each quest's
sender names (`GET /api/asks/{id}`), and this machine's events, rules files, `landings.json` and `driver.json`'s standing
answers. From a session's events it reads the driver's notes before its instruction, each instruction, and each acceptance
a landing's press kept there (D100), recognised by the note's own writer (`LandingRules.Note`, `HandNote`). What building
it settled:
- **What an id names.** A session by its record's id, a quest by its id, and a commit by at least four hexadecimal digits
  matched either way round against each session's evidence and each landing's tip. An id that names several kinds is
  refused, naming each kind with the form that reads it (`trace session <id>`). An id that names none is an answer, exit 1.
- **What stood when a session started** is read by comparing the moments the stores keep with the moment the session
  opened. That covers the standing answer, each go-ahead and how many of the person's words came before the start. A
  standing answer set after the start says that what stood before is not kept, since `driver.json` keeps the latest. A
  go-ahead answered after the start says the same, since an answer replaces the one before.
- **The carry-on is said from facts.** The trace names the record before each session on its quest, how that record
  ended, and whether it ran in the same tree. It also prints the driver's own start notes from the session's events, where
  the account walk's line names the session a carry-on carried on, if the walk wrote one. It never infers a carry-on.
- **Reads only, anywhere.** Every request is a `GET` and every file is opened to read. The host routes `trace` before it
  opens its machine log, because that open prunes old log files.
- **Exit codes.** 0 when the chain was read, its missing links said. 1 when nothing names the id, or several kinds do.
  2 when the words do not read, or a store the answer needed did not answer.

**What it cannot reach, and why:**
- **A quest's operations themselves.** A local host answers the state they replay to, and `/api/quests/operations` is a
  shared host's door.
- **A merge into the line's own commit.** `landings.json` records only branch landings (WSR5). A merge keeps the person's
  acceptance in the session's record, naming the branch, the line and how many commits, but not the merge commit, so a
  trace from that commit finds nothing. The session's own commits are found through its evidence.
- **An ended session's rules.** `SpawnSettings` removes the file when its run ends, so only a running session's are read.
- **An earlier standing answer, or an earlier answer to a go-ahead.** Each store keeps only the latest.
- **The carry-on as a stated link.** The starting sentence that named it is replaced by the conclusion's note (D51 rule 4).
- **A teammate's machine-local half**: its account, tree, events and rules.
- **The machine log.** Lines such as `account.rotated` are not one of the six stores, and are not read.

Proof: `TraceTests` (27 cases), seen failing on a stub that read nothing. They cover each entry point (a quest with two
sessions and a carry-on, a session, a commit by three spellings), what stood at each start, a chain with missing links, a
merge read from its acceptance, a running session's rules, a quest on no ask, a teammate's record, a departure with its
hold and a cut instruction, nothing found, an id naming two kinds, an unreachable service, and the terminal's words. Each
sabotage turned its test red: a write into the home, the instruction's words printed, the before-or-after comparison
flipped, the cut size misread, and the acceptance not recognised.
`DriverCommandTests` holds the usage and the routing ahead of the log. `HelpCoverageTests` holds the exemption.

Not covered: no real install's records were traced. The screen's door to the same read is a row of its own. The `Process`
half and the rehearsals were not run by this branch.
