## D83 — A session that holds its quest and stops is waiting on the person, who answers it to carry on (2026-09-27)

**Decision.** A take through a session's own connector is written on its record (`took`). A session
that ends its turn cleanly still holding its quest parks as `awaiting-person`, quoting its last words.
That covers one that took the quest itself, and a resume or a carry-on of a quest this machine
already held. Only a session that did not take the quest reads as a stand-down. The person answers a
parked driven session: `POST /api/sessions/{id}/answer`, the page's *answer and carry on*, or
`daoris-driver answer <session> "…"`. The record ends `completed` with the words kept. The quest is
then carried on in the same tree at the next tick, as after a cut-off (D80), and the session is
handed the answer. A park is never resumed by itself, so D79's and D80's clean exits that used to
conclude `failed` to bound a loop now park instead. A messy exit is still a failure.

**Why.** FG5's verify session took its quest, did everything it could, and ended its turn holding it
with three questions for the person. The record said *"stood-down: someone else has it"*, and nothing
would ever carry the quest on. The quest's state alone cannot tell a session's own take from another
taker's, and the connector already knows which session it speaks for (PERM2).

**Rejected.**
- **Reading the take off the wire** (the ACP tool call and its result). The record moves on facts the
  service holds (D46 §4), and the service saw the take arrive.
- **Answering in the composer.** A driven session takes no person's line (INT4i), and its process is
  gone by the time it parks.

*Amended by D124 (WSSETUP9, 2026-10-01): what may park a session narrows to what no source holds and only the person
can give: a sign-in, a go-ahead for an act outside the repository or on a production system, a preference nothing
records. A choice between options is no longer a reason by itself, and a park says what the session looked at.*

*Amended by D131 (ANSWER1, 2026-10-02): the answer goes on in the session that asked. Once ANSWER1b lands, the answer
keeps the record parked; at the next look its record moves back to working and its harness conversation resumes with the
answer, on the same account, adapter and tree. The carry-on in a new session is the fallback, saying why.*

**BGWAIT1, built 2026-10-08: a turn that ends on the session's own background work is not a question.** The owner's real
case: a driven session on the install backgrounded its gates, said *"Gates are running in the background (lint → prettier
→ …); I'll pick up when they finish."*, and ended its turn holding its quest. This rule read the clean end as asking the
person, and quoted those words as the question. Held by `AcpBackgroundTests` (seven, against a fake agent shaped on
CONSOLE2a's frames) and the park rule's table in `ObservationTests`.
- **What the wire says, in the adapter's own frames** (`docs/2026-09-28-console2-streams-evidence.md`). On
  `claude-agent-acp` a command the agent backgrounds is a task the harness announces, `async_task_spawned`, and ends,
  `async_task_state_update` (`stopped`, then `completed` in one breath; the last state is the ending). The Bash call
  completes at once. The prompt's answer can arrive while the task still runs: CONSOLE2a's first run did. When the task
  ends, the harness wakes its agent itself, with no prompt open, so that woken turn has no answer on the wire. These frames
  arrive only where the client asks for AIR's `asyncTasks`, which a session whose streams are kept does (the shell's loop).
- **What changed.** The protocol door keeps the background work its session's own id announces (`AcpBackground`). When a
  driven quest session's turn ends while any of it runs, the run asks the driver whether its quest stands as a park would
  leave it: `Observation.Parks`, which is this rule's own condition, now one method the conclusion reads too, over the quest
  and the take as the conclusion reads them. If yes, the session stays open and its record stays working. A note says it
  waits, and on what. When the wire says every piece has ended, any started meanwhile included, and 250 ms have passed for
  the last word, a note names each in its last state. Then a prompt of Daoris's own: *"Your background work has ended:
  `<name>` (<state>); …. Go on with your quest from where you left off."* That prompt's answer is the turn's end the run
  waits for; where the agent takes prompts mid-turn (STEER1) it is folded into the woken turn. Then the loop reads again.
  Words held for a turn's end go first, and words that go at once still go while it waits. A turn that ends with nothing
  running ends as before, without the driver being asked, so a real question still parks.
- **Its bounds are the session's.** The person's stop and the timeout kill the process, and the reader with it; the
  driver's shutdown cancels the wait. An agent stream that ends during the wait is the door's failure (*"the ACP agent's
  stream ended while the session waited for its own background work…"*), never a clean turn a park would quote. Work that
  never ends (a dev server) holds the session until the timeout fails it, and D80 carries it on, bounded by the strikes.
  Before, the same turn parked and held its tree until the person answered.
- **It narrows D105 §3** to everything but this case. A turn whose quest closed, was declined or waits on another
  repository, an intake and a conversation still end with their background work.
- **Rejected.** Reading the agent's words ("I'll pick up when they finish"): a self-report, which D46 §4 keeps out, when the
  harness's own frames say it structurally. Leaving the wake to the harness: its woken turn ends with no answer on the wire,
  so nothing would say when to conclude. A bounded wait of its own: D105's reason stands, the bound is the timeout either way.
- **What the gates do not cover.** The headless loop keeps no streams, so it asks for no `asyncTasks`, its wire carries no
  task, and its turns end as before. The native door's harness ends its background work with its turn
  (`docs/2026-09-30-console3-native-streams-evidence.md`), so a native session that hands off still parks; it would need a
  coded ending of its own (a note code and both catalogues) or a go-on with Daoris's words. An answered park's resumed run
  (`Driver.Continue.cs`) is handed no judgement and ends as before. The real adapter's answer to a prompt sent while its
  woken turn runs was not seen; the steer evidence's shape (`docs/2026-10-03-steer-evidence.md` §1, §6) is assumed. The
  driver's reading of the quest for the judgement runs only in a real driven session, which no gate in the fast half starts.

### BGWAIT1b — the resumed run waits too (2026-10-12)

The gap named above is closed. The start's judgement is one helper, `Driver.WaitsOnBackground`, and the answered park's resumed
run (`Driver.Continue.cs`) is handed it with `resumed: true`: it reads the quest again and answers `Observation.Parks` as a
start's does, asking the ledger whether it took the quest only for a start, since a resumed run carries on this machine's take.
Where the quest was closed as the look planned it (the `closed` that `ConcludeResumedAsync` computes), the helper is null and
the run never waits (D105 §3, D137 §2.3). The protocol door asks for `asyncTasks` at `initialize`, before either open or
resume, so a resumed session gets its task frames. Proof: `BackgroundWaitJudgementTests` (the helper over the in-process
ledger) and `AcpBackgroundTests`' resume case; the real resumed run waiting is the full set's.

### BGWAIT2 — the woken turn's end is the turn's end (2026-10-19)

The owner: *"one session just failed."* A driven session on the install finished its work in the turn its harness woke by
itself (it committed and closed its quest done), and Daoris then waited 48 minutes on its own go-on, until the 90-minute
timeout killed it. The record said `failed`, *"the ACP agent's stream ended before it answered — the agent exited"*. BGWAIT1's
admission above held: the real adapter's answer to a prompt sent into a woken turn was never seen, and there is none.
- **What the adapter does** (`claude-agent-acp` 0.79.0, `acp-agent.js`, read). Only a turn's settling answers a prompt, with
  no timer (l.1383-1464). A turn becomes active on its prompt's echo (l.2015) and settles at a user turn's `result` or an idle
  it owns (l.2782-2916). A `result` whose origin is autonomous (l.233-239: a task notification, a peer, a coordinator, an
  observer) is skipped by the settling (l.3546-3573), and the idle after it is absorbed (l.2864-2875); the adapter's own comment
  says a user turn misread so "hangs the prompt un-detectably". What it sends for that result is its `usage_update`, carrying
  `_meta["_claude/origin"]`, where it measured the usage (l.3505-3521). So a go-on whose echo the woken turn takes is never
  answered. The install's woken turn ended with two identical `usage` events and no turn: the go-on's echo activating it and its
  one result carrying the task notification's origin is an inference, unobserved. `session/cancel` is the session's, not one
  turn's (l.4457-4666): rejected below.
- **What was built.** The door reads its own updates in the wire's order from the session's opening (`AcpWake`): a woken turn is
  one that began after the work's last ending, with no prompt open, by the agent saying, thinking, calling or planning, and it
  ends at a `usage_update` whose origin is one of those kinds, said twice or once. That end ends the turn as an answered turn
  does, a `turn` event with no counts, and the run's loop reads again what runs and what its quest says: a real question
  still parks, and work the woken turn started is waited for again, its own woken turn ending it. Daoris's go-on goes only
  where nothing at all was said for `AcpWake.Quiet` (60 s; the wake was measured at 1.6 and 2.4 s, CONSOLE2a) with no turn
  running and no prompt open, as a fresh turn the adapter answers. The person's words go as before and are never cancelled.
  A stream that ends in the breath after the work's last ending no longer cuts the wait short of the woken turn's end said
  before it (`AcpBackground.EndedAsync`).
- **The timeout, said and judged.** `Driver.WaitAsync` tells the door the timeout before it kills the tree (`AcpEnding`,
  `AcpSession.Ending`), so the stream's end is `AcpEnded`, recorded by `ended.timeout`'s own line, not `door.failed`; a stream
  that ended before keeps its own diagnosis. A run that brought its quest to done before the timeout concludes `completed`,
  saying the done and the timeout (`Observation.AfterTimeout`), on a start and a resumed run alike: a done outranks a kill as it
  outranks a messy exit. Only a done this run reached: a run that went on with the person's words after its quest had closed is
  judged on them (MSG1b), so its timeout stays failed and its words wait. A quest still taken at the timeout stays failed, and
  D80 carries it on. The person's stop is read first, as before. The intake's, the reviewer's and the helper's timeouts are left
  as they were: none waits on background work, and none has a quest of its own to reach done.
- **Rejected.** Cancelling the go-on once the woken turn's end is seen: `session/cancel` targets the session, so it can land on
  the person's words admitted meanwhile, and it leaves the session's cancelled flag set until the next turn's echo (l.2017).
  A quiet that ends a woken turn which said no end: silence is never read as success.
- **What is left.** A woken turn that began and whose end is never said (no `usage_update`, where the adapter measured none)
  waits for the timeout, now completed where its quest reached done. A wake later than the bound meets the go-on as before. The
  person's words sent while a woken turn runs are taken by it on this adapter, by the same reading, and their answer may never
  come: unobserved, and bounded the same way. The headless loop asks for no `asyncTasks`, so none of this reaches it, and the
  native door's cutoff is BGWAIT1c's. Proof: `AcpBackgroundTests` (the woken turn's end, the fallback, the ends that are not
  one, the long call, the second work, the person's words before, at and after the end, the stream's end, the shutdown, the
  timeout said in both orders) and `ObservationTests`' timeout case; the installed run is the next install's.
- **2026-10-19, BGWAIT2c.** The stream's end no longer overtakes what the wire said before it: the background wait and the woken
  turn's wait read whether the stream is over before what the reader took, since the reader takes every update before the
  stream's end completes. A woken turn's end said before the stream's end is the turn's end however the two reach the wait;
  a stream that ends while the work runs, or before any woken turn's end, is still the door's failure. The FIX-LOG has the race.
- **2026-10-19, BGWAIT2d.** The background-ended note names the work the turn ended on: `AfterBackgroundAsync` holds the running
  work (ids and names) it read before the quest's read and hands the ids to `EndedAsync`, which watches them as well as what runs
  after the read, so work that ended during the read is no longer left out ("ended: " and an empty list). The BGWAIT2c order stays.
- **2026-10-20, BGWAIT1c.** BGWAIT1's native door is built as its coded ending: a native turn whose own background work its
  harness ended with the turn is cut off (`ended.background-cut`) and carried on, where it parked. D126's SESSTRUE1 note.

### DONEEND1 — the wait ends with the quest (2026-10-20)

On the install two driven sessions whose quests closed done went on working, showing *Stop…* and holding a slot of the cap,
until the 90-minute timeout killed them (one 14 minutes after its close), and the kill left a test's scratch in its tree. The
quest was read once, at the turn's end: a quest that closed while the session waited, its agent closing it in a turn its harness
woke for one piece of work while a long test run went on, was never seen, and only the timeout ended the wait.
- **The rule, kept whole.** A session waits on its own background work only while `Observation.Parks` holds, now read for as
  long as it waits, not once. `AcpSession` races the background wait against a look: the same judgement
  (`Driver.WaitsOnBackground`) read every `BackgroundLook`. Once it no longer parks (done, declined, released, or a new question
  to another repository) and no turn runs, the session ends, saying so (`background.wait-over`, naming
  the work still running), and that work ends with the session as D105 §3 has it.
- **A turn is never cut.** A turn its harness woke, or one a prompt opened, is the agent's: the session ends at its said end
  (`AcpWake.IdleAsync`). A look that cannot be read says nothing, and the session looks again: an unread look read as a no would
  end a session whose quest still waits, which then parks on the person.
- **The look costs nothing beside a tick.** It runs every fourth tick of the driver's own (`pollSeconds` × 4, a minute at the
  default). Each reads what a tick reads, the board with its closed quests and, while the quest is taken and until this
  session's take is read once, the sessions (a take stays its session's, so it is read no more). A waiting session costs a
  quarter of a tick, and its run ends within a minute of the close; the service has no single quest's read to make it cheaper.
- **The agent's tree.** An agent whose session ended with its own background work still running may never exit, that work
  holding it, and the driver waited on its exit bounded only by the timeout. It now gets 10 s after its stdin closes, then its
  tree is ended (`Driver.EndedLingeringAsync`), the work with it, said on its console. Its exit is read as its session's clean
  end: on this door the agent exits 0 once its stdin closes whatever happened, and the kill was Daoris's own, so a done stays
  *the quest reached done.* and a stand-down a stand-down, never a failure on the kill's exit.
- **Rejected.** Cutting the woken turn the moment its quest closes: its last words or clean-up would be lost, and a turn is the
  agent's. Ending the wait on a quiet with the quest closed: silence is never read as success, and BGWAIT2 rejected it.
  Recording the kill's exit: it would make a done read as a messy exit and a stand-down a failed run, carried on by D80.
- **What is left.** A woken turn whose end is never said still holds the session until the timeout when its quest closed in it,
  completed by `AfterTimeout` as before. Once all the work has ended, a quest closed during the wake's quiet still meets the
  go-on, one turn the agent answers. Whether the real adapter lingers after its stdin closes with a background shell running was
  not observed; the bound holds either way. Proof: `AcpBackgroundTests` (the close during the wait, a woken turn running as it
  closes, an unread look), `BackgroundWaitJudgementTests` (the judgement read again, start and resumed),
  `BackgroundNotesTests`, the page's census and `noteLines` case, and `DoneEndTickTests` (Process: a real stand-in that
  backgrounds a test run, closes its quest and idles, ended completed in under a minute of a three-minute timeout, its test run
  ended with its tree).

### BGWAIT3 — an ending its agent was never handed is owed its wake (2026-10-21)

The owner's report: a driven session on the install ran a test class with Claude Code's background Bash, wrote that it would
close its quest when the tests finished, and ended its turn. The page said 等你处理, and nothing woke it.
- **What the adapter sends** (`claude-agent-acp` 0.84.0, the install's pin, with Agent SDK 0.3.284; `async-tasks.js` and
  `acp-agent.js`, read, no model turn run). With AIR's `asyncTasks` asked, a background Bash is announced by
  `async_task_spawned` at the call's result, which the adapter reads itself for the task's id and its output file (*"Command
  running in background with ID: …"*, *"Output is being written to: …"*), or at the SDK's `task_started` where that says the
  command was backgrounded. The spawn names the command (`name`, `description`), `taskType: "shell"`, `canStop: true`, the
  `outputFilePath` and the `toolCallId`, so no `async_task_progress` is needed to place it. It ends on the SDK's `task_updated`
  or `task_notification` as `async_task_state_update` with `completed`, `failed` or `stopped` (`killed` and `cancelled` read as
  `stopped`); the SDK's level of live tasks may close it `stopped` first, and the ending edge corrects it. A Monitor is the same
  frames with `taskType: "monitor"`, announced at its `task_started`; the adapter handles it no other way. A woken turn's end
  is still the autonomous result's `usage_update` with `_meta["_claude/origin"]` (l.3955-3971), so BGWAIT2's reading stands,
  and the adapter's own comment says the CLI answers endings already queued at a turn's end with one model call after it
  (l.4028-4037).
- **What the session's record shows.** Its console's own lines say the task announced and ended `completed`: the frames came.
  The record's events keep no task frame, and only a wait writes a note, so no note was no frame. The harness's output file
  for the task ends `[exited with code 0]`, written about 2.6 s before the turn's end and after the agent's last step (a tool
  search that loaded Monitor's schema; no Monitor ran). The agent's last words said the command still ran: it was never handed
  the ending. With nothing running at the turn's end BGWAIT1 did not apply, and the session closed five seconds after the
  turn and parked, before its harness could wake its agent.
- **What changed.** `AcpWake` keeps where each piece of work's last ending was read, and the agent's own last step (a call not
  stamped as a subagent's). An ending read after that step, after the last woken turn began and after Daoris's last go-on is
  owed a wake (`AcpWake.Unseen`). A turn that ends with nothing running and such an ending asks the park rule as BGWAIT1's
  does; where it would park, the note says the work ended and the session goes on (`background.work-ended`, no new code), and
  the session waits for the woken turn's end, or sends the go-on after `AcpWake.Quiet`, as BGWAIT2 does. The go-on tells every
  ending read before it. The bounds are BGWAIT1's: the person's stop, the timeout, the driver's shutdown, and a stream that ends
  meanwhile is the door's failure. An ending that a later step was handed is not owed one, so a real question after it parks at
  once.
- **Every protocol-door session asks for `asyncTasks`**, not only one whose streams are kept, which closes BGWAIT1's
  headless-loop gap. Where no stream takes the frames, `AcpSession.Map` and `Render` keep none: a raw row would carry the
  harness's output path into the record. `nativeSubagentSessions` stays with the streams that route a subagent's session.
- **The page.** While it waits the record stays `working`, so the rail's group is working (only `awaiting-person` is the
  person's, `SessionGroups.WaitsOnYou`); no word changed.
- **Rejected.** Reading the agent's words (*"still running … I'll close the quest when it finishes"*), as BGWAIT1 rejected.
  Reading the call's result and the output file's last line in the driver: the adapter reads the same result text already, and
  the SDK's task events give the ending, where the file's line is the shell's on an exit and was never seen for a stopped task.
  Waiting for a wake after every ending in the turn: an ending handed to a later step gets no wake, and every such park would
  cost the quiet and a go-on.
- **What is left.** How the CLI hands a queued ending to a step is read from the adapter's comments and CONSOLE2a's second run
  (its ticker ended inside a held turn, and the agent was still woken after it), not from the CLI's source. An ending read in
  the breath between a step's result and the next model call may be handed to it and still read as owed a wake, which costs
  the quiet and one go-on. Words held for the turn's end go first, and their turn may take the ending, under the same bound.
  Proof: `AcpBackgroundTests` (the install's case on 0.84.0's frames, the go-on told once, a quest that does not wait, a real
  question after a step that was handed the ending) and `AcpTests` (what each session asks for, the frames no stream takes).
  `ObservationTests`' park table is unchanged: the rule is the same, read by the door for this case too.
