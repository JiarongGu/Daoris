## D336 — Only the owner's kinds wait on the owner: one number, one card, answered where it stands; the rest is shown as with the agents (2026-11-20)

**Decision (UISH6, UIDS16, UISE2, UISE3, UISE4, UISE9, UISE10, the window redesign `docs/2026-11-19-window-design.md` §5 and §9;
amending D196 §5, D40's landing, and D152 §3 with desktop-app rule 29 for the keys on a wait).**

1. **The owner's kinds** are Question, Go-ahead, Release, Sign-in, Money and Confirm it was you. One census in the driver,
   `AttentionOwnership`, classes every attention kind as the owner's or the agents' with its doctrine reason, both sides of a
   conditional kind named (a go-ahead the repository's declaration keeps is the owner's, otherwise its approver role's; trusting a
   folder is the owner's only where the declaration does not decide it), and fails a kind it does not class. An agents' kind is
   counted, amber, only while no appointed role in its workspace takes it, and says so; otherwise its role answers it, recorded as
   the role's, and Overview lists it uncounted and without amber under *With the agents* (交给智能体).
2. **Work the agents could not finish is not a wait.** A step that failed after its retries and another agent's try is reported
   as a *gave up* line in *Since you left*, with what was tried and its doors (*Try again*, *Ask about it*), uncounted and without
   amber (doctrine rule 1: work you cannot finish is reported as gave up, never asked). It becomes a wait only where a direction
   question names it, as a Question.
3. **One number**: the owner's waits across every workspace, not deferred, in the waits pill, the taskbar and the window's title;
   the scope never filters it. Places wear marks, never counts.
4. **One card**: every wait, on Overview, in a session's dock or pinned on its object, is a `WaitCard` with the same parts (what
   it is in the asker's words, what yes does, what no does, where to look, how it is known, who asked on which model), one id
   wherever it shows, answered in one press or key. A field its asker did not supply reads "not said by its asker" and is counted.
5. **Keys on a wait** (Y, N, 1–4): a key whose act can be taken back runs with the act's 5-second *Undo*; a key whose act cannot
   is itself a held press, held 800 ms as the button is (D340). This amends D152 §3 and rule 29, which kept keys off acts that end
   or remove: the hold is what makes such a key safe.
6. **Overview answers in place**: *Waiting on you*, *Moving now* and *Since you left*, read without opening anything, as three
   columns from 75rem of main width, two from 56rem (the waits on the left), and one below. Its tiles, quest card and repository
   bars go.
7. *Not mine* hands a kind over by writing the act's holder in the one record *Give this act to…* edits (D340), with its rules;
   it is never offered on a sign-in, deleting data or Confirm it was you. *Later* (an hour, this evening, tomorrow, when I'm back)
   replaces *Set aside*; waits with one cause merge into one card that holds them, ordered by a real deadline, then what it holds,
   then age.

**Why.** The purpose: answer what waits in one press with enough said beside it. Today *What needs you* holds 36 kinds, most of
them manager questions (the band's story: trust a folder, try again twice, allow a fetch, five reviews), each kind with its own
card, none saying what no does, and Overview only links to them. Doctrine rule 1 is the closed list, and it reports work the
agents gave up on instead of asking; where the product makes its own agents act against it, that is a defect.

**Rejected.**

- *Counting every attention row*: the number would stop meaning "yours".
- *A failed step as a wait on the owner*: doctrine rule 1 reports it as gave up; asking would make the owner the agents' manager.
- *Hiding agents' rows while the roles are built*: work held by an unanswered row would vanish; it is counted until a role takes
  it.
- *Set aside, and set all aside*: a set-aside wait hides work still held.
- *No keys on waits* (D152 §3 as written): the queue would need the pointer for every answer.
- *Overview as a summary that links elsewhere* (D196 §5): an answer takes two places.
