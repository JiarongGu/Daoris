## D153 — Finished history is cleared from this machine by a listed press: a closed quest's work, an ask's, a closed quest's failed sessions, or a workspace's; a remote's copy is forgotten here, never deleted (2026-10-07)

**Decision (HIST1).** On 2026-10-07 the owner's install held 35 closed quests, 76 sessions that had served them and
24 MB of their transcripts, and nothing in Daoris could clear any of it: a delete takes only an open quest nobody
started on (D95) or a conversation that served no quest (D126 §5.4), and archive only hides. It was purged by hand, and
the owner asked for quest and session clean-up to be planned. `docs/2026-10-07-history-clearing-design.md` is the
contract; its §0 is what was true at `121503bf`, with eight hazards a removal meets (H1–H8). It settles:

1. **Clear** (清除) removes from this machine what it kept of finished work: records, the words in them and the home's
   files for them. Four scopes, each listed first and then pressed (D88's way): **a closed quest's work** (the quest,
   the closed questions its sessions asked, applied again, as `AskWork.Read` reads a quest's work; their logs; every
   session that served them; what the home kept); **an ask's work**, whole or not at all (D95's rule), so a quest an
   ask asked is cleared only with its ask; **a closed quest's failed sessions** alone, this machine's; and **a
   workspace's finished history**, every unit that may go, with the home's left-over files.
2. **It refuses, per unit and naming the piece**: open or taken work (`HISTORY_OPEN`); a live session, a teammate's
   that still reads as running, or a landing still running (`HISTORY_LIVE`); anything waiting on the person: a parked
   session, a quest held for acceptance, an undismissed conflict, an ask proposed or open, a pending rule proposal
   (`HISTORY_NEEDS_YOU`); open work naming it: an awaiting quest, an open quest its session published, a chain's open
   next step (`HISTORY_AWAITED`); a quest an ask asked (`HISTORY_ASKED`); a tree here (`HISTORY_TREE_HERE`); a landing's
   standing branch (`HISTORY_LANDING_STANDS`); unpushed moves or records (`HISTORY_UNPUSHED`); and keeps a teammate's
   failed session (`HISTORY_NOT_OURS`). Nothing to clear is information.
3. **A record that never left the machine simply goes**, row and log together. **A quest a remote numbered is
   forgotten**: its rows, log, sessions (this machine's and its copies of a teammate's) and files go, and
   `quest_forgotten` keeps its id. The fetches skip a forgotten quest's operations and records while the cursors move
   past them, so no later move and no cursor at zero brings it back. Nothing is pushed and nothing travels: no
   tombstone, no new operation. A new machine's first fetch brings it, since the team's history is that machine's to
   hold. The same words published again are refused here in the closed quest's words.
4. **The store's numbers never go back.** `quest_machine` keeps the highest operation sequence issued and a one-row
   `session_revision` the highest revision, so no removal reissues a number the remote or the push cursor passed (H1,
   H2). It is HIST1a and ships first: H2 reaches D126's *Delete…* today.
5. **A session of an open or taken quest is never cleared**, so the strikes, RETRY1's mark and the carry-on read what
   they always read (D58, D80). Once the quest is closed nothing is counted from its sessions, and `driver.json`'s
   entries for it go with it.
6. **What the home holds is enumerated with its owner** (design §2): in `knowledge.db`, `quests` with `quest_log`,
   `sessions`, `asks` cleared; `quest_machine`, `quest_cursor`, `quest_passes` and `session_cursor` kept. Under the home,
   each session's conversation, transcript, files, harness id, leftover marker, `.cannot.json`, `.new-session.json`,
   held words, closed automatic landing and archive mark; kept files; settled rule proposals; landing traces; abandon
   entries; an empty tree folder; the intake's room only when the workspace keeps no ask. Kept: the machine log, usage,
   accounts and the harness's own storage, the registry, the plugins, Ask Daoris's proposals, and folders Daoris did not
   write (`salvage/`). One helper removes a session's files for both a clear and D126's delete.
7. **The doors.** The quest's page (*Clear from this machine…*, *Clear failed sessions…*), the ask's page, and the
   workspace's page, whose Details reads *Kept on this machine* per workspace with *Clear history…*. Sessions offers
   none. At a terminal, `daoris-driver history`, `history clear --workspace`, `quest clear [--failed]` and
   `ask --clear`, each listing until `--yes`. The service's local-only `GET /api/history` and `POST /api/history/clear`
   judge in one class, `HistoryDesk`; the driver's `HistoryClearing` answers `HISTORY_PLAN` and `HISTORY_CLEAR`. Ask
   Daoris may propose one through a `clear` kind whose card is the first press; Apply is the person's second press, and
   no MCP tool removes a record. The log gains `history.cleared`, counts only.
8. **Nothing clears by itself**, as D126 §5.5 and §11 say: no age, no size cap, no tick. What changes is that the
   person sees what the home keeps.

The build is HIST1a–h, the design's §9.

**Why.** Finished work's history grows with every session, the lists ask for all of it, and the only way to shrink it
was a hand purge that ran into hazards no one had written down: a sequence that can go back, a cursor whose removal
fetches everything again, a row whose log must go with it. A person should be able to clear what is finished, once it
is truly finished: nothing in progress, nothing waiting on them, no tree or standing branch, and the team's copy
complete. A wired workspace is where history grows fastest, and its remote already keeps the team's record, so this
machine forgets its own copy without touching theirs.

**This amends:**
- **D95**: a done or declined quest, and an ask whose work closed, may be cleared from a machine; *clear* no longer
  means only leaving the list. D95's delete stays the act for a mistake and the only removal that travels.
- **D126 §5.1, §5.4, §5.5 and §11**: a session that served a quest is cleared once its quest is closed, with it or as a
  failed session; a landing's trace does not keep a cleared session; a teammate's record is forgotten here with its
  quest.
- **The sync design's §8**: the fetches skip forgotten quests, and the store keeps its high-water marks.
- **D94 §4** (`history.cleared`), **D110** (the `clear` kind) and **D116's glossary** (*clear*, 清除).

**D46 §3–§4, D47 §4, D51 rule 7 as D88 and D132 amended it, D58, D68's one lock, D132 and D37 stand.**

**Rejected.**
- **Refusing a clear on a wired workspace.** The largest histories would never be cleared, to protect a copy nothing
  here can harm.
- **A clear carried by absence.** A fetch by cursor brings an absence back the first time a cursor is at zero, or half
  back as an orphan conflict: D95's reasoning, applied on one machine.
- **A clear that travels**, as a `deleted` tombstone or a `cleared` operation. One person's tidy would take the team's
  record from every machine.
- **Trimming a remote's history.** The team's decision, and a shared deployment has no clear door.
- **Clearing a session of an open quest**, with or without a tally keeping its strike. D58 rejected the tally, and
  without it the clear is a *Try again* nobody pressed.
- **Clearing a quest an ask asked on its own.** The ask reads from what is left, and as a proposal once none is.
- **Refusing a quest a teammate's session served.** The copy carries no transcript, and refusing would keep a team's
  history here for good; a teammate's record is still never cleared alone.
- **The clear removing landed trees and branches too.** Two removals with two proofs stay two presses (D88, D132).
- **Clearing from Sessions**, **taking conversations that served no quest in the bulk**, **choosing units one by one**,
  **clearing by age or size**, **an undo**, and **naming it *Delete*, *Clean up* or *Archive*** (design §8, §11).

**Built 2026-10-07 (HIST1a): the store's numbers never go back** (point 4, design §0.3, §3.3). `quest_machine` gains
`sequence`, the highest this machine has issued, and a one-row `session_revision` beside `session_cursor` keeps the
highest revision. The next sequence is one past the larger of the log's highest for this machine and the mark
(`QuestStore.AppendAsync`); the next revision is one past the larger of the table's newest and the mark
(`SessionStore.NextRevision`). A store from before starts each mark at what it holds: the sequence when the column is
added, from this machine's own rows only, and the revision when the table is made. What building it settled:
- **Triggers move the marks**, in the statement that writes the number: `quest_sequence_issued` after an insert into
  `quest_log`, and `session_revision_written` and `session_revision_moved` after an insert into `sessions` or an update
  of its revision. Revisions forced it: each is issued by one statement, most outside any transaction, and a second
  statement to move the mark would be a second transaction another host could write between. Quests took the same
  shape, as the registry's tombstones are written, so no way into the log can forget the mark, and an operation of this
  machine's that a fetch keeps back raises it too.
- **H1 loses a move by two routes.** Besides the remote's answer to a retry, the next pass's fetch brings the remote's
  operation back under the reused number, and `IntegrateAsync`, which finds an operation by machine and sequence, takes
  it for the new move and numbers it, so nothing is pushed at all. The sync test failed this way before the build.
- **D95's delete reaches H1 only narrowly**, read from the code and not reproduced: it removes a history outright only
  when nothing of it was numbered and the quest does not travel, so a remote holds such a number only when a push's
  answer was lost and the quest stopped travelling before the delete. Numbered rows are removed by the hand purge and by
  HIST1b's forget, and the quest sync test removes them as those do, by the rows. D126's delete reaches H2 directly, and
  the session sync test goes through the ledger's delete.
- **The numbers have gaps after a removal.** Nothing reads them as gap-free: a remote keys an operation by machine and
  sequence and orders by its own position, a cursor compares, and no code steps one back.

**Built 2026-10-07 (HIST1b): the service clears closed records** (points 1–3 and 7; design §1, §2.1, §3, §6.3).
