## D229 — A line's behaviors are counted as facts in its own context, which the session sees; the supervisor acts on them, held by an appointed agent or by rules the person sets and Daoris reads with no model; today's caps become those rules, seeded at today's numbers and converted on the person's press (2026-11-03)

**Decision (CONTEXTDRIVE1, the owner's direction of 2026-11-03; designed under D191, proposed for its reads, revised after the Opus
read).** Per `docs/2026-11-03-context-counters-design.md`, within the structure and with no second route (D228).

1. **The counts.** What a line does is counted by one pure read, `LineCounts.Of`, each count from the one record that already
   carries it (the desktop store, the service's records, the event files, the turn lines). No total is stored. A line has one key,
   `LineKeys.Of`: walking back by `from` on this machine, the quest's id where a part is a driven run of a quest, else the first
   part's id. The moves, every count and COMMS1g's admission read it, so a hand-over, a restart and D79's resume keep a line's
   counts. A count is a session's, a line's, a quest's or a role's, and reads *exact*, *at least* or *unknown*, with why; the
   answers-alone count is never unknown (COMMS1 §13 point 8), and a count a line does not have is absent.
2. **The session sees its course** in its brief and a go-on's words, in Daoris's words, as facts composed from codes and numbers,
   never an instruction and never another agent's text. `line_read` gains no counts: the service never opens the desktop store.
3. **A rule** is *when this count reaches n here, do this*, over the counts that reset: answers alone, failed sessions, failures in a
   row on one choice, transient tries, a part's quiet or limited end, and moves a day. Its actions are the doors that exist: wait for
   the person, ask before the next move, wait for a reset, move to another choice, ask the supervisor, none here. Each hold is
   released by the door it has today and kept where that release is kept: *Go on with this answer* and *Not now*, *Try again* with
   `forgiven` or `transientReleased`, a yes in `role_moves`. A count that never resets (minutes, tokens, hand-overs, quests and
   results) takes no rule; a refusal's and a budget's move stay the walk's.
4. **A rule decides at the executor that acts on its count today**, handed where it reads a fixed number or a policy's move now:
   the answers-alone rule in `AnswersAloneHold.Holds`, judged inside COMMS1g's serialized admission, its hold the mark every reader
   already honours; the failures rule at the planner's start; the tries rule at a run's start and past its episode; the condition
   rules where the staffing course reads a policy's move. D212's one course decision gains no step and keeps its order. A rule acts
   between turns alone, never on an unknown count, never ending a running or quiet session, and never answering a question. The
   tries rule cannot be removed, and a move needs a moves rule, each judged on every line's effective set.
5. **A rule is a holder of the supervisor role** (D208), kept in the desktop store and set through the role door (`SET_ROLE`,
   `SET_ROLES`, the terminal's role verb, `role_propose` applied by the person's press). It grants nothing, so it is set as a role's
   policy is, while an agent holder is still appointed only by the owner's press in the window. Rules are inherited by place, the
   most specific place holding a rule on a count replacing the rest, with no layering. The store's step leaves the writer stamp
   where it is: a raise refuses only an older build's writes, never its driving.
6. **An agent and a rule.** The rule is read first and its act stands; the agent's answer is refused where it would make a go-on a
   rule holds, at the keeping and the release; the agent judges a count only where a rule refers it, once per point, through the
   supervisor's third act, *course*, read against the rules again at its release. A rule's own act is kept in the decision record
   with no question, final as written, so no sweep settles it.
7. **Defaults, the owner's answers of 2026-11-03.** Today's numbers are seeded as rules on every home, so no line runs differently
   until the person changes a rule: answers alone 2 in a row, the home's failed sessions, five transient tries, and each applied
   role policy's moves and `most`. The cap stays a setting of the machine, outside the rules, with machine load, a shared host's
   protocol and Daoris's own nudges.
8. **Conversion.** An existing home converts through a `rules` part of ONEWAY1's conversion door: only applied policies' moves become
   rules, a stale policy's moves and a `retry` move become none, and `driver.json` stops writing `strikes` it did not hold. On a home
   whose rules are reached, the old doors (`SET_STRIKES`, `driver strikes`, the policy's moves and `most`) are refused in the row
   that makes the rules decide, which lands with the door, the part and the editors as one wave; each old reader and door is deleted
   with its register entry once the part's census ends.

**Why.** A line's drive was steered by numbers each counted and acted on in its own place, so the person could not see them
together nor set a hard stop where they wanted one, and an appointed agent had no one place to read what a line had done. Counting
from the records that already carry each behavior, under one key, keeps every count durable without a second store; making each cap
a rule the person sees keeps every home as it runs today while no number stays fixed in the product (D225's note of 2026-11-03);
reading each rule where its count is acted on today, and releasing each hold by its own door, adds no second hold, release or
course step; and holding the rules as the supervisor's gives the agent supervisor and the person's hard limits one place, one judge
and one order. The cap stays a setting because it bounds the machine's load, which no line shows.

**Rejected.** A table of counts beside their records. A new feed to the session, or counts read by the service. Rules inside each
role's policy record. A rule set through *Appoint* alone. A model judging every go-on. A rule ending a running or quiet session,
watching a role's day, or over a count that never resets. One hold row and one release for every rule. A course step of its own. A
raised writer stamp. A removable tries rule. An unknown count read as zero or as the bound. A notification for a rule's act. The cap,
the channel's bounds, Daoris's carry-ons and the walk's passes as rules.

each number becomes a seeded rule, and `SET_STRIKES` with `driver.json`'s key is refused on a reached home and retires at the
census's end. D208: points 1-2 gain the `rule` holder, set through the role door; point 4 gains the act *course*, the defer bound
`course` and a rule's act kept with no question. D210 point 3: the hold past the episode's tries becomes a rule that cannot be
removed; the schedule stays. D225's note of 2026-11-03: its supervisors are this decision's; COMMS1g keeps the count durably, keyed by
`LineKeys.Of` (restating COMMS1 §13 point 8's key), judges the `at` it is handed inside its admission, takes store step 20, and builds
no rule. D226: its applied moves on a condition and `most` become rules naming the role, a `retry` move and a stale policy's moves
