## D176 — A brainstorm is a bounded driver operation of its own: Daoris assembles one frozen context packet from its own knowledge, shows it to the person, and hands it alike to agents of different makers, whose structured proposals it validates, synthesizes with their provenance and challenges where they are hard to reverse; the result is a ranked document, never a change (2026-10-19)

**Decision (BRAINSTORM1).** The owner filed `docs/2026-10-19-cross-agent-brainstorming-design.md` on 2026-10-19 from a
sibling workshop repository and asked for it to be taken into the design: one scoped question fanned to agents of
different makers, structured proposals back, one synthesis that flags what is hard to reverse for a skeptical check, and
a ranked document rather than a change. The same day they added that Daoris itself manages the context, the knowledge
and the cache, and that this is what makes a freer and more capable agent system. Nothing in Daoris fans one question
out today: a reviewer (D155) reads a quest's candidate for a landing, a helper (D163) answers one working session, and
each reads the repository for itself. A read-only design opinion from Codex was taken on where this belongs, its
slices and its traps; this entry adopts its placement, slices and traps, and adds the packet the owner asked for.

1. **A brainstorm is its own bounded operation of the driver.** One request, one frozen packet, a fixed sequence of
   passes (generate, synthesize, challenge), one document, and records and a lifecycle of its own.
   - **The doors.** `daoris-driver brainstorm "<question>" [--repository <r>] [--attach <file>…] [--agents <a,b>]`
     writes a request, and `brainstorm show|send|stop|clear <id>` and `brainstorm list` follow it. As a helper's ask
     does, the verb writes a request the driver's look takes, so every pass runs under the loop's cap, in whichever
     process holds the loop (D104); with none, the request waits, and the verb says so. The CLI's twin,
     `daoris brainstorm …`, reaches the running Daoris through its service seam (`service/service.ts`) and never spawns
     an agent itself. The doctrine commands stay offline and have no runtime dependency.
   - **What it reuses.** Both doors' adapters; the roster's walk (`HarnessRoster.WalkAsync`) and the reviewer's walk over
     makers (`ReviewerChoice`); D175's own profiles; the reviewer's restricted posture as a new default (D72); one-turn
     passes (`OneTurn`); the helper's file shapes (an ask its writer never changes, a state the driver alone writes);
     the usage record.
   - **What it never is.** Not the second opinion: there is no candidate, no gate, no landing hold and no *reviewed*.
     Not an ask or a quest: nothing is published, nothing travels, nothing becomes work. Not a code-changing session:
     the rooms hold no checkout, and the posture writes nothing but the result. Not a landing or a verdict. Its output
     is never the person's words, a requirement or a step's done; a person or the caller decides what to build, through
     the doors that already exist.
   - **On the fabric (D173).** Each pass is a participant with an issued, restricted binding (ADR-B, ADR-F). It reads its
     room, gives one result, and holds no publishing right, no quest or channel tool and no account secret. The records
     are the brainstorm's own, one store, until a fabric primitive for artifacts takes them over with a mapping (ADR-A).
   - **Fan-out is Daoris's fixed plan, never an agent's choice.** No pass may ask for a brainstorm, a helper or a
     reviewer, or post to an ask's channel. The synthesizer is a pass with a schema, not a coordinator (D1, D65).

2. **The context packet is Daoris's: assembled from its own knowledge, frozen, shown, and cached once for every agent.**
   - **Assembled with no model**, from three things. The question (at most 4,000 characters, the helper's bound) and the
     person's constraints. The asker's attachments, content-addressed under the home (D65 §2). And Daoris's own
     knowledge of the asker's repository: the knowledge service's search scoped to that repository's records (decisions
     and their notes, knowledge, documents, and the declared index's rows with their lines, D151 §6), asked in more than
     one wording (D129 §2). With no service, the declared index's files answer (D151 §6). The packet says which tier
     found its sources: `meaning`, `words` or `files`. A question with no repository is the question and the
     attachments alone.
   - **Frozen.** It is written once as `packet.json`, schema-versioned (`packet: 1`), and identified by the digest of its
     content. Each source names its kind, its repository-relative path and lines, the revision it was read at (the
     commit for a file of the checkout, the digest for an attachment), why it was taken (the search's words and tier),
     and its truncation: the characters kept, of how many. What was found and left out is listed with why: the bound,
     or the person removed it. It never holds a machine path, an account's name or a credential.
   - **Bounds, first values** read again on the first real run (BRAINSTORM1c): at most 24 sources, 12,000 characters each
     and 60,000 in all; the question and the attachments first, then sources by the search's rank.
   - **Shown before it goes to another maker.** The person sees the packet whole, on the page or by
     `brainstorm show <id>`, and sends it with their own press, `brainstorm send <id>`. Removing a source or adding an
     attachment makes a new packet with a new digest, and a sent packet is never changed. The disclosure policy may let
     the person say once, per workspace and maker, that its packets go without a show (point 9).
   - **Cached once.** One packet serves every pass of a brainstorm, byte for byte. Each generator, the synthesis and the
     challenge are handed the same file in their rooms, by its digest, and a repair or a re-run reuses it rather than
     assembling again: every agent and every retry reads what the person saw. A new brainstorm whose sources have not
     moved (the same revisions, attachments and bounds) reuses the kept packet by its digest, and says so; a moved
     source makes a new packet. The packet is kept with the brainstorm's records in `data/` (D172), not in `cache/`:
     assembled again from moved sources it is a different packet, so it cannot be fetched again. The packet leads every
     instruction unchanged, so a maker that caches a prompt's prefix may reuse it across passes; Daoris claims nothing
     about that cache and measures none.
   - **No agent crawls alone.** A pass's room holds the packet and its instruction, nothing else: no checkout, and no
     repository path named to it. Its posture offers reading in the room and its one result tool: no shell, no
     knowledge search, no web. What a pass would need and the packet lacks is a field of its answer (`needs`), shown to
     the person, who may add it to a new packet. Whether each harness's posture keeps a read inside the room is measured
     per adapter (BRAINSTORM1c, D53), and the room's contents are the floor until it is. D107's reading by default is a
     repository's session's, and a pass is not one.
   - **Independently.** Each generator gets the packet and its fixed instruction only: never the caller's conversation
     or memory, never another pass's answer, and never which other makers run.

3. **Each generator answers once, structured and bounded, and Daoris validates it.**
   - **The shape**, given through one typed result tool, `brainstorm_give`: `{ brainstorm: 1, proposals: [{ idea,
     rationale, effort, risk, reversible, undo, confidence, assumptions, sources }], needs: [], none }`.
   - **The scales**, each with `unknown` explicit and never read as its best value. *Effort*: `small` (within one
     session or a day), `medium` (several sessions, within a week), `large` (beyond a week, or more than one
     repository), `unknown`. *Risk*: `low` (a failure shows at once and touches only the change), `medium` (a failure
     reaches other parts or shows late), `high` (a failure reaches data, other repositories, people or a release),
     `unknown`. *Reversible*: `yes` (reverting the change undoes it, and nothing outside the repository moved), `partly`
     (something stays: a migration, a message sent, a published name), `no` (data removed, a verdict changed, history
     rewritten, a publication), `unknown`; `undo`, how it would be undone or why it cannot, is required unless `yes`.
     *Confidence*: `low`, `medium`, `high`.
   - **The lengths**: an idea at most 200 characters, a rationale 1,200, an undo 400; at most five assumptions and five
     needs of 200 each; at most eight proposals; the whole answer at most 20,000 characters (`helper_answer`'s bound).
     `sources` names the packet's source ids only. An answer with no proposal is valid only with `none` saying why.
   - **Validation is Daoris's, by the schema, with no model.** A missing answer, a field outside its scale or bound, an
     unknown source id, an idea given twice in one answer, or proposals beside `none`, fails the pass; it is never an
     empty success. **At most one repair**: the pass's next turn is handed the validator's codes in fixed words, within
     its own minutes, and a second failure is the pass's, kept with its codes. Daoris never repairs an answer, never
     trims one into validity and never keeps part of an invalid one.
   - **The flags are Daoris's, read from the fields.** A proposal whose `reversible` is not `yes` is flagged *hard to
     reverse*; one whose `risk` is `high` or `unknown` is flagged *risky*. A later pass may add a flag with its reason,
     and none lifts one.

4. **One synthesis dedupes and ranks, and Daoris keeps the provenance.**
   - **One pass**, on the maker the policy names (point 9), in a fresh session. It reads the packet and every validated
     proposal, each under an anonymous id (`P1`…`Pn`, ordered by Daoris with the makers interleaved), and never which
     maker gave which, so it cannot favour its own maker's.
   - **It answers through `brainstorm_synthesize`**: groups, each with a title (200), the merged idea (400), its members
     by id, its rank and why (600), and any flag it adds with its reason; and what it left out, with why. Validated:
     every proposal id appears exactly once, in one group or left out with a reason; the ranks run 1 to n with no gap or
     tie; no id is invented; every flag Daoris set on a member stays on its group. Point 3's one repair applies.
   - **Provenance is re-attached by Daoris** after validation: each member's maker, adapter, pass and account (shown on
     this machine only). So the document says who proposed every idea in a group, and how many makers named it.
     Agreement is shown as that count and never ranks by itself: two makers agreeing establishes no fact (D155 §8), and
     ranking by overlap would bury the one-maker idea the diversity is for.
   - **The ranking follows the person's criteria** (point 9), handed to the synthesizer in fixed words. The rank is the
     synthesizer's claim, and the document says so.

5. **The skeptical pass challenges, bounded, and performs nothing.**
   - **Bounded**: one pass and its one repair per brainstorm, over the flagged groups (at most eight, by rank), on a
     maker other than the flagged proposals' makers where one is ready, else the same maker fresh, labelled as such.
   - **It reads** the packet and the flagged proposals, anonymous. Per proposal it answers through
     `brainstorm_challenge`: each assumption held, not held or unknown, with why; reversibility judged on point 3's
     scale, with why; the consequences it names; and a verdict, `stands`, `weakened`, `unsupported` or `unknown`.
   - **It performs nothing.** It has a generator's room and posture, and no tool but its result. Its findings are claims
     shown beside each proposal: nothing is removed or re-ranked by them, a disagreement is shown and never settled by a
     model, and there is no second round and no debate until agreement.
   - **Required or advisory by the policy** (point 9). Where it is required and did not finish, the proposal is held out
     of the ranking, *not challenged* with why; *Challenge again* (a new spend, said as one) and *Rank it unchallenged*
     are the person's presses.

6. **Every pass and the whole request are bounded, and what failed is said.**
   - **Time.** Each pass is one turn by construction, plus its one repair, within its own minutes. The whole request has
     its own deadline, with the synthesis's and the challenge's minutes reserved from the start, so the generators end
     at the deadline less those reserves. A pass past its minutes is stopped; a request past its deadline stops what
     runs and keeps what answered.
   - **Partial coverage.** When a maker fails (no ready account, a limit, its minutes, two malformed answers, a crash),
     the document says which makers answered, which did not, and why, by code. When fewer than two makers answered, it
     says *one maker answered*, and that this is no cross-maker brainstorm.
   - **No synthesis.** When the synthesis fails, the document is the validated proposals, unranked, by maker, with
     Daoris's flags and its fold of exact duplicates (the same idea text, normalized, from two makers is one row naming
     both), and says *unranked: the synthesis did not finish*, with why.
   - **Stop, again, and a restart.** The person's *Stop* ends every pass and keeps what answered. A request is idempotent
     by its id: sent again, it is the same brainstorm, never a second spend. A Daoris that stops mid-brainstorm keeps
     what answered and, coming up, ends the passes that ran as `interrupted`. Nothing runs again by itself; *Run the
     missing passes* is the person's, and is said as a new spend.

7. **Accounts are the person's walk, usage is measured and attributed, and diversity is by maker.**
   - **The walk, unchanged** (D125, D130). Each pass (generator, synthesis, challenge) takes its account by the scope of
     the asker's workspace, or the machine's for a question with no repository: its list is the whole set, with its
     cool-offs, sign-ins and *start on*, and only D175's own profiles. A maker with no ready account is `unavailable`
     with the walk's code. A pass never runs on an account the scope does not list, and never on the other maker in its
     place.
   - **The cap.** A pass takes a slot under the machine's cap K, as a reviewer does (D155's amendment of D130). A full cap
     waits within the deadline.
   - **A limit** met mid-pass is D125's: the account cools, no strike is counted, and the pass may start once more on the
     next ready account of that maker's list, as its one repair, within its minutes.
   - **Usage** is measured per pass, counted toward its account, and attributed to the brainstorm as its own kind in the
     usage view. Unknown stays unknown, and absent is never zero (D57, D130 §7). The budget is passes and minutes, which
     Daoris controls; tokens and spend are reported after, never a cap (D54).
   - **Diversity is by maker** (D155 §4). Claude Code by either door is one family, and another account is the same
     maker. Two doors of one maker, or a feasibility role on a maker already present, are listed apart and never counted
     as a second maker. Daoris names no model (D24); the record says the model where the adapter reports one.

8. **The document is kept on this machine for the person; another repository gets its result only through its own
   session; and the owner's install starts nothing unasked.**
   - **Kept** in `data/brainstorms/<id>/` (D172): the request, the packet, each pass's record (maker, adapter, account,
     model where reported, minutes, usage, and its validated answer or its failure's codes), the synthesis, the
     challenge, and the document as JSON with its Markdown rendering. Each file is written atomically by one writer. The
     rooms are under `work/` and removed when the brainstorm ends. It is served to this machine only (D47 §4): never a
     quest operation, never synced, never on a shared host.
   - **Seen by the person** on the page and at the terminal (`brainstorm show`): Daoris's words in English and Chinese,
     the agents' as they wrote them.
   - **Another repository's caller** gets its result only through its own session. A session Daoris runs reads it with
     `brainstorm_read`, or is handed it as its next turn marked as agents' claims (the helper's delivery), never as the
     person's words. A session the person runs elsewhere reads the CLI's answer. Daoris never writes the document into
     any repository: what to build from it goes through the asker's own session, or through a quest or an ask the person
     publishes with the document attached.
   - **Retention**: kept until the person clears it, by a listed press (D153's rule) or `brainstorm clear`, which removes
     the records and the packet.
   - **Unarmed.** Nothing starts without a person's press: no brainstorm starts by itself, on a failure or on a
     schedule; an agent's ask waits for the person's yes; and a workflow step is armed only by the person's own
     workflow. Development and the rehearsals run it on
     isolated homes and stub agents, never on the owner's accounts, workflows or closure graphs. A run on real makers
     (BRAINSTORM1c) spends only accounts the owner names for it.

9. **The person's policy: named open choices, with the defaults proposed here.** `brainstorms` and
   `workspaceBrainstorms` in `driver.json`, a workspace's whole over the machine's, both twins on one table. A request's
   flags choose within the policy, never beyond it (D157 point 4's rule): `--agents` names makers the policy lists.

   | Choice | Proposed default | Left open |
   |---|---|---|
   | Who may start one | The person only, at the terminal or on the page. An agent's ask (BRAINSTORM1d) waits for the person's yes, `ask`; `off` refuses it | Whether `on` is ever offered |
   | Disclosure | The packet is shown each time before it goes | `listed`: the person's standing yes, per workspace and maker |
   | The makers | Claude, then Codex, each by the account walk; at most four | A third maker; a feasibility role |
   | The fewest makers | Two; fewer is said, never refused | — |
   | The synthesizer | The first maker of the request with a ready account | A named maker |
   | Minutes | 10 per generator, 10 for the synthesis, 10 for the challenge, 30 for the whole request | — |
   | Proposals per maker | At most eight | — |
   | The ranking | Answers the question as asked; then reversible before not; then lower risk; then lower effort. Agreement is shown, never a key | The person's own criteria, in words |
   | The skeptical pass | Required where reversible is `no` or `unknown` (unknown is never shown reversible); advisory where it is `partly` or the risk is `high`; none otherwise | Off; required for every proposal |
   | Retention | Kept until the person clears it | An age |

   With two makers, a brainstorm runs at most eight turns: two generators, one synthesis and one challenge, each with
   its one repair. The minutes are not measured; BRAINSTORM1c reads them again.

10. **The no-model tier** (D24) assembles and freezes the packet (by words, or from the files), validates, folds exact
    duplicates, flags from the fields, keeps the provenance and the bounds, and renders the unranked document. The
    proposals, the ranking, the merged ideas and the challenges need an agent, and the document says which passes ran.
    With no agent ready at all, the brainstorm ends *no agent could answer*, and the packet is kept.

**Why.** What a brainstorm across makers adds is divergence: two models trained apart fail apart, so the same question
put to both surfaces options neither finds alone. Everything here protects that divergence and makes it comparable. One
frozen packet means every agent answers the same question about the same evidence, so their differences are theirs, not
the differences of what each happened to read; the person sees it before it leaves, so disclosure is a choice and not a
side effect; and it is assembled once, from the knowledge Daoris already indexes, so no agent spends its turn finding its
way (the owner's point; ORIENT1 measured a third of a branch's calls spent finding its way, D151). Structured answers on defined scales are what let a
synthesis rank and let Daoris flag what is hard to reverse from facts rather than prose. Anonymity in the synthesis and
provenance kept by Daoris keep a maker from favouring itself and keep every idea's origin visible. A skeptical pass that
challenges and performs nothing mirrors the practice this repository already has with a second agent (D155): a reader
that did not write the work, whose words are claims. Placing it as its own operation keeps each existing contract
whole: the opinion's gate, the helper's one answer, the ask's requirements and the quest's travel all stay what they
are, and the brainstorm reuses their machinery without inheriting their obligations.

**Rejected.**
- **The second opinion as its home** (D155): bound to a quest's candidate, base and tip, the gate's order and the
  landing; a brainstorm has none, so it would need a made-up candidate, could hold or settle a landing, and would blur
  *reviewed*, which is the person's alone (D154 point 8).
- **A helper** (D163): one agent answering one working session, from a clone of its tree, as that session's next turn;
  a fan-out from it is a helper asking helpers, which D163 refuses, and a brainstorm needs no working session.
- **An ask or a quest** (D65, D32, D79): an ask's words are the person's requirements and become work, and a quest is
  taken by whoever drives a repository and replays on every machine (D68); proposals kept as either become work nobody
  chose, and carry the packet to every machine.
- **The ask's channel** (D167): it carries claims and deliberately starts no turn and spends no account; fanning out from
  it lets agents talk each other into turns, D155's rejection.
- **A code-changing session, a landing or a verdict**: the output is a document, and the person or the caller decides.
- **A workflow step first** (D157, D173): a graph is an opt-in pattern over the operation, never its home (ADR-A). The
  step comes last, as an adapter and a kind of its own; `opinion` renamed would conflate two contracts.
- **Spawning from the CLI or from the service**: accounts, streams and processes are the desktop's; the CLI's doctrine
  commands stay offline with no runtime dependency, and a shared host serves no UI and spawns nothing.
- **Building it in the sibling workshop**: the design's own argument, a tool that does everything, one level up.
- **Each agent crawling the repository**: diverging contexts make the proposals incomparable, spend each maker's turn on
  finding instead of thinking, and disclose to each maker what it chose to read rather than what the person saw.
- **Handing the caller's conversation or memory**, and **one maker's answer to the next**: anchoring, which removes the
  divergence the fan-out is for (D155's rejection of handing over the worker's conversation).
- **Prose answers**, **Daoris repairing or trimming an answer into validity**, and **an empty answer read as success**.
- **The synthesizer knowing which maker said what**, and **ranking by agreement**.
- **The skeptic acting on what it challenges**, **a second round**, and **agents debating until they agree** (D155, D163).
- **Daoris choosing makers among whatever is installed** (D23, D155), **an account the scope does not list**, and **two
  doors of one maker counted as two makers**.
- **A cap in tokens or spend** (D54, D130 §9): a pass is one turn, and Daoris sees only its own share of an account.
- **The packet under `cache/`**: assembled again from moved sources it is a different packet, so it cannot be fetched.
- **The document on a shared host or as quest operations**: it carries the asker's records and this machine's accounts.
- **Starting one by itself** on the owner's install.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (BRAINSTORM1a, the stub round trip).** Built in the driver's own module, `Brainstorms/` (its `Domain`
the packet, the validators, the synthesis's anonymity, the document and the instructions), with the look in
`Loop/DriverBrainstorms.cs`, beside the helpers'; the structure gate names it a fifteenth module. What the build settled
that this entry left open:
- **Where the records are.** Two rows of D172's home table, in all three twins: `brainstorms` (data), one folder per
  brainstorm holding `request.json` (the verb's, never changed), `sent.json` (the person's send, naming the digest they
  saw), `state.json`, `packet.json`, `passes/`, `answers/` and `document.json` with `document.md`; and
  `brainstorm-rooms/*` (work), one room per turn, removed when the brainstorm ends.
- **The digest** is `sha256:` of the packet's content as written (LF, every field but `packet` and `digest`), so the file
  read back proves itself; a room holds the frozen file byte for byte.
- **The result tool's file.** `brainstorm_give` and `brainstorm_synthesize` are a file contract here: each turn's spawn names
  the file its tool writes (`DAORIS_BRAINSTORM_ANSWER`, outside the room, never in its instruction), and the stubs write
  it as the connector's tool will. The connector's tools themselves, and their posture (D72's amendment), are BRAINSTORM1a2;
  until then a turn is handed the reviewer's read-only rules held to its room, and no connector.
- **A pass's record is the brainstorm's own:** a turn runs on the driver's run machinery (`HoldAsync`, moved first) under a
  local id, `brainstorm-<id>-<turn>`, and opens no session record on the service, so nothing of it syncs. Its transcript is
  kept under `passes/`.
- **The repair** is a second turn, `<pass>r`, in a fresh room, handed the validator's codes in fixed words.
- **Daoris's flags on a group are carried by Daoris, not checked:** point 4's "every flag Daoris set on a member stays on its
  group" is met by construction, Daoris's flags beside any the synthesis adds, so a synthesis is never refused for leaving
  one out.
- **Makers** are told apart by the adapter set's family (`AgentFamily`): the native stub and the ACP stub are one maker, so
  their two answers are listed apart and the document says stubs are no maker diversity.
- **With no service** the packet's sources are the declared index's files whole (`RepositoryIndexes`), tier `files`; with
  one, `/api/search` in two wordings (the question, then its longer words), each hit's entry read whole, tier `meaning`
  where the search's header names the semantic tier, else `words`.

Not built here, each a row: the passes side by side within one deadline, the policy, the stop and the restart's
*interrupted* beyond a mark (BRAINSTORM1b); the connector's tools and posture (BRAINSTORM1a2); the CLI's twin through
`service.ts` and a family-rehearsal phase (BRAINSTORM1a3). What the checks do not cover: the driver's fast half holds the

**Note, 2026-10-19 (BRAINSTORM1a2, the result tools and the posture).** `brainstorm_give` and `brainstorm_synthesize` are the
connector's (`BrainstormTools`, the service's `BrainstormBox`), and a pass is handed them under the `brainstorm` posture
(`BrainstormPermissions`, D72's amendment). What the build settled:
- **The connector carries the turn whole**: `DAORIS_BRAINSTORM_ID`, `_TURN`, `_ANSWER` and a fourth, `DAORIS_BRAINSTORM_ROOM`,
  on the connector alone, since a maker hands a server only its entry's variables (CONNECTOR2). It is the pass's only server
  on either door, as a reviewer's is. A machine with no connector starts no pass (`no-connector`): a pass answers only through
  its tool, so no account is spent on a turn that cannot.
- **Each tool answers its own kind of turn** (`g…` give, `s…` synthesize) and refuses every other connector, writing nothing.
- **The tool judges as the driver will, and says it.** The service's `BrainstormChecks` twin the driver's validators; they share
