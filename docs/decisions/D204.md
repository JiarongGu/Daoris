## D204 — The person's git acts and writes (GITAPP2): the ref and remote acts, linear history edits from objects, and the working-tree acts in their own checkout behind a switch and one checkout lock, each write saying how it recovers; split from D202 and not yet read (2026-10-23)

**Decision (GITAPP2).** **Status: proposed, not yet read.** Split out of D202 in GITAPP1's round 3 (D191), when the steward narrowed
D202 to Review and the reader so they can be accepted and built first. The contract is `docs/2026-10-23-git-writes-design.md`, which
states round 2's write half unchanged and lists, as its open questions, Codex's round-2 findings 11-16 and the round-1 write findings
that read marks open or wrong (22, 24; 20's remainder). It is read later under D191; it is not read in GITAPP1's round 3, and nothing
of it is built before it is accepted.

What round 2 proposed, and this decision carries until its read:

1. **The ref and remote acts**: fetch, create, rename and delete a local branch, local tags, push one branch at a commit, through one
   `update-ref --stdin` transaction per act on the judged-delete pattern, with a recovery ref.
2. **Linear history edits with no working tree**: pick, revert and rebase a range of non-merge, non-root, unpublished commits onto a
   branch no tree has checked out, from `merge-tree --merge-base` and `commit-tree`, applied by one ref transaction.
3. **The working-tree acts in the person's own checkout, behind the switch** *Daoris may change this checkout's files* in Setup's
   Reach, off by default: stage and unstage by file, hunk and line from separate HEAD → index and index → worktree comparisons, commit,
   amend, stash, switch and resolve, in a Changes tab, every act re-judged under one **checkout lock** that the merge landing and a
   conversation's start in the checkout also take. Refused: a hard reset, `clean`, any force, a remote delete, a recursive submodule
   write, `push -u`, and any act while a conversation runs there or a landing holds it. A session's tree is never written by a person's
   act.
4. **Every write says how it recovers**; plans name the hooks an act runs; Daoris's own fetch runs no maintenance.
5. **D198's prerequisites**: the doors installed for a remote act; GITAUTH1f and a grant the press writes for a person's push; GITAUTH1g
   for a tag push; a terminal writing verb a person's press through `PersonPresses`.

**Open before it can be accepted** (the design's §7): D109's line pull under the checkout lock; one operation per ref in a transaction;
conditional rollback per operation rather than create-only put-back; identity-checked stash drop or no Drop; bounded, interruption-safe
write outcomes with a durable act journal; stash conflicts not shipped before Resolve.

**Why split.** Review's speed and the reader are wanted first and stand alone: nothing in them writes, and every act reads through
them. Keeping the acts in D202 held the reads behind six write findings the reads do not depend on.

**Rejected**, as round 2 weighed them. The person's acts in a session's tree: its trace and its transcript would not explain
commits it never made, and its session may write there at that moment. Acts that discard work (a hard reset, `clean`, any force, a
remote delete): no recovery ref keeps uncommitted bytes. Rewriting history in a checked-out tree: the history edits run from
objects onto a branch no tree holds, so no working tree is touched. Keeping the acts in D202: they held the reads behind write
findings the reads do not depend on.

**This would amend**, on acceptance, D147 points 2 and 3, its rejection of checking out in the person's checkout, its design §2.8 and
§3.1, and D150 point 3, as the design's §6 says. Until then D147 and D150 stand as written.

**Note, 2026-10-31 (GITTXN1: the transaction builder exists; D204 is still proposed).** `RefTransaction` (`Workplace/RefTransaction.cs`) builds one `update-ref --stdin` operation per ref with its expectation, and the content holds' judged delete, its recovery-ref drop and its put-back run on it. Nothing is exposed to a person. The ref acts here are to be built on it when D204 is accepted.

**Note, 2026-10-31 (accepted after round 3).** The design (`docs/2026-10-23-git-writes-design.md`) was read three times under D191 (`docs/2026-10-31-git-writes-read-codex.md`): Codex returned rounds 1 and 2; the Opus foundation reader accepted round 2 after named changes; round 3 took every named change of both reads, and Codex confirmed each closed. What it decides, beyond round 2's proposal: one lock per checkout, keyed by its common directory, taken alone by every writer (the line pull and the whole promotion effect included) and shared by every session start in the checkout, driven or conversation, with no waiting acquisition under it; the line pull and the promotion deferred while a session is held there; the act journal a desktop-store family on the integration store's claim and step shape, each effect step in a job named for its act with its deadline journaled, nothing re-run on reconciliation; configuration and index recovered only under git's own lock; deletes' recovery under `refs/daoris/discarded/`, index snapshots and Resolve's stages alone under `refs/daoris/acts/`, which holds no commit for the clean-ups; delete and rename refusing Daoris's own branches; the fetch confined, `FETCH_HEAD` kept; Drop deferred. D147 points 2 and 3 and its design §2.8 and §3.1, and D150 point 3, are amended as the design's §11 says. Its rows are GITAPP2a to 2n, after GITLOCK1 and GITTXN2.

**Note, 2026-10-31 (GITAPP2a: the journal built over a fixture act).** What the code settled that the design left open. **The family's migration (store step 13) leaves the writer stamp where it is**: raising it would refuse a rolled-back build every family's writes. Each record carries its own `format`; a build reads one past its own, or a phase or step kind it does not know, as held, never taken or reconciled, refusing the checkout's writers. A build from before the family never reads it: the checkout lock keeps its writers from a live act, and nothing keeps them from one a crash left open. **One open act per checkout**, by a unique index on the common directory. **A fourth outcome, *partial***: some steps' postconditions read done and others not done, each read (the rename stopped between its ref and its section); *unknown* stays for a step that reads as neither or whose processes are unended. **The step's git joins its job at creation, suspended, and its pid is written while it cannot run**, so a cut before the pid write leaves a process that never ran, ended with the job's last handle. **A step said unended leaves its act open** for the reconciler, which ends a live step past its deadline through its job by name, or by its pid where the job is gone, and says one it cannot end with the pid, the Stop and the command to copy. **A reconciler that loses the take holds its writer for that look** unless the winner has closed the act; postconditions git cannot read write nothing and hold the writer too. **Under the checkout lock the journal waits on the store's write lock** (five seconds, a leaf whose holders wait for nothing; `LockWaits.Store`). A configuration section set in another scope refuses its move (`git config --show-scope`); an index's replacement is built on a copy in the git directory, removed after. The act ref's commit is objects only, made before the record so its id is journaled at *judged*; it expires thirty days after the close (provisional), deleted at its id by the driver's start, one moved since kept and said. Left to the first act a person can press (row c on): the standalone worker apart from the terminal, *Stop* as a press, the update's drain and the application's exit counting a running step, and *Finish* and *Undo*.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2h: stage and unstage built).** What the code settled that the design left open. **An act is one index step** over the path's entries, its `after` found first on a copy of the index in the git directory (`add -A`, `reset HEAD`, or `rm --cached` on an unborn branch for a file; `apply --cached --whitespace=nowarn`, `--reverse` for Unstage, of a patch built from the hunks for a hunk or lines), never on the index; the step then compares and replaces under `index.lock` as row a built it. **A press carries what the page read**: HEAD, the index entry (`mode blob`, empty where none) and the source's id (the worktree file's `hash-object --path` for Stage, HEAD's entry for Unstage), and the hunks by an id of their text; the plan and the judge under the lock each re-read them and refuse a difference, naming it. A file pressed whole has its faces read at the press and refused where the index entry differs from the one its list showed. **Lines**: an unchosen removal stays as context and an unchosen addition goes (reversed for Unstage), counts recomputed; a binary file, or one whose patch is not UTF-8, is pressed whole only. **Refused beyond §3's list**: a path in conflict (Resolve's), a checkout that is a linked tree (a session's, whatever the switch says) and the ledger not given. **Undo** is a second act (`stage-undo`) whose step is the first's rollback; it restores only where every entry still equals the act's result and names those that changed; an expired record, or one not *done*, is refused. The act ref holds the entries before and after. **`GIT_STAGE`, `GIT_UNSTAGE` and `GIT_UNDO` declare a new scope, `checkouts`** (the Changes tab's key), and are the window's in the pipe's census: no terminal verb names a hunk. Left: a list of past acts to undo after the page closes (Undo is offered for the last act pressed), and the other rows of §12.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2c: create a branch or a lightweight tag).** What the code settled. **A ref act asks no checkout switch and no session check**: it writes no working tree, and refs are shared by every linked tree already; it still takes the checkout lock alone and runs through the journal, so every other writer waits for it. **Create and its undo are two acts** (`ref.create`, `ref.undo`), each journaled whole: the undo is a `delete` at the id the create's record holds, refused where the ref stands anywhere else, is gone, or the record is no closed create. **Daoris's own `daoris/` namespace is refused for a branch**, as is a name that has a ref above or below it. **The person's confirmation is the page's own step** (the name, the commit and its short id, then *Confirm*); a terminal's `--yes` is the same route asked for its words and waits for the window's Confirm. **Unknown stays unretried**: `unknown` is said as the §10 sentence and offers no Undo. Left: the standalone worker apart from the terminal (the verb runs the act in its own process when no Daoris runs, Ctrl-C cancelling only before the effect), *Stop* and *Finish* as presses, the application's exit and the update's drain counting a running step, and Ask Daoris's card.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2m: pick, revert and rebase onto, without a working tree).** What the code settled (`Workplace/GitHistoryEdits.cs`). **The commits are made under the checkout lock, before the record**: `ActPlan.Make` runs once the judgement holds, so the configuration is read at the plan and again under the lock right before `merge-tree` starts, and a conflict, an empty result or git's no refuses with only objects written and nothing journaled; the ref step journaled is then one transaction, the branch's `update` from the tip read, its old tip's `create` under `refs/daoris/discarded/<branch>` (the next free number after it), and a rebase's remote-tracking refs `verify`d at the ids read. **A plan pins its ids** (the tip, the commit built on, each commit taken), and the press carries them back; one moved refuses. **Configured programs refused, named with their scope**: a `merge.<name>.driver` in any scope, `merge.renormalize` beside a filter's `clean`, `smudge` or `process`, and `commit.gpgSign`, since Daoris signs no commit it makes (`commit-tree --no-gpg-sign` besides). **Occupancy** counts a tree rebasing or bisecting the branch, read from its own git folder. **The put-back is the delete's way** (`ActPlan.After`, under the same lock): a tree found on the branch once it moved, or a list git could not give, sets it back only from the tip the edit produced and deletes its recovery ref at the old tip in one transaction, the act closing *not done* with why. The committer is `GitAuthor`'s (the checkout's own identity first, else origin's identity's `commitsAs`); a pick and a rebase keep each commit's author, name, email and date; a revert's message is git's. A commit whose message names another encoding is refused. **The plan is the press's own `plan: true` form** (`GIT_HISTORY_EDIT`, a rule on `plan` in the pipe's census), so the page reads it through a mutation and no new read is told by event. Progress is the terminal's (`made k of n`); the page says it works until the answer. Left: an undo (the old tip stays in *Kept for recovery*), the window's progress, the standalone worker, and Ask Daoris's card.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2e: rename a branch or a lightweight tag).** What the code settled. **One transaction moves the name and keeps the old tip**: the old name deleted at the tip, the new one made, the recovery ref under `refs/daoris/discarded/` made or verified, so the old name's reflog is not carried and the new name starts its own (said on the page and by the terminal). **Finish and Undo are acts of their own** (`ref.finish`, `ref.undo`): a rename stopped between its ref and its section is closed *partial*, and *Finish* moves the section alone while *Undo* moves the ref back alone, each judged under the lock against the journaled steps (the new name at the tip and the old gone, the old section as journaled and none at the new name); an undo of a done rename, or of a Finish, moves both steps back, and an undo of a rename already finished is refused in favour of the Finish's. **A target that appeared at another value before the effect reads *unknown***, not *not done*, because the postcondition is read per ref; nothing moved and the branch stays. The terminal verb has `--rename <new-name>` and no Finish. Left: Finish from the terminal and Ask Daoris's card.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2i: commit and amend built).** What the code settled. **A commit is a fourth step kind, `commit`**: one `git commit --cleanup=whitespace -F -` run in the act's job, so the hooks and their children end with it; nothing is passed to skip a hook, and the plan names the ones present (`pre-commit`, `prepare-commit-msg`, `commit-msg`, `post-commit`, `reference-transaction`, and `post-rewrite` for an amend). Git's auto maintenance is left on, as for the person's own commit. **The author is `GitAuthor`'s** (D198 point 5): the `commitsAs` of `origin`'s identity as `-c user.name/user.email`, only where the repository names none; a checkout where git has no identity at all is refused with the words to set one. **A press carries the tip and the index's tree the page read** (the tree made on a copy of the index, never writing it); the lock re-reads both, and refuses nothing staged, a file in conflict, and an amend with neither a changed tree nor a changed message. **Reconciled by `HEAD`**: the old tip means not done; a commit whose parents are the plan's (the old tip, or the old commit's parents for an amend) is done, and its message must equal the plan's unless a `commit-msg` or `prepare-commit-msg` hook is named, which may rewrite it; anything else is unknown. **A hook's rewrite is said** after a done commit (the files of the made tree that differ from the staged tree, a changed message) as the answer's `notes`; a hook's refusal is the commit step's words. **An amend keeps its old tip first** under `refs/daoris/discarded/amended/<branch>-<tip>` in a ref step of its own, and lets it go if the commit did not follow. There is no Undo of a commit (it is `reset --soft`, a row of its own). Left: the terminal verbs `git checkout <repository> commit|amend`.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2j: resolve a conflicted file built).** What the code settled. **A fifth step kind, `file`**: the worktree file's bytes replaced atomically (a copy beside it, then a rename) only where its SHA-256 still equals the plan's; the bytes it replaces and the bytes it writes are files in `<home>/act-files/<act>/`, made before the record and let go with the record's expiry; its postcondition is the file's digest (the plan's is done, the old one not done, any other unknown). **Two acts, as stage's two**: *Resolve* (`resolve`, the file step, the three stages held by the act's own ref) and *Mark resolved* (`resolve-mark`, one index step whose `after` is found by `git add` on a copy of the index, the stages before and the entry after held by its ref); Undo of the first puts the old bytes back where the file still equals what was written, of the second restores the stages where each entry still equals the mark's result. **The conflicts come from `merge-file --object-id -p` on the three stages**, with the conflict style set aside and a marker size of fifteen, read back as text between conflicts and the two sides of each; a press must take a side for every conflict, once each, so a file is never left half marked. Refused beyond §8: a file with no stage 1, 2 or 3 (a side deleted, or added on both sides, is kept or removed whole), a binary or non-UTF-8 file, one that begins with a byte-order mark, a link, and a mark of a file that still holds git's conflict markers. **Resolve and Mark resolved are the acts made in a merge, pick, revert or rebase**, so they alone of the Changes tab's acts do not refuse for `MERGE_HEAD` and its kin (everything else of §3 still holds); they never continue the operation. **An index step removes first and adds after**: `update-index --index-info` takes every stage of a path with a removal line, so an entry replacing stages 1 to 3 would have been removed by its own removal. Left: a conflict taken line by line, a side deleted or added on both sides, and the operation's own continue and abort.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2k: switch and stash Save built).** What the code settled that the design left open. **Two step kinds, `switch` and `stash`**, each one git run once in the act's job (`switch --no-guess <branch>`, never forced; `stash push`, with `--include-untracked` only when asked, as `-c` author words like the commit's), read back from HEAD against the branch asked and the commit it stood on, and from `refs/stash` against the tip the plan read and HEAD as the new stash's first parent; an exit code proves nothing. **A Save also reads `refs/stash.lock`** with git's other lock files before it runs, since the stash is common to every linked tree. **A switch is refused dirty on tracked files only**: untracked files are no dirt, and one in the way of the branch is git's own refusal, closed not done with git's words. It is also refused for the branch the checkout is on, a branch out in another tree (named by the tree's folder, never its path), a name under `daoris/` (the namespace create refuses) and a name that is no local branch. **Undone by switching back** means no Undo route: the answer's notes name the branch the switch left, and the next press is judged on the same terms, so it is refused if tracked files changed meanwhile. **Stash Save is refused where the shared stash moved** since the plan: the press carries the tip of `refs/stash` it read, since another tree's Save (a session's included) would otherwise be invisible to the page; the plan counts the other trees that share it. The Save's notes name the stash commit and `git stash apply <id>`, as Apply is row l and Drop stays deferred. The page offers local branches of kind `line` and `yours`; a session's or landed branch is not offered. Left: the terminal verbs `git checkout <repository> switch|stash`.

**Note, 2026-11-01 (GITAPP2f: fetch origin's branches).** What the code settled. **The command is fixed, not configured**: `--refmap=` with the refspec given turns every configured `remote.origin.fetch` off, and `--no-tags` and `-c remote.origin.pruneTags=false` outrank `tagOpt` and `pruneTags`, so a hostile setting moves nothing outside `refs/remotes/origin/*` and needs no refusal; the refusals are the settings that run another program or mirror, which beyond `uploadpack`, `receivepack` and `mirror` include `remote.origin.vcs`. **A fetch's postcondition is origin's state, which no local read knows**: the step is *done* on a recorded exit 0 and, with no end recorded, *not done* where the tracking refs stand as the plan kept them and *unknown* where they moved; the refs as they stood are the recovery, a fetch being repeatable, and a fetch has no Undo. **Offered** is read fresh from the install (both doors laid beside the home), the origin remote and its settings; a hold on origin (GITAUTH1a) refuses it under the lock as the line fetch's does. Left: Ask Daoris's card.
