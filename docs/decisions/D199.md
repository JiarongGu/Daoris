## D199 — Agents of different makers work in one session as a lead and its partners: another maker writes a piece in a clone of its own and the lead takes it in, a helper reads the work in progress, and each provider is one sheet of capabilities, declared, negotiated or measured (2026-10-22)

**Decision (COAGENT1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, round 2: a synthesis of three independent proposals (Claude's, Codex's and
Fable's, under D191), revised after Codex's read and Fable's foundation read of round 1 (the note below). The contract is
`docs/2026-10-22-coagent-design.md`; its §1 compares the three proposals question by question and says why each difference was settled
as it was, and its §12 keeps both reads, finding by finding.

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-22: Daoris's sessions work with Codex now, and with other makers' agents later, inside the same
session; that needs one interface every agent provider meets, so a later maker joins by declaring what it can do; the test plan is part
of the design, and the feature is built through the owner's install, which holds a Codex account signed in for Daoris. Read at
`11bfe3f70`, another maker's agent reaches a session's work as an opinion (D155), a helper reading the last commit (D163), a successor
(D163, D189) or a meeting with no tree (D177); an entry's parts may already run on two makers where a handed quest names its agent; and
no single place says what any provider can do (design §0).

1. **Working together in one session** is a session, the **lead**, with **partners** serving it: each a record of its own on another
   maker's agent, carrying `serves` (the lead) and `piece` (the ask) and its own entry, as a helper's record does, so no rule that holds
   for a helper is loosened for a partner. A piece belongs to the record that asked for it and passes along its `from` chain. A **helper**
   and a **reviewer** read, as before. One writer per tree: D51 and the one-session-per-tree question stand unchanged.
2. **A piece** is asked by the person (a press, a terminal verb, naming its agent) or by the lead (`partner_ask`, with no level) under
   the person's rule `partners` (`ask` by default, `on`, `off`), over the agents the rule lists, a named agent it does not list refused.
   The ask keeps the exact commit it grows from. Its partner works one turn within its minutes in **a clone of its own with no remote**,
   at that commit, and asks nobody. At its end, whatever its cause, Daoris commits what it left uncommitted, imports the tip into the
   repository as a frozen ref, and adds what it reads from git beside the partner's answer. At most two pieces are open per lead, counted
   from their reservation; a partner asks for no partner, helper, opinion, question or quest.
3. **A piece lands only through its lead**: the lead merges the frozen tip in its own tree, in its own turn, and the piece is taken in
   when its tip is an ancestor of the lead's branch. Every landing door refuses a partner's record; a piece never taken in stays as its
   ref until the person discards it.
4. **A partner is admitted only where its confinement was measured** on its harness version, adapter version and posture; otherwise it
   is refused at every start, the person's own included. Its role is enforced by the server from its binding (an allow-list of tools,
   held by a census), not only by rules an agent may not take.
5. **A helper reads the work in progress.** Its copy carries the asker's uncommitted bytes, captured at a turn boundary with a
   manifest, refused where it cannot be captured whole, and nothing is read back from it. The opinion's copy is unchanged.
6. **Changing who leads is the hand-over** (D163, D189), unchanged; the hand-over takes the lead's pieces along `from`.
7. **Each provider is one sheet**, `AgentCapabilities`, derived from the adapter, the manifest, the readings the roster keeps of what
   the protocol door negotiated, and a code table of measurements (agent, versions, posture, capability, outcome, evidence) held by a
   census to documents in the repository. Each capability says its source, declared, negotiated, measured or absent, and each absence has
   a named fallback; a different version or posture reads declared until measured again. Safety rests only on a copy or clone of its own
   and on measured rows. A plugin's harness joins by its manifest, which gains `fullPosture` and `askingPosture` and may not claim
   queueing, steering, resume, rules, limit words, lineage or confinement.
8. **The agents talk only through Daoris's connector and the co-working skill**, D188 whole: a piece's answer is an ordinary solicited
   answer under a stable key, delivered once to its lead or the lead's live successor, never into a left conversation, reopening an ended
   lead once per ask and counted by the answers-alone bound; a piece's notices make no turn happen; nobody but the person sends *now*.
9. **Accounts and usage** follow D197: each part on one account of its agent's one set, a partner's by the walk over the listed agents; a
   limit cools the account and ends the piece `limited` with its work kept; a partner's record takes a slot as a helper's does (CAPCOUNT1
   unchanged); usage is each part's, summed per maker on the head, absent never zero; each answer names the agent, account and version
   that ran, and the model where its door reported one; a partner whose piece a candidate holds is one of its families for D155's walk.
10. **D155's and D163's rejection of agents conversing with no person between stands.** The only message that moves a run is a piece's
    answer, which the person's rule allowed from an agent it listed; a partner cannot ask back, refused by the server; every answer is a
    go-on, so two in a row park the line on the person; what returns is a frozen commit the lead must take in, with facts Daoris read;
    agreement establishes nothing. What narrows is D163's rejection of writing helpers in a first version, which waited for a landing
    design: points 2 and 3 are that landing.

**Why.** The owner asked for another maker working inside the session, and only a writing part does work; a reader alone leaves every
change to one maker, and goes first only because it is small and safe. Most of the partner exists: the helper's record, box, choice,
copy and hand, the brief, the walk. A clone of its own keeps the partner's whole git state in the one folder its posture may write,
where a linked worktree would share the repository's refs, configuration and objects; Daoris's own commit and import make the delivery
independent of what the agent's sandbox allows. A measurement before a writing start, and refusals by the server, keep safety off
declarations and off rules an agent may not take. Fresh pieces need nothing unmeasured, where a kept conversation needs resume, which has
not run here.

**Rejected.** A second agent in the lead's tree, at once or in turn; a partner in a linked worktree of the repository; a partner's record
on the lead's entry; a reader in a copy at the last commit only; turns Daoris grants between the lead and a partner; an urgent level on a
piece; a conversation kept across pieces before resume is measured, and a swap that keeps both conversations (D189 point 6); a co-work
record beside the boxes; enrolling the person's conversation under the cap; a partner's work landed past its lead, or a cherry-pick read
as taken in; an unmeasured provider admitted as a partner; a role enforced only by rules; a manifest that claims what only the wire or a
measurement can say; an automatic repair after a limit in these rows; *co-work* and *writer*/*navigator* as names. The design's §1 and
§10 say why each.

**Amends,** once accepted: D163 (point 2 gains the partner; its rejection of writing helpers in a first version is spent; a helper's copy
carries the work in progress); D64 (the manifest's two postures); D72 (a partner's shape of the rules); D177 point 5 (its writing
participant is a session's partner in a clone of its own). D155 point 4 applies to a partner's piece, and its note says so. Each carries
a note pointing here. D51, D53, D131, D167 with ACCEPTALL1, D188, D189, D197, CAPCOUNT1, D24, D46 and D47 §4 stand.

**The rows** are the design's §9: COAGENT1a (another maker reads the work in progress: the first usable on the install, with M1 and M4),
1b (a partner writes one piece for a driven session, asked by the person; M2 in a disposable repository before its first use, then M3),
1c (a conversation's lead), 1d (the lead's ask under the rule; then the dogfood), 1e (the provider sheet whole and a maker joining by its
manifest, with the rehearsal's phase), 1f (measured both ways), 1g (a hand-over waits for the outgoing part's descendants).

**What the checks do not cover.** Documents only; nothing is built. The statements about today were read from the code at `6dc4d7803`
and checked at `11bfe3f70`; those about Codex's confinement, its connector's join, its commit in a clone, its turn counts and resume are
not measured (design §11). `verify` checks this entry's place and shape and the design's links, and none of their words.

  mirrored as a plain part, let through every door that refuses `serves`, and read as a free conversation by the cap (Fable F2; Codex 11,

  role rested on rules Codex does not take (Codex 10, 11; Fable F7): confinement measured before every start; an allow-list by role on

  holds; the home entry, the driver doors and the pipe words.
