## D199 — Agents of different makers work in one session as a lead and its partners: another maker writes a piece in a tree of its own and the lead takes it in, a helper reads the work in progress, and each provider is one sheet of capabilities, declared, negotiated or measured (2026-10-22)

**Decision (COAGENT1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, a synthesis of three independent proposals (Claude's, Codex's and Fable's,
under D191); Codex's read and Fable's are owed, Fable's because the design changes a foundation (the session record, an entry's
landing, a box between the service and the driver). The contract is `docs/2026-10-22-coagent-design.md`; its §1 compares the three
proposals question by question and says why each difference was settled as it was.

The owner set the direction on 2026-10-22: Daoris's sessions work with Codex now, and with other makers' agents later, inside the same
session; that needs one interface every agent provider meets, so a later maker joins by declaring what it can do; the test plan is part
of the design, and the feature is built through the owner's install, which holds a Codex account signed in for Daoris. Read at
`56f47f123`, another maker's agent reaches a session only from outside it (an opinion, D155; a helper, D163), after it (a hand-over,
D163, D189) or with no tree (a meeting, D177), and no single place says what any provider can do (design §0).

1. **Working together in one session** is an entry (D167) whose parts run on agents of more than one maker. The **lead** holds the
   entry's tree, its plan and its quest. A **partner** is a part of the same entry on another maker's agent that writes one **piece**
   in a tree of its own. A **helper** and a **reviewer** read, as before. One writer per tree: D51 and the one-session-per-tree
   question stand unchanged, since no second agent ever runs in the lead's tree.
2. **A piece** is asked by the person (a press, a terminal verb) or by the lead (`partner_ask`) under the person's rule `partners`
   (`ask` by default, `on`, `off`). Its partner is chosen as a helper is, another maker first; its tree grows from the lead's last
   commit; it takes one turn within its minutes, asks nobody, and answers with `partner_answer`, beside which Daoris puts what it reads
   from git itself (the branch, the commits, the files, those beyond the piece, the checks run). At most two pieces are open per lead,
   and a partner asks for no partner, helper or opinion.
3. **A piece lands only through its lead.** The answer reaches the lead as a solicited answer with the line that takes it in; the lead
   merges it in its own tree, in its own turn. An entry's Accept leaves a partner's part out, and a piece never taken in stays on its
   branch until the person discards it.
4. **A helper reads the work in progress.** Its copy carries the asker's uncommitted bytes, captured at a turn boundary with a
   manifest, refused where it cannot be captured whole, and nothing is read back from it. The opinion's copy is unchanged.
5. **Changing who leads is the hand-over** (D163, D189), unchanged.
6. **Each provider is one sheet**, `AgentCapabilities`, derived from the adapter, the manifest, what the protocol door negotiates and
   the evidence documents, never stored apart from them. Each capability says its source: declared, negotiated, measured (naming the
   harness version, so another version reads declared again) or absent, and each absence has a named fallback. Safety rests only on a
   tree or copy of its own and on measured rows. A plugin's harness joins by its manifest, which gains `fullPosture` and `askingPosture`
   and may not claim words at the next step, steering, resume, rules, limit words or lineage.
7. **The agents talk only through Daoris's connector and the co-working skill**, D188 whole: a partner's answer is a solicited answer at
   the ask's level, reopening its lead once per ask and counted by the answers-alone bound; an entry with no ask gains a channel of its
   own; nobody but the person sends *now*.
8. **Accounts and usage** follow D197: each part on one account of its agent's one set, a partner's by the walk; a limit cools the
   account and carries the piece on once; a partner's start takes a slot as a helper's does (CAPCOUNT1 unchanged); usage is each
   part's, summed per maker on the head, absent never zero; each answer names the agent, account and version that ran, and the model
   where its door reported one.
9. **D155's and D163's rejection of agents conversing with no person between stands.** The only message that moves a run is an
   answer the person's rule allowed; a partner cannot ask back; two answers alone in a row park the line on the person; what returns
   is a branch the lead must take in, with facts Daoris read; agreement establishes nothing. What narrows is D163's rejection of
   writing helpers in a first version, which waited for a landing design: point 3 is that landing.

**Why.** The owner asked for another maker working inside the session, and only a writing part does work; a reader alone leaves every
change to one maker. Most of the partner exists: a further part in a tree of its own, a tree grown from a named branch, the helper's
box, choice and hand, the brief, ACCEPTALL1's entry. A tree of its own is prevention where a shared tree could only detect, and for
Codex, which takes no rules file, it is the only floor. Fresh pieces need nothing unmeasured, where a kept conversation needs resume,
which has not run here. A sheet derived from what exists cannot drift from it, and a version on every measurement keeps a stale one
from passing as true.

**Rejected.** A second agent in the lead's tree, at once or in turn, with a snapshot compared after each reading (it amends the
exclusion every open relies on and detects a write only after it happened); a reader in a copy at the last commit only; turns Daoris
grants between the lead and a partner, after each changed turn or within a grant (they spend the second maker unasked, or bound what
D188 already bounds); a conversation kept across pieces before resume is measured, and a swap that keeps both conversations (D189
point 6); a co-work record beside the boxes and the record (D173's one-store guard); enrolling the person's conversation under the cap
(CAPCOUNT1's reason); a partner's work landed past its lead; a partner's edits applied as a patch; a manifest that claims what only the
wire or a measurement can say; *co-work* and *writer*/*navigator* as names (D167's word; a pair not built). The design's §1 and §10 say
why each.

**Amends,** once accepted: D163 (point 2 gains the partner; its rejection of writing helpers in a first version is spent; a helper's
copy carries the work in progress); D167 (a partner's part takes its lead's entry; ACCEPTALL1's Accept leaves it out); D64 (the
manifest's two postures); D72 (a partner's shape of the rules); D177 point 5 (its writing participant is a session's partner in its own
tree). Each carries a note pointing here. D51, D53, D131, D155, D188, D189, D197, D24, D46 and D47 §4 stand.

**The rows** are the design's §9: COAGENT1a (a partner by the person's press, Codex the second maker, with the first measurements on the
install), 1b (the lead's ask under the rule; then the dogfood), 1c (a helper reads the work in progress), 1d (the provider sheet and a
maker joining by its manifest, with the rehearsal's phase), 1e (the line's channel), 1f (measured both ways), 1g (a hand-over waits for
the outgoing part's descendants).

**What the checks do not cover.** Documents only; nothing is built. The statements about today were read from the code at `6dc4d7803`
and checked at `56f47f123`; those about Codex's confinement, its connector's join, its turn usage and resume are not measured
(design §11). `verify` checks this entry's place and shape and the design's links, and none of their words.
