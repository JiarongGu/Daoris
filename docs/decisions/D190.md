## D190 — A run bound to a graph version is governed by its graph: two engines, never on one run; the run is the unit of its node record; `WorkflowProcesses` stays the binding and gate interface; the look goes on beside an opinion that owes only the person's next press, a go-ahead holds the landing, and a look the work adds stays the gate's hold (2026-10-20)

**Decision (FABRIC-CUTOVER1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, pending the owner's review, after another maker's read. No row
builds before the owner's yes.

Read at `d3d0d15ce`, neither engine governs a run bound to a schema-2 (graph) version. The steps-driven process holds it as
unrun (`WorkflowProcesses.cs:60`), so every landing door refuses it, and the node runtime skips any quest bound to a named
version (`NodeRuntime.cs:270-272`) and arms on the choices alone (`NodeRuntime.cs:222-226`). This decides the cutover and what
the block's other rows left open: FABRIC-WORKFLOW2b's two named differences, FABRIC-WORKFLOW2c, FABRIC-GRAPH1e, FABRIC-GRAPH1j,
FABRIC-RUN1f, and the unit a quest's opinion acts on (D173's FABRICRUN1 note). The contract is
`docs/2026-10-20-fabric-cutover-design.md`.

1. **Two engines, never on one run.** A run bound to a schema-1 version stays D157's to its end. A run bound to a schema-2
   version is the node runtime's. No run is governed by a decoded graph, so a steps version reaches the runtime only as a new
   version a person saves as a graph, never by a migration. A version's meaning is what its engine does with it, and its run
   keeps it (D157 point 6). The decoder keeps canary 38's read-only comparison, the drawing of a steps version as a graph,
   and the graph such a save writes. FABRIC-RUN1c's left part is withdrawn. Design §1.
2. **The run is the unit of its node record.** A bound schema-2 run is the runtime's unit `run:<run>`. Its canonical record is
   its binding, and its node record holds every node of its graph. Each node observes the subject its kind names: `claimed`
   the run's work (its chain's quests here); the gates, the landing's nodes and a plugin's nodes the run's branch, at its tip;
   a `go-ahead` the run's ask. So a quest's opinion and look act on its run's branch and are recorded on the run's record.
   Current's graphs never govern a run's quests or branches. Design §2.
3. **Armed and governed by binding.** A run bound to a schema-2 version arms the runtime until its record says it ended, though
   its graph names no performed node; a saved graph version no run binds arms nothing; a home with no runs folder costs one
   stat. The runtime is the record's one writer, and begins a `go-ahead` to observe D135's answers. A door keeps the person's
   word on the record, and the next pass applies it. A schema-1 run is unchanged. Design §3.
4. **`WorkflowProcesses` stays the binding and gate interface.** The binding is unchanged. A graph version is read as a process
   from its gate nodes' options, each choosing among what the repository declares. A named version's graph holds one `handed`
   and at most one `opinion` and one `look`, judged where a version is read in both twins. A run's landing gate gains the
   graph's part, after the look: the run's record evaluated read-only with the door's own reads, holding while `handed` is not
   offered. The opinion is asked, and the look set up, only once their node has begun. Design §4.
5. **The look goes on beside an opinion that owes only the person's next press.** Disputed findings and commits since are
   answered by the look's *Reviewed* or the landing's *Accept…* (D155), so the `opinion` node is reached when they are all its
   gate owes, and the landing door still judges them. An opinion being read, unread, or required with none to give holds the
   look. Design §5.1.
6. **A go-ahead holds the landing in a graph,** until the person's yes or their skip with words. Only a reach or the person's
   skip satisfies an `after` wire (§E2). Schema 1 keeps going on past one it cannot start. Design §5.2.
7. **A look the work's choice adds stays the gate's hold, outside the graph.** The run's graph and its digest stay the
   version's (D157 point 6; the workflow design §4.6), and D154's gate holds every landing door, as in schema 1. Design §6.
8. **The graph rules.** No look and no landing can start with no opinion passed, on any way (FABRIC-GRAPH1e), judged wherever a
   graph is, under `protected-order`. Two observed nodes of one plugin step are refused where a steps version is saved as a
   graph, and nowhere else, since no run binds a decoded graph (FABRIC-GRAPH1j). Design §7.
9. **A reach survives an edit that leaves its place.** On a live graph, an acceptance binds its node's place (its declaration,
   the wires into it, whether it is an entry) where it bound the whole graph, and a skip, a rejection, a failure kept for the
   person and a firing hold by it. An acceptance kept before reads as it was, so nothing lapses at the upgrade
   (FABRIC-RUN1f). Design §8.

**Not settled: FABRIC-CLOSE1k,** a quest bound at its take to a closure graph Setup has edited since. Keep the bound graph, or
make its close the person's: the owner's choice, set out with each one's cost in design §9.

**Rejected** (design §11): running a schema-1 run on its decoded graph; each node on its subject's own record; the look held
behind a disputed opinion; leaving the look's timing to the review's gate; a wire or receipt an opinion's reading satisfies;
the landing going on past a go-ahead not started; a node the work's choice adds under a derived digest; a whole-graph lapse
kept with a warning; the landing door judging on the runtime's record alone; two landings, looks or opinions in a named
version's graph.

*2026-10-20, revised after two makers' reads (D191):* Fable and Codex read the first design independently, and the steward checked
each of their 21 findings against the code at `d0e51b54b`; all but one held as stated (design §13). The decision stays proposed,
and the same two read the revision before the steward settles it. Nothing in it waits for the owner. What changed:

- **Point 1 stands, its premise corrected.** A quest bound at its take to a closure graph is that graph's whatever its run, so a
  named run's quest could have two engines. The design now names the one writer of each record (the quest's, its branches', the
  run's) for every combination of a run's binding and a closure binding (design §1).
- **Point 2 keeps the run's own record and gives each node a subject.** The pass names each node's subject (the run's newest quest
  here, its newest session's branch at the tip read, or the run itself); a binding names its subject and lapses when it moves, so a
  replaced branch at an equal commit carries no reach over, and each node binds its own subject's revision, so a commit no longer
  lapses `claimed`. Keeping each node on its subject's record instead is rejected: those records have other writers first, and a
  run's subjects move as its chain grows (design §2).
- **Attribution.** A run's plugin nodes are told under its own session's branch, which no other run is told; a go-ahead answers a run
  only where its sessions or quests asked it; `handed` is the newest session's work handed on, and a new session's work lapses it
  (design §2.4).
- **Point 3.** Arming already counts a home's kept closure graphs; a run's `go-ahead` begun by the runtime is named as a change of a
  pressed kind; the runtime starts no plugin's performed node for a run until its doors read it (design §3).
- **Point 4's door no longer evaluates.** It reads the run's record and lands only where the last pass made `handed` active at the
  commit the door would land; otherwise it holds, says what the graph waits on, and asks for a pass (design §4).
- **Point 5 is replaced.** The look's timing is the review gate's, whose set-up waits while an agent reads the opinion, not a wire
  from the opinion; the opinion node is reached only when settled, never on a dispute; the graph rules stop asking the opinion to
  reach the look, and the decoder follows them (design §5.1).
- **Point 6 stands,** the person's skip built before the doors hold on a graph (design §5.2). **Point 7 stands** as a compromise both
  readers accept, revisited once a reach survives an edit (design §6).
- **Point 8.** FABRIC-GRAPH1e is dropped: `protected-wire` already holds the opinion before every landing. FABRIC-GRAPH1j reads a steps
  version by its shape and saves its synthetic nodes as authored ones (design §7).
- **Point 9 is replaced.** A live graph's run is named by its unit alone, and each node's place enters a skip's scope, a failure's
  and a rejection's run, the receipts' ids, the firing keys and the acceptance's graph; records written before read as they did
  (design §9).
- **Added: the binding moment.** A run is bound at its first start before its take, so the take names the graph its run is bound to
  and a schema-1 run's quest is never bound to a closure graph; a run's graph holding `quest.close` holds no `claimed` before it. This
  amends D157 point 11 (design §8).
- **No longer for the owner.** FABRIC-CLOSE1k keeps the bound graph, which already survives an edit, and its row is the saying; WORKFLOW1j
  and WORKFLOW1k are graph nodes, FABRIC-PERFORM1l's (design §8, §10).
- **Rejected,** now: the run's nodes on their subjects' records; the landed branch told after `handed`; one revision for a whole run;
  the door evaluating; an opinion reached on a dispute; a required wire from the opinion into the look; GRAPH1e's rule; a `claimed`
  read at a submission; the take's graph chosen apart from the binding; the person's close for CLOSE1k; a steps engine for `check` and
  `stage`. Leaving the look's timing to the review's gate, and the door judging on the record alone, were rejected first and are
  decided now (design §12).
- **Amends,** once accepted, D157 point 11 too; D157 and D173 carry notes pointing here.
- **The rows** are design §11, built in the order a, b, c, e, f, d, g, with RUN1f, GRAPH1j and CLOSE1k beside them. They replace the
  backlog's FABRIC-CUTOVER1a to 1g, FABRIC-GRAPH1e (removed), FABRIC-GRAPH1j, FABRIC-RUN1f, FABRIC-CLOSE1k, FABRIC-WORKFLOW2c and
  FABRIC-RUN1c's left part, and point WORKFLOW1j and WORKFLOW1k at FABRIC-PERFORM1l.
