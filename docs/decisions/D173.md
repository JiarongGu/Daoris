## D173 — Daoris extends agents' collaboration as a Collaboration Fabric: the owner's unified proposal accepted as written (2026-10-16)

**Decision (the owner's).** The owner brought in `docs/DAORIS_UNIFIED_COLLABORATION_ARCHITECTURE_AND_PLAN_2026-10-16.md`
and, asked how to settle its six architecture decisions (§12, ADR-A to ADR-F), chose *"Accept the proposal as written"*
on 2026-10-16, and said why: *"what we have right now is working, but I can see the limitation, so we need a better
foundation."* So, as that proposal states them:

- **ADR-A.** A Collaboration Fabric is the first-class substrate; a deterministic scheduler, a DAG or a workflow is an
  optional pattern over it, never the core ontology (§2). Quest, Chain, Workflow, Session, Helper, Ask and the driver
  are adapters and projections over it, kept with their meanings (§8: no second authoritative Quest or Session state).
- **ADR-B.** `Participant` and `RuntimeBinding` identity (§3), with explicit visibility levels, opaque to recoverable
  (§4), and partial visibility shown truthfully.
- **ADR-C.** The capability transport is MCP first, with a native adapter where a harness needs one, for exact supported
  harness versions, measured (§4).
- **ADR-D.** Messages are durable, correlated and deduplicated, and say queued, acknowledged, delivered and acted upon
  apart (§6), compatible with the existing sources of truth.
- **ADR-E.** Long-lived responsibility outlives the agents that act on it, with explicit handoff (§3 `Commitment`, §4).
- **ADR-F.** Credentials and permissions are scoped per participant: a descendant inherits no key, publishing right or
  account secret; the gateway enforces grants at the executor, never by model text (§7).

The proposal's principles (§1), phases and exit criteria (§10), ten canaries (§11) and review checklist (§14) are the
contract its work is accepted against. Its §9 stands: correctness work stays first, and the fabric's work runs beside it
in isolated branches. Per §12, the backlog holds only the C0–C2 slice; C3–C7 wait for contract tests to justify them.

### Guards for the build (a second opinion, verified against the code; they shape the build, they do not reverse the decision)

- **The fabric is the foundation; today's pieces move onto it, one concept at a time, with one source of truth each.**
  The owner chose a new foundation over growing today's pieces further. Daoris already has early forms of its parts: the
  knowledge connector's MCP tools (ADR-C's transport), an ask's channel and its entries (D167; ADR-D's records), quests
  with D163's hand-over and helpers (ADR-E's responsibility; the helper as one collaboration), the state pipeline
  (D169). C0 maps each primitive onto what exists and names which store becomes canonical; where a fabric primitive
  takes a concept over, it becomes that concept's store, with a mapping and a migration, and the old surface becomes
  its adapter (§8). Never two authoritative stores for one concept at once, and never a second mutation path beside the
  shared handlers (§14; D171 point 3).
- **A child's identity is issued, not inherited.** A native subagent that inherits its parent's environment would call
  as the parent (the connector reads `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`): a distinct restricted binding, issued locally, before a caller
  counts as a connected child (ADR-B, ADR-F). Reading keeps D107's rules; a change to Daoris keeps D89 and D170's
  proposal-and-Apply.
- **Measured, per harness and version.** Whether a native child inherits MCP servers and rules, carries a trustworthy
  identifier, and what a parent's stop does to it, is observed with real child-originated calls on Claude Code and
  Codex, never assumed (§4, §11). The owner approved small probes on their accounts on 2026-10-16.
- **The first slice stays C0–C2.** §10's illustrative vertical slice (a helper, a cross-tree message, an artifact, a
  delegated action) reaches C3–C4; the first rows prove binding and read-only access only.

### Rejected

- **One canonical execution graph or a scheduler that assigns all agent work** (the proposal's superseded table): agents
  negotiate, delegate natively and coordinate themselves; a graph or DAG is an opt-in plan, never the core.
- **Only Daoris-spawned root agents as participants**: harness-native subagents and their descendants are connectable.
- **Assuming every harness gives the same subagent telemetry**: capability and visibility levels, degraded honestly.
- **Holding ADR-A and growing today's pieces instead** (the second opinion's recommendation): the owner chose the new
  foundation, because what works today shows its limits.
- **Holding all six decisions until later**: offered, and not chosen.

### Supersedes

- `docs/DAORIS_FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`, now the proposal's Appendix A, and the proposal's own earlier sources, as its §0
  says: where they conflict, §0–§14 take precedence.
- D65's "no other engine" stands in this sense: the fabric is a substrate of interoperability, not a competing workflow
  engine (§9's revised interpretation).

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-C0, the contract audit, with both harnesses measured).** The matrix and receipts are
`docs/2026-10-16-fabric-contract-audit.md`. Each existing concept has one store, and the fabric's primitives map onto
them: messages onto the channel and a helper's files, commitments onto quests and hand-over, references onto knowledge ids,
authority onto proposal-and-Apply and the handed rules. The one store missing is a native child's binding to its part.

The second guard's hazard is observed, not inferred. On Claude Code 2.1.299 a native subagent, and its own subagent at
depth 2, call through the parent's very connection. On Codex 0.160.0 each thread starts its own server process, but from
the parent's entry. On both, the server sees the parent's `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`. What tells calls apart is the harness's own
lineage, never the environment. On Claude that is the `PreToolUse` hook's `agent_id`, joined to the call's
`_meta.claudecode/toolUseId`. On Codex it is `_meta.x-codex-turn-metadata`, which names the thread, its parent and the
spawn kind, and also the workspace's absolute path. Both harnesses hand the child the parent's allow and deny. Killing the
root's process alone ended the in-process child and terminated the servers, with no cancel and no child-end signal.

A Claude agent defined with its own `mcpServers` got a separate server with Daoris's environment, but it kept the parent's
server too. In one run, two siblings shared that one process, and it closed when the first sibling stopped. So it is no
per-child credential.

Codex refused every MCP call by default until the server's tools were approved, and `--ephemeral` broke its spawn.

C1 and C2 are go. The binding is observed and keyed to the existing part (C1a). A child's calls are read-only by their
lineage, never by an argument, with an unreadable lineage treated as a child (C2a). The install's real doors, ACP for both
and codex-acp's approvals, are C2b's to measure.

Rejected by the audit:

- **The environment as a child's identity.** Both harnesses give the child the parent's.
- **Claude's agent-scoped server as a per-child credential.** It is per type and shared by siblings, and its lifetime is
  the first sibling's.
- **An identity a tool's arguments carry.** The model writes those; the harness writes `_meta`.
- **A new session record per child.** C1a binds a child to the part it serves (§8: no second Session state).

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-PROBE1, the probe kit tracked).** C0's scratch kit is rebuilt as `tools/fabric-probe/`. It has
the stand-in server, which logs every message synchronously (`server.mjs`), and the hook logger (`hook.mjs`). The launchers
(`launch.mjs`) keep a run in `_fixtures/fabric-probe/<run>/` and start every process with the run's own `DAORIS_HOME`, the
session's `CLAUDE*` variables and every inherited `DAORIS_*` variable dropped. The receipt (`receipt.mjs`) prints ids and
never a path. `tools/fabric-probe.test.mjs` holds them without a harness. Its plain run on Claude Code 2.1.299
(`claude-sonnet-5-5`, scenario `child`: 39 s, 4 turns, 0.089 USD) reproduced run A. One server process and one `initialize`
served the root and its subagent, both under the root's `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`, and the hook's `tool_use_id` met each call's
`_meta` toolUseId, naming the subagent's `agent_id`. It also showed where a Claude child's parent is written: the stream's
`task_started` names the agent by `task_id`, the same id as the hooks' `agent_id`, beside the `tool_use_id` of the `Agent`
call that started it, and that call's `PreToolUse` names its caller.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-C1a, a native child bound to its part).** The connector reads each call's `_meta` before its
tool runs (`CallLineageFilter`). It appends what the harness wrote there, ids only, to the session's lineage file,
`<home>/sessions/<id>/lineage.jsonl`, for a session the driver named. The driver hands Claude Code a lineage hook beside
its rules (`lineage-hook.mjs`, on the connector's calls and on each subagent's start and stop). It reads Claude Code's
stream for each subagent's start, parent and end, and appends the part's end when its run lets go. `ChildBindings` folds
those lines into the part's children: harness, native id, parent and depth, the signals that named each, connected or
observed, and active, finished, ended or lost. A child gets no session record, and the part's record stays canonical. The
file is the one new store; its twins are in `.claude/knowledge/twins.md`. What no harness said stays unknown: a parent
nobody wrote, a call with no lineage, a Claude call no hook named. Nothing is restricted yet; that is C2a's.

The receipts are from 2026-10-16, through `tools/fabric-probe/` with this branch's connector and hook.
`ChildLineageReceiptTests` holds the files the runs wrote.

- **Claude Code 2.1.299** (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, scenario `family`: 66 s, 5 turns, 0.156 USD). Two siblings and a grandchild
  under the first were each bound with a parent and a depth, from the stream's `parent_task_id`. Each call was joined to
  its agent by `tool_use_id`, 4 of 4, and each child finished by its own `SubagentStop`.
- **Codex 0.160.0** (`gpt-6.1-sol`, effort low, scenario `family`: 69 s, 263 463 input and 518 output tokens). The same
  family was bound from `x-codex-turn-metadata` alone. Four connector processes, one a thread, appended to one file and
  lost no line. Codex says no thread's end, so each child ends with the part and is never `finished`.
- **Claude Code, scenario `kill`.** The root process alone was killed 24 s in, while its child's call was held open; the
  killed root wrote no result, so usage was not measured. No `SubagentStop` and no cancel came, and the part's unclean end
  marks the child `lost`.

Canary 1 holds: the hook decides nothing, and every call answered as before. Canary 2 holds: a grandchild is placed under
its parent where the harness surfaced one, and a child with no known parent has none. Canary 9 holds: a signal a harness
lacks stays unknown or partial, as Codex's missing types and ends show.

Not measured: either harness over ACP, so whether `_meta` and the hook reach Daoris's protocol doors (C2b's), and a Codex
kill (the audit's run G saw no end signal). The driver's own path (the hook from `SpawnSettings`, the stream's watch, the
end) is held by its tests, not yet by a session an installed driver ran.
