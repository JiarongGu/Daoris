## D173 — Daoris extends agents' collaboration as a Collaboration Fabric: the owner's unified proposal accepted as written (2026-10-16)

**Decision (the owner's).** The owner brought in `docs/DAORIS_UNIFIED_COLLABORATION_ARCHITECTURE_AND_PLAN_2026-10-16.md`
and, asked how to settle its six architecture decisions (§12, ADR-A to ADR-F), chose *"Accept the proposal as written"*
on 2026-10-16, and said why: *"what we have right now is working, but I can see the limitation, so we need a better
foundation."* So, as that proposal states them:

- **ADR-A.** A Collaboration Fabric is the first-class substrate; a deterministic scheduler, a DAG or a workflow is an
  optional pattern over it, never the core ontology (§2). Quest, Chain, Workflow, Session, Helper, Ask and the driver
  are adapters and projections over it, kept with their meanings (§8: no second authoritative Quest or Session state).
- **ADR-B.** `Participant` and `RuntimeBinding` identity (§3), with explicit visibility levels, opaque to recoverable
  (§4), and partial visibility shown truthfully.
- **ADR-C.** The capability transport is MCP first, with a native adapter where a harness needs one, for exact supported
  harness versions, measured (§4).
- **ADR-D.** Messages are durable, correlated and deduplicated, and say queued, acknowledged, delivered and acted upon
  apart (§6), compatible with the existing sources of truth.
- **ADR-E.** Long-lived responsibility outlives the agents that act on it, with explicit handoff (§3 `Commitment`, §4).
- **ADR-F.** Credentials and permissions are scoped per participant: a descendant inherits no key, publishing right or
  account secret; the gateway enforces grants at the executor, never by model text (§7).

The proposal's principles (§1), phases and exit criteria (§10), ten canaries (§11) and review checklist (§14) are the
contract its work is accepted against. Its §9 stands: correctness work stays first, and the fabric's work runs beside it
in isolated branches. Per §12, the backlog holds only the C0–C2 slice; C3–C7 wait for contract tests to justify them.

### Guards for the build (a second opinion, verified against the code; they shape the build, they do not reverse the decision)

- **The fabric is the foundation; today's pieces move onto it, one concept at a time, with one source of truth each.**
  The owner chose a new foundation over growing today's pieces further. Daoris already has early forms of its parts: the
  knowledge connector's MCP tools (ADR-C's transport), an ask's channel and its entries (D167; ADR-D's records), quests
  with D163's hand-over and helpers (ADR-E's responsibility; the helper as one collaboration), the state pipeline
  (D169). C0 maps each primitive onto what exists and names which store becomes canonical; where a fabric primitive
  takes a concept over, it becomes that concept's store, with a mapping and a migration, and the old surface becomes
  its adapter (§8). Never two authoritative stores for one concept at once, and never a second mutation path beside the
  shared handlers (§14; D171 point 3).
- **A child's identity is issued, not inherited.** A native subagent that inherits its parent's environment would call
  as the parent (the connector reads `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`): a distinct restricted binding, issued locally, before a caller
  counts as a connected child (ADR-B, ADR-F). Reading keeps D107's rules; a change to Daoris keeps D89 and D170's
  proposal-and-Apply.
- **Measured, per harness and version.** Whether a native child inherits MCP servers and rules, carries a trustworthy
  identifier, and what a parent's stop does to it, is observed with real child-originated calls on Claude Code and
  Codex, never assumed (§4, §11). The owner approved small probes on their accounts on 2026-10-16.
- **The first slice stays C0–C2.** §10's illustrative vertical slice (a helper, a cross-tree message, an artifact, a
  delegated action) reaches C3–C4; the first rows prove binding and read-only access only.

### Rejected

- **One canonical execution graph or a scheduler that assigns all agent work** (the proposal's superseded table): agents
  negotiate, delegate natively and coordinate themselves; a graph or DAG is an opt-in plan, never the core.
- **Only Daoris-spawned root agents as participants**: harness-native subagents and their descendants are connectable.
- **Assuming every harness gives the same subagent telemetry**: capability and visibility levels, degraded honestly.
- **Holding ADR-A and growing today's pieces instead** (the second opinion's recommendation): the owner chose the new
  foundation, because what works today shows its limits.
- **Holding all six decisions until later**: offered, and not chosen.

### Supersedes

- `docs/DAORIS_FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`, now the proposal's Appendix A, and the proposal's own earlier sources, as its §0
  says: where they conflict, §0–§14 take precedence.
- D65's "no other engine" stands in this sense: the fabric is a substrate of interoperability, not a competing workflow
  engine (§9's revised interpretation).
