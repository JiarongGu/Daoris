## D173 — Daoris extends agents' collaboration as a Collaboration Fabric: the owner's unified proposal accepted as written (2026-10-16)

**Decision (the owner's).** The owner brought in `docs/DAORIS_UNIFIED_COLLABORATION_ARCHITECTURE_AND_PLAN_2026-10-16.md`
and, asked how to settle its six architecture decisions (§12, ADR-A to ADR-F), chose *"Accept the proposal as written"*
on 2026-10-16, and said why: *"what we have right now is working, but I can see the limitation, so we need a better
foundation."* So, as that proposal states them:

- **ADR-A.** A Collaboration Fabric is the first-class substrate; a deterministic scheduler, a DAG or a workflow is an
  optional pattern over it, never the core ontology (§2). Quest, Chain, Workflow, Session, Helper, Ask and the driver
  are adapters and projections over it, kept with their meanings (§8: no second authoritative Quest or Session state).
- **ADR-B.** `Participant` and `RuntimeBinding` identity (§3), with explicit visibility levels, opaque to recoverable
  (§4), and partial visibility shown truthfully.
- **ADR-C.** The capability transport is MCP first, with a native adapter where a harness needs one, for exact supported
  harness versions, measured (§4).
- **ADR-D.** Messages are durable, correlated and deduplicated, and say queued, acknowledged, delivered and acted upon
  apart (§6), compatible with the existing sources of truth.
- **ADR-E.** Long-lived responsibility outlives the agents that act on it, with explicit handoff (§3 `Commitment`, §4).
- **ADR-F.** Credentials and permissions are scoped per participant: a descendant inherits no key, publishing right or
  account secret; the gateway enforces grants at the executor, never by model text (§7).

The proposal's principles (§1), phases and exit criteria (§10), ten canaries (§11) and review checklist (§14) are the
contract its work is accepted against. Its §9 stands: correctness work stays first, and the fabric's work runs beside it
in isolated branches. Per §12, the backlog holds only the C0–C2 slice; C3–C7 wait for contract tests to justify them.

### Guards for the build (a second opinion, verified against the code; they shape the build, they do not reverse the decision)

- **The fabric is the foundation; today's pieces move onto it, one concept at a time, with one source of truth each.**
  The owner chose a new foundation over growing today's pieces further. Daoris already has early forms of its parts: the
  knowledge connector's MCP tools (ADR-C's transport), an ask's channel and its entries (D167; ADR-D's records), quests
  with D163's hand-over and helpers (ADR-E's responsibility; the helper as one collaboration), the state pipeline
  (D169). C0 maps each primitive onto what exists and names which store becomes canonical; where a fabric primitive
  takes a concept over, it becomes that concept's store, with a mapping and a migration, and the old surface becomes
  its adapter (§8). Never two authoritative stores for one concept at once, and never a second mutation path beside the
  shared handlers (§14; D171 point 3).
- **A child's identity is issued, not inherited.** A native subagent that inherits its parent's environment would call
  as the parent (the connector reads `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`): a distinct restricted binding, issued locally, before a caller
  counts as a connected child (ADR-B, ADR-F). Reading keeps D107's rules; a change to Daoris keeps D89 and D170's
  proposal-and-Apply.
- **Measured, per harness and version.** Whether a native child inherits MCP servers and rules, carries a trustworthy
  identifier, and what a parent's stop does to it, is observed with real child-originated calls on Claude Code and
  Codex, never assumed (§4, §11). The owner approved small probes on their accounts on 2026-10-16.
- **The first slice stays C0–C2.** §10's illustrative vertical slice (a helper, a cross-tree message, an artifact, a
  delegated action) reaches C3–C4; the first rows prove binding and read-only access only.

### Rejected

- **One canonical execution graph or a scheduler that assigns all agent work** (the proposal's superseded table): agents
  negotiate, delegate natively and coordinate themselves; a graph or DAG is an opt-in plan, never the core.
- **Only Daoris-spawned root agents as participants**: harness-native subagents and their descendants are connectable.
- **Assuming every harness gives the same subagent telemetry**: capability and visibility levels, degraded honestly.
- **Holding ADR-A and growing today's pieces instead** (the second opinion's recommendation): the owner chose the new
  foundation, because what works today shows its limits.
- **Holding all six decisions until later**: offered, and not chosen.

### Supersedes

- `docs/DAORIS_FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`, now the proposal's Appendix A, and the proposal's own earlier sources, as its §0
  says: where they conflict, §0–§14 take precedence.
- D65's "no other engine" stands in this sense: the fabric is a substrate of interoperability, not a competing workflow
  engine (§9's revised interpretation).

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-C2a settled before it is built, with a second maker's opinion).** The scout found that the

1. **Fail closed, for every session the connector names.** A restricted tool runs only for a caller proven a root: a

   refused, and is never read as a root. An id that is malformed is kept as malformed, not dropped to look absent,
   in `CallLineage` and in the hook (with the driver's fold as its twin). A connector naming no session (a person's
   own agent) keeps today's behaviour, as a scope this guard does not protect.
2. **Staging waits on a measurement, not on a bypass.** The ACP doors are measured before C2a reaches the install:
   C1a's lineage file, written by the owner's own sessions on the build that carries it, shows whether an ACP root's
   calls join. If they do not, the door is made to carry lineage. An unprotected ACP door would be the owner's
   choice, recorded as an amendment here, never a default.
3. **The class table** covers every registered tool, derived from the registration, so a tool added later is refused
   to a child until it is classed.
   - Read: `knowledge_search`, `knowledge_get`, `knowledge_repositories`, `knowledge_convergence`, `registry` and
     `quest_list`.
   - Refused to a child: the row's six mutations, `channel_read` (it moves the parent's mark), `helper_answer`,
     `opinion_answer`, `review_serve`, `review_ready`, every `*_propose` and `knowledge_refresh`.
   - `quest_publish` is the one fixed exception (the plan's §4: a subagent may ask another repository), in its plain
     shape only: no attachments, never published as the session's ask, and no review or workflow proposals.
   - `daoris.me` is not a tool yet, and is not part of this row.
   - No grant of a mutation to a child in this row.
4. **The refusal** comes from the filter, before the tool runs, as an error result. It says that nothing was done, and
   why: the caller is a subagent, or its lineage could not be verified (the second never claims the first). It says
   that the reads remain, and that the request belongs in the subagent's result for its parent, or in a quest.
5. **The Claude join** reads the session's file from its end, bounded in bytes and time and cancellable, matches the
   exact tool-use id, and refuses on a conflicting match or a timeout. Its budget is a starting value until measured.
   The rehearsal's stand-in sends a genuine root lineage and asserts the error flag; its `review_ready` justifies no
   bypass.

This is enforcement on calls a harness mediates, not authentication against code that runs as the same user: a
child that can start processes could start another connector. That residual is named here for C2b and the grants.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (the end of a piece of work, the owner's direction).** Asked how cleanup works today, and told that no
watcher reads git (the watch's look judges every session branch each poll, a platform plugin is asked a pull request's state,
ending a conversation removes its tree, and *Accept* makes the landing branch), the owner: *"because the end cycle is
different depending on the workspace and its config, so if we have a git watcher as the end, we should clarify this as the
new design of the fabric"*. What it sets for the fabric's design:

1. **When a piece of work ends is one fabric event, not several paths.** Today the end is read in four places that each
   judge it alone: the look's tidy, the plugin's pull-request query, a conversation's end, and *Accept*'s landing. The
   fabric names one end for each piece of work, and those places read it.
2. **The end is observed in git**, by a watcher over the repositories that hold Daoris's work (their refs, and the
   remote's state as a fetch or the platform's plugin reports it), not inferred from a session's state or a press.
3. **What counts as ended is the workspace's and the repository's end cycle**, read from their configuration (the commit
   rule, the landing rule, the platform plugin's pull request), so one workspace ends at a merge on the line, another at a
   pull request completed, another at *Accept*'s branch pushed.
4. **Design first** (FABRIC-END1): the event, the watcher, each configured end cycle, and what reads the end (cleanup, the
   page's *Accept* and its foot, a quest's close, the record). It is checked against the C0 audit's stores and D88, D102,
   D148 and D164 before anything is built.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (events over polling, the owner's direction).** On the end of work, the owner went on: *"so this should
be an event using a watcher instead of polling every 15 s? I think we have a lot of polling logic that can be improved"*.
What it sets:

1. **What the machine can say when it changes is an event, not a poll.** Git's refs, a worktree's head and Daoris's own
   records, request folders and locks are files: a watcher hears them change, with no process started (process starts are
   slow on this machine, under antivirus).
2. **A watcher is backed by a slow reconciliation**, never trusted alone: on Windows a watcher drops events under churn (a
   buffer overflow during an install) and git writes through lock files and renames. The reconciliation is minutes apart,
   not seconds.
3. **What only a server knows** (a pull request completed on a platform) comes from the platform's webhook where a
   reachable service can take it, else from a fetch that is asked for when something suggests it is due, not on a clock.
4. **Design first** (FABRIC-EVENT1): an inventory of every poll in Daoris (the loop's look, the page's queries, the account
   readings, the knowledge server's freshness, the request folders, the host's checks) with each one's interval, what it
   waits for, its cost, and the event that could replace it. FABRIC-END1's end of work is its first consumer.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-EVENT1 and FABRIC-END1, the design: proposed, revised on a second maker's opinion, pending
the owner's yes).** The design is `docs/2026-10-16-fabric-events-design.md`: the inventory of 28 waits, the shape, the
probe's numbers, the cycles, the rejected shapes, the canaries, seventeen rows and five questions for the owner. Nothing is
built. Codex reviewed it, supported the direction and asked for revisions before any build; each point was checked against
the code and adopted. It proposes:

1. **An event is a wake, never a fact, and never the whole look** (§A3). A source says a topic and a subject; it is
   debounced (250 ms quiet, 2 s cap, a coalescing and not a barrier) into its topic's due set, which a judge drains
   atomically, one judgement per subject at a time, a hint arriving meanwhile kept for the next. A hint never runs today's
   look, which syncs with the remote before it plans (Driver.cs:332). Each topic has a reconciliation deadline; a failed
   read leaves its subject unknown and tried again. The judge writes its one store, and the page hears `STATE_CHANGED`
   through D169's publisher.
2. **The sources** (§A4): one `ChangeWatch` primitive in the driver library outside `Domain`, with spelled-out recovery
   (generations, reopen, an absent or replaced root, a resume) and bounds; a watcher per repository's common git dir,
   never per tree, and on the home's record folders; the local service's long-poll, `GET /api/changes`, sampled from a
   dedicated connection's `data_version` and answering families by their own revisions, never the database, since the
   look's own sync pass rewrites a row each time (QuestSync.cs:91); a fetch at moments that say it is due, never on a
   clock.
3. **Measured first, on this machine under its antivirus** (§A5): a watcher on a repository's common git dir heard 16 of
   16 ref writes before git exited, renames included, with no process started. One run shows it can work here, not that
   it keeps working: overflow, sleep, long holds, handles and a fetch are the build rows' to measure.
4. **The end of a piece of work** (Part B): the unit is the tree's branch; the words are *claim* (the agent's done, never
   the end), *handed* and *ended* (the line holds the committed tip, proven by git). The cycle is resolved as *Accept*
   resolves it, a named workflow's bound landing included (TreeLands.cs:346-350); no `end` key. The ref watcher wakes one
   end judgement per repository that moved, with the 5 min tidy kept as the reconciliation. Auto-landing stays on the
   look until the ledger, config and gate wake it too.
5. **The store** (§B5): `Ended { At, By, Tip, Line, On, OnAt, Repository }` on the `landings.json` entry and on the
   `session-branches.json` entry, its codes the clean-up's own. An end stops holding when its tip, its line's commit, its
   cycle's line or its repository changes. An ended session branch's entry is kept as a trace on both forget paths
   (BranchReplays.cs:432, TreeRemovals.cs:206). Ends are written only by the process holding the home's lease as its loop.
   The ledger keeps the claim, and the service is unchanged.
6. **The readers** (§B6) take the end from the store: the look's automatic tidy, the page and *Accept*'s foot, the CLI,
   Ask Daoris and a quest's close (shown beside the claim, never changing the ledger). The person's own removals (the
   sweep's press, LAND3's tidy) take handed work that is not ended, and are not moved to the end.
7. **The owner's direction overrides** the scout's *no watcher yet*, on the measurement. D147:62 (a timed fetch), D148's
   timer rejection, D88:150-153 (landed only on another branch stays) and OutputTail's *polled, not watched* stand. The
   fetch-when-due moments would amend D148 point 2's list of occasions (§Q5). Both slow clocks stay until the watchers'
   recovery is measured.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (the fabric events design accepted, with the owner's amendments).** Asked to accept
`docs/2026-10-16-fabric-events-design.md` and three of its questions, the owner accepted it and started its building, and
answered the three in their own words, which amend it:

1. **On whether a push may count as done:** *"so we can define a clear end of work: each repo or workspace might have a
   different cycle of work"*. The end of work is **defined** per workspace and repository, not only derived from the
   landing rule: a cycle names the steps its work passes (for example claimed, handed, pushed, its pull request
   completed, on the line), and which step is its end. The proofs stay: a step is reached only on git's or a plugin's
   evidence, and a removal still needs the work held where it cannot be lost.
2. **On how the server's end is learned:** *"so this will be a plugin; with an extra plugin we can define a different
   end for the fabric"*. A plugin may contribute a step or an end to a cycle, and how it learns it (its own watch, its
   platform's hook, a query) is the plugin's. The fabric takes the plugin's event, and its evidence, as any other event.
3. **On the slow clocks:** *"so instead of using a 15 s [clock] we should build them into the event system, so the
   fabric can define a different event as its chain"*. Fixed clocks are not the core: a timer is one event source among
   the others, and the fabric defines events and their **chains** (an event that leads to the next, such as the refs
   moving, the end judged, the work tidied, the quest closed), each chain the workspace's to configure.

The design is revised with a part on the event chain, the cycle's definition and plugin-defined events, before the rows
that depend on them. The first two rows, the change watch (FABRIC-EVENT2) and the end judgement extracted (FABRIC-END2a),
stand under every answer and are built now.

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-CHAIN1, the design's part C: proposed, revised on a second maker's opinion, pending the
owner's yes).** Part C of `docs/2026-10-16-fabric-events-design.md` answers the three amendments above, and its §C1 says
what it changes in parts A and B. Nothing is built. Codex reviewed it and asked for revisions before acceptance; each
point was checked against the code and adopted, and its two choices are left to the owner (§Q1, §Q2). It proposes:

1. **A cycle per workspace and repository** (§C2). Its steps are `claimed`, `handed`, `pushed`, `pull-request`, `line` and a
   plugin's declared steps. Its end is any of them but `claimed`, each reached only on git's or a plugin's evidence. It is
   set as a `cycle` on the landing rule: D164's inheritance comes with it, `driverconfig.ts` is its twin, and its Setup row
   is *Work ends*. A named workflow's `end` governs where one is chosen, as D157 §2.3 places such fields; every named
   workflow holds a landing (WorkflowNamed.cs:491), and an older version reads `line`. A repository with no rule lands by
   merge (Landing.cs:979-984) under the default cycle, `line`, which is today's behaviour exactly. A cycle that names what
   its rule cannot reach is refused at the door that sets it.
2. **The end apart from the removal** (§C3). *Held*, the line's proof, alone lets the look's automatic tidy go. An end
   defined before the line never allows a removal by itself (D88:150-153 stands). An end not yet reached is *pending*,
   *unreadable* or *unreachable*, said apart. Whether the tidy waits for an end after the line is the owner's (§Q1); a
   deadline, if any, counts from `Held.Since`, which is persisted.
3. **A plugin's step** (§C4). The manifest declares only the plugin's own `steps` (both twins); `pushed` and
   `pull-request` stay built in. There is a loop point, `work/watch`, and a fifth kind of point, the event `work/step`: a
   real change to a wire that ignores notifications today (Hooks.cs:349-351). Its sender is the process Daoris started,
   never a field. It is believed only for a unit the process was told of, bound to that unit's tip, and for a step a
   rule names. It is bounded in bytes, rate and queue, replayed after a drop on a `resync` frame, kept as the plugin's
   word, and never removes anything. How the plugin learns the step is its own.
4. **Events and chains** (§C5, §C6). The event types are part A's sources, the end's facts, the plugins' events and
   `timer`: each topic's reconciliation deadline expires as an event among the others. Fact links fire on a change; the
   tidy is an effect link, pending until done, retried on the wakes that can unblock it and recovered after a crash. A
   removed unit becomes a trace, which is never judged again, so a chain converges. Whether a workspace may configure
   more than its end, from a bounded menu, is the owner's (§Q2).
5. **One store per fact** (§C7). Part B's `Ended` is kept as `Held`, a plugin's own steps as `Steps` (at most two per
   step), and a built-in step's event writes the existing `PullRequestState`. *Ended* is derived at read, never stored.
   A chain's in-flight state is memory only, rebuilt by the start's reconciliation.

after the line; whether chains go beyond the end; whether the end reaches the quest's record; and the plugin's new due

**Note, 2026-10-16 (FABRIC-CYCLE0, human steps and the end's cleanup in the design: proposed, pending a second maker's

1. **A human step** (§C2) is a step a person reaches by a press, `{ "human": id, "label": …, "after": … }` in the cycle's

  and is waited for for up to 10 s. **Why the lock and not RPC1b now:**
  - The lost write is a race on two files. A lock in the two record classes closes it for every writer, the verbs
    included, at once.
  - RPC1b would move every trees verb onto the instance's pipe to close the same race. That is a larger change to the
    verbs' contract, and it would still leave a verb alone and the migration writing the files.
  - With the lock, RPC1b is a choice about who acts, no longer what keeps the files whole.
  - The rule that only the lease's holder writes `Held` stands beside the lock: the lock keeps each write whole, and the
    rule keeps one judge.
- **Lapse, on standing branches only (canary 7).** A fact lapses when the tip moves, when the cycle names another line,
  when `On` no longer descends from `OnAt`, or when the `.git` folder is another. A trace is never judged again.

  1. `At` is the last judgement that wrote the fact, so a look that changes nothing writes nothing.

owning their processes. Nothing is dispatched, believed or recorded yet: no caller tells the awaited set or takes the events (GRAPH1,
PERFORM1b), so no work process starts on an installed Daoris.

- **The wire** (`Plugins/Domain/WorkWire.cs`, the one writer and reader of each message, Daoris's side and a plugin's):
  `hook/work/watch` `{ generation, resync, units: [{ repository, workspace, root, branch, tip, line, pullRequest, awaiting }] }`,
  answered and never read; `work/step`, the plugin's notification with no id, `{ step, state, code?, evidence?: { ref, url,
  environment, message }, outputs?, at, id, repository?, branch?, commit?, invocation? }`; `hook/work/perform` `{ unit, step,
  stepVersion, invocation, idempotencyKey, subjectRevision, inputs, deadlineAt }`, answered `{ invocation }`; `hook/work/reconcile`
  `{ unit, step, invocation, idempotencyKey, subjectRevision }`, answered `{ state, outcome? }`. The handshake keeps `work/watch`,
  `work/step` and `work/perform` (`HookPoints.Work`) and the capability `work.perform@1` (`IHookChannel.Capabilities`);
  `protocolVersion` stays 1.
- **Bounds, before anything is believed:** a frame over 64 KB from a work plugin's process (choice 5) is dropped unread (`BoundedLines` reads stdout
  in blocks and passes an over-long line over without keeping it); 60 events a minute are taken from one plugin (`EventRate`); 256
  wait, the oldest dropped first; four requests are in flight per process. Each refusal is a `plugin.step` line of plugin, code and
  count, at most once a minute per plugin and code (`RefusalTally`; counts held past their minute are written at a later look), never
  the frame's words. The codes: `too-long`, `too-fast`, `queue-full`, `shape`, `not-sender`; §C4's belief codes are PERFORM1c's.
- **Supervision** (`HookSet`, holding `WorkPlugins`): a plugin whose `hooks.points` names a work point is started by the loop that
  holds the home's lease (`HomeLease.HeldHere`, asked at each look) when the awaited set (`HookSet.Watch`) names one of its steps, and
  stopped (`shutdown`, ended 2 s later) once none does (`plugin.stopped` why `idle`), once the lease is not this process's
  (`not-held`), when it is switched off or updated, and when the loop ends. A verb never holds a hook set, and a `--share` loop never
  starts, hears or tells one. An exit Daoris did not ask for waits 1, 5, then 15 minutes (`WorkRestarts`), and three within an hour
  mark the plugin unsound; Daoris's own stop never counts. **What the person sees:** the console's line (*started again in 1
  minute*, or *not started again until you try again*) and the plugin's health failing at `process`, `exited`, then `unsound`.
- **Generations and resync:** each start is the plugin's next generation, every event read is tagged with the generation of the
  process it was read from, and one not of the current generation when it arrives or is taken is refused (`not-sender`), so an answer
  from a process since restarted is never applied. `HookSet.TakeSteps` gives `WorkStepHeard(plugin, generation, frame)`, read by
  shape. The first frame to each generation says `resync: true`, and so does the next after any drop. `PerformAsync` and
  `ReconcileStepAsync` go only to the current, listened process that named `work/perform` and the capability, and return its
  generation; a request cannot cross generations, since a process's pending calls fail as it exits.
- **What is persisted: nothing.** Generations, waits and queues are the loop's own, in memory, as its record of a plugin's health is
  (D119 §2): a new loop starts at generation 1 with nothing queued, so nothing a dead loop heard can be taken. **PERFORM1b owns the
  attempts:** written on the unit's canonical record by its one writer before dispatch (§D6), with NODE1c's `Cleared` history and
  WIRE1e's attempt numbers increasing across runs, keeping the generation `PerformAsync` returns.
- **The manifest** (UNIT1d's choice 8): a performed step whose `hooks.points` does not name `work/perform` is refused when read
  (`perform-point`), after the step's own shape, on both twins. A resource's `work/cleanup` stays PERFORM1d's.

**Note, 2026-10-17 (FABRIC-PERFORM1b, durable execution; with FABRIC-WIRE1f).** The design's §D6 and §E2's attempts, on PERFORM1a's

- **The unit's record** (`Trees/Nodes/NodeRecord.cs`, `NodeRecordFiles`): `<home>/workflows/units/<digest>.json`, one per unit, its name
  the first 32 hex of the SHA-256 of the unit's id (which the file holds), beside D157's `workflows/runs/`. It keeps every node's status
  on the unit (bindings, scopes and acceptances whole), the attempts (`NodeAttempt`: node, attempt, plugin, the `work/perform` call

- **The performer** (`Trees/Nodes/WorkPerformer.cs`), over `IWorkPerformPort`, which `HookSet` implements with PERFORM1a's fenced
  calls. `StartAsync` applies the evaluation's start through the one table and writes the status, the attempt (`recorded`) and the
  start's firings in one write, then sends; acknowledged, the attempt is `sent` with its generation; a failed or unanswered call
  makes the node and the attempt unknown; a cancelled token (the loop ending) writes nothing more. `Hear` applies a completion to the

  `Keep` writes an evaluation's moves and their history.
- **Choices §D6, §E2 and the PERFORM1a review left open, made here** (for review):
  1. **The record is the driver's own file per unit**, not the unit's existing record: a quest's and an ask's are the service's to
     write (UNIT1a), and a branch's (`session-branches.json`, `landings.json`) hold trees and landings. One writer, the driver in the
     loop that holds the lease (PERFORM1a's `HookSet` refuses a call elsewhere); the lock guards against a verb or a second loop.
     *Changed by review:* the lock held only the final replace, so two starts read the same ready node and both dispatched; it now holds
     the read, the judgement and the write, and the lease is asked inside it (`not-held`), so a loop that lost it writes nothing.
  2. **The key and the invocation are random per attempt** (`k-` and 32 hex, `i-` and 24 hex), never derived from the unit, node and
     attempt: a record lost or reset would number its attempts from 1 again, and the plugin's durable deduplication would take a new
     attempt for an old one. Both are written before the first send and never change; a redelivery sends the same call.
  3. **What is asked after** (§D6's *after a restart, after its deadline, after a lost connection*): an attempt `recorded` (a loop
     ended between the write and the send), `unknown`, or `sent` by a loop since ended, to a process since replaced, or past its
     deadline. Since a new loop's generations start again at 1, the performer keeps the invocations its own loop delivered and asks
     after every other. The node is moved active → unknown first and written, then asked; reconciliation needs the plugin's process.
  4. **The answers, by §D6's table:** `done` records its outcome as `work/step` would; `not-started` delivers again with the same key
     where the attempt is still current, and otherwise settles it and lifts the gate (`NodeLifecycle.Reconciled`); `running` waits to the
     deadline, or a fresh timeout past it (`askAt`); `failed` follows `transient`; `unknown` holds it for the person. A step declaring
     `idempotent` alone is delivered again with its key (the plugin deduplicates); one declaring neither is held: never redelivered by
     itself. *Changed by review:* an `idempotent`-only attempt was given a made-up `not-started`, so one whose node had been stopped was
     settled and its gate lifted while its effect might still run, and its completion was later dropped as a replay. Idempotency allows
     the same key again; it does not say the effect never began. Such an attempt is redelivered only while it is current, its answer
     awaited by `work/step`; one its node has moved off is held (`not-current`), its gate kept, and its late completion still settles it.
  5. **The generation that answered:** an answer is taken only from the attempt's plugin (§C4's first rule: the sender is the process)
     and only at the generation now current (`not-sender` otherwise, PERFORM1a's code). A completion binds to its invocation, not its
     generation, so a later process's replay of an outcome lost with the earlier one is taken; an outcome the attempt already has is a
     `replay`, applied once. An earlier attempt's outcome is a stale one's (§D3): it settles that attempt and lifts the gate, moving
     nothing.
  6. **Retries:** only a failure whose code the step's `perform.transient` lists, with tries left under its `retry`, gets a `retryAt`

     after it, and its next start is a new attempt with a new key. A failure without a code is `unspecified`, never transient.
     *Changed by review:* the delay is the next try's within the binding (`Tries + 1`, what `retry` bounds), not the attempt's lifetime
     number, which keeps growing across bindings and runs and indexed a later delay after a person's new attempt or a new run.
  7. **An outcome that does not bind** (the one table refuses it: an undeclared output, another schema, the declared evidence schema
     missing) holds the attempt with the refusal's code; the node stays where it is, its gate held, for the person. Evidence is the
     plugin's reference at the declared schema, else the node's kind; an output's schema is the one its reference names. Whether any of
     it is believed is PERFORM1c's.
  8. **`Keep` writes over nothing it did not read:** a status is written only where the record still holds what was evaluated, so an
     outcome applied between the read and the write is never lost (`moved` otherwise). *Changed by review:* compared inside the lock, and

  9. **Bounds:** every unsettled attempt is kept, with the newest 50 settled ones (the invocation binding a replay needs) and the newest
