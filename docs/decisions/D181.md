## D181 — What a person waits on reads the facts the event judges keep; nothing a person presses runs a machine-wide read, and independent judges run at once (2026-10-19)

**Decision (the owner's direction).** The owner, 2026-10-19, after an Ask Daoris message sat queued for minutes and a
bound of thirty seconds was offered as the fix: *"no, the issue is not a timeout; 30 s blocking is still blocking, please
fix the logic properly: we need some parallel working, proper threading"*, and then: *"isn't it that we have a proper
event system instead of this blocking?"* We do. The events design's parts A and B (`docs/2026-10-16-fabric-events-design.md`,
D173's events note) are built for the sources and the topics (FABRIC-EVENT1 to 6: the change watch, the look's topics,
the home's record files, the service's change stream, Ask Daoris's polls made publishes). What was not finished is the
other end: the consumers still pull. Ask Daoris's start awaited its own reading of every repository and tree (git with no
timeout) before it began (HELPSTUCK1, D89's note), the look runs its independent work one after another and awaits each
(a read-only Codex inventory of the waits, 2026-10-19: the remote sync, setup plans, the snapshot's serial reads, opinion
and helper judgments, packet freezing, plugin consideration of every start, every start until its record opens, ending
hooks and landing selection), the account walk holds one gate across every account's probe, and git, agents and plugins
share four process-start places.

1. **A person's press never waits on a machine-wide read.** Ask Daoris's start, a message in a conversation, a press on
   the page, a terminal verb: each reads the facts as last judged, starts at once, and says plainly what is not known yet
   (a repository not yet judged is *not read yet*, never a guess and never a wait). HELPSTUCK1's thirty-second bound stays
   only as the backstop for a fact that has never been judged at all.
2. **The machine's facts are kept by the judges the events wake** (part A §A3): per repository, its lines and branches,
   its session trees, its history plan and its work inventory, each judged on a `refs` or record wake and at its topic's
   reconciliation deadline, written once to a store the readers read, and published to the windows through the state hub
   (D169). Ask Daoris's room is written from those facts, not from a read of its own.
3. **Independent work runs at once.** Each topic's judge runs on its own, a subject never judged twice at once, a topic
   bounded in how many subjects it judges together (§A3); the look's parts that do not depend on each other (the remote
   sync, landing selection, opinion and helper judgments, packet freezing, plugin consideration) leave its serial path. What
   must exclude stays exclusive and narrow: a tree's one writer, a branch's one push, a refresh token's one probe, the
   machine's cap of sessions. Reserve atomically, recheck before an effect, hold no lock across git, HTTP, a process start
   or an agent's question.
4. **Every outside call is bounded and cancellable**: git (whose calls have no timeout today), HTTP to the service, an
   agent's status question, a plugin. A call that does not answer in its bound leaves its subject unknown and is tried
   again at the next wake, never holding anyone.
5. **A person's process starts first.** An agent start the person asked for has its own place, never queued behind
   background git; background git is bounded in how many it starts at once.
6. **Measured before and after** (LOOKWAIT1): every look, judge and start writes its stages' times to the machine log,
   so a slow part names itself.

**The rows** (each replaces or absorbs the earlier one named): PARALLEL1a (the help room from the judges' facts, the start
at once; absorbs HELPROOM2), PARALLEL1b (the look's independent work off its serial path, reservations; absorbs LOOKWAIT2),
PARALLEL1c (the account gate narrowed; was LOOKWAIT3), PARALLEL1d (a person's process starts first; was PROCSTARTS1 and
PROCSTART3), PARALLEL1e (every outside call bounded and cancellable), with LOOKWAIT1 first.

**Rejected.**
- **A bound as the fix** (HELPSTUCK1's thirty seconds, alone): it limits a wait the person should never have.
- **More threads around the same pulls**: parallel pulls of every repository on every press still cost a press the
  machine; the facts are kept once, by the judges, and read.
- **A second event system or publisher** (part A §A1 point 5): the judges and the state hub exist.
- **Dropping an exclusion for speed**: the tree's one writer, the branch's one push, the token's one probe and the cap stay.

**Amends D89** (Ask Daoris's start: the room from the judges' facts, the bound a backstop), **D173's events note** (the
consumers' half), **D125** (the account walk's gate). **Held by:** the PARALLEL1 rows.

## Note (2026-10-19, PARALLEL1a: the machine's facts kept by judges, and Ask Daoris's start on them at once)

Points 1-4 for Ask Daoris. `MachineFacts` (`Platform/`) is the store: one owner (the shell's `DriverLoop.Facts`), an immutable
snapshot swapped whole under a writers' lock and read with none, so a read never waits on a judgement. Each topic has a judge, a
deadline, a bound and how many subjects it judges at once; a subject is never judged twice at once, and one woken meanwhile is
judged once more after; `Changed` tells each landing, which is what D182's push reads. A judgement runs in the context the facts
were started in, never its waker's, so a press's flow (a test's seam, a landing's memo of git's answers) never reaches a judge.

It schedules its own judgements rather than ride `ChangeWatch`'s drain: that drain takes a topic's due subjects as one batch and
awaits the batch, so one judgement that does not answer would hold every other subject of its topic until its bound. Past its
bound a judgement's token is cancelled and its place given back; its subject stays judging, never judged beside it, until it ends.
A topic's `Rest` makes a burst of wakes one judgement each rest, for the readers that walk the whole home; an ask afresh
(`JudgedAsync`) does not wait it out.

The room's topics (`HelpFacts`): the service's answers and the roster (deadline a minute, since the asks and the registry move no
ledger family), each repository's line by its checkout's guess (the one per-repository git, woken by that repository's `refs`),
and, each judged whole, the sessions' look, each workspace's history and the work inventory, which their readers gather across the
home. `DriverWatch.Heard` tells the facts each move it hears: a ref once the ref watch judges it, every topic's wake as it comes,
never a plain nudge, which is a press. The room is written from the snapshot (`HelpRoutes.HelpMachineOf`) with no git and no
service call; what is not judged yet is said *not read yet* (a repository's line, the sessions, the history, the work), the
conversation is told first by `help.room-not-read-yet`, and a room that said so is written again as the facts land
(`HelpRoomCatchUp`). HELPSTUCK1's 30-second bound now holds only a home whose facts were never judged and with no room written;
with one written, the start serves it at once.

Not taken here: the sessions, the history and the work judged per repository rather than whole; the facts published to the windows
through the state hub (D182); and wakes for a window whose home a headless loop drives, which judges at the deadlines alone, since
the change sources run where the lease is held.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (PARALLEL1de, points 4 and 5 as built).** *Point 5.* `ProcessStarts` keeps its four places, and a start
says whose it is (`StartClass`): the background (git, `ChildProcess`'s questions, a plugin's hooks, the usage probe, an agent
the loop starts) holds at most three, and a place that frees goes to a person waiting before the background waiting. A
reserved place, not a priority alone, since four background starts held in an antivirus scan would hold every place a
priority could hand on. A conversation's agent, a run going on with the person's words (ANSWER1a), a terminal, a press and
the host the window waits on are a person's; `ProcessStartsCensusTests` names each site's class. The runtime's own start lock
still has a person's start wait for those already inside `CreateProcess`, at most three, never for the queue behind them.
*Point 4.* Git runs under `GitBounds` (a question or a ref's move two minutes, any other work ten) unless its caller passes
its own (a fetch, a replay); past it the tree is ended and the answer is the failure its callers already read as unknown.
The streamed reads (`ReadGitAsync`, `GitBytesAsync`) are bounded alike and end git's tree on cancellation, which they left
running. The service's HTTP already had 30 seconds and its caller's token (`DriverHttp.Timeout`); an agent's status
questions (`HarnessSelection`, `ClaudeUsage`, `CodexUsage`) and a plugin's calls (`HookPeer`, `LandingPlugins`) already had
bounds, and their waits for a start place now take their caller's token, as `STOP_TASK`'s question to the agent does.
**Left as they were, each for its reason:** a tree's move or removal seen through on no token (`TreeSweeps`, `PassLeftovers`,
`ContentHolds`) now ends at its kind's bound, never cut halfway by a caller; a session's start and a go-on run wait for their
place on no token, since a run's record and the person's words go back only for a failure before its spawn; Agents' install,
update and sign-in outlive their request and stop by `HARNESS_CANCEL`; Ask Daoris's placed start (`RunPlacedAsync`,
`ChatRunner`) is PARALLEL1a's, and `HarnessRoster`'s probes on no token PARALLEL1c's.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (PARALLEL1b with LOOKWAIT1, points 3 and 6 as built).** *Point 6.* Each stage of a look, and of the work
beside it, is a timed span (`LookStages`): `watch.looked` carries them as `stages`, and a stage still running five seconds after
it began is written as `look.slow` while it runs, so a hung one is named (their shapes are the machine-log design's §4). The
remote sync's gate says its queue apart from its hold.

*Point 3.* The watch hands each look's driver `Wanted` (a nudge, an ending, the `look` topic's deadline), and under it: the
whole look's remote sync and the sync after an ending run beside the look on the running set's own task, one pass at a time and
one more after it where asked, and a set's circles sync at once. The lost-claim stop stays a look's, so a stop is said by the look that made it: every whole look's, and
the first look's after a pass beside ended while a session here held a quest. Its questions and a snapshot's carry-on claims
are asked four at once, and a snapshot's four reads at once. A start is reserved before it begins (its quest or ask, the tree
it goes back into, and its place under the cap, handed to the planner as `Snapshot.Starting` from the reservations read before
the snapshot and at the plan, so a record that opened while the snapshot was read is held by one of the two), asks the plugins
on its own, each answer its own start's veto, and is awaited only until something else wants the loop; one still preparing runs
on beside, and what it comes to joins a later look's report. An opened one wakes the loop; a hold waits for the next look, so a
hold never spins it. A start a plugin will hold still counts against that look's intake slots. The plugins hear each ending,
and a brainstorm's or a meeting's packet is frozen, as their topics' judges beside the look (`RunningSessions.Judge`: one
judgement per subject, two at a time per topic). These are effects the loop runs and waits for at its close, as it does a
landing pass, not facts kept for readers, which are `MachineFacts`'. A one-look run and a gate's run-until-idle keep every part
in the look, since each reads one look whole.

*What stays exclusive:* a tree's starting lock (`TreeLock.TryStarting`) and the ledger's one session per tree, a branch's one
push, the cap, now counting the starts still opening, and one judgement per subject. *Left to rows of their own:* the opinion
and helper judgments, which sit starts and take slots, and the landing selection, whose wakes FABRIC-END7 spends, as their
topics' judges keeping facts the plan reads; the terminal requests under one gate (`SessionRequestWatch`), and the node
runtime's pass after each plan.
