## D177 — Agents of different makers meet in one conversation Daoris runs: it relays every message, holds the shared context they read and grants whose turn it is, within the person's bounds; a meeting talks, and work stays in each repository's own session; the brainstorm is one protocol on it (2026-10-19)

**Decision (AGENTROOM1).** The owner, 2026-10-19, of the brainstorm D176 places: *"its core is a way to make different
agents/models work in the same session, so use Daoris as its communication platform, or even with a shared context"*,
and before that, *"since Daoris itself can manage the context, the knowledge and the cache, this can give a freer and
more advanced agent system"*. D176's note of that day reserved this entry for the broader thing. Nothing in Daoris puts
two makers' agents in one conversation today: a reviewer (D155) and a helper (D163) each answer one session alone, an
ask's channel (D167) carries claims but deliberately starts no turn, and the brainstorm keeps its generators apart by
design. A read-only opinion from Codex was taken on the shape, its relation to what exists, the shared context, turns,
work, a first slice and the traps; this entry adopts it, and names the thing.

1. **A meeting is the person and named agents of different makers, in one conversation Daoris runs.** *Meeting* (会议)
   is the parent's word, for review: *room* is already a pass's sandbox (D53, D176), *panel* a region of the window,
   and *session* one line of work in one repository (D167).
   - **Who is in it.** The person, and each agent they name, each on its own harness and on an account the person
     permits (D125's walk, D176 point 7). An agent is a participant with an issued, restricted binding (D173's ADR-B and
     ADR-F), as a brainstorm's pass is.
   - **Daoris relays every message**, with who said it. No agent addresses another agent's process, and none reaches
     another by any path but Daoris. Authorship comes from the binding, never from what a message claims, and a message
     quoting another participant is the sender's claim.
   - **A protocol may hold delivery back** until a point it names: *relays every message* means delivery by the
     protocol's barrier, not an immediate broadcast that would end the independence a brainstorm needs (point 6).
   - **Kept on this machine.** A meeting's records, its packets and its transcript are the machine's (D176 point 8): no
     shared host carries them, and nothing in them travels as a quest operation.

2. **The shared context is Daoris's: a versioned packet, refreshed between turns, shown before it goes.**
   - **What it holds**: what the person allowed from the repository's knowledge (D176 point 2's assembly, unchanged),
     the meeting's transcript so far, and the sources each participant was given. *What an agent read* means what Daoris
     observed it receive (a tool's result, a supplied file); what it says it read is labelled as its own claim.
   - **Versioned**: each refresh is a new packet with a new digest (D176's freezing), so every turn names the packet it
     answered from, and a retry reuses that packet.
   - **Shown**: before a refreshed packet goes to a maker, the person sees what it adds beyond what they already
     sent: a new repository, a new kind of record, another maker's words. D176 point 9's policy may let them say once,
     per workspace and maker, that it goes without a show.
   - **What it cannot be**: a model's hidden reasoning or state, or a maker's own cache. One maker's prompt cache never
     serves another; Daoris claims nothing about any maker's cache (D176 point 2). What it saves is its own assembly.
     Every participant still reads the whole packet each turn, and a long transcript multiplies that cost, which is why
     the bounds count rounds and minutes (point 4).

3. **The privacy line is the maker that receives it.** Relaying an agent's answer sends its contents to another maker,
   through another account. The person's approval names the makers and each repository a packet draws on. Approving one
   repository never exposes another of theirs, and a private repository's records go only where the person said they
   may.

4. **Daoris grants the turn, within the person's bounds.**
   - **Round-robin first**, in the order the person named the agents, and the person may name the next speaker.
     Later, an agent may ask for the floor through a tool that queues the request; it never starts a process.
   - **A turn ends** on what Daoris observes of the harness: it completed, failed, was stopped, or passed its deadline.
     An agent's submitted answer does not prove its process ended.
   - **Bounds**: rounds, messages, minutes per turn and minutes in all, with D176 point 6's reserves and its one repair
     carried over. Tokens are attributed per account as each harness reports them, and an unknown stays unknown. No
     token or money cap is promised where a harness offers none to enforce (D176's rejection).
   - **What spends**: D167's channel never starts or extends a run, and stays so. A meeting is where Daoris may run
     turns, and only within a budget the person approved with their press. A message posted to a meeting outside a
     granted turn spends nothing.

5. **A meeting talks; work stays in each repository's own session.** A meeting has no checkout, and membership grants
   no right to write anywhere (D45: a session changes its own repository). What a meeting wants changed, it asks for
   through the doors that exist (an ask, a quest, the person's own word), and what comes back is a claim in the
   transcript. A participant that writes, if one is ever wanted, writes in its own repository's session and tree, one
   live writer to a tree, under a decision of its own.

6. **What exists becomes protocols on the meeting, over time and never wholesale.**
   - **The brainstorm (D176) is one protocol**: one frozen packet, generators answering apart with their replies held
     back from each other, an anonymous synthesis, the skeptical pass. Its packet becomes the meeting's first packet, its
     proposals and synthesis its messages under that barrier, and its bounds, accounts and policy (BRAINSTORM1b) the
     meeting's. What D176 built stands as it is.
   - **The opinion (D155) and a helper (D163)** are other protocols that may reuse the meeting's participants,
     attribution, context and bounds. Their obligations stay their own: an opinion reads a candidate before a landing, a
     helper answers one working session. Neither gets a meeting's transcript by itself, since each protects a fresh
     reading. Neither moves now.
   - **D167's channel** already has the shape (the person and agents, attributed claims, delivery marks, work local to
     each repository). The meeting adds the turns Daoris runs, within a budget.

7. **The first slice (AGENTROOM1a)**: a meeting the person starts, that talks only, with two named agents, a packet the
   person reviewed, one bounded round, and a second round only by their press, its packet refreshed with the first
   round's messages. It reuses the brainstorm's machinery (`DriverBrainstorms`, the connector's turn-scoped tools), and
   waits for BRAINSTORM1b's bounds. Its doors are the driver's verb and the CLI's twin, as the brainstorm's are; the
   page follows BRAINSTORM1d's. Proof: scripted processes holding the relay, the refreshed packets' digests, the
   attribution, a stop and a restart; then one measured run on accounts the person names, as BRAINSTORM1c asks.

**The traps**, each a test its slice owes (Codex's list, kept):
- **Another context quoted**: a canary seeded in another conversation and another repository; a fresh participant
  cannot read it, and no packet or relay sent out carries it. This proves the boundary tested, not every leak.
- **A stalled turn**: a silent stub passes its deadline; Daoris ends the turn, frees its place, and says what is missing.
- **A limit mid-round**: a harness's limit cools that account alone (D125), counts no strike, and allows only D176's
  bounded retry on the same maker.
- **A restart** around a dispatch and around an answer's write: accepted messages kept, an uncertain turn marked
  interrupted, nothing spent again by itself.
- **Two meetings on one account**: the machine's cap holds, and that account's turns run one at a time at first.
- **The person leaving**: closing the view lets only the round they granted finish, then the meeting waits; *Stop*
  ends it at once.
- **An agent answering for another**: a forged participant or turn is refused by the binding, and a stale submission
  with it.

**Rejected.**
- **Agents talking to each other directly**, or through anything but Daoris.
- **A shared hidden memory, or a claim of sharing a maker's cache** (point 2).
- **Participants that write, in the first slice**, and **a meeting with a checkout** (point 5).
- **An agent starting a turn, another agent or another meeting by itself**; **consensus decided automatically**.
- **Meetings on a shared host, or as quest operations** (point 1).
- **Moving the opinion and helpers onto it now** (point 6).
- **A cap in tokens or money** (point 4).

**Amends D176**, whose packet, proposals, synthesis, bounds and policy become the brainstorm protocol's parts on a
meeting (point 6), and **D167**, whose channel stays unable to spend while a meeting may, within the person's budget.
**Held by:** nothing built here; AGENTROOM1a's row names its proof.

**Note, 2026-10-19 (AGENTROOM1a, the first slice built).** Point 7 is built in the driver: `Loop/DriverMeetings.cs` at the look,
`Brainstorms/Domain/MeetingPacket.cs`, `Brainstorms/MeetingFiles.cs` and the verb `daoris-driver meeting ask|show|send|stop|list|clear`
(`Brainstorms/MeetingCommand.cs`), held by `MeetingTests`, `MeetingCommandTests` and `MeetingPacketTests`. What the build settled:
- **The packet is the meeting's own**, `meeting: 1`: D176's assembly of the question and its sources, unchanged and written by the
  brainstorm's own writer, then the round, the participants in the person's order (`a1`… with maker and adapter, never an account)
  and the transcript so far, each message with its round, participant, maker, adapter, text and the digest of the packet it
  answered. Frozen and digested as the brainstorm's (LF, the digest of the content); neither packet reads as the other.
- **The records sit under the brainstorms' entry, apart**: `brainstorms/meetings/<id>/` holds the request, `sent/<round>.json`,
  `stop.json`, `state.json` (with its round), `packets/<round>.json`, `transcript.json` (what was relayed, and what was refused),
  `passes/<turn>.json`, `answers/` and `read/`; the rooms are `brainstorm-rooms/meetings/<id>/<turn>/`. The brainstorm's reader skips
  the folder and its verb refuses `meetings` as an id, since a brainstorm's clear of it would take every meeting. The service's desk
  lists nothing there (it has no `request.json`), but its clear, `DELETE /api/brainstorms/meetings` and so the CLI's
  `daoris brainstorm clear meetings`, would remove every meeting: a service row.
- **A turn is a brainstorm's pass** (`PassAsync`, its slot, its minutes, the round's deadline, its repair, a limit's restart, a crash,
  the stop and its record at each start), run in sequence in the person's order, one message per participant per round: the
  policy's pass minutes a turn, its request minutes a round. A stalled turn is cut at its minutes and the round goes on; the gap is
  said in the round's line and by `show`.
- **Within a round, no turn reads another's message of that round.** Every turn reads that round's packet alone, byte for byte as
  the person sent it, so each message names exactly what it answered and nothing reaches a maker that the person did not read first
  (point 2's show). The round's messages reach the others in the next round's packet. Relaying within a round, under point 2's
  "goes without a show" policy, is a later protocol's.
- **Authorship is the binding**: a turn's id (`r1-a1`) maps to its participant in Daoris's records; the answer is
  `{ meeting: 1, text }`, and nothing else in it is read, so a message claiming another participant, by a field or in its words, is
  kept as its sender's. A turn's answer is read once, at its turn's end, and moved to `read/`; one found in `answers/` at any other
  moment (a second answer to a turn already read, or one written before its turn) is refused, said and removed unread.
- **The second round** is frozen at the first's end, shown by `meeting show` with what it adds beyond the packet sent, and goes only
  by `meeting send <id> --round 2`: a send that names no round sends the first alone, so one repeated from a terminal's history
  never spends another. It is refused while a round runs and after a stop. Two rounds are this slice's bound; a round in which no
  agent spoke ends the meeting `no-agent`.
- **The stop and the restart** are the brainstorm's: the stop ends the round's turn at once, starts no later turn and freezes no next
  packet; a driver coming up ends a meeting left running as `interrupted`, marking the turn it ran, and a turn recorded answered but
  never relayed, interrupted, keeps every message relayed, and runs nothing again.
- **No account lock exists for turns.** The roster's `ProbeLock` and `Busy` guard an account's status question, not a session. So a
  meeting's turns are one at a time by its sequence, and two meetings are held by the machine's cap (one starts per free slot), not
  by their account: the lock across meetings is a row of its own.
- **No real agent takes a turn yet.** The connector's `brainstorm_give` carries proposals, not a message, so with no test stand-in a
  turn is `unavailable`, `no-message-tool`, and nothing is started or spent. The tool (`meeting_say`) is the service's row, the CLI's
  twin waits for a service door, and the page follows BRAINSTORM1d's.
- **The policy** is the brainstorm's (`BrainstormRules.Resolve`): an agent it does not list takes no turn, and fewer than two that
  may ends the meeting before it starts, `not-in-policy`.

What the checks do not cover: every turn ran on a scripted stand-in in the driver's fast half; no process and no real maker ran.

*2026-10-19, BRAINSTORM-RESERVED1.* The service's `BrainstormDesk` now reserves `meetings` as the driver does
(`BrainstormFiles.Reserved`): `Find`, `Show`, `Send` and `Clear` refuse it, case-insensitively and before the disk, as not a
brainstorm ("`meetings` holds the meetings' records, not a brainstorm."), so `DELETE /api/brainstorms/meetings` and
`daoris brainstorm clear meetings` answer 404 with that sentence and leave every meeting's records. Before, `Clear` removed the folder.
