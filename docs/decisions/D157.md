## D157 — How work moves is a workflow: a named, versioned list of steps of fixed kinds, drawn as a flowchart and chosen by workspace, repository, kind of task and the task; today's rules are drawn first as Current, with no migration; after the landing, a pull request, checks and stages a plugin speaks for; and Ask Daoris proposes a change as a diff of the person's part (2026-10-09)

**Decision (WORKFLOW1).** The owner, 2026-10-09: *"when doing work with Daoris we should be able to set up a workflow for
each repo or for a task type… presented as a flowchart, so we can define what work is highly co-worked with a human and
what can be fully automated; this includes how to raise changes, merge, even CI/CD (which includes multiple different
services and steps); this is more like LangGraph or n8n, more like workflow management… co-work with Codex for a good UI/UX
design, and this should be set up with Ask Daoris"*. Read at `6e465e1f`, how work moves is six separate rules (the landing,
D87 and D145; the review, D154; the second opinion, D155; go-aheads, D135; plugin hooks, D148; the quest's holds, D133 and
D144), each with its own key, doors and Setup row, composed only by the landing gate's fixed order (D155 §7), none drawn,
and nothing after the landing. The contract is `docs/2026-10-09-workflow-design.md`. A read-only UI/UX proposal from Codex
was taken first, and the design's §11 adopts, amends or rejects each of its points.

1. **A workflow is data the driver reads**: a named, versioned list of steps of fixed kinds, each with the fields its kind
   takes. No step runs a script, evaluates an expression or calls a service except through a plugin the person installed.
   A workflow never composes the agent's work: the chain stays the intake's and the person's (D65), and is the workflow's
   `work` step. D65 point 4's word moves (a chain was *a workflow*); its *no engine, no coordinator agent* stands (D1).
2. **Before the landing the order is the runtime's; after it, the person's.** A workflow turns the opinion and the look on
   and sets them, and never reorders D155 §7's gate. After the landing it composes a sequence of the pull request, checks,
   stages and go-aheads, with outcome branches (`wait`, `send-back` once, `stop`) and groups joined on all. Unknown is
   never failed and never passed. No edge goes backwards, so there is no loop.
3. **Participation and executor are said apart** on every step: *Agent alone*, *Agent + you* (a named checkpoint), *You*,
   and *Automatic · Daoris* or *Automatic · `<service>`*. No single slider.
4. **A workflow holds the process; the repository keeps its declarations.** Under a named workflow, a landing's `form` and
   `autoAccept`, a review's `required` and environment, and an opinion's `on`, `required`, `recheck` and reviewers are its
   steps'. A landing's `pattern`, `plugin` and `tidy`, a review's `environments`, and an opinion's `reviewers`, `verify` and
   `minutes` stay declared on Setup. A step chooses among what is declared, never beyond it, as D155 §2.4's task level does.
5. **Where it lives.** Each workflow is a file of its own, `<home>/workflows/<id>.json`, holding its versions; the choice
   is `driver.json`'s `workflows` (by repository) and `workspaceWorkflows` (by workspace, which also declares its kinds).
   Both machine-local, both twins on one table.
6. **Versions are whole and never edited.** A run keeps the version it started on; editing changes new work only. Current
   is not versioned: it is read live at each gate, as today.
7. **Current first, never a migration.** One pure function per twin derives a repository's workflow from its rules, each
   step with its source and its limit. Named workflows sit beside it, from built-in presets or *Save as a workflow…*. There
   is one authority per piece of work: its named version, or the keys under Current.
8. **The kinds, as the runtime stands**: `work` and `landing` built; `opinion`, `look`, `pull-request` and `go-ahead`
   partial, each limit said where it is offered; `check`, `stage` and `all` not built. A kind this build does not run is
   never offered, and a file naming one is kept and refused where it would be chosen.
9. **CI/CD: the service owns its pipeline; Daoris coordinates around it**: order, the commit each stage is bound to, who
   says yes, the waits, the failure shown, the record. Two new plugin points: `work/checks`, a query (D148 §3.2's held
   point, its order answered by its place after the landing), and `work/stage`, an act on a stage the plugin's manifest
   declares. **Production is only ever the person's press.** A deploy whose outcome is unknown is never run again by itself;
   a failed check sends back once, then waits for the person; nothing is rolled back.
10. **Choosing**: the task's choice, then the repository's for the task's kind, its default, the workspace's for the kind,
    its default, then Current. A repository's choice replaces its workspace's whole. A kind is declared by a workspace, with
    optional `paths`. The person sets the kind and the choice; the intake proposes. **An agent never lowers the person's
    part**: it sets a kind or a workflow on the person's quoted words only where `Involvement.Lowers` finds nothing lowered,
    else it proposes (REVIEWENV1b3's narrowing, made a table). Where a kind's workflow lowers the person's part, the
    chain's changed paths are checked against the kind's, and a mismatch holds for the person's choice; nothing switches
    by itself.
11. **The run** is a chain's work in one repository, bound at its first start on the machine that drives it, mostly derived
    from the records that exist. What only it knows is kept in `<home>/workflows/runs/<id>.json`. A run waiting on a
    plugin's answer asks at the look, backing off, and nothing is asked for a run nobody waits on.
12. **The screens.** A *Workflow* tab on a repository's and a workspace's page: a vertical list drawn as a graph, no free
    canvas, a step's settings in the drawer with its terminal twin. No place on the activity bar. The run is a *Workflow*
    view in the session's side bar, beside *Timeline* and *Review*, whose presses are their owners'.
13. **Ask Daoris** proposes through a `workflow` kind. The driver judges it as the editor does. Its card leads with *Your
    part*, then the diff by step id, the outward acts made standing, what is needed first and what it applies to, with
    *Apply*, *Open in the editor* and *Not now*. Apply is the person's press in the window. *Land without my review* is an
    automatic pull request, never an automatic merge.
14. **The no-model tier** draws Current, chooses, compares involvement, checks paths, and reads every step's state from
    records and plugins' answers. Drafting from words is Ask Daoris's, and proposing a kind the intake's, each naming its
    tier.
15. **Nothing set anywhere is today's behaviour**: every repository follows Current, every gate reads the rules as now, and
    the new tab draws them.

The build is WORKFLOW1a–n, the design's §14: the picture first (a–c, adding no authority), then named workflows, the
choice, the gate, the editor, Ask Daoris and the intake (d–i), then the services (j–m), and the look on the install (n).

**Why.** What the owner asked for first is one picture of what is co-worked and what is automated, and every fact for it
exists already, spread across six rules and a fixed gate. Drawing those rules as they stand gives the picture without
moving an authority. A workflow as a list of fixed kinds keeps each step's meaning in code that already enforces it, so the
graph can be the person's to draw without becoming an engine. Splitting process from declaration lets one workflow serve a
whole workspace while each repository keeps its own facts. Choosing by kind is the one thing no rule can say today
(*documentation straight to a pull request, a feature looked at first*), and a table of the person's part keeps an agent
from choosing its way past a gate. After the landing, the owner's CI/CD is services Daoris cannot and should not run: a
plugin speaks for each, as one already pushes and opens the pull request.

**Rejected.**
- **A free canvas**, and **a programming canvas** with expressions, scripts, commands and loops (D65, D1, D154 point 5).
- **Daoris running a platform's tool, holding its credential, or running its own CI engine** (D87, D100, D148).
- **A marketplace of connectors, a designer of credentials, and a single autonomy slider.**
- **Migrating today's rules into workflows**: it rewrites the person's file, an older build reads none of it, and nothing set
  must stay today.
- **The manifest, the registry, or the service's store as a workflow's home** (D154 §1.2's reasons; WSP1, WSP2).
- **Merging fragments** of a repository's and its workspace's workflows, and **reordering the gates before the landing**.
- **An agent lowering the person's part**, on its reading or on a quote; **a kind inferred from the changed paths**; and **a
  workflow switched by itself** on a mismatch (D54, D74).
- **Merging with nobody** as *land without my review* (D145, D51 rule 6), and **a standing say-so for a production act**
  (D37).
- **Running an unknown deploy again, or sending back more than once, by itself**; **a rollback or atomicity promised across
  stages or repositories**.
- **Several repositories as a step** of one repository's workflow (D32), and **a run's state read from the conversation**.
- **Asking a platform on a timer for a run nobody waits on** (D148 point 2 stands there).
- **A place on the activity bar now** (D66), and **naming the card's press *Confirm***, which is D156's terminal
  confirmation (D116).
- **A model judging a step done, a branch's outcome, or a task's kind** (D24, D54).
