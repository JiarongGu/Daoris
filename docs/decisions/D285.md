## D285 — A large ask is built by a layered team on the engines that exist: parts are quests on one ask, checks are capped and shared instead of agents, every hand-back is observed, workers send record intents, and the parts land as a train through the line's integration (2026-11-12)

**Decision (TEAMWORK1, 2026-11-12, under D284).** The owner directed that Daoris may add managing, supervising and other structure
so that a large task is handled by several agents, supporting each and narrowing none. This repository's own cycle had measured
where a builder's time goes: 45 to 123 minutes each, 75 to 90% of it inside builds and tests; five builders held the machine at 91%
CPU and doubled each check; 5 of 6 merges failed a check the builder had not run, and none once builders ran the merge's own checks
first; the backlog and the area documents conflicted at most merges until builders stopped writing them; finished builders left
processes and trees behind. Two independent proposals answered one brief (Codex, on gpt-6.1-sol, and Fable 5.2), and the steward
chose between them point by point. The contract is `docs/2026-11-12-teamwork-design.md`, built in its §11 order.

1. **No new engine.** A part is a quest on one ask, published under `(ask, part)`, with its own line and its own workflow run; two
   parallel parts never share one continuation identity. The team is a membership record (`TeamMember`) with a parent projection
   that stores no state of its own, over a versioned plan (`TeamPlan`, `TeamPart`, `PartPack`). Parts land through the line's
   integration queue (`IntegrationRequest`, `IntegrationCandidate` and the rest), never a queue of the team's own. The supervisor is
   the existing one (`SupervisorLook`, `Loop/Supervise/`) with a typed team subject, woken by events. The lead plans through a door
   every session may use (`team_plan`), so a worker can split its own part; depth and re-plans are counts the supervisor reads.
2. **Cap checks, not agents.** A check broker runs declared checks under the machine's existing admission (`MachineAdmission`,
   `HeavySlot`, `HeavyExecutor`), one expensive build or test at a time on this machine to start. A worker's turn ends while its
   check waits (`ended.awaits-check`) and resumes on the verdict. The team's pace is two active workers by default and three at most
   on the measured machine: a default the person changes, a pace and never a deny.
3. **One verdict store for the machine**, keyed by gate, the digest of the exact inputs that reach it, the configuration and the
   toolchain. Exact-input reuse first; reach-based reuse only where the merge tool's existing contract (`verdictStanding`) gives it;
   a pass is never reused because the command's text matches. The integrator replays standing verdicts.
4. **The hand-back is observed, not self-reported.** Daoris reads the base, the touched paths, the reached checks with their
   verdicts and the processes and trees left behind; it runs a missing check itself rather than refusing; it tracks the processes a
   part's run started by run, process id and creation time and stops them at the end. The checks are the landing's own selection,
   never a second list.
5. **Conflicts are planned out, never locked out.** A keel part lands shared contracts, hubs and wiring first, small and reviewed
   hardest. A claim on a path is soft: a collision becomes an ordering edge and a word to the worker. Workers never edit the records
   (the backlog, the archive, the changelog, the area documents): they send record intents, which the integrator applies one at a
   time, an area change addressed by its rule number.
6. **Integration is a train.** Ready parts batch onto the ask's candidate with one reached run per batch, and a bisect names the
   failing part, which goes back to its worker while the rest land. A code conflict goes back to the responsible worker, never
   rewritten. The ask is delivered only when its required parts have landed and its final acceptance checks pass; a part's quest
   Done is not the team's delivery.
7. **The person sees one item:** its conversation with the lead's updates and plan revisions; a parts table (part, role, state,
   waiting on, evidence) with landed and built apart and no invented percentage; steering of the target, priority, order, staffing
   and concurrency, and words to everyone or to one part; questions and approvals through the existing obligation keys, so nothing
   is counted twice. Its chrome is worded in English and Chinese.
8. **Supervision re-plans through the lead's door**, recording what changed and why, keeping the ids and splitting into linked
   children. It goes to the person only for a decision the evidence cannot supply: requirements that conflict, a product trade-off,
   a budget, an owner no record settles, or a workflow step that names the person. A long build is a fact to investigate, never a
   refusal. No act is reserved to the person and nothing is held by default (D284).
9. **Measured, and raced.** `tools/dev-metrics.mjs` carries causal ids (ask, part, plan revision, session, submission, check, review,
   candidate) and the delivery, wait, quality, reuse, cost, leftover and latency measures. The same ask is run by one agent at the
   same commit and read blind by another maker; cost and wall time per accepted requirement is the headline.
10. **Built smallest useful slice first**, each slice its own row with its proof: a, the observed hand-back and the broker with its
    turn-ending wait, which helps a single agent too; b, the verdict store; c, the warm build base; d, the team records and the
    lead's door; e, the supervisor's team subject; f, the train; g, the person's view; h, record intents; i, the race and the bench;
    z, the installed proof on a real large ask.

**Rejected.**
- *A new team engine*: a scheduler and a landing queue beside the workflow runtime and the integration queue, two ways to move work
  (D228); and parallel parts as one quest chain, which cannot express a join.
- *Capping agents instead of checks*: thinking runs on the providers' machines and checks run here; a fixed worker count limits
  agents for a cost only checks have, and stays wrong as the store and the warm base cut what each check costs.
- *Self-reported hand-backs*: the builder's word was what the red merges had; what git, the store and the run's job say is read.
- *Workers editing the records*, merged cleverly afterwards: measured as conflicts at most merges until they stopped.
- *Path locks that refuse an edit, role-shaped tool allow-lists, and a supervisor's approval before acts*: each narrows an agent
  (D284).
- *An agent integrator, and the person's Accept, for every part*: deterministic work given to a model, and a person answering per
  part for what they see as one item.
- *A polling supervisor*: a 4.3-minute pass, most finding nothing.
- *Reusing a pass because its command's text matches*: the inputs, configuration and toolchain are what it judged.

**Supersedes in part**, noted in its file: D180's notes of 2026-10-19 (development keeps its five workers beside a full set: a team's
pace is two, three at most on this machine) and of 2026-11-04 (in a big scope builders hand back running no check and merges carry
none: every hand-back's reached checks run, by Daoris where the worker ran none).
