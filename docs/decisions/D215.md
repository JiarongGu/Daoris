## D215 — A repository's integration is a fabric workflow Daoris runs (2026-10-28)

**Decision (INTEGRATE1, proposed 2026-10-28, accepted 2026-10-28).** **Status: accepted** by the steward under D191, after three rounds (the
acceptance note below). Round 3 was written by Claude Opus 5.5 from main at `7ebeba1ba`. Both makers read round 1 at `6f1ee69cb` and asked
another round: Codex, 26 findings, 10 blocking; Claude Fable, 30 findings, 6 blocking. Both read round 2 at `cd12f0399` and accepted after
named changes, which round 3 makes (the notes below; the design's §14 to §16). It changes a foundation (how work reaches a line, who may move one and when, a protocol between Daoris and a repository's
runner, and rules D87, D145, D192, D207 and D209 rest on), so by D191's note of 2026-10-24 no part of it is accepted on one maker's read. Nothing
is built. FOUNDATION1 (D212) stays the building priority; the rows that run beside it are marked in the design's §11. The contract is
`docs/2026-10-28-integration-design.md`.

The owner directed on 2026-10-28 that a repository's merging not depend on a queue the parent runs by hand: Daoris gets a proper merge step and
completes it, the deeper git-workflow logic configured in the fabric, a merge task becoming a landing task that includes rebasing when the line
moved. The same day the owner directed that the foundation move away from unmanageable scripting. Read at `7ebeba1ba`: this repository's work
lands as branches the install never merges, and the parent integrates them with `tools/merge-branch.mjs` (6,837 lines), a detached runner it
starts, which batches, gates, bisects and fast-forwards; every other repository's merge rule merges one press at a time into the checkout, with no
gates and no batch. In the queue's records of 2026-10-26 to 28, 9 of 29 candidates were sent back only on failures its judge could not reproduce
or place, against 3 with a member pinned, and the candidate after the day's last promotion came back red five hours later; the steward dropped,
requeued and resolved by hand, ended runners a drop left running, and found the install's home variable in the gates' environment.

1. **The principle** (design §3). The procedures the development's day depends on are product: fabric workflows with steps, events and tests,
   shown on the page and operable by the appointed steward. A script stays only as a check or generator that reads the repository, a harness
   that proves the product from outside it, an instrument a person starts, or a named harness adapter for an agent Daoris does not start. The
   design's §7 places each of the day's scripts and procedures, the prune of subagents' worktrees and the shell-edit guard among them.
2. **Two homes, two acceptances apart** (§4.1).
   - **The repository's facts** are in its `daoris.gates.json`. The key `integration` holds `by`, `gates`, `commits`, `scratch`, `merge`,
     `message` and `env.pass`, and D192's `verification` runner is extended. They run only as the owner accepted them by their blob, in an
     acceptance of their own (`integrationGates`), so integrating needs no verification pipeline.
   - **The install's policy** is on a **branch** landing rule with no plugin, key `integrate`: `request`, `commit`, `batch`, `order`, `gates`,
     `unconfirmed`, `conflicts`, `overlap` and `line`. It is inherited whole with the rule (D164), and refused on a merge rule and beside a plugin.
3. **The shapes** (§4.2, §4.7). A branch for review and a pull request through a plugin stay as today. Merge, rebase-and-merge and squash run
   through integration, on a branch rule, so a build without integration makes the branch and merges nothing. Today's merge rule is carried by
   the transport from its row g as the named legacy exception: owner-pressed, ungated, one at a time, promising no gate. The line moves only
   forward to a gated commit; integration never pushes or rewrites the line or a pushed ref.
4. **One door for every request** (§4.6).
   - **A unit not yet landed** is requested only through its own landing, by the person's Accept or the rule's `autoAccept` at a done, its
     whole landing gate judged at the commit it pins. **The steward's act never makes a landing**, since every acceptance of what is delivered
     stays the owner's (D209 point 3).
   - **A unit already landed** (under the old rule, by an older build, before the rule carried `integrate`, or handed back at the flip) is
     requested from its **recorded landing**. The landing is a standing entry of the machine's landings, bound to its recorded unit and its exact
     recorded tip (the branch standing there, else refused as moved), its gate proof the landing's own recorded review, opinion and workflow,
     and it needs no tree where LAND3's tidy took it. It is requested from the owner's card, the steward's act where the rule allows, or a
     come-up sweep under `request: done`.
   - **A records branch** is requested by the owner's card or the steward's act. A session's branch, or a landing branch no record holds, is
     refused at that door.

   Each later revision is requested again by the same rule, and only a done under `autoAccept` requests by itself. `done` and `steward` require
   declared gates; the human step after a local integration is the owner's push.
5. **One integration run per line, outside every run** (§5.1, §5.2, §5.6). Its steps are fixed and each is a fabric event: request (with the
   commit check), form, replay, resolve, conflict, gates, judge, promote, settle, tidy and close. No node is added to any graph and no run's record
   changes: the unit's `handed` is the branch its landing made (`work.landed` unredefined), and its `line` observes the promotion
   (`work.integrated`).
   - **Where it runs:** a leased integration tree under the trees home. A candidate's processes run in one job, closed by a drop, a stop or its
     end before its admission is let go.
   - **Its state:** Workplace's store family, recovered by ancestry. Sightings and known red are Review's.
   - **Its fence:** promotion re-reads the line's committed `integration.by` and every requester's authority immediately before the line moves,
     so two integrators never both promote.
6. **Mechanical first** (§5.2 steps 3 and 4). A member the line holds is contained. One an earlier member holds is contained **provisionally**,
   settling only when that member promotes. Generated folders, union records with their joins, the backlog, the router and the repository's
   declared row tables settle by rule, and a line by its accepted writer; no rule keeps one side's copy. A conflict of meaning goes to the
   member's session through the rebase words, or holds. A landing agent is deferred to a later design.
7. **The gates** (§4.3, §5.5, §5.9). The accepted gates run through D192's `verification` runner, extended by modes (`plan`, `probe`,
   `commits`) and inputs (the base, the accepted declaration's text, the gates to run, the failure, a caller-handed tree). The runner keeps
   schema 1 where no mode is asked, and every unknown case of D192's and more is unknown, never a pass. The runner reads its gates and commands
   only from the accepted blob Daoris hands it. Every heavy gate runs in the machine's one admission (HEAVYQUEUE1), in an environment Daoris
   composes: every `DAORIS_` variable, git's repository-locating variables and the merge tool's switches removed, the runner's own inputs on its
   job alone.
8. **A failure is confirmed before it is blamed** (§4.5, §5.3).
   - **The confirm run:** the failing run is repeated once on the candidate's tip as composed, before any base run. It holds every admission
     place by ordinary entries any writer counts.
   - **Its outcomes:** recurring, the failure is judged as D207 judges it, known red's expiry among its cases. Not recurring, it is a sighting,
     and the rule's `unconfirmed` decides by reach: `split`, `hold` or `land`. Where the admission cannot be shown, the confirm run is
     inconclusive and read as `split`.
   - **The default:** `split` for both, permanently. `land` is the owner's choice alone, refused where no whole-set verification runs on the
     line, the backstop being later tips, standing evidence, sweeps and nightly runs.
   - **Measurement:** prospective, from the design's row b. Three sightings of one unit in a week are shown with the fix the unit needs.
9. **After landing** (§5.7). A later commit is contained, superseded or new, never waiting work. A superseded one is said with the line's
   commit, kept on a recovery ref, and kept visible as a pending disposition until its session or the steward answers. A new one is offered by
   the request rule. A unit's note is written from its record. A tree goes by its rule with the repository's declared scratch disposable, never
   forced.
10. **Who acts** (§8).
    - **Daoris** runs every step under the accepted declaration.
    - **The steward** gains `request` (a records branch or a recorded landing, never a landing), `drop`, `requeue`, `recheck`, `decide` (by
      `split` or `hand-back`, never `land`) and `order`. Each is recorded with its appointment's generation, judged at form, re-checked at
      promotion and cancelled by a revoke. None is allowed for a repository whose landing pushes or a rule with no gates, and none releases what
      the owner holds.
    - **The owner** accepts each declaration's blob; sets `request: done` and `unconfirmed: land`; decides `land` on a held candidate; and keeps
      pushes, publication, history rewrites and forced removals.
11. **Same-file rows** (§9). A quest's declared `paths` (a record field with one writer) are compared at its start with every open quest's. The
    overlap is said on both and, under `overlap: wait`, the later start is held until the earlier is integrated. Two running units that change
    one path are each told.
12. **The tool** (§6).
    - **Taken by Daoris:** the queue, the candidate, the resolvers, the judge, the findings and the run go into Workplace (with the verdict
      port), Review, Workflows, Events, Settings and Roles.
    - **Kept by this repository:** the gate plan, the censuses, the CI and the stage's evidence, as its declared runner and tools.
    - **Retired:** the queue's verbs and the merge in place, at a flip both integrators' promotion fence holds. It goes in order:
      1. **The running runner is a fenced one.** The fence lands alone, and the runner records the code identity it loaded. An enqueue refuses
         behind a runner of another identity, so the unfenced runner drains and ends by itself, and a fenced runner then shows its refusal of a
         `by: daoris` line.
      2. **The owner's one sitting.** The owner accepts the flip branch's declaration and sets the install's rule.
      3. **The flip promotes.**
      4. **The leftovers.** This repository's landings, those whose tree was tidied among them, enter through the recorded-landing door; the
         parent's own branches through the records-branch door.

**Why.** Every repair the steward made by hand on 2026-10-28 was a part of integration that lived in a script's process or the parent's
environment: cancellation, the environment, a decision on a held candidate, two mechanical resolutions. Built as product, each becomes a step the
page shows and a role may operate, and one implementation replaces two that disagreed about gates, batches and where the merge is made. Riding a
branch rule leaves an older build unable to perform a merge the rule meant to gate. Requesting only through the unit's own landing keeps every
gate a run already has. The repository's facts belong with its files and its trust choices with the install, so neither can lower the other, and
the runner reading only the accepted blob keeps a member from choosing its own commands. A failure that does not reproduce cost most of the day.
Repeating the failing run as composed with nothing heavy beside it tells load from a deterministic defect better than a class run alone, but proves
no cause, so the strict policy stays until the owner chooses otherwise where a whole-set verification backs that choice.

**Rejected** (design §10): keeping the tool as the integrator; Daoris carrying this repository's gate plan; a runner contract of its own beside
D192's; the runner reading its gates from the candidate's tree; `integrate` on a merge rule; a node added to any graph by this design; a pipeline
marked `integration`; a node per step on each unit; promoting by a merge in the checkout; a weight field on an admission entry; `land` as a default
or flipped by a replay; landing every unconfirmed failure; re-running a red candidate until it passes; confirming by the class alone; a wider
window; a line rule keeping one side's copy; containment in an unpromoted member settled at once; Daoris resolving a conflict of meaning; a landing
agent in this design; `request: done` or `steward` with no gates; the steward's `decide land`; `autoAccept` on a merge rule; the whole
declaration in either home; requiring a verification pipeline to integrate; two integrators, or a flip fenced only at its doors; reverting a
promoted candidate; moving the rehearsals into the product; detecting same-file rows by a model's reading.

(1) What holds a contained member is its `containedIn`, `{ line: true }` for the line or `{ branch, request }` for an earlier member, in fields apart from any branch's name: the line's marker had been the word `line`, so a container whose branch was named `line` settled the member it held as already in main, and when that container was handed back the member was neither promoted nor judged again.
