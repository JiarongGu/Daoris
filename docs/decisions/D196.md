## D196 — Everything that comes to the person is in one notification view the title bar opens; a terminal's press waits there in plain words and is never drawn across the page; Overview keeps a summary; what is read is kept once per machine, and what announces depends on what waits and on its urgency (2026-10-21)

**Decision (NOTIFY1, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, in its first round under D191: Codex reads it next, and the steward says
whether Fable reads too (design §11). On 2026-10-21 the owner reported that a terminal's press waiting for the window's Confirm is
drawn across the top of every page until answered, says the route's raw name (`SESSION_INPUT`) beside its sentence, and expires in
two minutes (five did that day before the owner acted); and that a session's messages are listed one by one on Overview, which
makes it long and hides what it is for. The owner directed one notification center for everything that comes to the person,
opened from the title bar with unread counts, each item answered or dismissed in place, the press announced without blocking the
page in plain words, and Overview kept to a summary. The contract is `docs/2026-10-21-notification-design.md`.

1. **The inventory** (design §1). What reaches the person today, in sixteen rows, each with where it is drawn, what counts it, what
   announces it and whether it blocks the page. The press's band is the one that blocks; an ending and an answer are said and kept nowhere;
   the notifications behind the window and the look's toasts are English; one count reaches the taskbar for two windows; an agent's
   ask to hand its session over has no row; nothing an agent says on a channel reaches the person outside the ask (§1.1).
2. **One view, *Notifications***: a side bar view the title bar's bell opens and closes, movable to the panel like every view,
   reached from View and the palette; the bell counts what needs the person in open's hue, with a dot while anything is unread. It
   holds four groups in one column: the presses waiting for confirmation, *What needs you* as judged today, messages, and *Done and
   failed* (§2.1-§2.3). A row of *What needs you* is answered by its own acts or opened by its door, and never dismissed; a message is
   opened or marked read; a notice is opened or dismissed; there is no *Confirm all*. A shell's alone.
3. **A terminal's press waits there and nowhere across the page** (§3). Its sentence names the act, the session as Sessions names
   it and the words it carries, quoted; every other value is listed under it; the route's name and every value raw are under
   *Details*. Which values the sentence names is a table in the modules beside the pipe's census, and every `Person` route has its
   act's words. In front, a toast with *Open* in the focused window; behind, a system notification in the window's language naming
   no value, and the window's taskbar button lit until a window comes forward; never the center opened or the focus moved. Its two
   minutes start when the card itself is first in view, and stay two; its ten minutes to be found stay; an unanswered end becomes a
   notice. Only the person confirms, in the window, its default no (D156).
4. **Overview keeps a summary** (§4): a line per group with its count and oldest wait, and unread messages and notices, each a door
   into the view; then its tiles and lists. Every row moves to the view with its words and acts; Overview's activity badge moves to
   the bell.
5. **Read and unread** (§2.4-§2.5). What needs the person and the presses are open until settled, never read or unread. A notice is
   read once shown in a window in front; a channel's messages are read on the channel or by *Mark read*, through the person's channel
   mark (D167). One machine, one read state: the channels' marks stay the service's, and the notices with whether each is read are the
   driver's record `data/notices.json`, kept a week and at most 200, written by the one process that holds the home's lease under its
   keep lock. Each main window shows its own workspace's items and every machine-wide one, and wears its own count on its taskbar
   button. Judged in .NET in a pushed slice, `notices`; the page words and draws (D182).
6. **What announces, by what waits and by urgency** (§5). D188's levels mapped onto the person: an agent's ordinary message is
   counted, its urgent message to everyone is a toast in the window, and nothing an agent sends reaches the desktop; the system
   notification and the lit button are kept for what waits on the person with a clock or holds work, and what the person waits on.
   The notify switch governs every announcement but a press's, and never what the view keeps. The look's lines stop being toasts:
   news becomes a coded notice, the rest stays the log's.
7. **The rows**, in build order: NOTIFY2a, the view and the press; 2b, Overview's summary; 2c, a count per window; 2d, the
   announcement behind the window in the person's language; 2e, *Done and failed* and its record; 2f, messages by urgency; 2g, the
   hand-over ask as a row; 2h, the look's lines; 2i, the terminal's read (design §9, which says why they are not NOTIFY1a and on).

**Why.** The press was drawn across every page because it had to be found; CONFIRMSEEN1 made it findable in other ways (a row, a
balloon, ten minutes), so the band can go where the person chooses to look, and the announcement can do the finding. One place for
what arrives answers the owner's two reports at once: Overview can be short only if what it listed has a home, and a press can leave
the page only if there is a place that is not the page. A side bar view is that place because it is the same on every view and
stands beside the session the person attends. Keeping what was read once per machine is what two windows on two workspaces need,
and keeping the announcements to what waits or holds work, never an agent's message on the desktop, follows D188.

**Rejected** (design §7): keeping the band, smaller or folded; a popover; a drawer; a tenth place; Overview as the center; opening
the center by itself; confirming anywhere but the card, or all at once; dismissing a wait; a message read by being listed; one number
mixing waits and unread, or two numbers; an unread count on the taskbar; an agent's message on the desktop; a press's values in the
system's notification; the page composing each notification as it arrives; a pausing, longer or later bound; notices in memory alone
or derived from the records; the look's lines in the view as they are; 通知中心 as the name.

layout §4 (`data/notices.json`); the notify switch's words; the frame model's and the dock design's views. D156 point 4 and the
person-door design §4.2 hold.

**Held by:** the rows' proofs (design §9): the card's tests in en and zh with the census's wording test; Overview's and the view's
