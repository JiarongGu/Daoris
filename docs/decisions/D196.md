## D196 — Everything that comes to the person is in one notification view the title bar opens; a terminal's press waits there in plain words and is never drawn across the page; Overview keeps a summary; what is read is kept once per machine, and what announces depends on what waits and on its urgency (2026-10-21)

**Decision (NOTIFY1, accepted 2026-10-21).** **Status: accepted** (the steward, under D191), revised twice under D191: after
Codex's read of round 1 (twenty-eight findings, eighteen of them blocking, all taken in round 2) and after Fable's read of design
§2 to §3 and §5 of round 2 (fourteen findings, one blocking, all taken in round 3), which D191's note asks since the design changes
a decided rule others rest on (D156's CONFIRMSEEN1 note), adds a record written by two processes and a mark the service keeps.
Every finding was checked against the code and taken (design §11). On 2026-10-21 the owner reported that a terminal's press waiting for the window's Confirm is drawn across the top of
every page until answered, says the route's raw name (`SESSION_INPUT`) beside its sentence, and expires in two minutes (five did
that day before the owner acted); and that a session's messages are listed one by one on Overview, which makes it long and hides
what it is for. The owner directed one notification center for everything that comes to the person, opened from the title bar
with unread counts, each item answered or dismissed in place, the press announced without blocking the page in plain words, and
Overview kept to a summary. The contract is `docs/2026-10-21-notification-design.md`.

1. **The inventory** (design §1). What reaches the person today, in sixteen rows, each with where it is drawn, what counts it, what
   announces it and whether it blocks the page. The press's band is the one that blocks; an ending and an answer are said and kept
   nowhere; the notifications behind the window and the look's toasts are English; one count reaches the taskbar for two windows; an
   agent's ask to hand its session over and a press at the service's door have no row; nothing an agent says on a channel is
   announced, or counted outside the ask (§1.1). A Confirm is taken past its time when the timer fires late (§0).
2. **Built in four steps** (§0.2), Codex's smaller shape: the presentation first, on the transports that exist and changing no store
   (NOTIFY2a to 2e), the pipe's half (2a) and the view (2b) fixing the owner's three reports; then one serialized notice record
   (2f); then the channels' summaries on the service's own marks (2g); and throughout, arrivals said by their identity apart from
   the snapshots that list them.
3. **One view, *Notifications*** (§2.1-§2.3): the title bar's bell shows it where it stands, the right side bar by default or the
   panel where the person moved it, and counts what needs the person in open's hue, each press once, with a dot while anything is
   unread; while any press waits unshown, it wears the warning hue with the presses' own count. Four groups in one column: the presses waiting for confirmation, of a terminal and of the service's door, read from the
   confirmation channel alone and never copied into a row; *What needs you* as judged today; messages; and *Done and failed*. A row
   of *What needs you* is answered by its own acts or opened by its door, and never dismissed; an ask's messages are opened or
   marked read; a notice is opened or dismissed; there is no *Confirm all*. A shell's alone.
4. **A terminal's press waits there and nowhere across the page** (§3). Its sentence names the act, the session as Sessions names it
   with its repository and short id always beside (and its workspace where a repository's name is in two), the words it carries
   quoted, its files by their paths whole, and in words a value that chooses how the act runs (`SESSION_INPUT`'s `said`); every
   other value is listed under it and none leaves the card's view; the route's name and every value raw are under *Details*. Which
   values the sentence names is a table in the modules beside the pipe's census, and every `Person` route has its act's words. The
   card's decision region is bounded whatever the press carries; it is *shown* when it lies whole in its area, or fills it with
   its buttons' row in view, in the view's open tab, its group unfolded, in a window in front and under no scrim, judged again at
   each change of any of these; its two minutes start then and stay two; its ten minutes to be found stay; an answer or a word that
   it is shown at or past its time ends it as expired, the clock read first, nothing runs, and the refusal is toasted since the
   card has gone. In front, a toast with *Open* in the focused main window, said on the press's own event by its id and staying
   until the view opens or the press ends; behind, the main window's taskbar button lit while a press waits unshown, and a
   notification naming no value, in the window's language from 2d; never the view opened or the focus moved. A press at the
   service's door is counted (by the page's poll until its door is pushed, NOTIFY2j), toasted, said twice at most across two
   windows, and says its time left; its two minutes from its ask stand, since its grant is used within them. Only the person
   confirms, in the window, its default no (D156), and a press is answered by its id, never rebuilt from its sentence.
5. **Overview keeps a summary** (§4): a line per group with its count and oldest wait, each a door into the view, the unread lines
   joining with the rows that produce them; then its tiles and lists. Every row moves to the view with its words and acts; Overview's
   activity badge moves to the bell.
6. **Read and unread** (§2.4-§2.5). What needs the person and the presses are open until settled, never read or unread. A notice is
   read once shown in a window in front. An ask's items are read on its page or by *Mark read*, through the person's channel mark
   (D167), never by a session's panel, which shows a part, nor by being listed; the count is the service's own, the one the ask's row
   says, and for the person passes over their own posts and Daoris's lines; a teammate's ask's mark is kept by this machine's
   service (2g). A conversation's answer is a notice, read by opening it, by *Mark read* or by its conversation read in a window in
   front. The notices with whether each is read are the driver's record `data/notices.json`: a map keyed by id, written by any
   process on the home under the file's keep lock from its read to its rename, the writer pushing its own write, read again at each
   change a home watch hears or at the topic's deadline, each notice keeping its workspace, a week and at most 200, the oldest
   first. Only the writer holding the lock sets an unreadable record aside, a reader keeping its last snapshot; a write refused is
   logged and dropped, since the sessions' records keep what happened. Each main window shows its own workspace's items and every
   machine-wide one, and wears its own count on its taskbar button (2c). Judged in .NET in a pushed slice, `notices`; the page
   words and draws (D182).
7. **What announces, by what waits and by urgency** (§5). D188's levels mapped onto the person: an agent's ordinary message is
   counted, its urgent message to everyone is a toast in the window, and nothing an agent sends reaches the desktop; the system
   notification and the lit button are kept for what waits on the person with a clock or holds work, and what the person waits on.
   An arrival is an event with its identity, said once per run by the host and once by the focused window, never a snapshot's field
   and never said later: accepted for news, and for a press because its signal persists while it waits unshown. Messages are
   judged against a watermark per ask whose first judgement is a baseline; a notice another process wrote is an arrival in the
   desktop by its host's re-read of the record. The notify switch governs every announcement but a press's, each new one's too, and
   never what is recorded, a conversation's answer included. The look's lines are logged as declared events first, then classed
   (§5.1): news a coded notice, a wait its row, the rest the log's, and the person's own cancel never said back.
8. **The rows**, in build order: NOTIFY2a, the pipe's half (a late answer or shown word refused, the press's id on its event, the
   balloon naming no route); 2b, the view, the press, its persistent signal and Overview's summary; 2c, a count per window; 2d, the
   announcement behind the window in the person's language; 2e, the hand-over ask as a row; 2f, *Done and failed* and its record;

**Why.** The press was drawn across every page because it had to be found; CONFIRMSEEN1 made it findable in other ways (a row, a
balloon, ten minutes), so the band can go where the person chooses to look, and the announcement can do the finding. One place for
what arrives answers the owner's two reports at once: Overview can be short only if what it listed has a home, and a press can leave
the page only if there is a place that is not the page. A view in the frame is that place because it is the same on every view and
stands beside the session the person attends. Building the presentation first on the transports that exist fixes what the owner hit
with no store changed, and each later step stands on what is built. Keeping what was read once per machine is what two windows on
two workspaces need, and keeping the announcements to what waits or holds work, never an agent's message on the desktop, follows
D188.
