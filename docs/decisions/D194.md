1. **The CLI hands the family to the driver's program.** `daoris driver|agent|plugin|tool …` runs `daoris-driver` with the same
   family and words; its output and exit are the run's. The program is the one `DAORIS_DRIVER_PROGRAM` names (the dev loop's and a
   rehearsal's build), else the one in this package's install at `app/daoris-driver/`; else the verb is refused, exit 2, saying where
   the program is, and nothing is done. Never `PATH`, a checkout's build found by itself, or a TypeScript fallback; a
   `DAORIS_DRIVER_PROGRAM` that is not an existing apphost (a `.dll`, a `.cmd` or `.bat`, a quoted value) is refused, exit 2. The two
   are one build, an install's `app/` or a checkout's: a host lacking a family, a sub-verb or a flag says its usage, exit 2. It starts
   through the CLI's one spawn site with the terminal's streams and its environment exactly, not the tools' `PATH`, which would change
   the npm the host's `agent install` finds, and the CLI writes nothing before it. At a terminal the host starts an agent's own
   installer or sign-in with the console's streams in turn, as the CLI's `relay` does, so the person answers its prompts. The host's
   0, 1 and 2 are the run's; a failed start is 2 with its message, a Ctrl+C's end 2 with nothing said, any other status or signal 2
   naming it. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+Break leave the CLI waiting for the host, which takes them in one handler for the four families. The
   doctrine commands reach no spawn and stay offline. A machine with the CLI alone, and a checkout naming no program, lose the four
   families. Design §1.1.
2. **The program is the verb.** Each family is a command in the driver library, as `access` is, routed by the host: its grammar, its
   usage, its switch through `InstanceDoor.PressAsync` (the running Daoris's client, else alone), and its words. A short change holds
   the lease from the top, a read and a long verb none, `VerbLeases` reading each family through its command's own reading.
   `DAORIS_DRIVER_CONFIG` names a home the driver family acts on alone; `DAORIS_HARNESS_CONFIG` names one for the agents' wiring,
   accounts, keys and versions, but the rules and their proposals are `DAORIS_HOME`'s and keep its door. Design §1.2.
3. **A long verb publishes under the lease.** A download, an agent's own installer or sign-in, and a key read from the person hold
   nothing while they wait; what they make is staged where no reader takes it and no other run meets it: a download's or a channel
   pin's staging named by its process and swept once that process is gone; a sign-in's new account folder, made first as the window
   makes it under an id drawn at random, and removed in a `finally` where the sign-in did not finish. Its end is published under
   `HomeLease.ForVerb`, waited for as the desktop's loop waits for an act and never refused, after a look for what another run
   published meanwhile. Beside a desktop that started meanwhile the lease holds nothing and each file's keep lock protects the
   publication: the writers of `tools.json`, `accounts.json`, `cooling.json` and `harnesses.json`, which take none today, take it in
   the rows that publish into them. Design §1.4.
4. **Each change is one typed operation in the driver library**, called by its route and by the command alone: typed input, the facts
   it judges by, a refusal or a write, and the press's own fields. The route keeps its payload's reading, a live process's coordination
   (a plugin stopped before its folder moves), its publication and its answer, `STATE` unchanged and unsplit; the command keeps its
   grammar, its reads and its words, and writes the route's payload from its input, held by a round trip through the route's reading.
   An operation's coded refusal maps to its code through one library table both read. The host does not reference the modules.
   Design §1.3.
5. **A person's press alone acts on the terminal's word**: the lease keeps one writer and is no confirmation, and with nothing running
   the terminal is the person's door (D50). With the window running a route's press is confirmed there; the route-less verbs D171's

**Why.** The judgement was already .NET's; what the TypeScript held besides was each verb's grammar and words, and with them a second
reader of every file, a second pipe client and a second lease, each held to the driver by a table. Making the driver's program the
verb leaves one of each and keeps the words a person types. Moving only the operation, not the route, gives the alone path the same
implementation with every bridge parser, coordination and answer where it is.

**Rejected** (design §6): a TypeScript fallback where the program is missing; the CLI keeping its grammar, reads and words and asking a
host verb for each write; each route's whole body moved into the library with `STATE` split into a `ChoicesAnswer` (the first reading);
the host referencing the modules; a failure category so that domain refusals exit 1 (the first reading); the local service as the alone
path; finding the program on `PATH`, in a checkout's build or by the home's install; a `.cmd` launcher, or a variable naming anything
but the apphost; handing the program the tools' `PATH`; a pump from the console's input into the window's `Send` for a terminal's
sign-in; one Ctrl+C handler for every verb; a lease held for a sign-in's or a download's whole run, and a sign-in staged and moved; a
publication refused after the lease's thirty seconds; a lock of the staging's own; the unlocked writers moved onto `JsonKeeping.Save`;
a marker in a sign-in's folder; a named harness file sending the whole agent family alone; a person's press alone only with the
person key; the marker's absence read as the person; folding `daoris plugin` into the host's `plugins`.
