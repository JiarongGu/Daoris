## D194 — A `daoris` management verb is run by the driver's own program: `daoris driver|agent|plugin|tool …` hands its words to `daoris-driver`, which is the running Daoris's client or acts alone under the home's lease, with one body per change in the driver library and one text per verb; the families' TypeScript retires (2026-10-21)

**Decision (PUSH1f2, proposed).** **Status: proposed**, for D191's reads: Codex's. The author judges that it changes no foundation (no
record's data model, no wire's shape, no lock's rule: the pipe, the lease and the sent-press record stay the driver's as built), so
Fable is not asked; the steward decides. It answers D182 point 6's alone half and D171's PUSH1f note's *Left*. Read at `49aa46e47`:
with no instance, each of the four families still writes with its own TypeScript editor (`service.ts:533-545`, `door.ts:170-185`),
about 18,950 lines with 887 shared and about 16,200 of tests, while every rule they apply has its driver twin and the route bodies
are thin wrappers over it. The contract is `docs/2026-10-21-alone-path-design.md`.

1. **The CLI hands the family to the driver's program.** `daoris driver|agent|plugin|tool …` runs `daoris-driver` with the same
   family and words; its output and exit are the run's. The program is found where `DAORIS_DRIVER_PROGRAM` names it (the dev loop's
   and a rehearsal's build), else in this package's install at `app/daoris-driver/`; else the verb is refused, exit 2, saying what
   installs it, and nothing is done. Never `PATH`, a checkout's build found by itself, or a TypeScript fallback. It starts through the
   CLI's one spawn site, the program itself and never its `.cmd` launcher, with the terminal's own streams and environment. The
   doctrine commands reach no spawn and stay offline. Design §1.1.
2. **The program is the verb.** Each family is a command in the driver library, as `access` is, routed by the host: its grammar, its
   usage, its switch through `InstanceDoor.PressAsync` (the running Daoris's client, else alone), and its words. A change holds the
   lease from the top, a read none, and a verb that runs a child or waits on the person only around the writes that end it. A file
   `DAORIS_DRIVER_CONFIG` or `DAORIS_HARNESS_CONFIG` names is written alone, never sent, holding nothing. Design §1.2.
3. **Each route's body lives in the driver library**, called by the route (which adds the nudge, the publish and the state) and by
   the command's alone act; a door reads a world the route hands from its loop and the host builds from the home. The driver's
   standing choices answer `ChoicesAnswer`, `STATE`'s fields read from the files, which the route composes with the loop's own; a
   refusal with a code is the library's `CodedRefusal`, its codes moved into the library and named by the modules' `Refusals` as
   before. The host does not reference the modules. Design §1.3.
4. **A person's press alone acts on the terminal's word**: the lease keeps one writer and is no confirmation; with nothing running
   there is no window to confirm in, and the terminal is the person's door (D50). From an environment carrying `DAORIS_SESSION_ID`,
   the marker Daoris hands a session it starts, a person's press that no instance's confirmation covers is refused, exit 1, saying
   where the person makes it: a marker, not a proof (D156 point 4). Design §2.
5. **One text per verb**: one words function, called over the route's answer and over the alone door's answer, the same shape; a
   refusal one line, `{verb}: {sentence} ({code})`; the domain's refusals exit 1 on both paths, the person's refusal and expiry 1,
   everything else 2. Held by a modules test running each verb both ways over one home. The port keeps today's text by a table of the
   CLI's cases, run once through the TypeScript verb before it retires. Design §3.
6. **The families' TypeScript retires row by row**, each module in the row that frees its last reader, found by a search recorded in
   the row; its twins row loses the CLI's column then, and a table another twin still reads stays and moves to the driver's fixtures.
   Design §4.
7. **The rows**: PUSH1f2a, the seam proved by the driver's standing choices; 2b, the driver's workflows and cycles; 2c, the tools; 2d,
   the agents' rules, trust and settings; 2e, the agents' tools and accounts; 2f, the plugins and the last census. Design §5.

**Why.** The judgement was already .NET's; what the TypeScript held besides was each verb's grammar and words, and with them a second
reader of every file, a second pipe client and a second lease, each held to the driver by a table. Making the driver's program the
verb leaves one of each, keeps the words a person types, and turns the twins' tables into the driver's own tests.

**Rejected** (design §6): a TypeScript fallback where the program is missing; the CLI keeping its grammar, reads and words and asking a
host verb for each write; the host referencing the modules; the local service as the alone path; finding the program on `PATH`, in a
checkout's build or by the home's install; starting it through its `.cmd` launcher; a lease held for a sign-in's or a download's
whole run; a person's press alone only with the person key; folding `daoris plugin` into the host's `plugins`.

**Amends,** once accepted: D50's as-built note (the management class spawns the driver's program through its one site, and reaches
the network for the service's verbs alone once the agents' and tools' downloads are the host's); D171's PUSH1f note (the CLI's pipe
client, lease and door leave with the families; the host's `InstanceDoor` is the one client); the terminal RPC design §9 (the client
sits in the driver's program, not `service.ts`). D182 point 6 stands and is what this builds. While proposed, D50, D171 and D182 carry
a note pointing here.

**Held by:** the rows' proofs (design §5), each family's both-ways modules test and text table, and the family rehearsal's `daoris`
phases unchanged.
